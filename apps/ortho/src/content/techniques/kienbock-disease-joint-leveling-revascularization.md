## Indications

Surgical joint leveling or revascularization for Lichtman Stage I–IIIA Kienböck disease, with the specific procedure chosen by ulnar variance; correction of fixed carpal collapse in addition for Stage IIIB; salvage procedures for Stage IV/advanced collapse with arthrosis.

## Positioning & setup

Ulnar variance must be measured on a true neutral-rotation PA radiograph (shoulder abducted 90°, elbow flexed 90°, forearm neutral) — pronated/supinated views falsely change apparent variance by several millimeters and must not be used for surgical planning, since variance is the primary determinant of which procedure is biomechanically effective.

## Steps

1. **Stage I–IIIA, ulnar-negative variance**: radial shortening osteotomy (joint leveling) — unloads the lunate by transferring load to the ulna; typically 2–4 mm shortening via a transverse or step-cut osteotomy with plate fixation.
2. **Stage I–IIIA, ulnar-neutral/positive variance** (radial shortening not biomechanically effective): capitate shortening/capitate-hamate fusion, or STT (scaphotrapeziotrapezoid) fusion — unloads the lunate by an alternative mechanism (shortening the capitate column or redistributing load through the distal row).
3. **Revascularization procedures** (used across Stages I–IIIA, particularly when variance is neutral or the surgeon wishes to directly address bone viability): pedicled vascularized bone graft from the distal radius (4+5 extensor compartmental artery, ECA) or metacarpal, sometimes combined with core decompression and/or temporary external fixation for lunate unloading/distraction.
4. **Stage IIIB** (fixed carpal collapse in addition to unloading): STT or scaphocapitate fusion (corrects scaphoid flexion and shortens the capitate column), combined with radial shortening if ulnar-negative, or a vascularized graft with structural (corticocancellous) support to restore lunate height.
5. **Stage IV / advanced collapse with arthrosis** (salvage): PRC, limited intercarpal fusion, wrist denervation, or total wrist fusion/arthroplasty; lunate prosthetic arthroplasty (pyrocarbon) is an option with renewed but still limited-evidence interest for unreconstructable lunate collapse without established pancarpal arthritis.

## Pitfalls

- Radial shortening is ineffective if the baseline is ulnar-neutral or -positive — confirm variance on a true neutral-rotation PA view before committing to this procedure; using it in the wrong variance group will not unload the lunate.
- Lunate excision with tendon/prosthetic interposition is largely historical given poor long-term results — do not default to it; modern pyrocarbon lunate prosthetic arthroplasty is the contemporary alternative, though evidence quality remains limited and it is not first-line.
- Stage IIIB requires correcting the fixed carpal collapse itself, not just unloading the lunate — joint leveling alone (without STT/scaphocapitate fusion or structural graft support) is inadequate once carpal height ratio has dropped below 0.54.

## Post-op protocol

Cast/splint immobilization 6–8 weeks (longer, up to 10–12 weeks, for limited intercarpal fusions until trabecular bridging is seen on CT); progressive AROM and strengthening from 8–12 weeks; return to heavy labor at 3–4 months. Vascularized graft procedures often follow a similar 8–12 week protected phase before loading.
