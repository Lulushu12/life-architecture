## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications**
- **Non-vascularized grafting**: scaphoid nonunion with a viable proximal pole (no AVN on MRI/intraoperative bleeding) and minimal deformity.
- **Vascularized grafting**: AVN of the proximal pole, failed prior grafting, or long-standing (>1 year) nonunion. Recent comparative literature continues to support reserving vascularized grafting for these AVN/proximal-pole/revision scenarios rather than routine use in all nonunions, given added morbidity (donor site, operative time) without proven benefit when the proximal pole is viable.

**Contraindications**
- **Absolute**: active infection at the operative or donor site; a proximal fragment too small to support any fixation regardless of graft type (consider proximal-pole excision/salvage pathway instead).
- **Relative**: heavy smoking or uncontrolled diabetes (impairs graft/wound healing and raises nonunion risk for any graft type); free vascularized MFC grafting in a patient/team without microsurgical capability or in a patient who cannot tolerate the added operative time and donor-site morbidity of a lower-extremity harvest; established SNAC-stage arthrosis (grafting will not address the arthritic component — proceed to the salvage ladder instead); very long-standing nonunion with severe carpal collapse and secondary arthrosis where salvage is more appropriate than an attempt at union.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Imaging**: CT with sagittal reconstructions along the true scaphoid axis to quantify humpback deformity, dorsal cortical angle, and lunate-scaphoid angulation — this defines how much corticocancellous wedge volume will be needed to restore length/angulation. MRI with gadolinium to assess proximal fragment vascularity, understanding that its sensitivity/specificity for AVN prediction is imperfect; many surgeons still rely on intraoperative punctate bleeding from the proximal fragment as the practical determinant of viability, so plan to make this assessment intraoperatively regardless of the MRI result.
- **Graft/implant selection and sizing**: for non-vascularized grafting, template the corticocancellous wedge size (iliac crest or distal radius donor) from the CT-measured humpback deformity. For pedicled vascularized grafting, plan the 1,2-ICSRA pedicle harvest from the distal radius. For free vascularized grafting, plan medial femoral condyle (MFC) harvest — the flap is based on the descending genicular artery, which arises from the superficial femoral artery roughly 14 cm proximal to the knee joint line, with the pedicle running lateral to the adductor magnus tendon; a pedicle length of about 5 cm is usually sufficient, but confirm recipient-vessel distance intraoperatively. Have a headless compression screw system available for fixation of the inset graft.
- **Equipment**: standard hand set plus curettes for necrotic bone removal; microsurgical set (loupes or operating microscope, microvascular clamps, 9-0/10-0 suture) for free MFC grafting; separate sterile lower-extremity prep/draping setup if MFC harvest is planned (two-team approach is efficient — one team exposes the scaphoid while the other harvests the flap).
- **Case-specific planning**: decide graft type before the day of surgery based on the vascularity workup, but be prepared to convert from non-vascularized to vascularized intraoperatively if the proximal fragment fails to bleed on decortication despite reassuring preoperative imaging.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

General anesthesia is typical for vascularized grafting (particularly free MFC, given the need for two simultaneous surgical fields and microsurgical anastomosis); regional block (supraclavicular/axillary) with sedation is reasonable for non-vascularized grafting or pedicled 1,2-ICSRA grafting alone. Position supine with the operative arm on a radiolucent hand table and tourniquet applied after exsanguination. For free MFC harvest, position the ipsilateral or contralateral knee (surgeon preference, typically ipsilateral for a two-team simultaneous approach) with a bump under the thigh and a separate sterile leg prep/drape and tourniquet, allowing knee flexion to about 90° for medial femoral condyle exposure. Bring fluoroscopy in for the wrist field. Mark the volar (Russe/FCR-interval) approach landmarks on the wrist, and mark the medial knee landmarks (adductor tubercle, medial femoral condyle) if MFC harvest is planned.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. **Expose the scaphoid** through a volar approach along the FCR tendon sheath, extending from the scaphoid tubercle to the distal radius, and open the radiocarpal capsule to expose the nonunion site.

2. **Debride the nonunion site.** Excise fibrous/sclerotic nonunion tissue back to bleeding cancellous bone on both fragments; curette any cystic change. Assess proximal fragment vascularity directly by observing punctate bleeding after decortication — this intraoperative assessment is the practical determinant of viability regardless of preoperative MRI findings, and a proximal pole that fails to bleed should prompt conversion to a vascularized graft plan even if not originally intended.

3. **Correct humpback deformity before fixation.** With the wrist held in neutral/slight extension, restore scaphoid length and angulation by opening the volar defect created by debridement; this defines the size and shape of the structural corticocancellous wedge graft needed. This step is essential regardless of graft type — failure to correct humpback perpetuates DISI and midcarpal malalignment even after the fracture eventually unites.

4. **Non-vascularized grafting (viable proximal pole).**
   a. Harvest a corticocancellous wedge from the iliac crest (or distal radius for smaller defects) sized to the humpback correction defined in step 3.
   b. Shape and press-fit the graft into the volar defect (Russe inlay/Matti-Russe technique), restoring scaphoid length and correcting the intrascaphoid angle.
   c. Provisionally pin the reduction, confirm scaphoid length/angle restoration on fluoroscopy, then place a headless compression screw across the construct for definitive fixation. Reported union rates ~85–95% in this favorable subgroup.

5. **Vascularized pedicled grafting (1,2-ICSRA).**
   a. Identify and mobilize the 1,2-intercompartmental supraretinacular artery pedicle from the dorsal distal radius, preserving its vascular supply from the radial artery.
   b. Harvest a corticocancellous bone segment on this pedicle from the dorsal distal radius metaphysis.
   c. Rotate the pedicled graft into the scaphoid defect (dorsal or volar depending on approach and defect location), inset and press-fit it, and fix with a headless screw or K-wires. Reported union rates ~80–90%.

6. **Free vascularized medial femoral condyle (MFC) grafting (established AVN/failed prior graft).**
   a. With a two-team approach, expose the medial femoral condyle through a medial knee incision, identify the descending genicular artery pedicle (arising from the superficial femoral artery approximately 14 cm proximal to the joint line, coursing lateral to the adductor magnus tendon), and harvest a corticoperiosteal or corticocancellous segment of the proximal, articular-adjacent MFC on this pedicle — typically 5 cm of pedicle length is adequate.
   b. Orient the graft so its periosteal/cortical surface will face **volarly** once inset — this preserves the vascularity of the flap and allows the cortical surface to provide the structural correction of the humpback deformity.
   c. Excise the necrotic proximal pole back to bleeding bone, template and shape the MFC graft to fit the defect, and secure it to the scaphoid waist/remaining fragment with a headless compression screw (e.g., Herbert-type).
   d. Perform the microvascular anastomosis under loupe or microscope magnification — the pedicle is anastomosed end-to-side to the radial artery or end-to-side to the dorsal carpal arch, chosen intraoperatively based on vessel length, caliber, and orientation relative to the recipient site.
   e. Confirm flap perfusion (color, capillary refill, Doppler signal) before releasing the tourniquet definitively and before closure. Union rates in modern series are commonly cited in the 85–100% range even in AVN nonunion, though this technique requires microsurgical expertise and added operative time.

7. **Screw vs plate.** Headless screw fixation is standard for all graft types above; a volar locking plate is occasionally used instead for unstable comminuted nonunion needing additional structural support, though evidence for plating is limited to small series.

8. **Closure.** Close the capsule and FCR sheath at the wrist, close the donor site in layers (iliac crest, distal radius, or knee as applicable) with attention to hemostasis (particularly at the vascular anastomosis site for free MFC grafts), and apply a well-padded thumb-spica splint or cast.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Treat the intraoperative bleeding test as the deciding factor for graft type, even when preoperative MRI seemed reassuring or equivocal — MRI sensitivity/specificity for AVN is imperfect, and a proximal fragment that fails to bleed after decortication should prompt conversion to a vascularized technique.
- Correct humpback deformity by restoring scaphoid length/angulation with the wrist held in neutral-to-slight extension before finalizing graft shape — trying to correct angulation after the graft is already inset is far harder than planning for it up front.
- For free MFC grafting, orient the graft's cortical/periosteal surface volarly — this both preserves the vascular supply carried in the periosteum and lets the sturdier cortical bone do the structural work of correcting the humpback deformity.
- Use a two-team approach for free MFC grafting (one team preparing the recipient scaphoid site while the second harvests the flap) to reduce combined operative and tourniquet time.
- Template pedicle length generously for free MFC grafts — roughly 5 cm is usually sufficient, but confirm the actual distance to the chosen recipient vessel (radial artery or dorsal carpal arch) intraoperatively before committing to the harvest length.
- Have both potential recipient vessels (radial artery and dorsal carpal arch) exposed and available before completing the MFC harvest, so the anastomosis site can be chosen intraoperatively based on vessel caliber and orientation rather than being locked in preoperatively.
- Confirm screw length and central placement across the graft-host junction with the same rigor as in primary scaphoid fracture fixation — a malpositioned screw can compromise an otherwise well-executed graft.
- Donor-site morbidity from MFC harvest is reported as minimal in modern series, but counsel patients about a knee incision/scar and brief activity restriction regardless.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- Failure to correct humpback deformity perpetuates DISI and midcarpal malalignment even after the fracture eventually unites — length/angulation restoration is not optional.
- Vascularized grafting adds donor-site morbidity and operative time without proven benefit when the proximal pole is viable — reserve it for AVN, failed prior grafting, or long-standing (>1 year) nonunion rather than using it routinely.
- MRI/intraoperative-bleeding assessment of proximal fragment vascularity is imperfect; do not treat a negative or ambiguous vascularity study as fully ruling out AVN — many surgeons still weight intraoperative bleeding as the practical determinant.
- Free MFC grafting requires microsurgical expertise for the anastomosis; this should factor into case selection and surgeon/team readiness, not just the biological indication.
- Orienting the MFC graft's periosteal surface dorsally (rather than volarly) compromises both its vascular supply preservation and its ability to structurally correct the humpback deformity.
- Anastomotic thrombosis is the principal early complication specific to free vascularized grafting — confirm flap perfusion by direct inspection and Doppler before final closure, and have a low threshold to explore/revise if perfusion is equivocal.
- Screw malposition at the graft-host junction can undermine fixation stability even when the biological graft choice was correct — apply the same central-axis, appropriate-length screw principles used in primary fracture fixation.

## Postoperative Protocol

Thumb-spica immobilization 6–8 weeks (longer, 10–12 weeks, for vascularized MFC grafts with a structural block). Serial CT to confirm union before advancing loading — union is commonly assessed at 10–12 weeks postoperatively but may take up to 4–6 months. AROM and progressive strengthening follow confirmed union; return to unrestricted heavy use only after union is confirmed, often 4–6 months total.
