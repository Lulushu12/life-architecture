## Indications

The definitive procedure for moderate-to-large ulnar-positive variance causing impaction, whether or not a TFCC tear is present. Simultaneously tightens the TFCC/radioulnar ligaments (improving DRUJ stability) and tensions the interosseous membrane. Milder cases (typically ≤2–3 mm positive variance) with central impaction can instead be managed with an arthroscopic or open wafer procedure, which avoids osteotomy healing time but does not address DRUJ or ECU mechanics.

## Positioning & setup

A dedicated cutting/compression guide/jig system is used to ensure parallel cuts at the ulnar diaphysis and avoid malrotation.

## Steps

1. Confirm ulnar-positive variance on a true neutral-rotation PA radiograph (shoulder abducted 90°, elbow flexed 90°, forearm neutral) — pronated/supinated views falsely change apparent variance and must not be used for planning.
2. Approach the ulnar diaphysis and perform a transverse or step-cut/oblique osteotomy using a dedicated compression plate/jig system.
3. Shorten 2–4 mm to bring variance to neutral or slightly negative.
4. Apply the compression plate construct, ensuring parallel cuts and correct rotation.
5. For chronic DRUJ instability not adequately addressed by TFCC repair/USO alone, add DRUJ stabilization (ligament reconstruction with tendon graft, or arthroplasty for an arthritic DRUJ) as a separate procedure.

## Pitfalls

- Overshortening or malrotation causes DRUJ incongruity/impingement pain; use the dedicated cutting/compression guide specifically to avoid this.
- Symptomatic hardware requiring plate removal is reported in up to 20–30% of cases in some series — discuss this with patients preoperatively as an expected trade-off.
- Step-cut/oblique osteotomy designs increase bone contact area and may reduce nonunion risk versus a simple transverse cut, but comparative outcome data show both oblique-locking-plate and transverse-nonlocking constructs can achieve reliable union with attention to technique — construct choice is less critical than surgical execution.
- Undercorrection leaves residual impaction symptoms — confirm final variance intraoperatively/postoperatively rather than assuming the planned shortening was achieved.

## Post-op protocol

Splint/brace for 2 weeks, then removable brace. AROM of the wrist/forearm (pronosupination) is started early (within 1–2 weeks) to avoid stiffness — forearm rotation is the ROM domain most at risk and should be tracked specifically. Avoid axial loading/heavy gripping until the osteotomy shows bridging callus (typically 8–12 weeks). Full unrestricted use/sport by 3–4 months.
