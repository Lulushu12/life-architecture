## Indications

End-stage ankle osteoarthritis (≈70–80% post-traumatic — rotational ankle fracture, chronic instability, pilon fracture — rather than primary/idiopathic) that has failed non-operative management (activity modification, NSAIDs, lace-up/Arizona brace, weight loss, diagnostic/therapeutic intra-articular steroid injection) and is beyond the window for a joint-preserving supramalleolar osteotomy. Choice between arthrodesis and total ankle replacement (TAR) is individualized by age, activity demand, deformity, bone quality, and adjacent-joint status through shared decision-making rather than dictated by a single trial.

**TAR candidacy**: older (>50–55 years), lower-demand patients with preserved/correctable coronal alignment (generally <10–15° deformity with modern designs able to address more via concurrent osteotomy/ligament balancing), adequate bone stock, and no significant talar avascular necrosis, Charcot, or uncontrolled diabetic neuropathy (relative/absolute contraindications).

**Arthrodesis**: the reliable reference standard across ages/demand levels, at the cost of stiffness and long-term adjacent-joint arthritis.

## Steps

- **Arthrodesis**: open compression screw fixation is standard; arthroscopic arthrodesis is an option for patients with minimal deformity.
- **Total ankle replacement**: modern fixed- and mobile-bearing implant designs; correct coronal/sagittal deformity concurrently with osteotomy or ligament balancing rather than relying on the implant alone to accommodate malalignment; address hindfoot deformity (PCFD/cavovarus) before or simultaneously with TAR, as an uncorrected hindfoot is a leading cause of edge-loading and early failure.

## Pitfalls

- Uncorrected coronal/sagittal deformity is a major driver of TAR edge-loading and early failure — correct it concurrently, not after the fact.
- Arthrodesis nonunion is ~5–10% for isolated tibiotalar fusion in healthy hosts, rising substantially (15–30%) in smokers, avascular necrosis, Charcot/neuropathic patients, or TTC/pantalar constructs.
- Progressive adjacent-joint (subtalar, midfoot) arthritis over 10–20 years is near-universal after arthrodesis and is the principal long-term trade-off vs. TAR.
- TAR-specific complications: periprosthetic fracture, component subsidence/loosening, syndesmotic nonunion (designs requiring syndesmotic fixation), wound healing complications from the thin anterior soft-tissue envelope, deep infection (~1–2%, with higher revision-surgery morbidity than nonunion after failed fusion), and eventual revision (bearing exchange or conversion to fusion) in a meaningful minority by 10–15 years.
- The **TARVA trial** (Goldberg et al., *Ann Intern Med* 2022; HTA report 2023) — a UK multicenter RCT of ~303 patients aged 50–85 randomized to TAR vs. arthrodesis — found both groups improved substantially and similarly on the MOXFQ at 3 years, but with different complication and reoperation profiles between arms; the trial is explicitly described as inconclusive regarding superiority of either procedure, so neither option should be presented as categorically better.

## Post-op protocol

**Arthrodesis**: 2 weeks splint NWB; 6 weeks NWB in cast (non-Charcot) — for Charcot/neuropathic patients extend NWB to 8–12+ weeks given impaired bone healing biology; serial radiographs (± CT if union uncertain) before progressing; gradual weightbearing progression over 4–6 weeks once union is confirmed; brace/orthotic support and PT for 3–6 months; return to sedentary work ~2–3 months, physical work/sport typically 6–9 months and only after solid union.

**Total ankle replacement**: protocol varies by implant/approach, but a common pathway is splint NWB 2 weeks, then transition to a CAM boot with progressive weightbearing beginning around 2–4 weeks (faster than arthrodesis since press-fit/cemented fixation does not require bony union across a joint); early ROM exercises started once the wound is stable; formal PT emphasizing ankle ROM, gait training, and proprioception from 4–6 weeks; return to low-impact activity 3–4 months; avoid high-impact/running activity indefinitely per most surgeons given implant longevity concerns. Modern designs report ~90% implant survivorship at 10 years in registries and selected series.
