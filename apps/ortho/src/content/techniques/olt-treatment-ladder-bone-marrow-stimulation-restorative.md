## Indications

Osteochondral lesion of the talus (OLT) that has failed a trial of protected weightbearing, NSAIDs, and activity modification (6–12 weeks), or that presents with imaging features (Hepple stage, size, cyst, displacement) predicting failure of non-operative management. Procedure choice is size- and stage-based (see Hepple / Berndt-Harty classification).

## Positioning & setup

Restorative procedures (OATS, particulated allograft, ACI) typically require a medial or lateral malleolar osteotomy for posterior/central lesion access; bone marrow stimulation is performed arthroscopically. Weightbearing CT (WBCT)–based lesion mapping increasingly guides pre-operative size/containment planning.

## Steps

1. **Primary lesion <100–150 mm² (~<10–15 mm diameter), Hepple 1–2b, or failed non-op**: arthroscopic **bone marrow stimulation** (microfracture/drilling) — reliable first-line surgery with good short/mid-term outcomes.
2. **≥100–150 mm², cystic (Hepple 5), or failed prior bone marrow stimulation**: restorative options — **osteochondral autograft transfer (OATS/mosaicplasty)** from the ipsilateral knee, **particulated juvenile allograft cartilage**, or **autologous chondrocyte implantation (ACI/MACI)** — typically requiring a medial or lateral malleolar osteotomy for posterior/central lesion access.
3. **Large lesions with subchondral bone loss**: bone grafting with or without bone marrow stimulation ("sandwich" technique), or **fresh osteochondral allograft** for very large or revision lesions where autograft would create unacceptable donor-site morbidity.
4. **Hepple 3 (acute, displaceable fragment of adequate size)**: primary **fixation** (headless compression screws or bioabsorbable pins) rather than excision, preserving native cartilage — best results in acute, large, reducible fragments.

## Pitfalls

- WBCT-based lesion mapping increasingly guides the size/containment decision more accurately than conventional (non-weightbearing) CT.
- Always assess and correct concurrent hindfoot malalignment (varus/valgus) or chronic ankle instability — uncorrected malalignment is a major driver of OLT recurrence/progression.
- Persistent pain/incomplete relief after bone marrow stimulation occurs in a meaningful minority even at long-term follow-up; 10-year survivorship data confirm durable but not universal benefit, with a meaningful subset requiring secondary procedures over time — this reinforces size/lesion-quality selection rather than universal first-line use of bone marrow stimulation.
- Donor-site morbidity (ipsilateral knee pain) is a recognized trade-off with OATS.
- Graft delamination/failure is a recognized risk with ACI.
- Subchondral cyst recurrence can occur regardless of technique.

## Post-op protocol

**Bone marrow stimulation**: non-weightbearing or touch-down weightbearing 2–6 weeks depending on lesion size/location; early passive ROM/CPM encouraged to promote fibrocartilage organization; progressive weightbearing from 6 weeks; full activity 3–4 months.

**Restorative procedures (OATS/ACI)**: more conservative — non-weightbearing 6 weeks, protected weightbearing to 10–12 weeks, full activity typically not before 6–9 months given the biology of graft incorporation/maturation.
