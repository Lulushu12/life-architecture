## Indications

Isolated, symptomatic patellofemoral OA (Iwano grade III–IV) with preserved tibiofemoral compartments, typically in patients younger than the usual TKA population, whose maltracking (if any) has already been or can be concurrently addressed. PFA is **not** appropriate when instability/maltracking is the dominant driver without correction, in inflammatory arthropathy, or with any meaningful tibiofemoral chondral disease (these progress to needing TKA conversion). Patellofemoral OA with lateral maltracking/instability as the dominant driver needs realignment (tibial tubercle osteotomy, MPFL reconstruction), not isolated PFA.

## Positioning & setup

Not detailed in the monograph beyond standard patellofemoral joint exposure; pre-operative skyline/Merchant imaging plus CT or MRI measurement of TT-TG distance and trochlear dysplasia (Dejour A–D) is used whenever instability/maltracking is part of the symptom complex, since isolated wear without instability is treated differently from dysplasia-driven arthrosis.

## Steps

1. **Confirm isolated PF disease and exclude a maltracking-dominant presentation** that would instead require realignment surgery (tibial tubercle osteotomy, MPFL reconstruction) rather than, or in addition to, arthroplasty.
2. **Select implant design family:**
   - **Inlay**: trochlear component recessed flush with native cartilage; less dependent on precise rotational alignment; a more "cartilage-replacement" philosophy.
   - **Onlay**: resurfaces and extends proximally onto the anterior femoral cortex; more anatomically constrained trochlear geometry; requires more precise rotational positioning, analogous to TKA femoral component placement.
   - A pooled meta-analysis (76 studies, >4,000 patients, search through January 2025) found both design families deliver good implant-specific survivorship, with implant technique/generation mattering more than the inlay/onlay distinction per se.
3. **Address any concurrent maltracking** at the time of arthroplasty if instability is present but not the dominant driver, rather than deferring it.

## Pitfalls

- **Progression of tibiofemoral compartment OA** requiring conversion to TKA is the most common reason for late revision — strict patient selection (truly isolated PF disease) up front is the main mitigation.
- **Persistent anterior knee pain** is the leading cause of early revision.
- **Patellar maltracking/instability** can occur if the trochlear component is malrotated — reinforces the need for precise rotational positioning, especially with onlay designs.
- **PFA-to-TKA conversion** carries a small but real risk of inferior outcomes versus primary TKA if patellar bone stock or trochlear geometry is compromised — another reason to be strict about isolated-disease selection criteria at the index procedure.
- Modern (second-generation) implants have markedly improved on the poor track record of first-generation designs; counsel patients using contemporary rather than historical survivorship data — international registry data show approximately 90% survival at 5 years and 82% at 10 years, and a 2025 series in isolated Iwano grade IV disease reported 86% survival at 10 years. Most revisions occur early (within ~2.5 years).
- Comparative GRADE-assessed evidence (2025) supports PFA over TKA in appropriately selected isolated PF OA on the basis of better early functional recovery, while acknowledging TKA's superior long-term implant survivorship as a trade-off to discuss with patients.

## Post-op protocol

Generally follows a UKA-like accelerated pathway (immediate weight-bearing as tolerated, early ROM, faster functional recovery than TKA) given the absence of tibiofemoral bone resection — return to low-impact activity by 6–8 weeks, full recovery by 3 months in most series.
