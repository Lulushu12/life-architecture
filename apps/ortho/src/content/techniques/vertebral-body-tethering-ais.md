## Indications

Vertebral body tethering (VBT) received FDA **Humanitarian Device Exemption** approval for skeletally immature patients with progressive idiopathic scoliosis of 30–65°. Ideal candidates: Sanders stage 2–4/Risser 0–2 with substantial growth remaining, flexible curves correcting to <30° on bending/traction films, thoracic curves (the best-studied pattern). It is best framed as a **narrow, well-selected alternative** to fusion, not a general substitute — long-term (10-year+) durability data are still maturing.

## Positioning & setup

Placed via anterior vertebral body access to allow screw placement along the convexity of the curve; the monograph does not detail further room/positioning specifics beyond the anterior approach implied by the technique.

## Steps

1. **Confirm candidacy**: Sanders stage 2–4/Risser 0–2, curve magnitude 30–65°, flexible curve correcting to <30° on bending/traction films, thoracic pattern preferred (best-studied).
2. **Place anterior vertebral body screws** along the convex side of the structural curve.
3. **Connect the screws with a tensioned polymer cord**, applying asymmetric compression across the convex growth plates to modulate growth (growth modulation, not fusion) and gradually correct the curve as the patient continues to grow.
4. **Assess intraoperative curve correction** — inadequate intraoperative correction is a recognized predictor of later treatment failure.

## Pitfalls

- **Tether breakage**: reported in a meaningful minority of cases in early-to-mid-term series; FDA IDE cohort follow-up found roughly 50% cumulative tether breakage by 36 months.
- **Overcorrection** is a recognized risk of the growth-modulation mechanism.
- **Conversion to fusion** occurs in a non-trivial proportion of patients at longer follow-up.
- **Durability declines over time** in reported cohorts: ~85% success at 1 year, 75% at 2 years, 62% at a mean 3.8 years in FDA IDE cohort follow-up — counsel patients that success is not guaranteed to be durable past skeletal maturity.
- An umbrella review (2024) found the underlying VBT evidence base still heterogeneous and low-to-moderate quality overall, supporting continued restriction to well-selected, flexible thoracic curves rather than broad adoption as a fusion alternative.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a VBT-specific rehabilitation protocol distinct from general fusionless-surgery recovery; follow-up centers on serial radiographic monitoring of curve correction, growth, and tether integrity, with close surveillance for the failure modes above (breakage, overcorrection, need for conversion to fusion) through skeletal maturity and beyond.
