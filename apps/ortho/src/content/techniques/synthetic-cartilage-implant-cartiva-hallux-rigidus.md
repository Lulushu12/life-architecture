## Indications

A motion-preserving alternative to arthrodesis for Coughlin-Shurnas grade 3 (and selected grade 4) hallux rigidus, for patients who prioritize retained 1st MTP motion and accept a somewhat higher reoperation likelihood than fusion. Arthrodesis remains preferable for grade 4, high-demand laborers, or patients who prioritize the single most durable outcome.

## Positioning & setup

Dorsal approach to the 1st MTP joint, as for cheilectomy/arthrodesis. Minimal bone resection is required compared with arthrodesis preparation, consistent with the implant's motion-preserving intent.

## Steps

1. Access the 1st MTP joint and perform minimal bone resection of the metatarsal head articular surface.
2. Press-fit the polyvinyl alcohol (PVA) hydrogel implant into the prepared metatarsal head.
3. Confirm implant seating and joint motion intraoperatively before closure.

## Pitfalls

- The pivotal **MOTION randomized trial** (Baumhauer et al., *JBJS* 2016) found the implant non-inferior to arthrodesis for pain and function at 2 years, but mid-to-long-term series report a **secondary/revision surgery rate meaningfully higher than fusion** — commonly cited in the roughly 8–14% range at 4–7 years across published cohorts, though the true rate is series-dependent (one 5-year series reported only 5% revision with no loosening; a separate shorter-follow-up cohort reported an 18.2% total reoperation rate including MUA for stiffness).
- Recognized failure modes are **implant subsidence** and **progressive metatarsal-side degeneration**.
- Counsel patients that the implant trades a small-but-real revision-risk premium for motion preservation, and that **revision to fusion after a failed implant remains a reliable salvage**.
- Roughly a third of patients in some cohorts develop postoperative stiffness requiring manipulation under anesthesia (MUA) — set expectations preoperatively.
- Not an independently superior procedure to arthrodesis — it is a different risk/benefit trade (motion vs. durability), and patient selection (grade, activity demand, motion priority) should drive the choice.

## Post-op protocol

- Similar early protected weight-bearing to cheilectomy: postoperative shoe, weight-bear as tolerated.
- **Earlier initiation of ROM** than after arthrodesis, given the motion-preserving intent.
- Return to activity generally parallels cheilectomy timelines — **6–8 weeks** for most activity.
- General return-to-sport gating: near-full pain-free active ROM, pain-free single-leg heel-rise/hop, swelling resolved, graduated activity progression.
