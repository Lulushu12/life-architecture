## Indications

Applies to subaxial cervical fracture-dislocations meeting SLIC ≥5 or AO Spine type C, or B2/B3 morphology with a neurologic (N) deficit — unifacet/bifacet dislocation, facet fracture-dislocation, and multi-column subaxial instability. This includes fractures in patients with ankylosing spondylitis/DISH (M3/M2 modifier), which require a distinct management approach.

## Positioning & setup

Closed reduction under fluoroscopy uses Gardner-Wells tongs/traction in an awake, cooperative patient with serial neurologic exams during the procedure. Anterior or posterior surgical positioning is selected based on which column requires direct access (see Steps).

## Steps

1. **Attempt awake closed reduction** with serial neurologic exams under fluoroscopy (Gardner-Wells tongs/traction) for unifacet/bifacet dislocation in an awake, cooperative patient — historically performed without pre-reduction MRI when the patient can be reliably examined during traction, though practice is trending toward pre-reduction MRI when readily available, particularly if the patient is obtunded.
2. **Select anterior approach (ACDF with plate)** when the primary pathology is anterior column/disc compression: direct discectomy, decompression of retropulsed disc/bone, and fixation; also provides direct access for corpectomy if a comminuted body fracture (AO A3/A4) is present.
3. **Select posterior approach (lateral mass screw-rod fixation)** for predominantly posterior tension-band/facet injuries (B1/B2, unilateral/bilateral facet dislocation) — allows direct facet reduction and is more robust for pure ligamentous instability.
4. **Select combined anterior-posterior fixation** for highly unstable, multi-column injuries (AO type C, three-column disruption) or when anterior-only fixation is judged insufficient (e.g., comminuted body with posterior ligamentous disruption).
5. **For ankylosing spondylitis/DISH (M3/M2 modifier)**: treat as long, rigid-lever-arm fractures — image the entire spine given the high rate of non-contiguous fractures, avoid manual reduction/extension maneuvers that can worsen displacement, and use long-segment posterior fixation spanning well above and below the fracture given the inherently unstable, brittle, poorly vascularized ankylosed bone.

## Pitfalls

- In an ankylosed spine (AS/DISH), avoid manual reduction/extension maneuvers — these can worsen displacement in brittle, poorly vascularized bone; use long-segment fixation instead of standard-length constructs.
- Whole-spine imaging should be obtained with a low threshold in AS/DISH patients, given the disproportionately high rate of missed or under-treated fractures and epidural hematoma in this population.
- Choose the approach (anterior, posterior, or combined) based on which column dominates the injury pattern — anterior for disc/body compression, posterior for tension-band/facet injury, combined for three-column AO type C injuries or when anterior-only fixation is judged insufficient.
- Pre-reduction MRI practice varies; obtunded patients or those who cannot be reliably examined during traction generally warrant pre-reduction MRI when readily available.

## Post-op protocol

Post-op fixation patients typically mobilize with brace support per surgeon protocol, with formal ROM restrictions (avoid combined flexion-rotation for facet injuries, avoid extension in extension-type injuries) until early fusion consolidation (~6–8 weeks). Interval imaging at 6–12 weeks confirms healing/stability. Progressive resistance exercise and dynamic core stabilization follow once fusion is trending (3–6 months). Return to sedentary work is often possible by 6–8 weeks for stable fixation; heavy manual labor typically requires 3–6 months, longer if fusion levels are extensive or a neurologic deficit persists. Return-to-sport/contact-activity clearance requires solid radiographic fusion (≥4–6 months), full pain-free ROM, and normalized strength (≥90% of contralateral/baseline), and clearance for contact/collision sports remains individualized and controversial, particularly for constructs involving C1-C2.
