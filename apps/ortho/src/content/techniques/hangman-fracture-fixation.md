## Indications

Surgical fixation is indicated for Levine-Edwards Type II/IIA hangman fractures that fail or are unsuitable for halo vest treatment, and is mandatory for Type III fractures (bilateral pars fracture with facet dislocation). Type I fractures are stable and treated with a rigid collar rather than surgery.

## Positioning & setup

Choice of approach depends on injury pattern: anterior positioning/exposure for C2-3 discectomy and fusion when disc/ALL disruption and translation predominate; posterior positioning for C1-C3 instrumented fusion or direct pars screw fixation when facet dislocation or posterior element integrity favors a posterior approach.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the injury pattern on CT and MRI** — assess C2-3 disc/ALL disruption, translation, and angulation to differentiate Levine-Edwards types and determine instability.
2. **For halo-amenable Type II injuries**: attempt reduction and halo vest immobilization first; Type IIA specifically should not be treated with axial traction given the risk of catastrophic distraction through the disrupted disc.
3. **Anterior C2-3 discectomy and fusion (ACDF)**: indicated for injuries with significant disc/ALL disruption and translation, providing direct restoration of the C2-3 segment.
4. **Posterior C2 pars/pedicle screw fixation**: direct pars screw fixation of the fracture, or C1-C3 posterior instrumented fusion, for Type III with facet dislocation, or when posterior element integrity allows direct fracture fixation while preserving C2-3 motion.

## Pitfalls

- Do not apply axial traction to Type IIA injuries — the anteriorly-opened disc space and flexion-distraction mechanism make traction likely to worsen distraction rather than reduce the fracture; treat with extension and compression in a halo, or go to primary surgical fixation.
- Type III's facet dislocation component is what mandates surgery — reduction and fixation (posterior C2-3, or combined) is required regardless of how displaced the pars fracture itself appears.
- Choose ACDF when the pathology is primarily anterior (disc/ALL disruption with translation) and posterior C2 pars/pedicle fixation when posterior element integrity allows direct fracture fixation while preserving C2-3 motion — approach selection should follow the dominant injury pattern rather than surgeon preference alone.

## Post-op protocol

Post-op management follows the general upper cervical fracture rehabilitation pathway: wound care and pain control acutely (0–2 weeks); formal ROM restrictions until early fusion consolidation (~6–8 weeks) in the subacute phase (2–12 weeks), with interval imaging at 6–12 weeks; progressive loading and brace weaning once radiographic fusion is confirmed (3–6 months). Return to sedentary work is often possible by 6–8 weeks for stable fixation; heavy manual labor typically requires 3–6 months. Return-to-sport/contact-activity clearance requires solid radiographic fusion (≥4–6 months), full pain-free ROM, and normalized strength, and remains individualized and conservative for cervical spine injuries.
