## Indications

- **Arthroscopic Bankart repair**: recurrent anterior shoulder instability, ISIS ≤3–4, on-track Hill-Sachs lesion, glenoid bone loss <13.5% (acceptable up to <20% in lower-demand patients). Also considered as primary treatment (within weeks of injury) for first-time high-risk dislocators (<25 years, male, contact/collision, ISIS ≥4, off-track).
- **Remplissage** (added to Bankart repair): off-track Hill-Sachs with subcritical or acceptable glenoid bone loss. It is an adjunct to Bankart repair, not a stand-alone procedure, and is not appropriate as sole augmentation when glenoid loss approaches or exceeds ~20–25%.

## Positioning & setup

Arthroscopic technique; standard shoulder arthroscopy portals are used to access the anteroinferior glenoid for anchor placement and, when remplissage is added, the posterosuperior Hill-Sachs defect and infraspinatus tendon/posterior capsule.

## Steps

1. Diagnostic arthroscopy: systematic circumferential evaluation, including the axillary pouch, to identify concomitant lesions (SLAP tear, posterior labral extension, HAGL) that must be addressed to avoid persistent instability.
2. Identify and mobilize the anteroinferior labrum; recognize an ALPSA (medialized, healed-down labrum) which requires full mobilization rather than simple re-tensioning.
3. Place 3–4 suture anchors low on the glenoid face, starting at the 5–7 o'clock position.
4. Advance the capsulolabral tissue superiorly and laterally to restore the labral bumper and capsular tension.
5. Address any concomitant SLAP lesion or posterior labral extension in the same setting.
6. If the Hill-Sachs lesion is off-track (or borderline with contact/overhead sport demand), proceed to remplissage: place 1–2 anchors within the Hill-Sachs defect, pass sutures through the infraspinatus tendon and posterior capsule, and tie to fill the defect, rendering it extra-articular and preventing engagement.

## Pitfalls

- Anchor placement too far medial on the glenoid neck under-restores the labral bumper effect.
- Missing an ALPSA leads to inadequate mobilization and residual instability.
- Failing to recognize off-track status pre-operatively is the single largest driver of "unexplained" recurrence after isolated Bankart repair.
- Remplissage does not correct glenoid-side bone loss — do not rely on it as sole augmentation when glenoid loss approaches or exceeds ~20–25%; a bone-block procedure is needed instead.
- A small, generally non-significant loss of external rotation (a few degrees) is reported after remplissage on meta-analysis but does not appear to compromise overall shoulder function or 5-year return-to-sport rates.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–4**: sling immobilization (neutral to slight internal rotation, per surgeon preference); passive/active-assisted ROM starting within the first 1–2 weeks (pendulum, passive elevation to tolerance, external rotation limited to 0–20° to protect the repair); no active internal rotation loading, no lifting.
- **Weeks 4–6**: discontinue sling; progress passive/active ROM toward full elevation and external rotation as tolerated; begin isometric rotator cuff/scapular stabilizer activation; avoid combined abduction-external rotation ("apprehension position") loading.
- **Weeks 6–12**: progressive resistance strengthening (rotator cuff, scapular stabilizers, deltoid), restore full ROM, closed-chain stability drills, begin sport-specific proprioceptive work around week 8–10.
- **Weeks 12–16**: sport-specific/return-to-throw or contact-preparation progressions; plyometric and eccentric loading for overhead athletes.
- **Return to non-contact sport**: ~4–5 months. **Return to contact/collision sport**: typically 5–6 months, contingent on full painless ROM, ≥90% strength symmetry on isokinetic testing, negative apprehension, and successful sport-specific functional testing — not solely time-based.
- Remplissage does not meaningfully alter this timeline in most protocols, though some surgeons extend early external rotation restriction slightly given the infraspinatus tenodesis component.
- Reported recurrence: isolated Bankart 10–20% (up to 67% in high-risk young contact athletes); Bankart + remplissage ~10% vs. 30% for Bankart alone at medium-term follow-up (Woodmass et al., *AJSM* 2024 RCT).
