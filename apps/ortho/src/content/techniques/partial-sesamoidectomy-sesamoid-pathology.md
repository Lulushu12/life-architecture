## Indications

Refractory sesamoiditis, osteonecrosis, or nonunion of the tibial or fibular sesamoid after failure of non-operative management (rigid orthotic with sesamoid cutout, activity modification, CAM boot immobilization 4–6 weeks). **ORIF/bone grafting with screw or tension-band fixation** is reserved instead for a symptomatic fracture nonunion in a young, active patient who wishes to avoid sesamoidectomy altogether.

## Positioning & setup

Approach to the affected sesamoid (the tibial sesamoid is far more frequently symptomatic than the fibular, given greater axial load transmission during push-off). Confirm the correct sesamoid and pathology category preoperatively — differentiate acute fracture, sesamoiditis/stress reaction, and osteonecrosis from an asymptomatic bipartite variant with MRI or bone scan when radiographs are equivocal.

## Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis (fracture, osteonecrosis, or refractory sesamoiditis) and that non-operative management has failed.
2. Approach the affected sesamoid and identify the fractured/necrotic pole.
3. Perform **partial excision** — remove only the fractured or necrotic pole — preserving the remainder of the sesamoid and the flexor hallucis brevis (FHB) sling.
4. For a symptomatic nonunion in a young active patient wishing to avoid excision, perform **ORIF/bone grafting with screw or tension-band fixation** instead.

## Pitfalls

- **Partial (not complete) sesamoidectomy is strongly preferred** whenever a partial excision can achieve symptom relief while preserving the FHB sling.
- **Complete tibial sesamoidectomy** materially increases the risk of iatrogenic **hallux valgus** from loss of the medial static/dynamic restraint.
- **Complete fibular sesamoidectomy** increases the risk of iatrogenic **hallux varus**.
- Excising the wrong sesamoid, or both sesamoids without clear indication, risks persistent pain and weakness of push-off.
- Any procedure that destabilizes the 1st ray sesamoid apparatus (including complete sesamoidectomy) redistributes load laterally and is a recognized mechanism for iatrogenic transfer metatarsalgia under the lesser metatarsal heads.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: protected weight-bearing in a stiff-soled postoperative shoe/boot, off-loading the forefoot.
- **Weeks 2–6**: progressive weight-bearing, transition toward regular supportive/stiff-soled shoes.
- **Return to running/impact/push-off-intensive sport**: often **8–12 weeks**, since the FHB sling and 1st MTP push-off mechanics need to accommodate the altered sesamoid anatomy.
