## Indications

Chronic PJI with an identified organism preoperatively, an adequate soft-tissue envelope for primary closure, absence of gross purulence or extensive bone loss requiring staged reconstruction, and a host fit for a single larger procedure. Historically reserved for a narrow European (originally Endo-Klinik) cohort; indications have broadened in recent registries and RCT data.

## Positioning & setup

Performed under a single anesthetic. The procedure requires a planned "clean" phase mid-case — a change of gloves, instruments, and draping between the explantation/debridement portion and the reimplantation portion — so this must be arranged in advance with a separate clean set of instruments and implants available.

## Steps

1. Complete removal of all components and cement.
2. Extensive debridement of infected/necrotic tissue and bone.
3. "Clean" phase: change gloves, instruments, and draping before proceeding to reimplantation.
4. Reimplant new components in the same setting, often using antibiotic-loaded cement, guided by the known organism's sensitivity for both local and systemic antibiotic selection.
5. Use local antibiotic delivery adjuncts as indicated — antibiotic-loaded bone cement, antibiotic-loaded calcium sulfate/hydroxyapatite beads, or intrawound vancomycin powder — to achieve local antibiotic concentrations exceeding the biofilm minimum biofilm eradication concentration (MBEC) while limiting systemic toxicity (evidence for vancomycin powder specifically remains mixed in recent high-quality RCT data).
6. Start empiric broad-spectrum systemic antibiotics at debridement, narrowing to targeted therapy once cultures/sensitivities return; add rifampin combination therapy (never as monotherapy) for confirmed rifampin-susceptible staphylococcal biofilm once the wound has stabilized/is closed.
7. Plan for a minimum 12-week total systemic antibiotic duration.

## Pitfalls

- Success depends heavily on preoperative organism identification and adequate soft tissue — two-stage remains the default for unknown-organism, poor-soft-tissue, or highly resistant-organism chronic PJI.
- Requires immediate intraoperative antibiogram-guided cementing capability and surgical team experience/infrastructure to execute safely — this determines whether a team should attempt one-stage rather than the clinical picture alone.
- The historical dogma that two-stage is the unconditional "gold standard" is being revised: a 2024 pooled meta-analysis (40 studies, ~8,700 patients) found no significant difference in reinfection or reoperation rates between one- and two-stage approaches overall, and a 2024 multicenter RCT (AAHKS-presented) reported 2-year success of 98% for one-stage vs 91% for two-stage — including patients with resistant organisms and draining wounds — though this remains a single trial requiring confirmation.
- The deciding factor for one- vs two-stage is patient/organism selection (known organism, adequate soft tissue, team infrastructure), not a fixed hierarchy of stages.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a distinct rehabilitation pathway for one-stage exchange — its rehabilitation section is explicitly organized around DAIR versus two-stage pathways. The antibiotic strategy follows the same systemic framework as the rest of the treatment algorithm: minimum 12-week total systemic antibiotic course (the ICM 2025-endorsed floor), with indefinite suppressive therapy considered in McPherson host C, difficult-to-treat organisms, or incomplete source control. Serial CRP/ESR monitoring and wound surveillance at follow-up apply as for any PJI treatment pathway, with a low threshold to re-image or re-aspirate if pain, swelling, drainage, or a marker plateau/rebound occurs.
