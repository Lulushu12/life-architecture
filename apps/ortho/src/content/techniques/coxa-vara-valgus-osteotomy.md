## Indications

Indicated for developmental coxa vara with a Hilgenreiner-epiphyseal angle (HEA) >60°, progressive deformity on serial imaging, a neck-shaft angle <100–110° with functional abductor weakness/limp, or a widening physeal defect ("Fish sign") suggesting risk of frank femoral neck non-union/stress fracture if untreated. Mild, non-progressive deformity (HEA <45–60°, minimal limp) can instead be observed with serial radiographs at 6–12-month intervals.

## Positioning & setup

Subtrochanteric level, with or without concurrent derotation.

## Steps

1. **Perform a subtrochanteric valgus-producing osteotomy**, with or without derotation, to restore the neck-shaft angle and normalize the HEA.
2. **Overcorrect the HEA slightly, to below ~38–40°** at the time of surgery, since some loss of correction with growth is expected and undercorrection is a well-documented cause of recurrence.
3. **Fix with a blade-plate or locking pediatric hip plate construct**, which provides more reliable angular stability during healing than simple screw fixation given the high shear forces across this region.

## Pitfalls

- Undercorrection is a well-documented cause of recurrence requiring repeat osteotomy, especially when surgery is performed very young with substantial remaining growth — deliberately overcorrect the HEA at the time of surgery.
- A simple screw construct is inadequate for the shear forces at this level — use a blade-plate or locking pediatric hip plate.
- Counsel families explicitly that recurrence requiring revision osteotomy is not rare, and that continued radiographic surveillance through skeletal maturity is required regardless of initial correction quality.

## Post-op protocol

- Weeks 0–6: protected weight-bearing (partial, per fixation construct), active-assisted ROM, avoidance of torsional/twisting loads across the osteotomy site.
- Weeks 6–12: progressive weight-bearing to full as radiographic union is confirmed; physiotherapy for hip and thigh strengthening, gait retraining.
- Months 3–4: return to full unrestricted activity/sport once solid radiographic union is confirmed and strength/ROM are symmetric with the contralateral limb.
- Long-term: continued radiographic surveillance through skeletal maturity regardless of initial correction quality, given the meaningful minority requiring revision osteotomy for recurrence.
