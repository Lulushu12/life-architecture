## Indications

Posterior C1-C2 fusion (Goel-Harms technique) is the workhorse for upper cervical instability that cannot be managed with anterior odontoid screw fixation or a collar: Jefferson (C1 ring) fractures with transverse atlantal ligament (TAL) disruption where the fracture pattern allows C1 lateral mass screw placement, and Type II odontoid fractures where anterior screw fixation is not feasible (comminution, reverse obliquity, associated TAL injury, poor anterior trajectory). It is also frequently chosen as primary treatment in many elderly odontoid patients given its lower nonunion risk with rigid segmental fixation, since it does not depend on precise fracture-line geometry.

## Positioning & setup

Requires fluoroscopic or navigated visualization for C1 lateral mass and C2 pedicle/pars/translaminar screw placement; adequate C1 lateral mass bone stock must be confirmed on preoperative imaging.

## Steps

1. **Confirm indication and screw feasibility** — for Jefferson fractures, confirm TAL disruption (rule of Spence overhang, or direct MRI TAL assessment) and that the fracture pattern allows safe C1 lateral mass screw placement; for odontoid fractures, confirm anterior screw fixation is not feasible or not preferred.
2. **Place C1 lateral mass screws** bilaterally.
3. **Place C2 pedicle, pars, or translaminar screws** (Harms/Goel-type construct) — Magerl transarticular screws are an alternative fixation option for the same segment.
4. **Connect with a rod-based construct**, often supplemented with structural bone graft to promote fusion.
5. **For odontoid fractures**, this construct achieves fixation without dependence on the fracture-line geometry that anterior screw fixation requires, at the trade-off of sacrificing roughly 50% of cervical rotation.

## Pitfalls

- Reserve occipitocervical fusion (rather than C1-C2 fusion) for fractures precluding safe C1 screw placement (severely comminuted lateral mass) or when there is concomitant occipital condyle/occipitocervical dissociation.
- Isolated posterior arch fractures with an intact anterior arch and intact TAL rarely need surgery — confirm TAL status before proceeding to fusion for a Jefferson-pattern fracture.
- The construct sacrifices approximately 50% of cervical rotation — a relevant counseling point, particularly for younger, active patients being treated for an odontoid fracture where an anterior screw might otherwise have preserved rotation.
- In frail elderly patients, this technique achieves substantially higher fusion rates than nonoperative management but carries real perioperative morbidity/mortality — shared decision-making incorporating frailty, baseline mobility, and patient goals is essential rather than defaulting to surgery based on fracture type alone.

## Post-op protocol

Post-op management follows the general upper cervical fracture rehabilitation pathway: wound care and pain control acutely (0–2 weeks) with brace-protected mobilization typically beginning within 24–72h; formal ROM restrictions until early fusion consolidation (~6–8 weeks) in the subacute phase (2–12 weeks), with interval imaging at 6–12 weeks; progressive loading and brace weaning once radiographic fusion is confirmed (3–6 months). Return to sedentary work is often possible by 6–8 weeks for stable fixation; heavy manual labor typically requires 3–6 months. Return-to-sport/contact-activity clearance requires solid radiographic fusion (≥4–6 months), full pain-free ROM, and normalized strength, and remains individualized and conservative for cervical injuries, particularly those involving C1-C2.
