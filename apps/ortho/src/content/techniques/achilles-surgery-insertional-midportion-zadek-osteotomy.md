## Indications

Insertional or midportion Achilles tendinopathy that has failed conservative management (eccentric/heavy-load loading protocols, heel lift, NSAIDs, ESWT). Technique choice differs by disease site given their distinct loading biomechanics and pathophysiology (insertional disease at the calcaneal enthesis, often with a Haglund deformity and retrocalcaneal bursitis; midportion disease at the hypovascular watershed zone 2–6 cm proximal to the insertion).

## Positioning & setup

Insertional procedures use a central-splitting or medial approach to the calcaneal insertion and Haglund prominence. Midportion procedures are approached directly over the diseased segment for open or percutaneous debridement.

## Steps

**Insertional:**
1. Haglund exostectomy + debridement of the diseased insertion through a central-splitting or medial approach.
2. If **>50% of the insertion is detached** during debridement, reattach with suture anchors (single- or double-row technique; outcomes are comparable between constructs, with double-row offering a biomechanically stronger footprint).
3. Add **FHL transfer** for augmentation in extensive debridement, revision cases, or lower-demand/elderly patients needing tendon strength support.
4. **Zadek osteotomy** (dorsal closing-wedge calcaneal osteotomy) is an alternative that leaves the tendon insertion undisturbed, unloading the insertion indirectly, with earlier weightbearing than detachment-reattachment techniques — a defined learning curve applies, and long-term recurrence data remain more limited than for traditional debridement.

**Midportion:**
1. Open or percutaneous longitudinal tenotomy/debridement of degenerative tissue.
2. **Gastrocnemius recession** for isolated gastroc contracture on Silfverskiöld testing — unloads the tendon and is increasingly used as an adjunct or alternative to direct tendon debridement.
3. **FHL transfer** when >50% of tendon cross-section is debrided, or in revision/chronic degenerative cases.

Endoscopic/minimally invasive approaches for insertional disease achieve comparable clinical outcomes to open surgery with reduced wound complication rates, supporting a shift toward less invasive technique where the surgeon has the requisite experience.

## Pitfalls

- Wound dehiscence/infection is notably higher with traditional detachment-reattachment insertional approaches — a driver of interest in minimally invasive/Zadek alternatives.
- Sural nerve injury is a recognized risk.
- Re-rupture and persistent insertional pain can result from inadequate debridement or heterotopic bone reformation.

## Post-op protocol

**Insertional detachment-reattachment**: splint/NWB 2 weeks; boot with heel wedge NWB to partial WB 2–6 weeks (bone-tendon healing at the calcaneal insertion is slower than midsubstance repair); progressive WB 6–10 weeks as wedges are gradually removed; formal PT with progressive ROM and eccentric strengthening from ~8–10 weeks; return to running/sport 4–6 months.

**Zadek osteotomy**: allows comparatively earlier progressive WB (often boot WB from 2 weeks) since the insertion itself is undisturbed, with osteotomy union (~6 weeks) guiding progression.
