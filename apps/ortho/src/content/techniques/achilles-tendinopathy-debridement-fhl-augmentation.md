## Indications

Reserved for Achilles tendinopathy (mid-portion or insertional) that has failed ≥6 months of conservative treatment (progressive loading, adjunctive injections/shockwave). Mid-portion surgery targets degenerative tissue debridement; insertional surgery additionally addresses bony impingement (Haglund prominence) and, when detachment is extensive, footprint reattachment.

## Positioning & setup

Approach and exposure follow site of disease: mid-portion procedures are performed through open or endoscopic access over the affected segment; insertional/Haglund procedures require exposure of the posterosuperior calcaneus and the calcaneal insertion.

## Steps

1. **Mid-portion:** open or endoscopic debridement of degenerative tissue/paratenon release ± tendon-splitting.
2. If >50% of tendon cross-section requires excision, augment with **FHL tendon transfer** (single-incision technique preserves strength, transferred through a bone tunnel or interference screw into the calcaneus) to protect against post-debridement rupture and boost push-off power.
3. **Insertional:** resection of the Haglund prominence + debridement of the diseased insertion.
4. If detachment exceeds ~50% of the footprint, re-anchor the tendon with suture anchors.
5. Consider FHL augmentation for extensive insertional disease, especially in lower-demand/higher-re-rupture-risk patients.

## Pitfalls

- Failing to augment with FHL transfer when >50% of the tendon cross-section has been excised raises re-rupture risk after debridement.
- Bony Haglund impingement requires resection of the prominence itself — insertional debridement alone is inadequate when a mechanical bony driver is present.
- Persistent pain occurs in an estimated ~10-25% of patients even after surgery — set expectations accordingly, particularly in patients who have not clearly failed a full loading-based conservative trial first.
- Wound healing problems are more common after insertional/Haglund resection than after mid-portion debridement.
- Sural nerve injury is a recognized risk during Achilles tendinopathy surgery, as with rupture repair.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a distinct staged rehabilitation timeline specific to post-surgical tendinopathy debridement; expected recovery is guided by the same principles as the general Achilles tendon reconditioning framework — progressive protected loading once the surgical repair/reattachment is secure, followed by graded return to activity — with FHL augmentation specifically intended to allow more confident progressive loading after extensive debridement by protecting against rupture and restoring push-off power. Reported outcomes: persistent pain in ~10-25% of patients even after surgery; wound healing problems more common with insertional/Haglund resection; re-rupture risk if FHL augmentation is omitted despite extensive debridement.
