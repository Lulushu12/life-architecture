## Indications

Displaced (>1 mm) or unstable (vertical-oblique/Russe III, comminuted) waist fractures, proximal-pole fractures, and undisplaced waist fractures in athletes/manual laborers wanting earlier return to function or after informed discussion of the SWIFFT trial trade-offs (faster union and earlier return to work/sport with surgery, but a higher complication rate than cast treatment — implant-related issues, CRPS, scar sensitivity — with no clinically important PRWE difference at 52 weeks or 5 years for undisplaced bicortical waist fractures).

## Positioning & setup

Fluoroscopic guidance is used throughout to confirm central screw placement on both AP and lateral views. Approach is chosen by fracture location: volar-percutaneous for waist/distal fractures; dorsal-percutaneous for proximal-pole fractures, which allows more central, longer screw placement along the true central axis, with the wrist flexed to improve access.

## Steps

1. Select approach by fracture location — volar-percutaneous for waist/distal fractures, dorsal-percutaneous for proximal-pole fractures.
2. For proximal-pole fractures, position the wrist in flexion for the dorsal approach; this gives better central purchase than a volar approach.
3. Place a headless compression screw (historically the Herbert screw; modern variable/differential-pitch designs such as Acutrak or OmniFix-type) centrally along the true longitudinal axis of the scaphoid, confirmed on both AP and lateral fluoroscopy.
4. For displaced or comminuted fractures needing direct visualization, humpback correction, or an associated perilunate injury, convert to open reduction rather than persisting percutaneously.

## Pitfalls

- Eccentric screw placement is the most common technical error and increases nonunion risk — the screw must be centrally placed along the true longitudinal axis on both AP and lateral fluoroscopy, not just one view.
- For proximal-pole fractures, a volar approach gives inferior central purchase compared to a dorsal approach with the wrist flexed — approach selection is not interchangeable for this fracture location.
- Screw prominence/malposition is a recognized hardware complication, and is especially technically demanding to avoid in proximal-pole fixation where central purchase is harder to achieve.
- Open reduction should be reserved for displaced/comminuted fractures needing direct visualization or humpback correction — do not force a percutaneous approach where fragment alignment cannot be adequately controlled closed.

## Post-op protocol

Short-term splint/removable brace for comfort 1–2 weeks (the screw provides enough stability for early motion), active range of motion (AROM) starting within the first 1–2 weeks, progressive strengthening from 6 weeks, return to sport/heavy manual work at 8–12 weeks pending radiographic union (union is typically evident by 6–8 weeks with rigid fixation). Return to contact/collision sport or loaded wrist-extension sport (gymnastics, football, climbing, racquet sports) is generally not before 3–4 months and should be confirmed with CT if there is any doubt about union.
