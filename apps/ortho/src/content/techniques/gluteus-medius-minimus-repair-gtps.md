## Indications

Confirmed partial- or full-thickness gluteus medius/minimus tears on MRI with a positive clinical correlate (weakness, Trendelenburg gait, refractory pain) after at least 6 months of failed conservative management (progressive abductor loading program ± injection).

## Positioning & setup

Technique parallels rotator cuff repair principles applied to the hip. Endoscopic approach is generally favored for lower morbidity and direct bursal visualization. Open repair — occasionally with local tissue augmentation or a synthetic/biologic patch — is reserved for large retracted tears or revision settings.

## Steps

1. Debride degenerative tendon.
2. Perform an anchor-based transosseous-equivalent repair to the trochanteric facets — gluteus medius to the posterosuperior and lateral facets, gluteus minimus to the anterior facet.
3. Favor the endoscopic approach for lower morbidity and direct bursal visualization.
4. Reserve open repair, with local tissue augmentation or a synthetic/biologic patch when needed, for large retracted tears or revision settings with severe fatty infiltration.
5. Consider adding iliotibial band release/bursectomy at the time of gluteal repair for coexisting external coxa saltans (snapping hip) or a thickened, symptomatic ITB — this is rarely performed as an isolated procedure now that GTPS is understood as primarily a tendinopathy.

## Pitfalls

- Outcomes deteriorate with chronic full-thickness tears and significant muscle atrophy (Goutallier grade ≥3), where repair integrity and functional recovery are compromised.
- Fatty infiltration (poor muscle quality) is the strongest negative predictor of persistent abductor weakness/Trendelenburg gait if the repair fails.
- Retear risk is highest in large, chronic tears with significant retraction.
- Repeated preoperative corticosteroid injection carries a theoretical concern for tendon attritional weakening/progression to full-thickness tear — a rationale for limiting injections to 1–2 per year rather than repeated dosing before considering surgery.

## Post-op protocol

| Phase | Timeline | Weight-bearing | Milestones |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Weeks 0–6 | Protected weight-bearing (crutches, brace in abduction/neutral rotation per surgeon preference) to protect repair | Passive ROM only, avoid active abduction, cryotherapy |
| II | Weeks 6–12 | Progress to full weight-bearing, wean brace/crutches | Begin isometric then gentle active-assisted abductor activation, gait retraining (avoid Trendelenburg compensation pattern) |
| III | 3–6 months | Full | Progressive isotonic strengthening, closed-chain single-leg work, return to low-impact activity |
| IV | 6–9 months | — | Return to running/impact activity once strength ≥85–90% of contralateral side and pain-free single-leg stance/step-down testing achieved |
