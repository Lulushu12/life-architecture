## Indications

Smillie **stage III** Freiberg disease — further central collapse of the metatarsal head with a **viable dorsal or plantar hinge of articular cartilage** remaining. This is the joint-preserving procedure of choice at this stage; earlier stages (I–II) are managed conservatively, and later stages (IV–V, loose bodies or flattened arthritic collapse) require debridement or salvage arthroplasty instead.

## Positioning & setup

Dorsal approach to the affected lesser metatarsal head (overwhelmingly the 2nd, occasionally the 3rd). Preoperative MRI is used to confirm the amount of viable articular cartilage remaining and to plan the osteotomy, since collapse visible on plain radiographs may understate or lag the true extent of cartilage viability.

## Steps

1. Confirm a viable dorsal or plantar cartilage hinge on the metatarsal head before proceeding — this is the prerequisite for joint preservation.
2. Perform a **dorsiflexion closing-wedge osteotomy** at the metatarsal neck (Gauthier/Kinnard technique).
3. **Rotate the viable plantar articular cartilage dorsally** into the load-bearing zone of the joint, effectively substituting healthy cartilage for the collapsed dorsal segment.
4. Fix the osteotomy per surgeon preference/construct stability.

## Pitfalls

- The procedure depends entirely on having a **viable cartilage hinge** — attempting it in stage IV–V disease (loose bodies, flattened arthritic collapse) is not appropriate; debridement/loose body excision or salvage (interpositional/implant/resection) arthroplasty is indicated instead.
- **Avascular progression despite osteotomy** can still occur.
- Hardware prominence and residual stiffness are recognized complications.
- If salvage arthroplasty/implant is later required, it carries the same general lesser-MTP arthroplasty complication profile (instability, transfer metatarsalgia) as other lesser-ray salvage procedures.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–4**: protected weight-bearing in a postoperative shoe/boot per fixation stability.
- **Weeks 4–8**: progressive weight-bearing and shoe transition once early union/consolidation is evident radiographically.
- **Return to activity**: typically **8–12 weeks**, guided by radiographic healing.
