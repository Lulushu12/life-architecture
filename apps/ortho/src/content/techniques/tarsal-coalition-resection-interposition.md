## Indications

Symptomatic tarsal coalition (calcaneonavicular or talocalcaneal) that has failed a non-operative trial (activity modification/NSAIDs, short-leg cast or boot immobilization for a flare, then a semi-rigid or UCBL orthotic). Resection with interposition is first-line surgery for a symptomatic calcaneonavicular coalition, and for a talocalcaneal (middle facet) coalition involving less than ~50% of the joint surface area with minimal secondary degenerative change or talar beaking on imaging, in an appropriately selected older child/adolescent. Subtalar or triple arthrodesis is reserved for extensive coalition, significant degenerative change, failed prior resection, or associated fixed hindfoot deformity — growth-sparing resection is exhausted first given the permanence of fusion.

## Positioning & setup

Preoperative CT is the gold standard for characterizing coalition extent, precise anatomy, and surgical planning, especially for talocalcaneal (middle facet) coalitions; MRI adds value for coalitions still fibrous/cartilaginous and for assessing secondary cartilage/joint changes not yet visible on CT/plain films.

## Steps

1. **Confirm coalition type and extent:** foot radiographs (AP/lateral/oblique — calcaneonavicular coalition visible on the 45° oblique as the "anteater sign"; talocalcaneal coalition suggested indirectly by the "C-sign" on lateral view and talar beaking, though plain films frequently miss it) plus CT for definitive characterization.
2. **Resect the coalition** and confirm restoration of subtalar/midtarsal motion intraoperatively.
3. **Interpose material** to prevent bridge reformation — fat graft, extensor digitorum brevis (EDB) muscle belly, or bone wax/cement, chosen by coalition location and surgeon preference.
4. **Reserve subtalar/triple arthrodesis** for extensive coalition, significant degenerative change, failed prior resection, or associated fixed hindfoot deformity, rather than as an initial approach.

## Pitfalls

- Recurrence of the coalition/bar after resection is a recognized failure mode.
- Persistent pain despite adequate resection should prompt consideration of an undiagnosed additional coalition or underlying degenerative change rather than assuming inadequate surgery.
- Interposition graft failure can undermine an otherwise technically adequate resection.
- Symptoms characteristically emerge as the coalition ossifies (calcaneonavicular ~age 8–12; talocalcaneal ~age 12–16) — timing of presentation should inform suspicion even when initial radiographs are equivocal.

## Post-op protocol

- Non-weight-bearing cast/boot for 2–3 weeks.
- Progressive weight-bearing in a removable boot with early subtalar ROM exercises to prevent re-adhesion of the interposition graft.
- Formal physiotherapy for peroneal strengthening and gait retraining, weeks 6–12.
- Return to sport typically 3–4 months.
