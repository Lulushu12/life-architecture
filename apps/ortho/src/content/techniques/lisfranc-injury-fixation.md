## Indications

Operative treatment is indicated for Nunley-Vertullo Stage II/III subtle Lisfranc injuries and for any frank Myerson-pattern diastasis/instability. Nunley-Vertullo Stage I and select truly nondisplaced (<2 mm) injuries confirmed on weight-bearing imaging are treated non-operatively.

## Positioning & setup

Weight-bearing AP/oblique/lateral foot radiographs and CT define bony detail and displacement; the entire midfoot should be imaged and stressed intraoperatively, since Lisfranc injuries frequently co-occur with calcaneocuboid and cuboid injuries.

## Steps

1. **Confirm displacement pattern** by Myerson type to plan which rays need fixation and in what direction.
2. **For ORIF**: place transarticular screws across the medial 3 TMT joints (1st TMT and 2nd TMT ± 3rd TMT, 3.5/4.0 mm) and/or dorsal bridge plating across the 1st–2nd and 2nd–3rd TMT joints (bridge plating avoids traversing articular cartilage with a screw).
3. **Fix the 4th–5th rays** (lateral column) with K-wires or a bridge plate only — avoid rigid screw fixation of the lateral column, which tolerates and requires more physiologic motion; lateral column injuries are essentially never fused primarily.
4. **Consider primary arthrodesis of the medial 3 TMT joints** instead of ORIF when the injury is purely ligamentous (no reconstructable articular surface), when the fracture-dislocation is comminuted/unreconstructable, or when presentation is delayed. Primary arthrodesis avoids a planned second hardware-removal surgery and directly addresses the joints most prone to post-traumatic arthritis (1st–3rd TMT).
5. **Prioritize anatomic reduction** above all else — it is the dominant predictor of outcome regardless of which construct is chosen.
6. **Plan elective hardware removal** at 3–6 months if transarticular screws were used, especially given documented breakage/loosening rates under physiologic load.

## Pitfalls

- Nonanatomic reduction (>2 mm residual displacement) is strongly associated with post-traumatic arthritis and poor functional scores, independent of the construct chosen.
- Rigid screw fixation of the lateral column risks breakage since that column requires physiologic motion — use K-wires or a bridge plate instead.
- The evidence comparing ORIF and primary arthrodesis is mixed: a 2024 RCT-only meta-analysis (5 RCTs, 241 patients) found no significant difference in patient-reported outcomes or complication rates, though primary arthrodesis trended toward fewer secondary procedures/hardware removals; a separate 2024 meta-analysis of 18 studies found better AOFAS and VAS scores with primary arthrodesis (AOFAS 84.4 vs 75.7); a large multicenter RCT found no difference in VAS-FA between arms. Practical synthesis: favor ORIF when joint surfaces are reducible/preservable (especially in younger, high-demand patients), favor primary arthrodesis when injury is purely ligamentous, comminuted, or presentation is delayed.
- Compartment syndrome of the foot is a classic association with high-energy Lisfranc injury — maintain a low threshold to measure compartment pressures.

## Post-op protocol

- 0–6 weeks: NWB, splint/cast, elevation.
- 6–10 weeks: transition to boot, begin protected/progressive weight-bearing guided by fixation construct stability and radiographic maintenance of reduction; bridge-plated/K-wire lateral column fixation generally allows earlier progressive WB than transarticular screws across weight-bearing medial rays.
- 10–12 weeks: full weight-bearing, gait retraining, arch-support orthotic consideration.
- 3–6 months: hardware removal (if transarticular screws used) typically performed at ~4–6 months before full return to high-demand activity, since retained screws across a mobile joint risk breakage under load.
- Return to sport for athletes: typically 6–9 months, later for high-demand cutting/jumping sports; counsel patients that post-traumatic arthritis is common even with anatomic reduction and good early function. Sedentary work 6–10 weeks, manual labor 4–6 months.
