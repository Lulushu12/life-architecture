## Indications

Surgical decompression is indicated for cubital tunnel syndrome that fails a trial of non-operative management (McGowan I-IIa) or presents with measurable intrinsic weakness or advanced disease (McGowan IIb-III). McGowan grade drives the operative threshold, and specific anatomic factors — not grade alone — drive the choice between in situ decompression and anterior transposition.

## Positioning & setup

Not specified beyond the operative approach itself; technique selection (in situ vs transposition) is the primary intra-operative decision point.

## Steps

1. **In situ decompression:** simple release of Osborne's ligament (the roof of the cubital tunnel, the arcuate ligament between the two heads of FCU) ± the intermuscular septum. This is favored as first-line surgery for most McGowan I-IIb cases without gross instability, given lower morbidity and equivalent outcomes to transposition in multiple RCTs/meta-analyses for mild-moderate disease.
2. **Anterior transposition** (subcutaneous or submuscular): reserved for nerve subluxation/snapping over the medial epicondyle, recurrent/revision cases, significant cubitus valgus or bony deformity, or when in situ release leaves inadequate nerve glide.
3. **Choose subcutaneous vs submuscular transposition** based on reliability needs versus recovery burden: submuscular transposition has higher perceived reliability for severe/revision cases but longer immobilization and higher wound morbidity than subcutaneous transposition.
4. **Medial epicondylectomy** is a less commonly used alternative technique.

## Pitfalls

- Nerve subluxation after in situ release favors converting to (or primarily choosing) transposition in predisposed anatomy.
- Wound complications are more common with submuscular transposition than with subcutaneous transposition or in situ release.
- Medial antebrachial cutaneous nerve (MABC) injury causes a painful neuroma if the nerve is not identified and protected during exposure — a common avoidable complication.
- Persistent intrinsic weakness in advanced (McGowan III) cases can occur regardless of technique — counsel patients pre-operatively that severe, longstanding disease may not fully recover.
- Incomplete decompression and new/worsened ulnar nerve symptoms are recognized complications independent of which technique is chosen.

## Post-op protocol

- **In situ decompression:** early motion with brief splint immobilization (days) mainly for comfort, progressive activity as tolerated; light duty in 1-2 weeks, heavier labor at 4-6 weeks.
- **Transposition (especially submuscular):** longer immobilization (1-3 weeks in a posterior splint/sling protecting the transposed segment), then progressive elbow ROM, with heavier lifting/resistance restricted 6-8 weeks.
- Sensory recovery precedes motor recovery after either technique; intrinsic recovery in McGowan III cases may remain incomplete regardless of technique or timing.
