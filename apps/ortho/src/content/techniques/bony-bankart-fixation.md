## Indications

Acute or chronic displaced anteroinferior glenoid rim fracture (bony Bankart) that is large enough to be biomechanically relevant — generally more than ~10–15% of the glenoid articular arc — and reducible to anatomic position. A bony Bankart converts a purely labral (soft-tissue) problem into a bone-loss problem and materially changes fixation strategy and recurrence risk.

## Positioning & setup

Arthroscopic setup as for standard instability surgery; CT is mandatory pre-operatively to assess fragment size, comminution, and healing potential before deciding on fixation vs. bone-block conversion.

## Steps

1. Confirm fragment size, comminution, and healing potential on CT before proceeding.
2. If the fragment is reducible and of adequate size (>~10–15% of glenoid arc) and not significantly comminuted: perform arthroscopic reduction and fixation with suture-anchor capsulolabral repair incorporating the bone fragment.
3. For larger or comminuted fragments, use suture-button or screw fixation to secure the fragment.
4. If the fragment is too comminuted or resorbed to fix, abandon fixation and treat as a bone-loss problem instead — proceed to Latarjet or other bone-graft reconstruction rather than attempting fixation of non-viable bone.

## Pitfalls

- Attempting fixation of a comminuted or resorbed fragment that cannot achieve stable biologic healing; recognize early and convert to a bone-block strategy instead.
- Underestimating fragment size or missing comminution without CT — CT is mandatory, not optional, before committing to a fixation strategy.
- As with soft-tissue Bankart repair, failing to address concomitant lesions (SLAP, posterior labral extension, HAGL) found on diagnostic arthroscopy risks persistent instability.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a rehabilitation protocol specific to bony Bankart fixation. General return-to-sport criteria described for instability surgery apply across procedures and are not purely time-based: full painless active and passive ROM within 5–10° of the contralateral side, rotator cuff/periscapular strength ≥90% of the contralateral limb, negative apprehension/relocation testing, and successful sport-specific functional progression before clearance to contact/collision sport.
