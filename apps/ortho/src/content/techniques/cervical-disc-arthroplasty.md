## Indications

Cervical disc arthroplasty (CDA) is an appropriate alternative to ACDF for 1–2 level radiculopathy in younger patients with preserved disc height, minimal facet arthropathy, no significant kyphosis, and no instability. It preserves motion and is designed to reduce adjacent segment degeneration risk. Contraindications: significant facet arthropathy, kyphotic segmental alignment, ossification/bridging osteophyte, prior fusion at the level, osteoporosis, active infection.

## Positioning & setup

Standard anterior cervical exposure and positioning apply, as for ACDF. CDA is reserved for radiculopathy in appropriately selected patients rather than myelopathy/OPLL, so the severe-stenosis-specific positioning precautions (awake fiberoptic intubation, avoidance of excessive extension) described for higher-risk decompressions apply mainly by extension of general anterior cervical surgery principles rather than being CDA-specific in the monograph.

## Steps

1. **Confirm patient selection**: 1–2 level radiculopathy, preserved disc height, minimal facet arthropathy, neutral/lordotic alignment, no instability, no prior fusion at the level, no significant osteoporosis or active infection.
2. **Perform anterior discectomy** as for ACDF, but with technique aimed at preserving the endplates and segmental motion rather than achieving fusion.
3. **Perform meticulous endplate preparation** and select correct implant sizing/positioning — these are the primary technical safeguards against implant subsidence or migration.
4. **Insert the arthroplasty device**, preserving segmental motion — this motion preservation is the intended mechanism for reducing (not eliminating) adjacent segment degeneration risk.

## Pitfalls

- **Heterotopic ossification (HO)**: reported in a meaningful minority of levels at long-term follow-up, rates varying by implant design and follow-up duration; can progressively restrict segmental motion and, if severe, functionally converts the arthroplasty into an ankylosed segment. Higher-grade HO is more common with longer follow-up.
- **Implant subsidence/migration**: uncommon but a recognized reason for revision; prevented primarily through meticulous endplate preparation and correct implant sizing/positioning.
- **Adjacent segment disease**: occurs at a lower rate than after ACDF per pooled long-term comparative data, but is not eliminated — motion preservation reduces, rather than removes, adjacent segment risk.

## Post-op protocol

- Faster early mobilization than fusion — there is no fusion to protect. Soft collar is optional/brief (days); early ROM is encouraged, since preserving segmental motion is part of the point of the procedure.
- Avoid prolonged immobilization, which could promote heterotopic ossification.
- Return to sedentary work often within 1–2 weeks; manual labor 4–8 weeks, guided by symptom resolution rather than a fusion-healing timeline.
- PT focus: early active ROM, cervical/scapular strengthening.
- **10-year comparative data** (2023–2025 meta-analyses of pooled RCT/cohort data, plus independent prospective RCT and single-center ≥10-year cohort data): CDA shows statistically superior overall clinical success, lower reoperation/revision rates, and fewer adverse events than ACDF at 10 years; NDI and VAS differences favor CDA but do not consistently reach minimal clinically important difference thresholds — the durability/reoperation advantage is more clinically meaningful than the pain-score advantage.
- Return to contact/collision sport is individualized — motion preservation does not remove risk to adjacent unprotected levels.
