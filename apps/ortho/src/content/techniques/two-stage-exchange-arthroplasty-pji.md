## Indications

Chronic PJI without a reliably identified organism preoperatively, extensive bone/soft-tissue loss requiring interval reconstruction planning, difficult-to-treat or multidrug-resistant organisms, a sinus tract requiring interval soft-tissue management, or host/local factors favoring a staged, lower-single-procedure-burden approach.

## Positioning & setup

A staged procedure across two separate anesthetic events, separated by a systemic antibiotic interval. Stage 1 is focused on radical debridement and antibiotic spacer placement; Stage 2 is a standard reimplantation setup once the interval criteria are met. Plan spacer antibiotic composition (heat-stable agent plus a Gram-negative-active agent) in advance, to be tailored once culture results return.

## Steps

1. **Stage 1 — debridement and spacer placement.** Perform radical debridement and remove all components and cement.
2. Place an antibiotic-loaded cement spacer — static or articulating. Articulating is preferred where bone stock allows, to preserve soft-tissue length and joint motion, ease reimplantation exposure, and improve interim function.
3. Compound spacer antibiotics by combining a heat-stable agent (vancomycin) with a Gram-negative-active agent (an aminoglycoside such as tobramycin or gentamicin), tailored to culture results once available.
4. **Interval.** Typically 6 weeks of systemic antibiotic therapy (IV or highly bioavailable oral), followed by a variable antibiotic-free "washout" window before reimplantation (commonly 2–4 weeks); total interval is commonly quoted as 6–12 weeks but varies by center and organism.
5. Reassess serum markers before Stage 2; repeat aspiration if the picture is equivocal.
6. **Stage 2 — reimplantation.** Reimplant once serologic/clinical markers trend toward normal and (if performed) repeat aspiration/intraoperative findings do not suggest persistent infection. Take deep cultures again at reimplantation regardless of preoperative work-up.
7. Narrow systemic antibiotics to targeted therapy per culture; add rifampin combination therapy (never as monotherapy) for confirmed rifampin-susceptible staphylococcal biofilm at Stage 2 once the wound has stabilized/is closed. Plan for a minimum 12-week total systemic antibiotic duration.

## Pitfalls

- Spacer-related complications during the interval: spacer fracture or dislocation, femoral/tibial bone loss around a static spacer, leg-length discrepancy, stiffness (especially with static spacers or prolonged intervals), and interval periprosthetic fracture.
- Reported two-stage infection-free survival is roughly 85–90% at mid-term; one-stage exchange in well-selected chronic PJI cohorts now approaches similar figures (roughly 85–98% in recent RCT/registry data) — selection criteria matter more than "stage number."
- Two-stage remains the default for unknown-organism, poor-soft-tissue, or highly resistant-organism chronic PJI.
- Even successful two-stage exchange (particularly of the knee) generally yields lower functional outcome scores than primary arthroplasty or aseptic revision — set this as a realistic expectation preoperatively.
- Reinfection risk is highest with McPherson host C, S. aureus/resistant organisms, inadequate debridement, and premature or incomplete antibiotic courses.

## Post-op protocol

**Stage 1 to reimplantation interval (weeks 0–6 to 12):** Protected weight-bearing per spacer construct stability — articulating spacers generally allow partial weight-bearing with an assistive device; static spacers or significant bone loss may mandate touch-down/non-weight-bearing, surgeon-specific based on spacer fixation and bone stock. Gentle ROM work with an articulating spacer to preserve soft-tissue excursion and ease reimplantation exposure; static spacers are managed more conservatively with immobilization/bracing.

**Post-reimplantation (Stage 2), weeks 0–2:** Standard early post-arthroplasty protocol — weight-bearing as tolerated (hip/knee primary-style protocol) unless bone-grafting, allograft, or extensile exposure dictates protected loading — with cryotherapy, edema control, and active-assisted ROM.

**Weeks 2–6:** Progressive closed-chain strengthening, gait training, and continued ROM progression, watching closely for recurrent drainage, fever, or rising inflammatory markers as red flags for reinfection.

**Weeks 6–12:** Advance to a full strengthening program, proprioception/balance work, and functional task-specific training (stairs, uneven terrain).

**Months 3–6:** Continue strengthening; formal outcome/functional scoring (e.g., HOOS/KOOS, Oxford scores) at follow-up to track trajectory against expected post-revision benchmarks (typically lower peak function than primary arthroplasty).

**Return to work:** Sedentary 6 weeks; moderate physical work 3 months; heavy manual labor individualized (often 4–6 months) and contingent on confirmed infection clearance.

**Return to sport/high-demand recreational activity:** Low-impact activity from roughly 3–4 months if progressing well; higher-impact/higher-demand activity deferred to 6–12 months, with explicit counseling that revision-for-infection constructs generally carry lower functional ceilings and stricter activity modification than primary arthroplasty, given elevated re-revision risk with high-demand loading.

**Monitoring:** Serial CRP/ESR at defined intervals as a treatment-response surrogate (not a stand-alone "cure" criterion), wound surveillance at every visit, and a low threshold to re-image or re-aspirate if pain, swelling, drainage, or marker plateau/rebound occurs.
