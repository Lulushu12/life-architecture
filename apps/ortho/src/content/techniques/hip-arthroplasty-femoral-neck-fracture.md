## Indications

Arthroplasty is standard of care for displaced (Garden III–IV) femoral neck fractures in patients over roughly 60–65 with low-to-moderate functional demand: fixation in this group carries unacceptable reoperation rates (30–40% for AVN/nonunion/screw cutout) versus ~5–10% arthroplasty revision at comparable follow-up. Arthroplasty is also used when reduction fails or the fracture is comminuted in younger patients initially planned for fixation.

## Positioning & setup

Posterior or anterolateral/direct anterior approach, surgeon-preference-driven. Cemented stem is the default for hemiarthroplasty in the elderly fragility-fracture population, with anesthesia vigilance for bone cement implantation syndrome around cementation. Tranexamic acid (topical or IV, typically 1 g at incision ± a second dose) is a standard adjunct to reduce blood loss/transfusion, applied with individualized caution in patients with recent VTE or high thrombotic risk.

## Steps

1. **Select hemiarthroplasty vs. THA** using the HEALTH trial framework (NEJM 2019, 1495 patients ≥50 years with displaced femoral neck fractures): the primary outcome (secondary hip procedure within 24 months) occurred in ~8% of both groups with no significant difference; serious adverse events trended higher with THA (41.8% vs. 36.7%); functional/pain scores modestly favored THA but fell below the threshold of clinical importance.
   - **Hemiarthroplasty** — appropriate default for most low-demand, older (>75–80), or cognitively impaired patients.
   - **THA** — favored for patients who were ambulatory without aids, cognitively intact, with reasonable life expectancy and higher functional demand, accepting a modestly higher dislocation/reoperation-for-instability risk in exchange for better long-term function.
2. **Select stem fixation** — cemented fixation is favored for hemiarthroplasty in the elderly per the WHiTE 5 trial (NEJM 2022) and subsequent 2023 meta-analyses (24 RCTs, ~3,470 patients): better Harris Hip Scores, lower periprosthetic fracture rates, no significant increase in mortality with modern third-generation cementing technique. Uncemented stems are reserved for relative cementation contraindications (severe cardiopulmonary instability) or select younger/biologically robust THA patients.
3. **Select approach** — posterior vs. anterolateral/direct anterior is surgeon-preference-driven; a posterior approach with capsular repair has an acceptably low dislocation rate and remains the most widely used.
4. **Implant and repair capsule/soft tissue** — robust posterior capsular repair (when a posterior approach is used) further reduces dislocation risk.

## Pitfalls

- Dislocation is higher with THA than hemiarthroplasty; further reduced with dual-mobility constructs or robust posterior capsular repair.
- Periprosthetic fracture risk is lower with cemented stems.
- Acetabular erosion is a hemiarthroplasty-specific long-term complication in more active patients — part of the rationale for choosing THA in higher-demand patients up front.
- Aseptic loosening is a longer-term concern, more relevant to THA given longer expected implant life.
- Serious adverse events trend higher with THA overall despite its functional advantages — individualize selection rather than defaulting to either implant.

## Post-op protocol

- Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT) postoperative day 0–1.
- Hip precautions (posterior approach: avoid combined flexion >90° + adduction + internal rotation) for 6 weeks if a posterior approach is used without robust capsular repair; often relaxed sooner with modern capsular repair techniques and anterior approaches.
- Assisted ambulation with walker/crutches transitioning to cane by 2–4 weeks as balance allows; formal PT for gait, transfers, and strengthening starting POD1.
- Radiographic milestones: AP/lateral films at 2, 6, and 12 weeks.
- Return to sedentary work typically 4–8 weeks post-arthroplasty once pain-controlled and independent with transfers/short-distance ambulation.
- Driving typically resumes at 4–6 weeks post-arthroplasty (later if right-sided posterior-approach THA, given braking-reflex concerns), per surgeon assessment and local licensing regulations.
- Universal secondary-prevention bundle: falls-risk assessment, bone mineral density testing (or presumptive treatment given the fracture itself qualifies as a major osteoporotic fracture), and anti-osteoporotic pharmacotherapy initiated ideally before discharge.
