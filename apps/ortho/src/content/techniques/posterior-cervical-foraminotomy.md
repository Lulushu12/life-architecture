## Indications

Posterior cervical foraminotomy is an excellent option for pure foraminal soft disc herniation or foraminal osteophyte causing unilateral single-level radiculopathy, without central stenosis or kyphosis. It avoids anterior approach morbidity (dysphagia, recurrent laryngeal nerve injury) and preserves motion without an implant. It is best suited to posterolateral, not central, pathology, and should be avoided for central/bilateral pathology, myelopathy, or significant kyphosis.

## Positioning & setup

The monograph does not detail procedure-specific positioning for foraminotomy beyond the posterior approach itself; the general precaution against excessive neck extension during positioning applies whenever significant coexisting stenosis is present, as for other cervical posterior procedures.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the pathology is posterolateral and single-level**: unilateral foraminal soft disc herniation or foraminal osteophyte causing radiculopathy, without central stenosis or kyphosis.
2. **Perform a keyhole laminotomy/foraminotomy** to decompress the exiting nerve root at the affected level.
3. **Preserve segmental motion** — no implant is placed, distinguishing this from ACDF and avoiding fusion-related morbidity.

## Pitfalls

- Not suited to central or bilateral pathology, myelopathy, or significant kyphosis — the procedure only addresses posterolateral, single-level compression and does not correct alignment.
- Because there is no anterior exposure, it avoids the dysphagia and recurrent laryngeal nerve risks specific to ACDF, but it also cannot address anterior-predominant or multilevel compressive pathology.

## Post-op protocol

- Return to sedentary work: 1–2 weeks. Return to manual labor: 4–6 weeks.
- Return to contact/collision sport: individualized — this is the lowest-morbidity option among the operative choices, since there is no fusion to protect.
