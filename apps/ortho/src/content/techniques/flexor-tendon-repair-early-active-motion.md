## Indications

Flexor tendon repair is indicated for FDP and/or FDS lacerations across all Verdan zones, including FDP avulsion injuries ("jersey finger," typically Zone I). Zone II ("no man's land") carries the highest technical difficulty and adhesion/re-rupture risk given the tight fibro-osseous sheath housing both tendons together.

## Positioning & setup

WALANT (lidocaine + epinephrine, no tourniquet) is increasingly the preferred anesthetic approach for elective and many acute flexor repairs, because it allows intra-operative active motion testing to confirm gliding and identify triggering/gapping before skin closure.

## Steps

1. **Core suture with ≥4-strand technique** (e.g., cruciate, Adelaide, Strickland-type) plus a circumferential epitendinous suture is the modern standard to permit early active motion (EAM) protocols. Two-strand repairs are biomechanically insufficient for EAM and predispose to gap/rupture.
2. **Preserve the A2 and A4 pulleys**, which are biomechanically critical; if repair bulk causes triggering, vent judiciously rather than sacrificing the pulley completely, to prevent bowstringing.
3. **Confirm gliding intra-operatively under WALANT** by having the patient actively flex/extend before closure, identifying triggering or gapping while it can still be addressed.
4. **FDP avulsion (jersey finger):** repair with a suture anchor or pull-out button depending on retraction level and bone stock. Delayed presentation (>3-4 weeks) may preclude primary repair, requiring staged reconstruction (silicone rod then tendon graft) or DIP fusion as salvage.
5. Follow the 2026 IFSSH consensus (Tang et al.), which codifies core-plus-epitendinous repair with early active motion as the international standard of care where resources/therapy access allow.

## Pitfalls

- Tendon rupture risk is highest in the first 3-6 weeks, especially days 7-21, when suture strength is lowest relative to healing tissue.
- Adhesion formation/stiffness is the major cause of poor outcome, especially in Zone II.
- Pulley bowstringing occurs if A2/A4 are over-vented rather than preserved or judiciously vented.
- Quadriga (over-advancement of FDP, avoid >1 cm advancement) causes incomplete flexion of adjacent, uninjured fingers.
- Lumbrical-plus deformity and infection are additional recognized complications.
- Re-rupture rates with modern EAM protocols in Zone II are commonly reported in the 4-10% range in specialized hand therapy settings — lower than older passive-motion-only protocols' adhesion/stiffness burden, but EAM requires close therapy supervision and patient compliance and should not be used without a structured, monitored therapy program.

## Post-op protocol

Early active motion (EAM) protocol, contingent on ≥4-strand core repair:

- **Days 0-3:** dorsal blocking splint (wrist ~20-30° flexion, MCP ~40-60° flexion, IP free/near full extension); begin place-and-hold or true active flexion (per surgeon protocol — e.g., modified Duran, Indiana/Strickland-based active protocols, or relative motion flexion for Zone I-III) within the first 3-5 days under hand therapy supervision.
- **Weeks 3-4:** progress from protected active motion to increased active ROM within the splint; initiate gentle blocking exercises as healing allows.
- **Weeks 4-6:** discontinue splint for controlled activities, progress AROM, begin light functional use.
- **Weeks 6-8:** begin passive stretching for residual stiffness, light resistance/putty exercises.
- **Weeks 8-12:** progressive strengthening; return to full unrestricted activity/heavy labor and contact sport generally not before 10-12 weeks, guided by tendon gliding and strength milestones rather than calendar alone.
