## Indications

Calcific tendinopathy deposits that have failed an initial NSAID/PT trial, particularly Gärtner type I–II deposits (dense, well-circumscribed) that are firm enough to yield calcium on aspiration. Gärtner type III (fluffy/resorptive-phase) deposits often resorb spontaneously and are technically harder to needle; favor watchful waiting/NSAIDs for these unless pain is severe. Arthroscopic excision is reserved as a further step for deposits refractory to at least one round of barbotage/ESWT with persistent significant symptoms.

## Positioning & setup

Performed under ultrasound guidance, using either a single- or two-needle technique for deposit access.

## Steps

1. **Confirm deposit morphology (Gärtner type) on plain radiograph** before proceeding — this is the best initial modality for characterizing the deposit and predicting how the needling will go.
2. **Access the deposit under ultrasound guidance** using a single- or two-needle technique.
3. **Fragment and aspirate the deposit**, typically combined with corticosteroid instillation at the same session.
4. **Counsel on expected course**: meaningful short-term pain reduction is typical by roughly 2 months, but effect size attenuates over longer follow-up, and a substantial minority of patients require repeat intervention (further injection, repeat barbotage, or surgery) — barbotage should be framed as a treatment that may need to be repeated, not a single definitive procedure.
5. **Escalate to arthroscopic excision** if the deposit remains refractory after at least one round of barbotage/ESWT with persistent significant symptoms — intraoperative fluoroscopy or needle localization helps identify the deposit at that point, and complete excision is not always necessary or possible; combine with cuff repair if a concomitant tear is debrided down to healthy tissue.

## Pitfalls

- Gärtner III deposits are technically harder to needle (soft, toothpaste-like); attempting aggressive barbotage on a deposit that is already in high-likelihood spontaneous resorption may not be worth the procedural burden — reserve intervention for severe pain in this subtype.
- Do not present barbotage as a one-time definitive treatment — network meta-analyses and dedicated efficacy studies (2023–2025) confirm effect size diminishes over longer follow-up and repeat intervention is common.
- High-energy focused ESWT and barbotage rank as the most effective nonoperative modalities in network meta-analyses (both outperforming sham and low-energy ESWT) — low-energy ESWT should not be substituted as an equivalent alternative.
- An acute, severely painful presentation with a resorptive-phase (type III) deposit can mimic septic arthritis or crystal arthropathy; rule this out before attributing the presentation to calcific tendinopathy.

## Post-op protocol

- Sling for comfort only (days).
- Early passive/active-assisted ROM within the first 1–2 weeks — there is no tendon-to-bone repair to protect.
- Progress strengthening as tolerated by pain.
- Overall course is much faster than after rotator cuff repair, unless a concomitant cuff repair was performed at the same setting, in which case follow the cuff-repair protocol instead.
