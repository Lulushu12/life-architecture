## Indications

Direct fixation is indicated when the posterior malleolus fragment involves ≥25% of the articular surface on lateral radiograph, or meets CT-defined criteria: incisural involvement, articular step-off >2 mm, comminution, or syndesmotic instability persisting after fibula/medial malleolus fixation. CT morphology per the Mason & Molloy classification now drives the decision more than a single fragment-size cutoff, since posterior malleolus fractures are increasingly understood as syndesmosis-ligament-avulsion equivalents rather than purely a size problem.

## Positioning & setup

Approach is selected by fracture morphology rather than fixed positioning: Type 1 fragments are addressed through the standard lateral/anterolateral fibular approach; Type 2A/2B fragments require a posterolateral approach (with accessory posteromedial exposure for 2B's intermediate fragment); Type 3 fragments require a posteromedial approach.

## Steps

1. **Classify the fragment on CT** using the Mason & Molloy system before committing to an approach.
2. **Type 1** (small extra-incisural posterolateral fragment): use the standard lateral/anterolateral approach for the fibula; do not fix the malleolus fragment itself — instead stabilize the syndesmosis directly with a screw or suture-button.
3. **Type 2A** (posterolateral fragment extending into the incisura, two-part): use a posterolateral approach for direct reduction and buttress/antiglide or lag screw fixation.
4. **Type 2B** (2A plus a comminuted/intermediate fragment): posterolateral approach ± accessory posteromedial exposure to address the intermediate fragment; may require two plates or fragment-specific fixation.
5. **Type 3** (posteromedial extension, often a large "die-punch" fragment): posteromedial approach with direct buttress plating, frequently combined with medial malleolus fixation.
6. **Reassess syndesmosis stability after posterior malleolus fixation**, not before — direct buttress fixation restores articular congruity, reduces anterior talar subluxation, and frequently restores syndesmotic stability without needing an additional syndesmotic implant.

## Pitfalls

- A 2023 interobserver reliability study (20 raters, 38 CT scans) found meaningful interobserver variability in this system, particularly in distinguishing Type 2A from 2B — do not rely on the classification in isolation for surgical planning; correlate with the CT images directly.
- Treating the decision purely as a fragment-size cutoff understates risk — the current framework treats an adequately sized fragment as a syndesmotic ligament avulsion (it carries the PITFL insertion), not just an articular fracture.
- Assessing syndesmosis stability *before* posterior malleolus fixation risks unnecessary syndesmotic implants, since restoring the posterior malleolus often restores stability on its own.

## Post-op protocol

Follows the same phased protocol as ankle fracture ORIF overall: splint/boot with strict elevation and NWB/toe-touch for the first 2 weeks, transition to protected weight-bearing from 2 weeks post-ORIF per contemporary evidence (WAX trial, 2024 AAOS guideline) if fixation is stable and anatomically reduced, progressive weight-bearing to full WB by 6–12 weeks, and return to impact activity by 12 weeks once radiographic union, full painless ROM, and ≥90% strength symmetry are achieved. Return to sedentary work 2–6 weeks, manual labor 3–4 months.
