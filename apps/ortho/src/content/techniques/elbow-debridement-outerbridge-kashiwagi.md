## Indications

Primary or post-traumatic elbow osteoarthritis in active patients with mechanical symptoms (loss of terminal extension/flexion, locking from loose bodies, impingement pain) and reasonably preserved joint space. Results are best when symptoms are predominantly osteophyte/impingement-driven rather than from diffuse chondral loss — this population is exactly where debridement-type surgery should be tried before total elbow arthroplasty, especially in throwers, laborers, and other young, active patients.

## Positioning & setup

CT is used preoperatively for loose-body mapping and planning. Nerve conduction studies are obtained when ulnar neuropathy is suspected clinically. No further positioning detail is specified in the source.

## Steps

1. **Choose an open or arthroscopic approach**: the classic open OK procedure performs ulnohumeral arthroplasty with olecranon fossa fenestration; the arthroscopic "column procedure" performs anterior and posterior compartment debridement, osteophyte excision, capsular release, and loose-body removal.
2. **Address osteophytes and impingement** at the coronoid/olecranon tips and the coronoid/olecranon fossae — this is the primary pathology in elbow OA, which is why debridement works even with preserved joint space.
3. **Combine with ulnar nerve decompression/transposition** when pre-op neuropathy is present, or when the planned flexion gain risks traction neuropathy — ulnar neuropathy coexists in up to 20–40% of elbow OA cases.

## Pitfalls

- Heterotopic ossification is a recognized complication and a specific driver of the early-motion post-op protocol.
- Incomplete relief results if diffuse chondral loss is present and unaddressed by debridement alone.
- Transient ulnar neuropathy can occur.
- Recurrent stiffness/loose body formation can develop over time; reoperation rate is reported at ≈4%.
- Candidacy hinges on generally preserved joint space and impingement/osteophyte-predominant symptoms rather than diffuse chondral loss.

## Post-op protocol

- Early motion protocol — continuous passive motion or aggressive active/active-assisted ROM starting day 1–3 postoperatively to prevent recurrent stiffness and heterotopic-ossification-related motion loss.
- Indomethacin or single-dose radiation prophylaxis considered in high-HO-risk patients (prior HO, head injury, burns).
- Strengthening deferred to 6 weeks.
- Return to manual labor/sport 3–4 months.
- Reported outcomes (2024 series): Mayo Elbow Performance Score improvement from ≈60.7 to ≈84.6, with low complication (≈2.8%) and reoperation (≈4.2%) rates.
