## Indications

Diabetic foot ulcer with infection, non-healing despite optimized offloading and wound care, or exposed bone/tendon requiring surgical management. Revascularization is prioritized before or concurrent with debridement whenever the WIfI ischemia grade indicates benefit — do not debride an ischemic wound without a perfusion plan.

## Positioning & setup

Not detailed at the approach/room-setup level in the source material; treatment sequencing (perfusion → source control → structural correction) is the operative decision point (see Steps).

## Steps

1. **Sharp debridement** to bleeding, viable tissue.
2. **Revascularization** (endovascular or open bypass) before or concurrent with debridement when the WIfI ischemia grade indicates benefit.
3. **Surgical resection of infected/necrotic bone with culture-directed antibiotics** for osteomyelitis — a growing body of evidence supports that some cases can be managed with prolonged antibiotics alone when radical resection is not feasible or would be excessively morbid.
4. **Achilles tendon lengthening or gastrocnemius recession** to reduce forefoot plantar pressure and promote healing/prevent recurrence of recalcitrant forefoot ulcers — a well-established adjunct, with the trade-off of a small risk of calcaneal gait/heel ulceration from overlengthening.
5. **Amputation level selection** (toe, ray, transmetatarsal, below-knee, above-knee) balances perfusion, tissue viability, function, and the likelihood of achieving a durable, shoeable/prosthetic-fitting residual limb.

Limb salvage is a multidisciplinary team effort — vascular surgery, infectious disease, endocrinology/diabetology, orthopedic/podiatric foot & ankle surgery, wound care nursing, and orthotics/prosthetics. The "toe and flow" model (aggressive local wound/bone management plus aggressive perfusion optimization) is associated with reduced major amputation rates in cohort data.

## Pitfalls

- Do not debride an ischemic wound without a perfusion plan; a WIfI ischemia grade ≥2 with a wound warrants same-week vascular surgery referral.
- Overlengthening the Achilles/gastrocnemius carries a small but real risk of calcaneal gait or heel ulceration — balance forefoot offloading against this trade-off.
- Recurrence after healing is high without ongoing offloading/footwear intervention (up to 40% at 1 year, 60%+ at 3–5 years), and can progress to osteomyelitis or major amputation in patients with uncontrolled infection or critical limb ischemia not amenable to revascularization — offloading and footwear are lifelong interventions, not episodic ones.
- Corticosteroid injections and repeated soft-tissue interventions are not part of this pathway; glycemic optimization, smoking cessation, and nutrition optimization are adjuncts that materially affect healing.

## Post-op protocol

Total contact casting (TCC) or instant-TCC changed weekly to every 2 weeks; typical healing trajectory for a well-offloaded, adequately perfused plantar forefoot ulcer is 6–8 weeks. Failure to reduce wound area by ≥40–50% at 4 weeks predicts non-healing and should trigger reassessment of perfusion, infection, offloading compliance, and adjunctive therapy.
