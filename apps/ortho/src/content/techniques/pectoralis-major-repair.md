## Indications

Complete pectoralis major rupture in young athletic patients or anyone seeking return to heavy pressing/contact sport, given the consistently demonstrated strength/endurance deficits (bench press, adduction, internal rotation) with nonoperative management. Nonoperative treatment is reserved for low-demand, sedentary, or partial-thickness (intramuscular) injuries where a residual strength deficit is functionally acceptable. Acute repair (<3–4 weeks from injury) is strongly preferred over delayed reconstruction given superior outcomes.

## Steps

1. **Establish time from injury at the first visit** — this single data point (</> 3–4 weeks) is the primary determinant of which technique to use and the strongest predictor of outcome.
2. **Acute repair (<3–4 weeks)**: perform direct tendon-to-bone repair using suture anchors or transosseous tunnels; cortical button techniques are increasingly popular for their fixation strength, particularly to meet the high failure load demanded in athletes.
3. **Identify and repair both sternal and clavicular heads separately** when both are torn — partial repair of only the more visible head under-restores strength.
4. **Chronic/retracted rupture (>4–6 weeks) or a large tendon gap**: use allograft (e.g., Achilles tendon) reconstruction to bridge the defect, since primary tendon-to-tendon apposition is not achievable at this stage.

## Pitfalls

- Failing to identify and repair both sternal and clavicular heads separately when both are torn is a specific, avoidable pitfall — partial repair of only the more visible head under-restores strength.
- Re-rupture is more common after chronic/allograft reconstruction than after acute primary repair; steroid users and delayed presenters carry elevated risk.
- Time-to-repair is the single most consistent predictor of outcome — early referral for suspected pectoralis major rupture (rather than delayed presentation) is the key practical lever available to improve outcomes, and is a message worth relaying to referring physicians/trainers.
- Chronic/allograft reconstruction outcomes are still favorable overall but generally somewhat inferior to acute primary repair, with higher complication rates (particularly re-rupture).

## Post-op protocol

- Sling/abduction brace 4–6 weeks with restricted external rotation and extension to protect the repair.
- Passive ROM early; active ROM from ~6 weeks.
- Resisted internal rotation/adduction strengthening deferred to ~10–12 weeks.
- Return to heavy bench-press/contact sport typically 4–6 months, with some protocols extending to 6–9 months for allograft reconstructions.
