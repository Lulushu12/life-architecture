## Indications

Cephalomedullary nailing (CMN) is mandatory for unstable intertrochanteric patterns: reverse obliquity (Evans V), transverse fractures, subtrochanteric extension, thin/fractured lateral wall (<20.5 mm, i.e. AO 31-A2 with an incompetent lateral wall or 31-A3), or gross comminution — the sliding hip screw's mechanism depends on an intact lateral wall to resist medialization of the shaft, and these patterns fixed with SHS have unacceptably high cutout/fixation-failure rates. For stable patterns (31-A1, most reducible A2, Evans I–II), CMN and a sliding hip screw (SHS) have statistically equivalent union and functional outcomes, so either is reasonable; SHS is a lower-cost, evidence-supported alternative for these patterns.

## Positioning & setup

Fracture table with fluoroscopy for AP and lateral imaging. Nail entry point for antegrade CMN is the tip of the greater trochanter (piriformis-entry nails are largely obsolete for standard trauma nailing).

## Steps

1. **Restore length, alignment, and rotation before final locking** — do not rely on the nail alone to reduce the fracture; achieve provisional reduction under fluoroscopy first.
2. **Select nail length** based on pattern — a long nail is required for any subtrochanteric extension (Evans IV) or reverse-obliquity component.
3. **Ream and pass the nail** through the greater trochanteric entry point, maintaining the achieved reduction.
4. **Place the lag screw(s)** — obtain a lateral fluoroscopic view to confirm central-central or slightly inferior lag screw position within the femoral head. Target tip-apex distance (TAD) <25 mm (Baumgaertner), which minimizes cutout risk (<2% below this threshold vs. >20% above).
5. **Consider a dual lag-screw (integrated screw-and-blade, or dual-screw) construct** in osteoporotic bone with rotationally unstable patterns for added rotational control.
6. **Lock distally** once length/alignment/rotation are confirmed satisfactory.

## Pitfalls

- Do not rely on the nail alone to reduce the fracture — restore length/alignment/rotation first; the nail should traverse an already-reduced fracture.
- TAD ≥25 mm sharply increases cutout risk (>20% vs. <2% below threshold) — this is the single most modifiable technical variable in CMN fixation.
- Loss of fixation/cutout is minimized by TAD <25 mm and central-inferior screw placement; varus collapse and medialization of the shaft is more of an SHS-in-unstable-pattern problem but can still occur with suboptimal CMN technique.
- Z-effect/reverse Z-effect is a recognized failure mode specific to dual-screw nail systems.
- Peri-implant femur fracture at the nail tip is more common with older, shorter, stiffer nail designs — mitigated with modern nail geometry and by avoiding a stress riser at the tip in osteoporotic bone.
- A 2023 systematic review found CMN carries a higher reoperation and refracture rate than SHS for intertrochanteric fractures overall — reserve CMN for patterns that mechanically require it (unstable per Evans-Jensen/AO) rather than defaulting to nailing for every pattern; a 2025 meta-analysis found CMN offers shorter operative time and less blood loss versus DHS/SHS, an efficiency argument distinct from the union/reoperation equivalence shown in earlier RCTs.

## Post-op protocol

- Weight-bearing as tolerated (WBAT) immediately for stable patterns — the sliding/telescoping mechanism is designed for it.
- For unstable/comminuted patterns, WBAT is still generally pursued with the CMN load-sharing construct, but with closer radiographic follow-up (2, 6, 12 weeks) to detect early collapse/varus migration; touch-down weight-bearing for 4–6 weeks is reserved for cases with poor bone quality, inadequate fixation, or major comminution where the construct is judged marginal.
- Union is typically confirmed by bridging callus by 12–16 weeks; nonunion is uncommon (<5%) given the metaphyseal/cancellous vascularity of this region.
- Physiotherapy progression: POD0–1 bed mobility/transfers/ankle pumps; week 1–2 gait training and hip/knee AROM with isometric quad/gluteal sets; week 2–6 progressive resistive exercise and device weaning; week 6–12 abductor/extensor strengthening and single-leg stance work; beyond 12 weeks continued strengthening and fall-prevention.
- Return to sedentary work typically 4–8 weeks; physically demanding work 3–6 months contingent on radiographic union and functional strength testing.
