## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications for DAIR (debridement, antibiotics, implant retention)**
- Early infection — generally within the first weeks post-fixation, before biofilm maturation, per the FRI consensus timeline (biofilm matures over roughly the first 2–4 weeks post-implantation, the biological basis for the DAIR time window).
- A stable implant providing adequate fracture stability.
- Healthy, well-vascularized soft tissue without extensive necrosis or a chronic sinus tract.
- Success rates for DAIR are generally highest when performed within the early symptom window combined with exchange of all accessible modular components — this combination is the strongest predictor of a good outcome.

**Indications for staged removal/exchange**
- Late infection (presenting well beyond the early post-fixation window, or with a long duration of untreated symptoms).
- A loose implant, or radiographic evidence of implant failure/nonunion.
- An unhealed fracture where retained hardware cannot reasonably achieve union in the presence of ongoing infection.
- Failed prior DAIR attempt.

**Contraindications / relative contraindications**
- DAIR is contraindicated in a grossly loose implant, established chronic infection with sinus tract, or extensive soft-tissue/bone necrosis requiring debridement so aggressive that implant stability cannot be preserved — proceed to staged exchange instead.
- Attempting implant retention purely to avoid a second procedure, against these criteria, is a contraindicated shortcut that predictably fails.
- Staged exchange with a temporary spacer/external fixator should be used cautiously in a host so medically compromised that a second major procedure carries prohibitive risk — in this C-host scenario, suppressive antibiotic therapy until fracture union (accepting persistent low-grade infection) may be a more realistic goal than attempted cure.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Confirm the diagnosis** against the FRI consensus criteria before committing to a strategy — ≥1 confirmatory criterion (fistula/sinus communicating with bone or implant, purulence at the implant, microorganisms from ≥2 separate deep samples or a single highly virulent organism, or histopathological confirmation) is diagnostic; suggestive criteria alone warrant further workup, not automatic reoperation.
- **Imaging**: plain radiographs to assess implant position, loosening, and fracture healing stage; CT for better delineation of the bone-implant interface and any occult loosening not evident on plain films; MRI (metalwork artifact permitting) for abscess/soft-tissue extent.
- **Timeline reconstruction**: establish precisely how long since the index fixation and how long symptoms have been present — this single data point most directly determines the DAIR-versus-exchange decision and should be documented clearly in the pre-operative note.
- **Implant stability assessment**: review serial radiographs for progressive lucency, screw loosening, or hardware migration; intra-operative implant-stability testing (manual stress testing of plate-screw or nail-interlock constructs) supplements imaging and should be planned as an explicit intra-operative decision point, not assumed pre-operatively.
- **Microbiology planning**: coordinate with the laboratory in advance for sonication of any removed implant/hardware component (dramatically increases culture yield over tissue culture alone) and for extended culture incubation (10–14 days) if a low-virulence organism (*Cutibacterium acnes*, coagulase-negative staphylococci) is suspected from prior sampling or clinical context (e.g., upper-limb or shoulder-adjacent hardware).
- **Spacer/temporary stabilization equipment** (staged-exchange pathway): have antibiotic-loaded PMMA spacer materials (moldable or pre-formed, matched to the anatomic site) and external fixation equipment available in case fracture stability cannot be maintained by a spacer alone.
- **Antibiotic strategy planning**: discuss the anticipated biofilm-active regimen with infectious disease pre-operatively (typically a rifampin-combination regimen for staphylococcal infection) — critically, plan the **rifampin start date for after debridement and once the wound has sealed**, not immediately post-operatively; early rifampin initiation (within the first several days after debridement, before wound sealing) has been associated with treatment failure, and monotherapy with rifampin at any point risks rapid resistance selection.
- **Modular component inventory**: for DAIR, confirm which modular components of the implant construct (interlocking screws, plate-attached elements) can realistically be exchanged intra-operatively, and have replacement components available — exchanging all accessible modular hardware is a core part of the DAIR strategy, not an optional extra.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- **Anesthesia**: general or regional/neuraxial per patient fitness and the anatomic site; these cases can run long if extensive debridement or spacer fabrication is required, so plan accordingly with anesthesiology.
- **Position**: standard positioning for the fixation construct and anatomic region involved (e.g., supine for most femoral/tibial plate or nail constructs, lateral decubitus for some proximal humeral or acetabular hardware) — essentially unchanged from the position used at the index fixation, since the same exposure is typically reused.
- **Tourniquet**: a pneumatic tourniquet may aid visualization during initial debridement of an extremity case but should be released before final assessment of tissue viability and bleeding at debrided margins, analogous to chronic osteomyelitis debridement.
- **Fluoroscopy**: position and test the C-arm arc pre-drape to confirm implant position, screw/interlock removal trajectory (if modular exchange or full removal is anticipated), and — for the staged-exchange pathway — spacer or external fixator placement can all be imaged without repositioning.
- **Draping**: reopen and extend the prior surgical incision where possible rather than creating a fresh approach, since this reduces additional soft-tissue devitalization; prep and drape wide enough to extend the exposure if debridement proves more extensive than anticipated.
- **Instrument setup**: have the manufacturer-specific extraction instrumentation for the existing implant available and confirmed in the room before starting (implant system, screw head type, any stripped-screw removal set) — discovering mid-case that the correct removal instrumentation is unavailable is a preventable and consequential delay, particularly if the implant needs to come out due to loosening discovered intra-operatively.
- **Surface landmarks**: mark the prior incision, any sinus tract or draining wound (planned for excision), and the anatomic implant location before incision.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. **Confirm the diagnosis** against the FRI consensus criteria intra-operatively if not already confirmed pre-operatively — obtain ≥3–5 deep tissue samples with dedicated instruments per site (never a superficial swab) before any irrigation or antiseptic is used in the wound.
2. **Re-open (or extend) the prior surgical approach**, excising the sinus tract and any chronically unhealthy skin/soft tissue en bloc where present.
3. **Assess implant stability directly** — manually stress-test the construct (plate-bone interface, nail-interlock integrity, screw purchase) under direct vision and fluoroscopy. This intra-operative assessment, combined with the pre-operative timeline and imaging review, is the definitive determinant of which pathway to follow; be prepared to convert from a planned DAIR to staged exchange if the implant proves looser than anticipated.
4. **DAIR pathway** (stable implant, early infection, healthy soft tissue):
   a. Perform aggressive debridement of all necrotic, infected, and granulation tissue while preserving the stable implant and viable bone.
   b. **Exchange all accessible modular components** (interlocking screws, removable plate elements) — this reduces residual bioburden and biofilm on hardware surfaces that can be removed without compromising fixation, and gives access for a more thorough synovectomy/debridement of the implant surface itself.
   c. Irrigate copiously (pulsed lavage, high volume — several liters, per local protocol) after debridement is complete.
   d. Consider local antibiotic-loaded carrier or collagen sponge placement in the wound bed before closure per local protocol/microbiology input, if not otherwise contraindicated by the wound status.
   e. Close in layers over closed suction drainage if indicated by wound depth/dead space.
5. **Staged-exchange pathway** (late infection, loose implant, or unhealed fracture):
   a. Remove all hardware, sending it for **sonication** culture (substantially increases diagnostic yield versus tissue culture alone).
   b. Perform radical debridement to bleeding bone, analogous in principle to chronic osteomyelitis debridement.
   c. Assess residual fracture stability after implant removal and debridement.
   d. Place a **temporary antibiotic-impregnated PMMA spacer** (molded to the defect, providing local antibiotic elution and some mechanical support) **or apply external fixation** for interim skeletal stability, choosing based on the fracture pattern, defect size, and soft-tissue envelope.
   e. Manage residual dead space with additional local antibiotic carrier (PMMA or absorbable calcium sulfate) and/or a muscle flap as needed.
   f. Close or apply negative-pressure wound therapy per wound status; plan the interval to definitive re-fixation (commonly on the order of several weeks, individualized to clinical/biochemical response) with the treating team.
6. **Initiate systemic antibiotics** once adequate deep-tissue/sonication sampling is complete — for staphylococcal infection with a retained implant (DAIR) or a definitive fixation construct to be placed at a later stage, plan a rifampin-combination regimen, but **start rifampin only after the wound has sealed**, not immediately post-debridement, and never as monotherapy.
7. **Definitive re-fixation** (staged-exchange pathway): proceed once infection is controlled (clinical and biochemical markers normalized, culture-directed antibiotic course underway or completed per protocol) and, ideally, once the fracture has healed enough to tolerate implant removal — or reconstruct concurrently with new fixation if bone stability cannot otherwise be maintained through the antibiotic-treatment interval.
8. **Individualize antibiotic duration** to clinical/biochemical response and surgical source control rather than a fixed universal number; early oral step-down is supported once the patient is clinically stabilized and the organism/susceptibility is known.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Treat the intra-operative implant-stability test as the real decision point — a pre-operative plan for DAIR should still be abandoned intra-operatively in favor of staged exchange if the implant is found looser than imaging suggested; do not force implant retention to match the pre-operative plan.
- Exchange **every** modular component that can be removed without destabilizing the construct during a DAIR procedure — partial exchange (leaving in easily-removable screws "to save time") measurably undermines the biofilm-reduction rationale of the whole strategy.
- Send removed hardware for sonication whenever any component is removed, even during a DAIR procedure where only modular parts come out — sonication culture of the removed screws/components can still substantially improve yield over standard tissue culture.
- Hold cultures for the full 10–14 days when a low-virulence organism is plausible; a single early-negative culture read at 48–72h in this context is not reassuring and should not be used to justify stopping antibiotics.
- Sequence the rifampin start deliberately — write it explicitly into the post-operative antibiotic plan as "start after wound sealing, not before," since the natural instinct is to start all agents simultaneously post-operatively.
- Reuse the prior surgical incision/approach wherever feasible rather than creating a new one — this limits additional soft-tissue devitalization in a bed that already has compromised biology.
- For a staged-exchange spacer, mold it to preserve working length and rough alignment of the limb even though it is not definitive fixation — this eases the later definitive fixation stage and helps prevent soft-tissue contracture during the antibiotic interval.
- Photograph and precisely document the debridement bed and any spacer/fixation construct at the index procedure — this materially helps the surgeon (who may not be the same person) planning the definitive re-fixation stage weeks later.
- Keep the multidisciplinary team (infectious disease, microbiology, plastics where relevant) engaged throughout the antibiotic interval rather than only at the two operative endpoints — the decision of when the patient is ready for definitive re-fixation is a team decision, not a fixed calendar date.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- Attempting implant retention outside appropriate indications (late presentation, loose implant, unhealthy soft tissue) risks nonunion and persistent infection.
- Repeated, poorly-targeted antibiotic courses select for resistant organisms — getting deep cultures right the first time is essential.
- Superficial swabs are inadequate for FRI sampling; use dedicated deep-tissue instruments per site.
- A single positive low-virulence culture is suggestive, not confirmatory, unless it recurs in a second sample — do not over-call infection on one *C. acnes* colony.
- Starting rifampin too early (within the first several days after debridement, before the wound has sealed) is associated with treatment failure — this is a specific, avoidable sequencing error distinct from the well-known rule against rifampin monotherapy.
- Incomplete modular-component exchange during DAIR leaves residual biofilm on retained hardware surfaces and undermines the entire strategy — plan exchange of every accessible component, not a partial swap.
- Underestimating implant looseness pre-operatively and proceeding with DAIR when the construct is in fact unstable predictably fails — always confirm stability intra-operatively regardless of the pre-operative plan.
- Failure to sonicate removed hardware forfeits a meaningfully higher culture yield compared to tissue culture alone, particularly for biofilm-forming, low-virulence organisms.

## Postoperative Protocol

**Weeks 0–2:** Protected/non-weight-bearing per fixation stability and soft-tissue status; wound and flap surveillance; early passive/active-assisted ROM of adjacent joints unless a re-look procedure is imminent.

**Weeks 2–6:** Progressive weight-bearing as fixation and infection control allow; continue systemic antibiotics per culture-directed duration; physiotherapy to restore joint ROM and prevent contracture.

**Beyond 6 weeks:** Strengthening progresses once bony union/soft-tissue healing is secure. Return to work is individualized to job physical demand and wound/bone status, often 3–6 months for physically demanding roles after major reconstruction. Return to sport only after radiographic union and infection-marker normalization sustained off antibiotics.
