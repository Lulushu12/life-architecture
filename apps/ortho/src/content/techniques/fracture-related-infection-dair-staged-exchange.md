## Indications

**DAIR (debridement, antibiotics, implant retention)** is appropriate for early infection — generally within the first weeks post-fixation, before biofilm maturation, per the FRI consensus timeline — with a stable implant and healthy soft tissues. **Staged removal/exchange** is generally mandated by late infection, a loose implant, or an unhealed fracture.

## Positioning & setup

Standard exposure for the fixation construct involved. Use dedicated instruments for deep tissue sampling at ≥3–5 sites (never a superficial swab) and send any removed implant for sonication, which dramatically increases culture yield over tissue culture alone.

## Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis against the FRI consensus criteria — ≥1 confirmatory criterion is diagnostic; obtain ≥3–5 deep tissue samples and hold cultures 10–14 days when a low-virulence organism (*Cutibacterium acnes*, coagulase-negative staphylococci) is suspected.
2. Assess timing since fixation, implant stability, and soft-tissue health to select the DAIR versus staged-exchange pathway.
3. **DAIR pathway:** aggressive debridement, exchange of modular components, and a biofilm-active antibiotic combination — e.g., rifampin combination therapy for staphylococcal infection once source control is achieved (never rifampin as monotherapy).
4. **Staged-exchange pathway:** implant removal, radical debridement, a temporary antibiotic-impregnated spacer or external fixation for interim stability, and culture-directed systemic therapy.
5. Proceed to definitive re-fixation once infection is controlled and, ideally, the fracture has healed enough to tolerate implant removal — or reconstruct concurrently if bone stability cannot otherwise be maintained.
6. Manage any residual dead space with a local antibiotic carrier (PMMA or absorbable calcium sulfate), a muscle flap, or both.
7. Individualize antibiotic duration to clinical/biochemical response and surgical source control rather than a fixed universal number; early oral step-down is supported once the patient is clinically stabilized and the organism/susceptibility is known.

## Pitfalls

- Attempting implant retention outside appropriate indications (late presentation, loose implant, unhealthy soft tissue) risks nonunion and persistent infection.
- Repeated, poorly-targeted antibiotic courses select for resistant organisms — getting deep cultures right the first time is essential.
- Superficial swabs are inadequate for FRI sampling; use dedicated deep-tissue instruments per site.
- A single positive low-virulence culture is suggestive, not confirmatory, unless it recurs in a second sample — do not over-call infection on one *C. acnes* colony.

## Post-op protocol

**Weeks 0–2:** Protected/non-weight-bearing per fixation stability and soft-tissue status; wound and flap surveillance; early passive/active-assisted ROM of adjacent joints unless a re-look procedure is imminent.

**Weeks 2–6:** Progressive weight-bearing as fixation and infection control allow; continue systemic antibiotics per culture-directed duration; physiotherapy to restore joint ROM and prevent contracture.

**Beyond 6 weeks:** Strengthening progresses once bony union/soft-tissue healing is secure. Return to work is individualized to job physical demand and wound/bone status, often 3–6 months for physically demanding roles after major reconstruction. Return to sport only after radiographic union and infection-marker normalization sustained off antibiotics.
