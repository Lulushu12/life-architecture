## Indications

Progressive collapsing foot deformity (PCFD) / tibialis posterior dysfunction (TPD) that has failed a structured 3–6 month trial of orthotic/brace management (custom UCBL or semi-rigid orthotic, gastroc-soleus stretching, PT for PTT strengthening) in a compliant patient. The specific construct is selected additively based on which components of the PCFD consensus classification are present (hindfoot valgus, TN uncoverage, forefoot abduction, forefoot varus/medial instability, ankle valgus/deltoid insufficiency) and whether each is flexible or rigid/arthritic.

## Positioning & setup

Not detailed at the approach/room-setup level in the source material; procedure selection and sequencing are the operative decision points (see Steps).

## Steps

Build the construct additively, adding components only as indicated by the deformity present:

1. **Base construct (flexible, no arthritis)**: medializing calcaneal osteotomy (MCO) + FDL tendon transfer to the navicular ± gastrocnemius recession or Achilles tendon lengthening for equinus contracture.
2. **Add spring ligament repair/reconstruction** when imaging or intraoperative exam shows attenuation contributing to talar head plantar-medial sag.
3. **Add lateral column lengthening** — Evans osteotomy through the anterior calcaneus, or calcaneocuboid distraction arthrodesis if the CC joint is arthritic — when talonavicular uncoverage is >40% or forefoot abduction persists after MCO.
4. **Add medial column stabilization** — Cotton osteotomy (dorsal opening-wedge medial cuneiform osteotomy) for flexible forefoot varus, or first TMT/naviculocuneiform fusion (modified Lapidus-type) for medial column instability/hypermobility.
5. **Add deltoid ligament reconstruction** (allograft or FDL-based) when ankle valgus is flexible and the deltoid is attenuated but the ankle joint itself is preserved.
6. **Convert to arthrodesis** for any joint with fixed/rigid deformity or arthritis: isolated talonavicular fusion (often sufficient, since TN motion drives most hindfoot motion), double (TN + subtalar) or triple arthrodesis for combined subtalar/talonavicular/calcaneocuboid disease, tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) fusion when ankle arthritis/valgus coexists with rigid hindfoot disease, or pantalar fusion for combined ankle + hindfoot + midfoot arthritis (rare, high morbidity, reserved for salvage).

For the tendon-driven TPD subset specifically: Stage I (tenosynovitis, no deformity) failing non-op → tenosynovectomy/debridement alone; Stage II (flexible deformity) → the ladder above; Stage III (rigid/arthritic) → triple or double arthrodesis; Stage IV (ankle valgus/deltoid insufficiency) → add deltoid reconstruction to a Stage III construct, or proceed to TTC/pantalar fusion when ankle arthritis is also present.

## Pitfalls

- Undercorrection of forefoot abduction or medial column instability is the most common cause of recurrent deformity after isolated MCO + FDL transfer — do not skip lateral column lengthening or medial stabilization when the consensus classification indicates it.
- Achilles/gastrocnemius equinus must be addressed concurrently or correction will recur.
- Sural nerve injury is a recognized risk with calcaneal osteotomy.
- Overcorrection to varus is a risk with lateral column lengthening.
- Wound complications are more likely with concurrent multiple incisions.
- Porous titanium wedge implants are an evolving alternative to structural allograft for lateral column lengthening/osteotomy augmentation, with high reported osseointegration and maintained correction.

## Post-op protocol

**Osteotomy/tendon transfer track (MCO + FDL ± lateral column lengthening)**: 0–2 weeks splint, non-weightbearing (NWB), elevation; 2–6 weeks NWB in cast or removable boot, with early passive/active-assisted ankle ROM initiated around 2–4 weeks if soft-tissue healing allows; 6–10/12 weeks progressive weightbearing in a controlled ankle motion (CAM) boot as radiographs show osteotomy consolidation; 10–12 weeks transition to supportive shoewear with orthotic/brace, begin formal PT for gait retraining and PTT/gastroc-soleus strengthening; return to low-impact work/activity by 3–4 months; return to sport/high-demand activity 5–6 months, contingent on radiographic union and functional strength symmetry.

**Arthrodesis track (triple, TTC, pantalar)**: 2 weeks splint NWB; 6 weeks NWB in cast (non-Charcot) — for Charcot/neuropathic patients extend NWB to 8–12+ weeks given impaired bone healing biology; serial radiographs (± CT if union uncertain) before progressing; gradual weightbearing progression over 4–6 weeks once union is confirmed; brace/orthotic support and PT for 3–6 months; return to sedentary work ~2–3 months, physical work/sport typically 6–9 months and only after solid union.

Return-to-work/sport general principles: sedentary work resumes earliest (often 2–6 weeks post-op depending on procedure); standing/walking-intensive work requires solid union/wound healing plus demonstrated weightbearing tolerance; return to impact sport requires radiographic union, full painless ROM, and strength ≥85–90% of the contralateral limb on functional testing.
