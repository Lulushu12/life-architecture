## Indications

Symptoms refractory to ≥6-12 months of conservative management (including a documented PRP or steroid trial) in lateral epicondylitis (ECRB origin) or medial epicondylitis (common flexor-pronator origin). Given ~80-90% of epicondylitis resolves within 12 months regardless of treatment, surgery is reserved for the minority who fail an adequate conservative trial.

## Positioning & setup

Can be performed open or arthroscopically for the lateral side. Arthroscopic approach allows concurrent evaluation and treatment of intra-articular pathology (plica, osteochondral defect, loose bodies) and is favored when intra-articular pathology is suspected on imaging.

## Steps

1. Confirm failure of ≥6-12 months conservative management, including a documented PRP or corticosteroid injection trial.
2. Approach: open or arthroscopic debridement/release of the ECRB origin (lateral) or flexor-pronator origin (medial).
3. For lateral cases with suspected concomitant intra-articular pathology, use the arthroscopic approach to allow simultaneous treatment of plica, loose bodies, or osteochondral pathology.
4. Debride the angiofibroblastic tendinosis tissue; the histologic process is disorganized collagen and neovascularization without inflammatory cells (not a true inflammatory "-itis"), so debridement of degenerative tissue is the therapeutic target rather than treatment of inflammation.
5. For medial epicondylitis, examine and protect the ulnar nerve intraoperatively — medial epicondylitis has up to 50% coexistence with ulnar neuritis.

## Pitfalls

- Aggressive ECRB debridement risks inadvertent violation of the lateral ulnar collateral ligament, producing iatrogenic lateral elbow instability — a critical technical pitfall to avoid during lateral-sided debridement.
- Always examine the ulnar nerve pre-operatively (Tinel at cubital tunnel) with medial epicondylitis given the high coexistence of ulnar neuritis.
- Persistent/recurrent pain occurs in an estimated ~10-15% of surgical patients — counsel accordingly, especially since natural history without surgery is favorable in the majority.
- Reserve surgery for genuinely refractory cases (≥6-12 months failed conservative care) given the strong self-limited natural history (~80-90% resolve by 12 months regardless of treatment).

## Post-op protocol

- Postoperative course generally mirrors the conservative rehabilitation framework, escalated once surgical healing allows: activity/ergonomic modification, progressive isotonic strengthening, and grip work.
- **Return to work/sport:** variable; post-surgical return to sport typically 4-6 months.
- Reported surgical success rate ~85-90% for symptom resolution.
- Complications: persistent/recurrent pain (~10-15%), lateral elbow instability if the lateral ulnar collateral ligament is inadvertently violated during aggressive ECRB debridement, infection, incomplete relief.
