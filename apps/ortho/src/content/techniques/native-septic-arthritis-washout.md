## Indications

Any confirmed or strongly suspected native septic arthritis warrants emergent joint decompression. Absolute indications for surgical (rather than aspiration-only) management: hip or shoulder involvement, failure to improve after 1–2 aspirations, loculated fluid on imaging, a prosthetic-adjacent native joint, an immunocompromised host, or presentation delayed beyond 3–5 days.

## Positioning & setup

Diagnostic arthrocentesis is mandatory before starting antibiotics whenever feasible, sending synovial fluid for cell count with differential, Gram stain, culture, and crystal analysis. Ultrasound guides aspiration of deep joints (hip, shoulder) and confirms effusion when clinical exam is equivocal.

## Steps

1. Aspirate the joint before starting antibiotics — delaying aspiration to "see the effect of antibiotics first" is a common and consequential error that both worsens diagnostic yield and delays source control.
2. Send fluid for cell count/differential (>50,000/µL with >90% PMNs strongly suggestive in a native joint; lower thresholds of >25,000–30,000 apply in immunosuppressed/early presentations), Gram stain (~50–75% sensitive), culture (~70–90% sensitive with adequate volume and rapid transport), and crystal analysis — a positive crystal result does not exclude septic arthritis, since crystal arthropathy can coexist with infection.
3. Select the washout approach by joint: **arthroscopic washout** is favored for the knee and increasingly the shoulder and hip in centers with arthroscopic expertise, with similar reoperation and functional outcomes to open arthrotomy and less morbidity and shorter hospital stay — though loculated/thick purulent collections may still require open debridement for adequate access.
4. **Open arthrotomy** remains preferred/necessary for the hip in many practices (deep joint, difficult arthroscopic access to the entire capsule, risk of femoral head vascular compromise from a tense effusion demanding truly rapid decompression) and for deep, multi-compartment, or heavily loculated disease anywhere.
5. Perform thorough lavage and debridement of the joint at the index procedure.
6. Reassess at 48–72h with a low threshold for repeat washout if clinical/biochemical improvement stalls — repeat washout is required in roughly 10–30% of cases, higher for *S. aureus*.
7. Reserve serial closed-needle aspiration alone for small/superficial joints (wrist, ankle) with rapid clinical response to a single or limited series of aspirations — it is not a substitute for surgical washout when operative criteria are met.

## Pitfalls

- Delaying aspiration for a trial of antibiotics worsens diagnostic yield and delays source control.
- A positive crystal analysis does not exclude septic arthritis — crystal arthropathy is the principal mimic and can coexist with infection.
- Cartilage degradation begins within as little as 8 hours of untreated infection (matrix metalloproteinase- and cytokine-driven chondrocyte catabolism) — the physiologic basis for treating septic arthritis as a surgical emergency.
- Hip septic arthritis: intra-articular pressure rise from a tense effusion can compromise the femoral head blood supply — a time-sensitive complication demanding truly urgent decompression.
- Serum CRP/ESR/WBC are adjuncts, not substitutes, for joint fluid analysis.

## Post-op protocol

**Weeks 0–2:** Protected/non-weight-bearing per stability and soft-tissue status; early passive/active-assisted ROM of adjacent joints unless a re-look procedure is imminent.

**Weeks 2–6:** Progressive weight-bearing as infection control allows; continue systemic antibiotics per culture-directed duration; physiotherapy to restore joint ROM and prevent contracture, particularly important after septic arthritis given the rapid cartilage injury described above.

**Beyond 6 weeks:** Strengthening progresses once infection is controlled. Return to work is individualized to job physical demand and joint status. Return to sport only after infection-marker normalization sustained off antibiotics.
