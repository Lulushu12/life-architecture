## Indications

Displacement >2 mm on CT (the operative threshold is a CT, not plain-film, measurement), or evidence of syndesmotic diastasis, in transitional ankle fractures (Tillaux or triplane) occurring during the ~12–18-month window of asymmetric distal tibial physeal closure around ages 12–14.

## Positioning & setup

CT with 1–2 mm cuts is the imaging standard for surgical planning — it more accurately quantifies articular gap/step than plain film and defines fragment number/orientation (2, 3, or 4 fragments depending on the plane of imaging and rotational forces for triplane fractures).

## Steps

1. Closed or open reduction of the displaced articular fragment.
2. **Percutaneous epiphyseal screw fixation** placed parallel to the residual open physis — never crossing an open portion of physis if it can be avoided, given the imminent-but-incomplete closure.
3. For a fully transverse triplane fracture with a large metaphyseal component, add a **metaphyseal screw or plate** as needed to control that fragment.

## Pitfalls

- Because these injuries occur near the end of growth, growth arrest is a comparatively minor concern relative to articular step-off, which is the primary driver of late post-traumatic ankle arthritis if left uncorrected — articular reduction is the priority.
- The 2 mm operative threshold is defined on CT, not plain radiographs — plain films can underestimate true displacement.
- Growth arrest is uncommon given the near-complete physis at the time of injury, but follow-up to confirm maintained reduction and eventual physeal closure is still standard given the low but non-zero risk.

## Post-op protocol

- Non-weight-bearing short-leg or long-leg cast for **4–6 weeks** (closer to 6 weeks for fixed/operative triplane fractures with a large metaphyseal component).
- Progressive weight-bearing in a walking boot thereafter, with physiotherapy focused on ankle dorsiflexion/plantarflexion ROM and gait normalization.
- Return to running typically at **8–10 weeks**, and full return to cutting/pivoting sport at **3–4 months** once radiographic union and symmetric strength/hop testing are achieved.
