## Indications

Recurrent or persistent posterior shoulder instability that has failed 3–6 months of structured rehabilitation (activity/technique modification and posterior capsule/rotator cuff strengthening), especially in collision athletes (e.g., American football linemen) and overhead/throwing athletes with symptomatic posterior labral tears.

## Positioning & setup

Arthroscopic technique in a tight posterior glenohumeral working space; low-profile all-suture anchors are an effective technical evolution for this confined space, with good outcomes reported in football-player cohorts.

## Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis clinically (jerk test, Kim test) and on imaging; consider reverse Hill-Sachs (anteromedial humeral head impaction) and posterior glenoid bone loss/retroversion on CT.
2. Arthroscopic posterior capsulolabral repair using suture anchors — increasingly all-suture anchors for low-profile fixation in the tight posterior space.
3. Plicate the redundant posterior capsule as part of the repair.
4. In bone-deficient or revision cases only: address reverse Hill-Sachs or posterior glenoid bone loss/retroversion with bone augmentation (posterior bone block/allograft) or glenoid osteotomy for pathologic retroversion.

## Pitfalls

- Reserve bone augmentation or osteotomy for genuinely bone-deficient or revision cases — it is not part of the standard primary repair.
- Posterior instability is frequently missed on an AP radiograph alone ("lightbulb sign," rim sign, trough sign); maintain a low index of suspicion after seizure, electrocution, or high-energy trauma, and obtain axillary views bilaterally in epileptic/electrical-injury dislocations given the risk of bilateral posterior fracture-dislocation.
- Reoperation and slightly slower return-to-sport timeline than anterior repair are recognized trade-offs (5–15% reoperation reported).

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–4**: sling with abduction pillow avoided (neutral position); passive ROM with internal rotation and cross-body adduction restricted to protect the posterior repair, while elevation/external rotation are generally allowed earlier than in anterior repair rehab (mirror-image logic).
- **Weeks 4–8**: progressive active ROM; begin scapular/rotator cuff isometrics.
- **Weeks 8–16**: progressive resistance strengthening, closed-chain and posterior-chain (posterior deltoid, cuff external rotators) emphasis.
- **Return to collision sport** (e.g., football linemen): typically 5–7 months — later than anterior stabilization given higher posterior-chain loading demands of the position and slower confidence in posterior capsule strength.
- Outcomes: recent systematic reviews/meta-analyses report ~90–92% return to sport/work and favorable patient-reported outcomes at short-to-midterm follow-up, including in collision-sport cohorts; minimum-10-year data show durable outcomes for isolated posterior capsulolabral repair.
