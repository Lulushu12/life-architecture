## Indications

MPFL reconstruction (MPFL-R) is the cornerstone soft-tissue procedure for essentially all surgical candidates with patellar instability — superior to MPFL repair, proximal realignment alone, or conservative management for reducing redislocation and improving Kujala scores. It is indicated for:
- Recurrent patellar instability.
- High-risk first-time dislocators with identifiable risk factors (high-grade trochlear dysplasia, patella alta, elevated TT-TG, young age, contralateral dislocation history, osteochondral injury) — reflecting a practice shift away from the older "two-strikes" (wait for recurrence) paradigm.
- As the base procedure in the ESSKA 2024 "à-la-carte" algorithm, to which bony procedures (TTO, trochleoplasty, derotational or distal femoral varus osteotomy) are added according to the patient's specific risk-factor profile.

## Positioning & setup

Standard knee arthroscopy/open hybrid setup for graft harvest and femoral tunnel work; fluoroscopy is required intraoperatively to confirm femoral tunnel position against the Schöttle point before final fixation.

## Steps

1. Harvest graft: gracilis or semitendinosus autograft is most common; allograft or partial-thickness quadriceps tendon autograft are alternatives.
2. Prepare the femoral tunnel at the anatomic MPFL origin, confirming position fluoroscopically against the **Schöttle point** — this is the single most critical technical step, as malposition is the leading cause of failure.
3. Fix the patellar side of the graft with an interference screw, suture anchor, or cortical button.
4. Tension the graft with the knee in **~30–45° of flexion, not full extension** — tensioning in extension risks overconstraint.
5. In patellar-sided avulsion MPFL tears (identified on preoperative MRI), favor reconstruction over repair given the higher failure rate of repair-only strategies for this tear pattern.
6. Assess for residual laxity after any concurrent bony correction (TTO, trochleoplasty); combine with MPFL-R when residual instability remains.
7. In skeletally immature patients, use physeal-sparing technique — adductor tubercle/distal femoral physis-respecting tunnel trajectories or soft-tissue-only fixation — to avoid growth plate injury.
8. Consider suture tape (internal brace) augmentation to protect the reconstruction and permit earlier, more aggressive rehabilitation.

## Pitfalls

- **Anterior/proximal femoral tunnel malposition** is the leading technical cause of failure and revision surgery — it causes overconstraint, loss of flexion, medial-sided pain, and early arthrosis. Always confirm the Schöttle point fluoroscopically before final fixation.
- Saphenous nerve injury is a recognized risk during hamstring graft harvest.
- Patellar fracture from the graft tunnel (stress riser) is a rare but recognized complication.
- Pooled recurrent instability after MPFL-R is ~3–8%, but this rate is meaningfully higher when bony risk factors (high-grade dysplasia, significant alta, TT-TG >20 mm) are left unaddressed — isolated MPFL-R is not always sufficient.
- Stiffness/arthrofibrosis and persistent anterior knee pain are recognized complications independent of tunnel position.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: hinged brace locked in extension for ambulation (unlocked for supervised ROM exercises); weight-bearing as tolerated with crutches (WBAT if isolated MPFL-R; touch-down/protected WB for ~4–6 weeks if a concurrent TTO is pending union); quad sets, straight-leg raises, patellar mobilization; ROM 0–90° by end of week 2.
- **Weeks 2–6**: progress ROM to full flexion; wean brace/crutches as quad control returns (typically able to perform SLR without lag before weaning brace); begin closed-chain strengthening (mini-squats, limited-arc leg press); stationary bike once ROM >100°.
- **Weeks 6–12**: full ROM expected; progressive strengthening (closed then open chain as tolerated); proprioceptive/balance training; light jogging around week 8–12 if quad strength ≥80% contralateral and no effusion.
- **Months 3–6**: sport-specific agility, plyometrics, cutting/pivoting drills introduced progressively from month 4.
- **Return to sport**: typically **6–9 months**, criteria-based — quadriceps strength ≥90% contralateral limb, single-leg hop test symmetry ≥90%, no apprehension on exam, completion of sport-specific agility progression without symptoms. If a bony procedure (TTO) was combined, radiographic union (typically by 8–12 weeks) is required before impact/pivoting sport.
