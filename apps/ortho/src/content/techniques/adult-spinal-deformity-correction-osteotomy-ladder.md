## Indications

Refractory pain/disability with SRS-Schwab ++ sagittal modifiers, progressive neurologic deficit, or curve progression in adult spinal deformity (either progression of an unfused idiopathic curve into adulthood, or *de novo* degenerative scoliosis). The planning target is age-adjusted sagittal alignment: PI-LL mismatch toward <10°, SVA <4–5cm, PT <20–25°, individualized using the Roussouly/Lafage sagittal shape classification rather than one fixed number for every patient.

## Positioning & setup

The monograph does not detail specific room/positioning beyond what is implied by the posterior instrumented approach used across the technique ladder below. Preoperative bone-health optimization (vitamin D repletion, bisphosphonate or anabolic agent such as teriparatide/romosozumab in osteoporotic patients) is a critical setup step — poor bone quality is a leading cause of instrumentation failure, and DEXA is obtained before major reconstructive surgery.

## Steps

Technique is chosen from an escalating ladder matched to deformity magnitude and rigidity:

1. **Decompression alone** — rare; reserved for pure stenosis without progression risk.
2. **Short-segment fusion** — for focal pathology.
3. **Interbody techniques** (ALIF/LLIF/OLIF with hyperlordotic cages) — for anterior column support, indirect decompression, and lordosis restoration.
4. **Long fusion to the pelvis** (S2-alar-iliac or iliac screws) — for larger deformity.
5. **Posterior column (Smith-Petersen) osteotomies** — roughly 10–15° correction per level.
6. **Pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO)** — roughly 30–35° correction per level, single-level correction of fixed sagittal deformity; higher blood loss and complication burden than Smith-Petersen osteotomy.
7. **Vertebral column resection (VCR)** — for severe, rigid, multiplanar deformity.

Circumferential minimally invasive (cMIS) strategies are increasingly used in lower-grade deformity to reduce blood loss and length of stay in appropriately selected patients.

## Pitfalls

- **Rod fracture** — reduced by multi-rod (accessory rod) constructs at the lumbosacral junction, a common failure site.
- **Proximal junctional kyphosis/failure (PJK/PJF)** — reduced by preserving the posterior ligamentous complex at the upper instrumented vertebra (UIV) and UIV+1, and by avoiding correction beyond age-adjusted targets.
- **Screw loosening and junctional fracture** — reduced by preoperative bone-health optimization in osteoporotic patients.
- **Overall complication burden is high** (commonly cited 30–70% depending on definition and procedure complexity): PJK/PJF (radiographic PJK in a substantial minority, revision-requiring PJF in a smaller subset), pseudarthrosis and rod fracture concentrated at the lumbosacral junction, surgical site infection (higher than in degenerative spine surgery generally), neurologic deficit (particularly with three-column osteotomy — several percent for PSO), major intraoperative blood loss, and significant medical complication rates given the older, more comorbid population; revision surgery is common at mid-term follow-up.
- Use the GAP score alongside SRS-Schwab in preoperative planning — mechanical complication rates rise sharply from ~6% (proportioned) to 47% (moderately disproportioned) to 95% (severely disproportioned) by GAP category.

## Post-op protocol

- **0–2 weeks**: inpatient stay commonly 4–7 days; early mobilization to reduce ileus/DVT risk; bracing per surgeon preference (often optional with rigid segmental instrumentation); multimodal pain control.
- **2–6 weeks**: progressive ambulation with formal PT for gait/functional training; BLT precautions maintained; continued VTE prophylaxis per individualized risk.
- **6–12 weeks**: gentle core activation exercises introduced; no high-impact activity; standing alignment films at 6 weeks to screen early for PJK.
- **3–6 months**: progressive strengthening; return to sedentary work often achievable by 6–12 weeks, physical labor deferred to 4–6 months.
- **6–12+ months**: CT fusion assessment around 1 year; individualized clearance for heavy labor/sport; ongoing PJK surveillance with standing films at 3, 6, and 12 months, then annually.
