## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications**: refractory pain/disability with SRS-Schwab ++ sagittal modifiers, progressive neurologic deficit, or curve progression in adult spinal deformity (either progression of an unfused idiopathic curve into adulthood, or *de novo* degenerative scoliosis). The planning target is age-adjusted sagittal alignment: PI-LL mismatch toward <10°, SVA <4–5cm, PT <20–25°, individualized using the Roussouly/Lafage sagittal shape classification rather than one fixed number for every patient.

**Contraindications / relative contraindications**:
- Active spinal or systemic infection — treat and clear before elective reconstruction.
- Uncontrolled medical comorbidity that precludes prone positioning, prolonged anesthesia, or the expected blood loss (severe cardiopulmonary disease, uncorrected coagulopathy).
- Unoptimized severe osteoporosis — a relative contraindication until bone health is addressed (bisphosphonate, teriparatide, or romosozumab), since poor bone quality is a leading cause of instrumentation failure.
- Active smoking or poorly controlled diabetes — relative; optimize before committing to a long fusion given the pseudarthrosis and infection risk.
- Frailty or limited life expectancy where the magnitude of surgical risk (30–70% overall complication burden) is disproportionate to expected benefit.
- Patient/family unable or unwilling to commit to the extended rehabilitation course and possible revision surgery.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Imaging**: standing full-length 36-inch (or whole-body EOS) films are mandatory — cropped lumbar-only films miss the pelvic and global sagittal picture. Supine bending/traction films assess flexibility; CT defines bony anatomy, pedicle morphology, and any pseudarthrosis; MRI evaluates neural elements and stenosis.
- **Bone health**: obtain DEXA before major reconstructive surgery; treat osteoporosis preoperatively, since this materially changes construct strategy (interbody support, cement augmentation, extending fixation).
- **Alignment targets**: measure PI-LL, SVA, PT, and calculate the GAP score alongside SRS-Schwab classification; individualize the numeric target using the Roussouly/Lafage sagittal shape classification rather than a single universal value.
- **Level and osteotomy planning**: template UIV/LIV, choose the sagittal-stable vertebra, select osteotomy level(s) and grade from the ladder below to achieve the planned correction, and plan pelvic fixation (S2-alar-iliac vs iliac screws) for long constructs.
- **Implants**: rod material/diameter (cobalt-chrome for greater corrective stiffness vs titanium), multi-rod (accessory/satellite rod) hardware for the lumbosacral junction and osteotomy site, interbody cage sizes for planned ALIF/LLIF/OLIF levels, PSO/VCR-specific instrumentation trays.
- **Blood management**: type and cross for multiple units, cell salvage setup, tranexamic acid protocol.
- **Medical optimization**: nutrition (albumin/prealbumin), cardiology clearance, anesthesia consultation regarding neuromonitoring-compatible anesthesia, medication reconciliation (hold anticoagulants appropriately).

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- **General anesthesia with total intravenous anesthesia (TIVA)** — inhalational agents blunt motor evoked potentials (MEPs) and degrade neuromonitoring signal quality, which is essential during osteotomy correction.
- Arterial line and large-bore IV access for anticipated blood loss; central line for larger reconstructions; Foley catheter.
- **Prone positioning on a radiolucent frame (Jackson table)** with chest and iliac crest bolsters, hips extended to allow physiologic lumbar lordosis, and the abdomen hanging free to decompress the epidural venous plexus and reduce intraoperative bleeding.
- Arms padded and abducted <90° to protect the brachial plexus; face/eyes padded in a horseshoe or foam headrest with periodic pressure checks (a recognized cause of postoperative vision loss in long prone spine cases).
- Where available, use a table capable of intraoperative extension/flexion (a "break" at the planned osteotomy level) to assist gradual, controlled closure rather than relying solely on cantilever rod maneuvers.
- Fluoroscopy (AP/lateral C-arm) or O-arm/navigation draped into the field for level localization and screw trajectory confirmation.
- **Baseline SSEP/MEP/EMG neuromonitoring** obtained before and immediately after positioning to confirm no positioning-related signal change, then followed continuously through every major correction maneuver.
- Skin marked at iliac crests, spinous processes, and any prior incisions; surgical level confirmed by fluoroscopy or radiopaque marker before incision.
- Cell salvage device primed; tranexamic acid bolus and infusion started per institutional protocol.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. **Timeout**, confirming planned levels, osteotomy grade, and neuromonitoring baseline.
2. **Midline posterior incision** and subperiosteal exposure from the planned UIV to the LIV/pelvis. Use a lateral, extrafascial dissection technique — exposing the fascia broadly before working beneath it — rather than multiple separate stab incisions, which meaningfully reduces blood loss over a long exposure.
3. **Place pedicle screws** at all planned levels (freehand, fluoroscopic, or navigated), confirming trajectory and containment at each level.
4. **Decorticate** posterolateral elements at levels not undergoing osteotomy to prepare the arthrodesis bed.
5. **Perform the selected osteotomy** from the ladder (escalating with deformity magnitude and rigidity — see table below):
   - **Smith-Petersen/Ponte osteotomy**: resect the ligamentum flavum, inferior and superior facets, and posterior elements at the level(s) chosen; repeat at multiple levels to distribute correction smoothly (~10–15° per level).
   - **Pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO)**: complete resection of the posterior elements and bilateral pedicles, followed by a wedge (eggshell) resection of the vertebral body cancellous bone, leaving the anterior cortex as a hinge. Place a temporary rod on one side while performing the contralateral bony resection to protect against uncontrolled translation. Close the osteotomy under continuous neuromonitoring, with a surgeon's finger or instrument on the dura to feel for buckling or kinking as the gap closes (~30–35° correction per level).
   - **Vertebral column resection (VCR)**: place bilateral temporary stabilization rods before beginning resection; complete circumferential removal of the posterior elements and vertebral body (with segmental vessel ligation as needed); place an anterior interbody cage/strut for column support before final rod tightening — reserved for severe, rigid, multiplanar deformity.
6. **Maintain provisional (temporary) rod fixation** throughout any three-column osteotomy to protect stability during resection and closure.
7. **Execute the correction/closure maneuver** under continuous neuromonitoring; if there is any signal change, stop, reassess, loosen the correction if needed, and consider an intraoperative wake-up test.
8. **Place interbody devices** (hyperlordotic ALIF/LLIF/OLIF cages, or an osteotomy-site cage for VCR) as planned for anterior column support and lordosis restoration.
9. **Contour and place final rods**, using multi-rod (accessory/satellite rod) constructs across the lumbosacral junction and the osteotomy site to offload the primary rods.
10. **Extend fixation to the pelvis** (S2-alar-iliac or iliac screws) for long constructs; confirm iliac screw trajectory under fluoroscopy.
11. **Final tightening and set-screw placement**; decorticate remaining posterolateral elements and pack autograft/allograft/BMP for arthrodesis.
12. **Meticulous hemostasis**, drain placement per surgeon preference, layered closure, consider vancomycin powder per institutional protocol, subcuticular skin closure.
13. **Final imaging** (fluoroscopy or portable film, AP/lateral) to confirm alignment, hardware position, and osteotomy closure before leaving the room.

### Correction ladder reference

| Technique | Correction/level | Column(s) resected | Relative morbidity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smith-Petersen/Ponte osteotomy | ~10–15° | Posterior column only | Lower; repeatable at multiple levels |
| Pedicle subtraction osteotomy (PSO) | ~30–35° | Three-column, single level | Higher blood loss, higher neurologic risk |
| Vertebral column resection (VCR) | Variable, largest single-level correction, multiplanar | Circumferential (anterior + posterior) | Highest — reserved for severe rigid deformity |

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Use the lateral extrafascial dissection technique instead of multiple paraspinal stab incisions to reduce blood loss during long exposures.
- Keep a temporary rod in place on the contralateral (or ipsilateral) side throughout PSO/VCR resection to prevent translation and protect the cord.
- Keep a finger or blunt instrument on the dura during PSO closure to feel for buckling or kinking as the osteotomy closes — this is often the earliest warning sign of impending neural compromise, ahead of a neuromonitoring change.
- Run TIVA anesthesia specifically to preserve reliable MEP/SSEP signal quality; brief the neuromonitoring team before and after every major correction maneuver.
- Where available, use a hinged/adjustable operative table to assist gradual PSO closure — this reduces reliance on forceful cantilever rod-reduction maneuvers.
- Favor multiple sequential Smith-Petersen osteotomies over a single large PSO whenever the deformity is flexible enough to allow it — the aggregate correction can match a PSO with meaningfully lower blood loss and neurologic risk; reserve PSO/VCR for truly fixed deformity.
- Use the GAP score alongside SRS-Schwab and an age-adjusted (Roussouly/Lafage-individualized) target rather than forcing every patient to identical PI-LL/SVA/PT numbers.
- Preserve the posterior ligamentous complex and facet capsule at the UIV and UIV+1 — do not violate the facet capsule cranial to the planned construct — to reduce proximal junctional kyphosis risk.
- In osteoporotic bone, consider cement-augmented or larger-diameter screws at the UIV, and extend the construct proximally if bone quality is marginal.
- Use tranexamic acid and cell salvage routinely given the blood loss magnitude typical of three-column osteotomies.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- **Rod fracture** — a well-recognized failure mode, concentrated at the lumbosacral junction; reduced by multi-rod (accessory rod) constructs at that level and across the osteotomy site.
- **Proximal junctional kyphosis/failure (PJK/PJF)** — reduced by preserving the posterior ligamentous complex at the UIV and UIV+1 and by avoiding correction beyond age-adjusted targets.
- **Neurologic injury** at three-column osteotomy (particularly PSO) — several percent risk; mitigated by temporary rod protection, controlled/gradual closure, and continuous neuromonitoring with a low threshold to pause and reassess.
- **Excessive intraoperative blood loss** — a defining risk of PSO/VCR; mitigated by extrafascial exposure technique, tranexamic acid, and cell salvage.
- **Screw loosening and junctional fracture** — reduced by preoperative bone-health optimization in osteoporotic patients.
- **Overall complication burden is high** (commonly cited 30–70% depending on definition and procedure complexity): PJK/PJF (radiographic PJK in a substantial minority, revision-requiring PJF in a smaller subset), pseudarthrosis and rod fracture concentrated at the lumbosacral junction, surgical site infection (higher than in degenerative spine surgery generally), neurologic deficit (particularly with three-column osteotomy — several percent for PSO), major intraoperative blood loss, and significant medical complication rates given the older, more comorbid population; revision surgery is common at mid-term follow-up.
- Use the GAP score alongside SRS-Schwab in preoperative planning — mechanical complication rates rise sharply from ~6% (proportioned) to 47% (moderately disproportioned) to 95% (severely disproportioned) by GAP category.

## Postoperative Protocol

- **0–2 weeks**: inpatient stay commonly 4–7 days; early mobilization to reduce ileus/DVT risk; bracing per surgeon preference (often optional with rigid segmental instrumentation); multimodal pain control.
- **2–6 weeks**: progressive ambulation with formal PT for gait/functional training; BLT precautions maintained; continued VTE prophylaxis per individualized risk.
- **6–12 weeks**: gentle core activation exercises introduced; no high-impact activity; standing alignment films at 6 weeks to screen early for PJK.
- **3–6 months**: progressive strengthening; return to sedentary work often achievable by 6–12 weeks, physical labor deferred to 4–6 months.
- **6–12+ months**: CT fusion assessment around 1 year; individualized clearance for heavy labor/sport; ongoing PJK surveillance with standing films at 3, 6, and 12 months, then annually.
