## Indications

Young, high-demand patients with advanced elbow destruction who are unwilling to accept total elbow arthroplasty's (TEA) lifelong lifting restrictions. A bone-preserving salvage alternative that keeps future conversion to TEA available later in life. Complication rates are markedly indication-dependent: ≈41.2% in post-traumatic OA versus ≈11.7% in inflammatory arthritis — an important counseling point when discussing this option with young post-traumatic patients.

## Positioning & setup

No specific positioning or setup detail beyond the graft and fixator construct is given in the source.

## Steps

1. **Interpose graft tissue in the ulnohumeral joint** — options are fascia lata autograft, Achilles tendon allograft, or dermal graft.
2. **Combine with a hinged external fixator** in many cases, to protect the interposed tissue and permit early motion.
3. **Counsel based on underlying diagnosis** — complication and failure rates differ substantially between post-traumatic OA and inflammatory arthritis (see Pitfalls).

## Pitfalls

- Overall complication rate is ≈38.4% and reoperation rate ≈23.5%, driven by graft attenuation/failure, recurrent stiffness, instability, and heterotopic ossification.
- Complication rates are markedly higher in post-traumatic OA (41.2%) than inflammatory arthritis (11.7%) — a key counseling point for young post-traumatic patients considering this option.
- The principal advantage over primary TEA in this population is bone-stock preservation for an eventual conversion to TEA later in life; it is a bridge procedure, not a first-line option, and debridement-type surgery (Outerbridge-Kashiwagi/column procedure) should be tried first whenever joint space allows.

## Post-op protocol

- When a hinged external fixator is used, it is specifically intended to protect the interposed graft while permitting early motion.
- Reported outcomes (2023 systematic review): mean MEPI pain score improves from 11.0 to 26.3, and functional score from 41.5 to 74.0.
