## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications.**
- Young, high-demand patients with advanced elbow destruction who are unwilling to accept total elbow arthroplasty's (TEA) lifelong lifting restrictions.
- A bone-preserving salvage alternative that keeps future conversion to TEA available later in life.
- Best suited to patients with inflammatory arthritis (lower complication burden — see below) but also used, with more cautious counseling, in post-traumatic OA.

**Contraindications.**
- Absolute: active joint infection, and insufficient bone stock at the distal humerus/proximal ulna to support the interposed graft and (if used) an external fixator construct.
- Relative: severe pre-existing elbow instability or collateral ligament insufficiency that the interposition/fixator construct cannot adequately control; significant heterotopic ossification bridging the joint (mechanical block is bony, not addressed by soft-tissue interposition alone); patients who would tolerate and prefer TEA's more predictable functional result and are not truly unwilling to accept its lifting restriction (TEA offers more reliable pain relief and function in appropriately selected lower-demand patients); post-traumatic OA with markedly deficient bone stock or scarred soft tissue, where the substantially higher complication rate in this subgroup (see Pitfalls) should be weighed heavily against proceeding; and patients unable to comply with a prolonged, often fixator-assisted, postoperative motion protocol.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Imaging**: AP/lateral (± oblique) elbow radiographs to assess bone stock, deformity, and joint destruction; CT for detailed assessment of bone loss and to plan the interposition/fixator construct in cases with significant deformity or prior trauma/hardware.
- **Graft selection**: choose among fascia lata autograft (avoids allograft cost/disease-transmission concerns, adds autograft-harvest morbidity/operative time), Achilles tendon allograft (avoids donor-site morbidity, allows a thicker/more standardized graft, at allograft cost and availability), and dermal graft (an increasingly used alternative with its own handling characteristics) based on surgeon experience, graft availability, and patient factors (e.g., avoiding autograft harvest in a patient with limited donor-site reserve).
- **Fixator planning**: decide preoperatively whether a hinged external fixator will be used to protect the interposed graft and permit early motion — if so, plan pin-site locations that avoid the ulnar nerve and preserve future TEA pin corridors, since this is intended as a bridge procedure.
- **Diagnosis-specific counseling**: identify whether the underlying diagnosis is post-traumatic OA or inflammatory arthritis before finalizing the surgical plan and setting expectations, since complication and reoperation rates differ substantially between the two (post-traumatic OA ≈41.2% vs. inflammatory arthritis ≈11.7% complication rate).
- **Instrumentation/equipment**: graft-harvest set (if autograft), graft-preparation instruments, standard elbow exposure instrumentation, and a hinged external fixator system if planned; have a bone-graft/allograft-bone contingency available if intraoperative bone loss exceeds preoperative imaging estimates.
- **Sequencing**: confirm that debridement-type surgery (Outerbridge-Kashiwagi/column procedure) has already been tried and failed, or is not appropriate given the degree of joint destruction, before committing to interposition — interposition is a bridge/salvage procedure, not a first-line option.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- **Anesthesia**: general anesthesia often combined with a regional block (supraclavicular/infraclavicular brachial plexus block) for intraoperative and postoperative analgesia, particularly valuable given the early motion protocol this procedure requires.
- **Position**: supine or lateral decubitus with the arm supported across the chest or on a hand table/bolster, elbow flexed for posterior exposure; if fascia lata autograft is used, position and prep the ipsilateral or contralateral thigh (per surgeon preference) as an accessible second sterile field.
- **Table setup**: tourniquet applied high on the arm after exsanguination; a padded bolster or arm-positioning device to hold the elbow at the flexion angle needed for exposure and later fixator application.
- **Draping**: circumferential prep/drape of the entire upper extremity above the tourniquet, plus the donor thigh if fascia lata is being harvested, prepped and draped as a separate sterile field.
- **Landmarks**: mark the olecranon tip, medial and lateral epicondyles, and the ulnar nerve course before incision; if a hinged external fixator is planned, mark and confirm the elbow's flexion-extension axis (a line from the center of the capitellum to the center of the trochlea) before pin placement, since fixator pins must be centered on this axis for the hinge to track true joint motion.
- **Equipment**: fluoroscopy is used intraoperatively both to confirm adequate bony resection/preparation and, critically, to confirm correct hinged-fixator axis alignment before final pin placement.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. **Exposure**: use a posterior approach (triceps-splitting, triceps-reflecting, or triceps-sparing depending on prior surgery and the extent of exposure needed), identifying and protecting the ulnar nerve throughout — mobilize and transpose it if indicated by preoperative neuropathy or the planned extent of resection/motion gain.
2. **Joint debridement and resection**: debride diseased synovium, remove osteophytes and loose bodies, and resect the distal humeral and proximal ulnar articular surfaces to a degree sufficient to accommodate the interposed graft while preserving as much host bone stock as possible — this conservatism is the procedure's central bone-preserving rationale relative to TEA.
3. **Contour the resected surfaces**: shape the distal humerus and proximal ulna to create stable, congruent surfaces that will hold the graft in position and support a functional flexion-extension arc without bony impingement.
4. **Harvest or prepare the graft**: 
   - **Fascia lata autograft**: harvest through a separate lateral thigh incision, taking a piece of adequate size and thickness to span the resected joint surfaces.
   - **Achilles tendon allograft**: prepare the allograft to the appropriate thickness and shape for interposition.
   - **Dermal graft**: prepare per the specific product/technique being used.
5. **Interpose and secure the graft**: position the graft over the resected distal humerus (and, per technique, wrap or drape it to also cover the proximal ulna), securing it with sutures through bone tunnels or anchors so it remains interposed and does not migrate through the early motion period.
6. **Assess stability and motion**: take the elbow through a passive flexion-extension arc to confirm the interposed graft remains seated, the joint tracks without gross instability, and there is no residual bony impingement.
7. **Apply the hinged external fixator** (when used): identify the flexion-extension axis fluoroscopically (center of capitellum to center of trochlea), place proximal humeral and distal ulnar pins clear of the ulnar nerve and radial nerve, and mount the hinge centered on the confirmed axis — an accurately centered hinge is essential, since a malpositioned axis both protects the graft poorly and restricts motion.
8. **Confirm fixator function**: cycle the fixator through its intended arc under fluoroscopy to confirm smooth tracking without binding, off-axis torque on the graft, or soft-tissue impingement at the pin sites.
9. **Closure**: release the tourniquet and achieve hemostasis; repair the extensor mechanism/triceps interval per the takedown technique used, and close the elbow wound and (if harvested) the thigh donor site in layers.
10. **Postoperative fixator/dressing setup**: dress the elbow wound and pin sites per standard external fixator pin-care protocol; confirm the fixator (if used) allows the planned early passive motion before the patient leaves the operating room.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Resect only as much distal humerus and proximal ulna as needed for a congruent, motion-permitting joint — over-resection defeats the procedure's core bone-preservation advantage over TEA and compromises future conversion.
- When using a hinged external fixator, confirm the flexion-extension axis fluoroscopically (capitellum-to-trochlea line) before final pin placement — an off-axis hinge is a common technical error that both under-protects the graft and mechanically restricts motion.
- Secure the graft robustly through bone tunnels or suture anchors rather than soft-tissue-only fixation, since early aggressive motion (the protocol's cornerstone) places real stress on the graft-bone interface before healing/incorporation occurs.
- Choose graft type based on realistic case factors: fascia lata autograft avoids allograft cost/availability issues at the expense of donor-site morbidity and operative time; Achilles allograft offers a thicker, more standardized graft without donor-site morbidity; weigh these against surgeon experience and patient-specific factors.
- Protect and, when indicated, transpose the ulnar nerve proactively — the combination of joint resection, graft interposition, and early motion creates more soft-tissue mobility around the nerve than a routine debridement case.
- Plan external fixator pin sites with an eye toward preserving future TEA stem corridors, since this is fundamentally a bridge procedure to eventual TEA in many patients.
- Counsel post-traumatic OA patients specifically and explicitly on the substantially higher complication rate (41.2% vs. 11.7% in inflammatory arthritis) before proceeding — this population's expectations should be calibrated differently from an inflammatory-arthritis patient undergoing the same procedure.
- Reinforce with the patient preoperatively that this is a salvage/bridge procedure, not a definitive solution equivalent to TEA in reliability — successful graft incorporation and functional gain (MEPI pain 11.0→26.3, functional 41.5→74.0 in reported series) is the realistic goal, not restoration of a normal elbow.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- Overall complication rate is ≈38.4% and reoperation rate ≈23.5%, driven by graft attenuation/failure, recurrent stiffness, instability, and heterotopic ossification.
- Complication rates are markedly higher in post-traumatic OA (41.2%) than inflammatory arthritis (11.7%) — a key counseling point for young post-traumatic patients considering this option.
- Graft attenuation or failure over time can lead to recurrent pain, instability, or loss of the interposition's spacing effect.
- An off-axis or malpositioned hinged external fixator both under-protects the graft during early motion and can mechanically restrict the achievable arc — confirm axis alignment fluoroscopically before final pin placement.
- Ulnar neuropathy is a risk from nerve mobilization/transposition and from the altered soft-tissue mechanics around the joint after resection and graft interposition.
- The principal advantage over primary TEA in this population is bone-stock preservation for an eventual conversion to TEA later in life; it is a bridge procedure, not a first-line option, and debridement-type surgery (Outerbridge-Kashiwagi/column procedure) should be tried first whenever joint space allows.

## Postoperative Protocol

- When a hinged external fixator is used, it is specifically intended to protect the interposed graft while permitting early motion — begin passive/active-assisted motion within the fixator's protected arc as early as pain and wound status allow, per the specific construct's tolerance.
- Reported outcomes (2023 systematic review): mean MEPI pain score improves from 11.0 to 26.3, and functional score from 41.5 to 74.0.
- Monitor pin sites closely for the duration of external fixator use, with routine pin-site care to reduce pin-tract infection risk.
- Progress from protected/fixator-assisted motion to unassisted active motion and light functional use as graft incorporation and wound healing allow, guided by clinical progress rather than a rigid fixed timeline; no further phased weight-bearing/return-to-work/sport milestones are specified in the source beyond the pain/function score improvements above.
