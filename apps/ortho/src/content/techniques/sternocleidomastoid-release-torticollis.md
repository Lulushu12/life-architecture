## Indications

Failure of an adequate physical therapy trial for congenital muscular torticollis (CMT) — persistent head tilt/rotational deficit (>15–30°) beyond roughly age 12–18 months to 3 years, or late presentation (>1 year of age) with a fixed contracture.

## Positioning & setup

Choice of technique depends on severity and surgeon preference; associated hip dysplasia (shared intrauterine positional mechanism) and positional plagiocephaly should be assessed/addressed as part of overall care around the time of surgery.

## Steps

1. **Confirm diagnosis and failure of non-operative treatment:** clinical exam (head tilt, chin rotation, passive ROM deficit, palpable sternocleidomastoid [SCM] mass/band); reserve cervical spine imaging for atypical presentations (painful onset, older child, no palpable SCM contracture, associated neurologic findings) to exclude atlantoaxial rotatory subluxation, Klippel-Feil, or posterior fossa/spinal cord tumor.
2. **Select release technique:**
   - **Unipolar (distal) release** at the clavicular/sternal origin.
   - **Bipolar release** (both origin and mastoid insertion).
   - **Z-plasty or endoscopic release** for improved cosmesis.
3. **Begin stretching immediately post-operatively** — a structured post-operative stretching/bracing protocol is essential to prevent recurrent contracture.

## Pitfalls

- Recurrent contracture can result if post-operative stretching is inadequate — the surgical release alone does not guarantee a durable result without the follow-on rehabilitation program.
- Residual head tilt/facial asymmetry can persist if treatment (non-operative or surgical) is delayed.
- Hypertrophic/keloid scarring is a recognized complication of the surgical incision.
- Screen for hip dysplasia (developmental dysplasia of the hip) given the shared intrauterine positioning mechanism with CMT — hip ultrasound is recommended regardless of surgical timing.

## Post-op protocol

- Stretching program and cervical range-of-motion exercises begin within days of surgery.
- A soft collar or positioning device may be used briefly.
- Home stretching continues for several months to prevent recurrent contracture.
- Full activity typically by 6–8 weeks.
