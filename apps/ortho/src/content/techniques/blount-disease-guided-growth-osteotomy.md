## Indications

Progressive infantile, juvenile, or adolescent Blount disease (tibia vara) that has failed or is not a candidate for brace treatment. Technique is selected by Langenskiöld stage, age, and growth remaining: guided growth for younger children with adequate growth remaining and mild-moderate deformity (roughly <4–5 years, Langenskiöld I–III); valgus osteotomy for more severe deformity, older children, or guided-growth failure; plateau elevation added for Langenskiöld IV–V; physeal bar resection considered for a focal Stage VI bar with substantial growth remaining.

## Positioning & setup

Guided growth uses a lateral proximal tibial (and, if needed, proximal fibular) hemiepiphysiodesis with an 8-plate/tension-band construct. Osteotomy correction may be performed acutely or gradually with an external fixator, the choice driven by deformity magnitude.

## Steps

1. **Stage the deformity** with a standing AP long-leg radiograph and Langenskiöld classification; check the Levine-Drennan metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle (MDA) — >11° in a child <2–3y is suspicious for pathologic rather than physiologic bowing.
2. **Guided growth (Langenskiöld I–III, younger child, mild-moderate deformity):** place a lateral proximal tibial (± proximal fibular) hemiepiphysiodesis construct. Lower morbidity than osteotomy, but requires reliable follow-up and works only while growth remains.
3. **Proximal tibial valgus osteotomy (more severe deformity, older child, or guided-growth failure):** correct acutely for smaller deformities, or gradually with an external fixator for larger deformities to avoid acute neurovascular injury/compartment syndrome.
4. **Elevation of the depressed medial tibial plateau:** add for Langenskiöld IV–V, where the epiphysis has become wedge-shaped or clefted.
5. **Physeal bar resection** (with fat or other interposition material): consider for a focal Stage VI bar with substantial growth remaining, sometimes combined with contralateral epiphysiodesis or guided growth if the bar is extensive.

## Pitfalls

- Rapid/large acute corrections risk peroneal nerve palsy and compartment syndrome — favor gradual correction for large deformities.
- Recurrence can follow undercorrection or premature removal of the guided-growth implant.
- Physeal arrest/bar formation can occur after osteotomy performed near the growth plate.
- Higher Langenskiöld stage at presentation correlates with higher recurrence risk after correction and a greater likelihood that guided growth alone will fail, favoring primary osteotomy in these cases.

## Post-op protocol

- **Guided growth:** weight-bearing as tolerated, no formal rehab beyond routine activity; radiographic follow-up every 3–4 months to monitor correction rate (typically 0.5–1°/month) and plan implant removal.
- **Osteotomy:** protected weight-bearing 4–6 weeks (longer with external fixator until adequate callus), progressive weight-bearing and knee/ankle ROM physiotherapy through weeks 6–12, full activity typically by 3–4 months.
