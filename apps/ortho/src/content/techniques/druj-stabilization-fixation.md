## Indications

Acute reducible DRUJ instability persisting after anatomic radius/ulnar styloid fixation; irreducible DRUJ dislocation; basilar ulnar styloid fracture with confirmed DRUJ instability after radius fixation. Instability may be occult until the radius is anatomically reduced and fixed — always reassess DRUJ stability intraoperatively after radius fixation, not just preoperatively.

## Positioning & setup

Perform pronation/supination stress testing under fluoroscopy with the elbow at 90° and forearm rotated to neutral, then stressed in pronation and supination while an assistant applies a dorsal/volar translation force to the ulna relative to the radius; compare translation and the "clunk" endpoint to the contralateral, uninjured side. Document the arc of forearm rotation within which the joint remains reduced and stable — this arc becomes the immobilization position if nonoperative/pin management is chosen.

## Steps

1. Reassess DRUJ stability intraoperatively after radius/ulnar styloid fixation is complete, actively testing in pronation, neutral, and supination.
2. For acute reducible instability: transfix the DRUJ with 1–2 smooth K-wires (radius to ulna), placed proximal to the joint and avoiding the sigmoid notch, with the forearm held in the stable rotational position identified on stress testing; combine with immobilization for 4–6 weeks.
3. For irreducible DRUJ dislocation: proceed to open reduction — most commonly due to entrapment of the ECU tendon (dorsal dislocation) or extensor tendons/periosteum within the joint; irreducibility itself is the key trigger for urgent open exploration rather than repeated closed attempts.
4. For a basilar ulnar styloid fracture with confirmed DRUJ instability: fix with tension-band wiring, a headless compression screw, or a small hook plate, restoring the foveal ligament attachment.
5. Isolated tip fractures or fractures without demonstrated instability generally do not require fixation, even if they appear radiographically un-united at follow-up.

## Pitfalls

- DRF-associated ulnar styloid tip nonunion is generally benign (no functional penalty per pooled meta-analytic data) when the DRUJ is clinically stable — do not over-treat.
- Isolated/basilar ulnar styloid nonunions are the exception: 2024 data show reduced motion, lower functional scores, more pain, and a higher rate of positive ballottement testing in this group — a distinction that should be drawn explicitly from the generally benign DRF-associated tip nonunion.
- K-wires are typically removed at 4–6 weeks once soft-tissue healing (TFCC/capsule) is presumed adequate; premature removal or premature rotational loading risks recurrent instability.
- Objective, reproducible DRUJ instability grading remains an unmet need despite routine use of ballottement/fovea sign/press testing — document findings carefully rather than relying on a single binary "stable/unstable" call.

## Post-op protocol

- **Phase 0 (0–2 wk)**: immobilized in the stable rotational position; digit AROM.
- **Phase 1 (2–6 wk)**: forearm rotation blocked until wire removal (~4–6 weeks); focus therapy on digit/elbow/shoulder motion.
- **Phase 2 (6–12 wk)**: progressive rotational ROM after wire removal.
- **Phase 3 (12–16+ wk)**: full strengthening once union/repair maturity confirmed.
- **Imaging**: post-op, then at wire removal (~4–6 wk), then at return-to-rotation milestones.
