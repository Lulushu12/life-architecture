## Indications

Elective arthroplasty, revision arthroplasty, and operative hip fracture management where reduction of surgical blood loss and transfusion requirement is desired. TXA is the single highest-yield blood-conservation intervention in elective arthroplasty and is supported for routine use in operative hip fracture management barring a genuine contraindication.

## Positioning & setup

Not applicable in the surgical-positioning sense — TXA is a pharmacologic adjunct given at defined timepoints relative to incision and closure rather than a positioning-dependent procedure.

## Steps

1. **Select a route based on clinical context:**
   - **IV:** 1g bolus at incision, optionally repeated as 1g at wound closure (or given as a 3-hour infusion), or weight-based 10–15mg/kg per dose.
   - **Topical/intra-articular:** 1–3g diluted in 50–100mL normal saline instilled into the joint before closure, with a dwell time before drain release if a drain is used — favored when systemic exposure is a specific concern.
   - **Oral:** approximately 1.95–2g given 2 hours preoperatively; comparable efficacy to IV in several arthroplasty trials, with lower cost and no IV-line requirement.
   - **Combined IV + topical:** used in high-blood-loss cases (revision arthroplasty, bilateral procedures, complex trauma) for additive effect.
2. **Screen for relative contraindications** before dosing: history of seizure disorder (TXA, especially at high IV bolus doses or via inadvertent intrathecal exposure, is proconvulsant), active arterial thromboembolic event within the preceding ~3 months (individualized discussion), and renal impairment (requires dose reduction, as TXA is renally cleared).
3. **Do not withhold TXA for a remote history alone** — active malignancy and remote (>12 months, stable) VTE history are not considered contraindications in current practice.
4. **Administer at incision** (IV bolus) and, if using a repeat/closure dose or topical adjunct, at wound closure as planned.

## Pitfalls

- The aggregate arthroplasty and hip-fracture literature — spanning many thousands of randomized patients — has not shown an increased rate of DVT, PE, MI, or stroke with TXA at standard doses, including in patients with a prior history of VTE, coronary disease, or stroke, once considered relative contraindications.
- This safety signal is procedure- and dose-context dependent: a large noncardiac surgery trial (POISE-3) in a broader, higher-cardiovascular-risk population found the composite vascular event outcome occurred in 14.2% (TXA) vs 13.9% (placebo) — the trial failed to formally establish non-inferiority on that outcome (upper CI bound exceeded the margin), a more modest and different signal than "increased risk," but a reminder not to extrapolate reassuring orthopedic-specific safety data uncritically to markedly higher cardiovascular-risk patients or non-orthopedic contexts. TXA did significantly reduce major bleeding in that trial (9.1% vs 11.7%).
- Trauma-specific major hemorrhage trials (non-ortho-specific) established a mortality benefit from early TXA in bleeding trauma patients, and a separate large trial in traumatic brain injury found TXA safe with a signal toward reduced head-injury-related death when given early — supportive context for routine use in hip fracture management, but drawn from a broader trauma population.
- A 2024 EFORT Open Reviews meta-analysis and a separate selection-bias-adjusted Journal of Arthroplasty analysis both reaffirm no increase in DVT/PE/mortality with TXA in TJA patients with a prior VTE history; a 2023 meta-analysis found single-dose and double-dose IV TXA equivalent in efficacy and safety.

## Post-op protocol

No dedicated TXA-specific postoperative monitoring beyond standard perioperative VTE and bleeding surveillance; TXA is folded into the broader blood-management and ERAS pathway rather than followed by its own distinct protocol. Restrictive transfusion threshold (Hb <8g/dL symptomatic or <7g/dL asymptomatic) applies regardless of TXA use.
