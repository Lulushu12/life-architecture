## Indications

Progressive tendon loading is the central conservative treatment for patellar tendinopathy ("jumper's knee"), used across Blazina stages I–III. Surgical debridement/tendon scraping is reserved only for patients who fail ≥6 months of a comprehensive, supervised loading program.

## Positioning & setup

- Isometric holds performed as a Spanish squat or leg extension hold.
- Eccentric decline squat program performed on a 25° decline board.
- Heavy slow resistance (HSR) training performed with leg press, squat, or hack squat equipment.

## Steps

1. **Isometric loading** (e.g., 5 × 45 sec at ~70% MVC) — used acutely for same-session analgesia and to allow continued training/competition during in-season management. The analgesic effect is well established, though the magnitude of superiority over isotonic loading for pain relief has been questioned by newer trials.
2. **Heavy slow resistance (HSR) training** (leg press, squat, hack squat — 3–4 sets, 6–15 RM, tempo ~3 sec concentric/3 sec eccentric, 3×/week, progressive load) over 12 weeks — comparable or superior VISA-P outcomes to eccentric decline squat protocols with better patient adherence/tolerance; 2024 mixed-treatment meta-analyses rank HSR and moderate slow resistance training highly for VISA-P improvement, generally favoring it over eccentric-only protocols for long-term functional gains.
3. **Eccentric decline squat program** (Alfredson-type/Silbernagel protocol, 25° decline board, 3×15 reps twice daily, 12 weeks) — an effective, well-validated alternative, particularly historically dominant before HSR data matured; expect discomfort during early sessions (acceptable pain, typically ≤5/10 that settles).
4. Combine loading with addressing kinetic chain contributors (ankle dorsiflexion, quadriceps/hip strength, landing mechanics) and load management (jump volume monitoring).

## Pitfalls

- Corticosteroid injection into or around the patellar tendon is **not recommended** — associated with increased risk of subsequent tendon rupture.
- PRP has mixed-quality evidence — some trials show VISA-P improvement over placebo/eccentric exercise alone at 6–12 months, but preparations and outcomes are heterogeneous; considered adjunctive/investigational for refractory Blazina II–III rather than first-line. Several 2023–2024 network meta-analyses continue to rank exercise-based interventions above injectable therapies.
- Extracorporeal shockwave therapy (ESWT) is a reasonable adjunct for refractory tendinopathy (stage II–III) after a failed initial loading program, with moderate evidence for pain reduction and low morbidity — not a substitute for loading.
- Dry needling/percutaneous tenotomy has modest evidence and is typically combined with structured loading rather than used standalone.
- A minimum of **12 weeks** of a structured, supervised loading program is required before considering it a "failed" conservative trial — many athletes need 3–6 months for full return to high-level jumping sport.
- Unaddressed loading errors can allow progression from tendinopathy to partial or complete rupture (Blazina IV).

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2 (isometric phase)**: isometric holds for analgesia (5×45 sec, ~70% MVC), activity modification (reduce but don't fully cease loading if tolerable), address training load.
- **Weeks 2–12 (isotonic/HSR or eccentric phase)**: progressive HSR (leg press/squat, 3–4 sets 6–15RM, 3×/week) or eccentric decline squat program (3×15, 2×/day) — expect gradual VISA-P improvement over the full 12 weeks; acceptable pain during loading (≤3–5/10 that settles within 24h) is not a reason to stop.
- **Months 3–6 (energy storage/plyometric phase)**: introduce jump/landing mechanics retraining, plyometric progression once pain-free with heavy loading, gradual return to full training volume.
- **Return to sport**: criteria-based — VISA-P score improvement plateaued or near-normal, pain ≤2–3/10 with full training load, symmetric single-leg hop/landing mechanics; may take 3–6 months for Blazina II–III, occasionally longer for elite jumping-sport athletes.
