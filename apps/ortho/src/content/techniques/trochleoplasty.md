## Indications

Trochleoplasty is reserved for high-grade trochlear dysplasia (Dejour B–D, particularly C/D) with a supratrochlear spur or convex/absent trochlear groove, where MPFL reconstruction alone carries persistently elevated redislocation risk. Per ESSKA consensus, it can be performed safely close to skeletal maturity in adolescents.

## Positioning & setup

Performed as an open bony procedure, typically combined with or followed by MPFL reconstruction when residual patellar laxity remains after the bony correction.

## Steps

1. Perform a **deepening trochleoplasty**, most commonly the **Bereiter or Dejour technique** — the most widely used approach, with the lowest recurrence in meta-analysis (~0.3–2.4% redislocation across techniques in a pooled analysis of ~1,000 procedures).
2. Preserve cartilage–subchondral bone continuity throughout groove deepening to protect chondral integrity.
3. Reassess patellar stability after bony correction; combine with MPFL-R when residual laxity remains.

## Pitfalls

- Technically demanding, with risk of chondral injury during groove deepening — overzealous deepening risks chondral necrosis or fracture of the osteochondral flap.
- Arthrofibrosis is a meaningfully reported complication, particularly in pediatric series (~13%).
- Patellofemoral overcorrection/stiffness is a recognized risk.
- Avascular change to the trochlear fragment is a rare complication.
- Underappreciating concurrent patella alta (failing to address height alongside dysplasia) can leave residual instability.
- Best performed in high-volume centers given the technical demand and complication profile.

## Post-op protocol

- Rehabilitation follows the same combined patellar-instability protocol used after MPFL-R with a bony procedure: brace-protected early ROM, protected weight-bearing pending osseous healing, progressive strengthening, and criteria-based return to sport at 6–9 months.
- Pediatric outcomes data show low recurrent instability (~1%) but a notable arthrofibrosis rate (~13%), reinforcing the value of a structured, closely supervised motion protocol in younger patients.
