## Indications

Applies to essentially all orthopedic surgical procedures with meaningful surgical site infection (SSI) risk, particularly arthroplasty and instrumented trauma fixation. Goal: administer effective antibiotic coverage timed to peak at incision, then stop promptly to avoid resistance pressure without sacrificing efficacy.

## Positioning & setup

Not applicable in the surgical-positioning sense — this is a preoperative and intraoperative pharmacologic protocol. Setup includes preoperative allergy history review (ideally through a formal allergy delabeling pathway) and, where relevant, preoperative nasal MRSA screening.

## Steps

1. **Administer cefazolin 2g IV** (3g if body weight ≥120kg) as the standard regimen, with infusion completed within **60 minutes before incision**.
2. **Redose intraoperatively every 4 hours**, or after **>1500mL blood loss**.
3. **Evaluate reported penicillin allergy against true cross-reactivity risk before diverting away from cefazolin.** True cross-reactivity is mediated by shared R1 side chains, not the shared beta-lactam ring, and cefazolin has a unique side-chain structure not shared with any penicillin — making cross-reactivity with cefazolin essentially negligible even in patients with a well-documented penicillin allergy. Reserve avoidance for a history of true anaphylaxis, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/TEN, or other severe immediate-type reaction; a vague reported rash, GI upset, or unknown-mechanism reaction should not trigger diversion.
4. **For true beta-lactam anaphylaxis:** use vancomycin or clindamycin, with infusion started within **120 minutes before incision** given the slower required infusion rate for vancomycin. Do not use vancomycin as a default "safe" substitute outside this indication — routine unnecessary vancomycin use is associated with higher SSI rates in some cohorts (inferior gram-negative and staphylococcal coverage relative to cefazolin, plus red-man reaction/nephrotoxicity risk) and drives resistance pressure.
5. **In MRSA-colonized or high-risk patients**, add a single dose of vancomycin as an adjunct to (not a replacement for) cefazolin, informed by preoperative nasal MRSA screening; consider universal or targeted decolonization (nasal mupirocin + chlorhexidine bathing).
6. **Extend to gram-negative coverage only if documented colonization or high MRSA-negative gram-negative risk**, as an escalation step rather than routine practice.
7. **Discontinue prophylaxis within 24 hours of surgery** — prolonged courses do not reduce SSI rates and increase resistant-organism and *C. difficile* risk.

## Pitfalls

- The historically taught ~10% penicillin-cephalosporin cross-reactivity figure is a substantial overestimate driven by older, impure cephalosporin preparations and shared testing methodology flaws — do not use it to justify routine avoidance of cefazolin in reported penicillin-allergic patients.
- Formal preoperative allergy delabeling pathways (e.g. using validated risk-stratification tools such as PEN-FAST, followed by direct oral amoxicillin challenge or referral for penicillin skin testing) measurably shift patients back onto cefazolin, reducing vancomycin/clindamycin utilization, operative-day delays, and associated SSI risk — this is increasingly built into pre-admission joint-replacement clinics rather than left to day-of-surgery clarification.
- Vancomycin's slower infusion requirement (120 minutes vs. cefazolin's 60) means it must be started earlier relative to incision — a common source of inadequate pre-incision coverage if not anticipated.
- Contemporary arthroplasty-specific data directly support cefazolin use even in patients with a documented penicillin anaphylaxis history; a 2023 cohort study reported a cefazolin reaction rate of only 1.3% in patients with a penicillin allergy label (mostly mild, self-limited rash).

## Post-op protocol

- Stop prophylaxis within 24 hours of surgery regardless of drains or catheters still in place.
- Perioperative glucose target **<200mg/dL** regardless of diabetic status to reduce SSI risk.
- No prophylaxis-specific follow-up beyond standard wound surveillance; persistent wound drainage beyond postoperative day 5–7 should trigger wound review and PJI workup rather than continued or restarted antibiotics.
