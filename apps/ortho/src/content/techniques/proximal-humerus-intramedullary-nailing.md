## Indications

Favored for surgical-neck-predominant 2-part proximal humerus fractures, particularly in osteoporotic bone, where a straight antegrade nail with multiplanar proximal locking provides load-sharing fixation through a smaller incision than plating.

## Positioning & setup

Superior approach, splitting (not detaching) the supraspinatus to access the entry point at the apex of the humeral head. Key structures at risk: the rotator cuff (supraspinatus) and the long head of biceps.

## Steps

1. Approach superiorly, splitting rather than detaching the supraspinatus.
2. Identify the entry point precisely at the apex of the humeral head — critical to avoid iatrogenic rotator cuff violation.
3. Insert a straight antegrade nail with multiplanar proximal locking, including calcar/inferomedial locking options in modern nail designs, for load-sharing fixation.
4. Complete fixation through a smaller incision with less soft-tissue stripping than an extensile deltopectoral plate approach.

## Pitfalls

- Entry point precision is critical — an imprecise entry point risks iatrogenic rotator cuff injury.
- Not appropriate for 3-/4-part or head-splitting patterns, where ORIF or arthroplasty is preferred.
- As with all proximal humerus fixation, tuberosity healing and rotator cuff integrity remain major drivers of functional outcome independent of nail-specific technique.

## Post-op protocol

No nail-specific rehabilitation timeline is described beyond the general proximal humerus pathway: early passive/active-assisted ROM, resisted strengthening deferred until radiographic union is evident (typically by 6–12 wk), and full unrestricted activity by 3–6 months once strength and ROM are near-symmetric with no residual instability.
