## Indications

Rockwood types IV–VI (all), and selected type III injuries in high-demand/overhead athletes or heavy manual laborers seeking the fastest predictable return.

## Positioning & setup

Superior "sabre" incision centered on the coracoid and distal clavicle. Key structure at risk: the suprascapular nerve, which lies deep to the coracoid during tunnel drilling.

## Steps

1. **Acute repair** (within 3 weeks of injury): primary coracoclavicular (CC) ligament repair augmented with a suture-button/suspensory device (cortical button systems) or allograft/autograft tendon reconstruction.
2. **Reconstruction technique options**: semitendinosus free graft reconstructing the conoid and trapezoid limbs (the "Weaver-Dunn modification") or anatomic double-tunnel techniques.
3. **Chronic/delayed presentations**: native ligament tissue is no longer usable — ligament reconstruction is required rather than primary repair.

## Pitfalls

- The suprascapular nerve is at risk deep to the coracoid during tunnel drilling.
- Chronic/delayed reconstructions carry inferior outcomes and higher loss-of-reduction rates than acute reconstruction — delayed high-grade AC injuries requiring surgery should not be deferred indefinitely.
- Loss of reduction is a recognized failure mode of suture-button/suspensory constructs, more common in chronic than acute reconstructions.
- AC joint post-traumatic arthrosis can develop even after operative reconstruction, in addition to after any grade of the original injury.

## Post-op protocol

- Sling/abduction brace 4–6 wk protecting the CC reconstruction.
- Passive/active-assisted ROM within brace limits from 2 wk.
- Full active ROM from 6 wk.
- Resisted strengthening from 8–12 wk.
- Return to contact/overhead sport at 4–6 months, once graft/construct maturation and full strength are confirmed.
