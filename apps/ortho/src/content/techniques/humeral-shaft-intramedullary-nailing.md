## Indications

Pathologic fractures (adds prophylactic fixation length), segmental fractures where plating both fragments would be morbid, polytrauma (faster fixation, less blood loss, earlier mobilization), and select proximal-third or very distal patterns where plate fixation is technically difficult.

## Positioning & setup

Antegrade nailing: superior entry through the rotator cuff interval. Retrograde nailing: posterior approach, olecranon fossa entry. Key structures at risk — antegrade: rotator cuff at the entry portal; retrograde: ulnar nerve, with risk of supracondylar fracture propagation.

## Steps

1. Choose antegrade versus retrograde nailing based on fracture pattern and clinical logistics (e.g., polytrauma favors nailing generally).
2. **Antegrade**: use careful entry portal technique to avoid rotator cuff injury; be aware of a technique-dependent risk of iatrogenic fracture propagation/comminution at insertion.
3. **Retrograde**: approach posteriorly with olecranon fossa entry; this avoids shoulder morbidity but carries risk of supracondylar fracture at the entry site.
4. Ensure adequate proximal and distal locking, especially for segmental/comminuted patterns.

## Pitfalls

- Antegrade nailing: shoulder pain is the most common patient-reported complaint post-nailing (reported in up to 10–20%).
- Retrograde nailing: risk of supracondylar fracture propagation at the entry site and elbow stiffness.
- The radial nerve is not directly visualized/exposed with nailing, which lowers but does not eliminate iatrogenic palsy risk from reaming/insertion.
- Recent comparative literature (2023–2025) is mixed: some registry/propensity-matched series show higher nonunion with plating versus nailing, contradicting older teaching; no single implant is unequivocally superior — individualize to fracture morphology (segmental/comminuted favors plate for length/rotation control unless polytrauma logistics favor nail), surgeon experience, and whether radial nerve exploration is independently indicated.

## Post-op protocol

- Elbow, wrist, hand motion from day 0; shoulder pendulum exercises early.
- Active shoulder/elbow ROM progressed as fracture stability allows.
- Resisted strengthening deferred until union confirmed (radiographic union expected by 12–16 wk).
- Return to heavy lifting/contact sport typically 4–6 months.
