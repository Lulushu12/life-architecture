## Indications

Elderly patients (generally ≥65-75 years, individualized by physiologic age/bone quality) with severely comminuted, unreconstructable native distal femur fractures (33-C3), particularly with osteoporotic bone unlikely to hold fixed-angle implants reliably; periprosthetic distal femur fractures around a failed or loose total knee arthroplasty where revision arthroplasty is indicated anyway (Su Type II/III with a loose or worn component); and salvage after failed ORIF/nonunion in an elderly patient.

## Positioning & setup

Standard positioning for a hinged knee arthroplasty. DFR uses a hinged distal femoral endoprosthesis, the same implant families used in oncologic megaprosthesis reconstruction.

## Steps

1. Resect the unreconstructable distal femur and prepare for a hinged distal femoral endoprosthesis, as in any hinged knee arthroplasty.
2. Preserve as much of the collateral ligament and extensor mechanism attachments as possible during exposure and resection.
3. Address leg-length and rotational alignment intraoperatively, as in any hinged knee arthroplasty.
4. Involve orthogeriatrics/anesthesia early given the comorbidity burden typical of this population.
5. Favor **primary DFR** (performed as the index procedure) over ORIF-first with DFR reserved for salvage, in appropriately selected frail elderly patients with unreconstructable fractures — comparative evidence shows primary DFR patients, despite being older and more comorbid, have significantly lower reoperation (12.2% vs 23.5%) and complication rates (15.7% vs 43.1%) than salvage DFR performed after failed ORIF.
6. For periprosthetic fractures, apply the Su classification: Type II/III patterns with a loose or worn component, or Type II/III in a low-demand elderly patient with poor bone stock, are the group in whom revision to DFR (rather than ORIF around a retained well-fixed component) is most strongly favored.

## Pitfalls

- DFR trades a shorter, higher-mortality-risk-adjusted recovery for a permanent large metal implant carrying its own long-term revision burden (aseptic loosening, infection, periprosthetic fracture around the stem) — counsel patients and families accordingly.
- A 2025 five-year district general hospital cohort (24 patients) reported DFR safely deliverable outside tertiary/oncologic centers, but with surgical site infection in 25%, 90-day mortality 8.3%, and 1-year mortality 16.7% — comparable to published tertiary-center data and to hip fracture mortality benchmarks, reinforcing that these patients need the same orthogeriatric, MDT-based perioperative pathway as fragility hip fractures.
- Periprosthetic fracture around the DFR/TKA stem itself is a long-term risk of any endoprosthetic reconstruction and requires the same vigilance and surgical planning as any periprosthetic fracture.

## Post-op protocol

- Immediate full weight bearing as tolerated is the principal advantage of DFR and should be encouraged from day 1-2 post-op as pain and medical status allow, following standard hinged total knee arthroplasty rehabilitation (early ROM, quadriceps activation, gait training with walker progressing to cane).
- Orthogeriatric/MDT perioperative pathway mirrors hip fracture care: same-day/next-day mobilization, delirium prevention, nutrition, bone-health/osteoporosis treatment initiation, and falls assessment, given the shared frail elderly population and comparable mortality risk profile.
