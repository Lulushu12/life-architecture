## Indications

Malignant primary spine tumors — chordoma (the most common primary malignant spine tumor in adults, notochordal remnant, sacrum/clivus predominant) and chondrosarcoma — are indicated for en bloc resection with wide margins per Enneking/WBB staging, since both are relatively resistant to conventional photon radiotherapy and margin adequacy is the dominant determinant of local control.

## Positioning & setup

**Biopsy planning is a surgical decision, not a radiology convenience**: the needle tract must be excisable en bloc at definitive surgery, so biopsy should be performed at (or in direct coordination with) the treating center — never as an unplanned outside referral — to avoid tumor seeding that compromises later margins. Preoperative imaging includes CT for bony detail and MRI for soft tissue/neural involvement. **Preoperative embolization** is used for hypervascular lesions (renal cell metastasis, aggressive hemangioma) to reduce intraoperative blood loss before resection.

## Steps

1. **Stage the tumor** using Enneking grade/compartment and WBB zone/layer mapping to define the resection corridor.
2. **Plan and perform biopsy at the treating center**, with the tract oriented so it can be excised en bloc at definitive surgery.
3. **Obtain preoperative embolization** if the lesion is hypervascular.
4. **Perform en bloc resection with wide margins**, per the WBB-mapped corridor — this is the mainstay for chordoma and chondrosarcoma given their relative photon radioresistance.
5. **For sacral chordoma specifically**: perform en bloc sacrectomy with the resection level dictated by proximal tumor extent; explicitly discuss the trade-off between oncologic margin and bowel/bladder/sexual function with the patient before surgery, since sacral nerve root sacrifice is often required.
6. **Consider adjuvant high-dose proton/carbon-ion RT or SBRT** for close/positive margins or unresectable disease (notably sacral chordoma), given resistance to conventional photon RT.
7. **For osteosarcoma and Ewing sarcoma** (multimodal disease, not primarily a resection-margin decision): neoadjuvant chemotherapy plus resection, with radiotherapy added for Ewing sarcoma.

## Pitfalls

- **Intralesional surgery carries a high local recurrence rate** and should be avoided when en bloc resection is feasible — historically a major problem for chordoma specifically.
- **Unplanned biopsy outside the treating center** risks tumor seeding along the needle tract, compromising the ability to achieve a wide margin at definitive surgery.
- **Functional deficits from planned nerve root sacrifice** in sacrectomy are an expected trade-off, not a complication of technical error — must be counseled explicitly preoperatively.
- **Wound complications** are common after large resections.
- **Late metastasis** is a recognized risk, notably for chordoma, warranting continued long-term oncologic surveillance beyond local control.
- Carbon fiber-reinforced (CFR-PEEK) instrumentation has shown at least equivalent clinical efficacy to titanium while improving postoperative imaging quality (reduced artifact), aiding surveillance for local recurrence — a reasonable consideration for reconstruction after resection.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a dedicated rehabilitation phase schedule for primary spine tumor resection distinct from general post-oncologic-spine-surgery recovery. Postoperative management centers on wound healing surveillance (given the elevated risk from large resections and any planned adjuvant RT), functional rehabilitation tailored to any nerve-root-sacrifice deficits (particularly bowel/bladder/sexual function after sacrectomy), and long-term oncologic surveillance for local recurrence and late metastasis.
