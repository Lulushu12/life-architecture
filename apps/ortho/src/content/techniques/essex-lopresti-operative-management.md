## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications:**
- Confirmed or strongly suspected Essex-Lopresti injury — a longitudinal radioulnar dissociation: radial head fracture + interosseous membrane (IOM) central band disruption + DRUJ injury — in an acute presentation, treated within days of injury.
- Any radial head fracture with wrist pain, tenderness over the DRUJ, or radial shortening >2–3 mm relative to the contralateral wrist on comparison films.
- Chronic/missed Essex-Lopresti with symptomatic longitudinal instability (persistent forearm/wrist pain, radiographically confirmed radial migration and ulnar impaction) — treated as a salvage reconstruction rather than the acute algorithm below.

**Contraindications / relative contraindications:**
- Radial head excision alone (without arthroplasty or fixation) is contraindicated whenever an Essex-Lopresti pattern has not been definitively excluded — resection guarantees proximal radial migration if the diagnosis is missed.
- Active infection at the operative site — treat/eradicate before implant placement.
- Severely comminuted, unreconstructable radial head in a patient who also has advanced radiocapitellar or PRUJ arthrosis — may be better served by a staged/salvage approach (discuss with the patient that outcomes are less predictable than acute treatment).
- IOM reconstruction in the acute setting is generally unnecessary (and therefore relatively contraindicated as a routine addition) when the radial head is restored and the DRUJ is stabilized early — reserve formal IOM reconstruction for chronic/missed cases.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Imaging:** true AP wrist radiograph with measurement of ulnar variance, compared to the contralateral wrist — radial shortening >2–3 mm relative to the contralateral side is diagnostic. Obtain this in **every** radial head fracture with any wrist symptoms; it is a low-cost, high-yield screening step that is frequently skipped, and the injury is easy to miss if it is not specifically sought.
- **MRI:** used selectively for equivocal IOM injury when radiographic ulnar variance is borderline; clinical stress exam under fluoroscopy (radius pull test/axial loading with radiographic monitoring of proximal radial migration) is often more practical intraoperatively than relying on MRI alone.
- **Implant/graft planning:** template radial head size from the contralateral (uninjured) elbow if available, or from the resected native fragments intraoperatively; have both monopolar and bipolar radial head implant options available if uncertain about final construct. For chronic cases anticipating IOM reconstruction, plan graft source (bone-patellar tendon-bone autograft, triceps fascia autograft, or synthetic/allograft options) and consent for the donor site.
- **Equipment checklist:** radial head ORIF small-fragment/headless screw set and radial head arthroplasty trial/implant system, DRUJ pinning wires (0.062in K-wires), TFCC repair suture anchors/instruments on standby, fluoroscopy for intraoperative stress testing (radius pull test) and ulnar variance confirmation, tourniquet.
- **Case-specific planning:** confirm with the patient/team whether this is an isolated Essex-Lopresti or part of a combined injury pattern (e.g., concomitant elbow fracture-dislocation); plan the sequence of radial head treatment before DRUJ/TFCC work, since restoring the proximal radial buttress is the foundational step.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- **Anesthesia:** general anesthesia or regional (supraclavicular/axillary brachial plexus block); general anesthesia is preferred if intraoperative stress testing of the DRUJ and forearm axial loading under fluoroscopy is planned, since a fully relaxed limb gives a more reliable stress exam.
- **Position:** supine with the arm on a radiolucent hand table, allowing free access to both the elbow (lateral Kocher/EDC-splitting approach for the radial head) and the wrist (dorsal approach for DRUJ pinning/TFCC work) without repositioning; forearm freely mobile for intraoperative pronation/supination testing.
- **Tourniquet:** pneumatic tourniquet on the upper arm, exsanguinate, inflate to standard limb pressure; note that stress-testing the DRUJ and performing the radius pull test are more reliable once the tourniquet is deflated and normal soft-tissue tension is restored, so plan a deflation point before final stability confirmation if time allows.
- **Fluoroscopy:** mini C-arm draped into the field, used for (1) intraoperative radius pull test — axially load and pull on the forearm while imaging the radiocapitellar joint and ulnar variance to confirm the diagnosis and assess reduction, (2) confirming radial head implant/fixation position, and (3) confirming DRUJ reduction before and after pinning.
- **Draping:** circumferential extremity prep from above the tourniquet to the fingertips, allowing simultaneous or sequential access to elbow and wrist incisions.
- **Landmarks marked:** Kocher interval (elbow, between anconeus and ECU) for the radial head; dorsal wrist landmarks (Lister's tubercle, extensor compartments) if open TFCC repair or DRUJ exploration is planned.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. Confirm the diagnosis intraoperatively if any doubt remains: with the tourniquet deflated, perform the **radius pull test** — grip the forearm distally and pull axially while imaging the radiocapitellar joint under fluoroscopy; proximal migration of the radius relative to the capitellum (or an increase in ulnar-positive variance at the wrist) confirms IOM incompetence.
2. **Treat the radial head first** — this restores the proximal radial buttress and is the foundational step of the reconstruction:
   - If reconstructable (simple/marginal fracture, ≤3 major fragments), perform ORIF using headless compression screws in the "safe zone" or a low-profile plate, buried beneath the articular cartilage where possible.
   - If comminuted/non-reconstructable, proceed to **radial head arthroplasty (RHA)** — a spacer/RHA is essentially mandatory in this injury, since resection alone guarantees proximal migration; size the implant to the resected native head fragments, erring toward undersizing (trial down, not up) if uncertain, to avoid overstuffing the radiocapitellar and PRUJ joints.
3. Confirm restoration of radiocapitellar length and congruent tracking through a full pronation-supination arc under direct vision and fluoroscopy before moving to the wrist.
4. **Address the DRUJ:** through a small dorsal wrist incision (or percutaneously under fluoroscopic guidance), reduce the DRUJ with the forearm held in neutral-to-slight supination, and place a radioulnar K-wire (0.062in) proximal to the sigmoid notch to provisionally stabilize the joint; this pin protects the healing/reconstructing IOM and TFCC while the radial head implant/fixation matures, and should remain in place for 4–6 weeks.
5. **Assess and repair the TFCC** if a large fragment (e.g., ulnar styloid base fracture) is present or frank instability persists after DRUJ pinning — repair via suture anchor or transosseous technique through a small dorsal or ulnar-sided approach as fragment size and instability pattern dictate.
6. **Reassess longitudinal stability** with the radial head construct and DRUJ pin in place: repeat the axial pull test under fluoroscopy — the radial head implant/fixation and DRUJ pin together should prevent further proximal migration.
7. **IOM reconstruction** is not routinely performed acutely — reserve it for chronic/missed cases with established symptomatic longitudinal instability. If indicated (typically at a later, staged procedure), reconstruct the IOM central band using autograft (bone-patellar tendon-bone or triceps fascia) or a synthetic graft, tensioned with the forearm in neutral rotation and the radial head implant/fixation restoring proximal length.
8. Irrigate, achieve hemostasis, close the elbow and wrist incisions in layers, and apply a well-padded long-arm splint or cast with the forearm in neutral-to-slight supination to protect the DRUJ pin and radial head construct.
9. At 4–6 weeks postoperatively, remove the DRUJ pin in clinic under fluoroscopic or direct confirmation of continued DRUJ stability before beginning forearm rotation.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Obtain a comparison AP wrist film of the contralateral side in every radial head fracture with wrist symptoms — this single low-cost step is the most reliable way to avoid missing the diagnosis before it becomes a resection performed in ignorance of the injury.
- Perform the intraoperative radius pull test with the tourniquet deflated whenever there is diagnostic uncertainty — tourniquet inflation can mask subtle proximal migration by altering normal soft-tissue tension.
- Treat the radial head before the DRUJ — restoring the proximal buttress first re-establishes the reference point against which DRUJ and wrist reduction are then judged.
- When sizing a radial head implant in this injury, err toward the smaller trial — overstuffing is a well-documented cause of capitellar erosion and pain, and an undersized implant is far easier to revise than an overstuffed one is to salvage.
- Place the DRUJ pin proximal to the sigmoid notch (not through the joint itself) to allow it to protect rotational stability without blocking eventual joint motion once removed.
- Counsel every patient explicitly that this is a "three-ring" injury (radiocapitellar joint, IOM central band, DRUJ/TFCC) — a construct that stabilizes only the radial head without protecting the DRUJ during IOM healing risks silent progression to chronic instability even after a technically good radial head reconstruction.
- Involve a hand/wrist-trained colleague early for the TFCC/DRUJ portion of the case if this is not routine practice for the primary surgeon — the wrist-side pathology is easy to underappreciate for an elbow-focused surgeon and vice versa.
- Document and communicate the diagnosis clearly in the operative note and to the patient — a missed diagnosis at this stage converts a straightforward acute reconstruction into a difficult chronic salvage problem.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- Do not resect the radial head without excluding this injury — wrist pain out of proportion or DRUJ tenderness on exam should prompt evaluation before resection. Average proximal migration can exceed 5–10 mm over months if unrecognized.
- Missed/chronic Essex-Lopresti with established ulnar impaction and radiocapitellar arthrosis is a salvage problem (ulnar shortening osteotomy, radial head revision arthroplasty, or IOM reconstruction) — this underscores why acute recognition changes the entire trajectory.
- Radial head arthroplasty-specific pitfall: oversizing the implant causes capitellar erosion, stiffness, and pain (overstuffing the radiocapitellar and PRUJ joints) — size to the resected native head fragments or trial down when uncertain.
- A DRUJ pin left in too short a time (before adequate IOM/TFCC healing) risks recurrent instability once forearm rotation resumes; conversely, leaving it beyond 6 weeks risks pin-tract complications and unnecessary stiffness — 4–6 weeks is the target window.
- Updated review evidence (2024) reinforces that early recognition (high index of suspicion with any radial head fracture plus wrist pain) and acute treatment (radial head reconstruction/arthroplasty plus DRUJ stabilization) produce substantially better outcomes than delayed/missed diagnosis managed as a chronic salvage problem.

## Postoperative Protocol

- **DRUJ pin:** removal at 4–6 weeks in clinic, then begin forearm rotation AROM progressively.
- **Radial head ORIF/RHA component:** sling for comfort 0–1 week with AROM begun within the first few days; progressive AROM including forearm rotation from 1–6 weeks, avoiding resisted supination against the healing/implant construct; strengthening once healed/implant stable at 6–12 weeks, with return to full activity by 3 months in most patients.
- **Return to work/sport:** light duty ~6 weeks; full manual labor/contact sport 3–4 months, contingent on union/implant stability and rotational strength.
