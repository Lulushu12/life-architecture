## Indications

Essex-Lopresti is a longitudinal radioulnar dissociation: radial head fracture + interosseous membrane (IOM) central band disruption + DRUJ injury. Acute recognition is paramount — an unrecognized IOM injury after radial head excision allows the radius to migrate proximally, transferring load to the ulnocarpal joint (ulnar impaction) and destabilizing the DRUJ and radiocapitellar joint chronically. Average proximal migration can exceed 5–10 mm over months if unrecognized.

## Positioning & setup

Obtain a true AP wrist film and measure ulnar variance, comparing to the contralateral wrist — radial shortening >2–3 mm relative to the contralateral side is diagnostic. A radial head fracture with ANY wrist symptoms warrants this comparison as a low-cost, high-yield screening step. MRI is used selectively for equivocal IOM injury, though clinical stress exam under fluoroscopy is often more practical intraoperatively.

## Steps

1. **Treat the radial head** — fix if reconstructable, or perform RHA if comminuted. A spacer/RHA is essentially mandatory to maintain the proximal radial buttress, since resection alone guarantees proximal migration.
2. **Address the DRUJ** — reduce and provisionally pin the DRUJ (radioulnar K-wire) for 4–6 weeks to protect the healing/reconstructing IOM and TFCC while the radial head implant/fixation matures.
3. **Repair the TFCC** if a large fragment or frank instability is present.
4. **IOM reconstruction** (autograft, e.g., bone-patellar tendon-bone or triceps fascia, or synthetic graft) is reserved for chronic/missed cases with symptomatic longitudinal instability — acute anatomic reduction and RHA alone can be sufficient if the diagnosis is made early.

## Pitfalls

- Do not resect the radial head without excluding this injury — wrist pain out of proportion or DRUJ tenderness on exam should prompt evaluation before resection.
- Missed/chronic Essex-Lopresti with established ulnar impaction and radiocapitellar arthrosis is a salvage problem (ulnar shortening osteotomy, radial head revision arthroplasty, or IOM reconstruction) — this underscores why acute recognition changes the entire trajectory.
- Radial head arthroplasty-specific pitfall: oversizing the implant causes capitellar erosion, stiffness, and pain (overstuffing the radiocapitellar and PRUJ joints) — size to the resected native head fragments or trial down when uncertain.
- Updated review evidence (2024) reinforces that early recognition (high index of suspicion with any radial head fracture plus wrist pain) and acute treatment (radial head reconstruction/arthroplasty plus DRUJ stabilization) produce substantially better outcomes than delayed/missed diagnosis managed as a chronic salvage problem.

## Post-op protocol

- **DRUJ pin:** removal at 4–6 weeks in clinic, then begin forearm rotation AROM progressively.
- **Radial head ORIF/RHA component:** sling for comfort 0–1 week with AROM begun within the first few days; progressive AROM including forearm rotation from 1–6 weeks, avoiding resisted supination against the healing/implant construct; strengthening once healed/implant stable at 6–12 weeks, with return to full activity by 3 months in most patients.
- **Return to work/sport:** light duty ~6 weeks; full manual labor/contact sport 3–4 months, contingent on union/implant stability and rotational strength.
