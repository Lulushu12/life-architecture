## Indications

- **Tension-band wiring (TBW):** simple transverse fractures at the level of the trochlear notch in low-demand patients (Mayo IIA).
- **Plate fixation:** comminuted, oblique, or distally-extending fracture lines, and osteoporotic bone (Mayo IIB/III).
- **Transolecranon fracture-dislocation (Monteggia/olecranon variant, Mayo III):** requires plate fixation with restoration of the trochlear notch and treatment of any associated coronoid or radial head injury.
- Displacement threshold for surgery: **>2 mm** articular step-off or extensor mechanism disruption (including a positive resisted-extension lag even with minimal displacement on static imaging).

## Positioning & setup

Assess skin integrity circumferentially before proceeding — olecranon and proximal ulna fractures are frequently open due to the subcutaneous location; treat per the BOAST open fracture standard (debridement <24h for low-grade injuries, IV antibiotics within 1h of arrival).

## Steps

1. **Tension-band wiring:** place two parallel 1.6mm K-wires plus an 18-gauge figure-eight wire (or a cannulated screw with a tension-band wire) across the fracture — this converts the extensor pull into interfragmentary compression.
2. **Plate fixation:** apply a pre-contoured locking olecranon (proximal ulna) plate for comminuted, oblique, or distally-extending fracture lines and for osteoporotic bone — this provides angularly stable fixation independent of an intact anterior cortex, allowing more reliable early motion.
3. **Hybrid alternative:** for simple transverse fractures, an intramedullary screw (± suture-tension band augmentation) offers a lower-profile construct that reduces hardware prominence while preserving fixation strength.
4. **Transolecranon fracture-dislocation:** reduce and fix the olecranon anatomically first to restore the trochlear notch and thereby reduce the ulnohumeral joint; then address any associated coronoid or radial head injury as in terrible triad.

## Pitfalls

- TBW is a dynamic compression construct that depends on an intact anterior cortical hinge under axial (extensor) load — it fails in oblique, comminuted, or distally-extending fracture lines; use plating instead in these patterns.
- Reported hardware-irritation/removal rates after TBW are high (roughly 9–80% across series) — counsel patients that a second surgery for wire removal is common.
- Because olecranon fractures are frequently open, treat per standard open-fracture principles (early debridement, antibiotics, staged fixation if grossly contaminated) to minimize infection risk.
- Nonunion risk is elevated after inadequate fixation of comminuted/oblique patterns treated with TBW.

## Post-op protocol

- **0–1 week:** splint immobilization for comfort/soft-tissue rest.
- **1–2 weeks:** transition to a hinged brace, begin gentle AROM (flexion typically well-tolerated early; extension against gravity/resisted triceps activity delayed).
- **6 weeks:** avoid resisted elbow extension (triceps loading against the fixation) until this point to protect the tension-band/plate construct from extensor-mechanism pull-off, then progress resisted strengthening.
- **3 months:** full strengthening; return to contact/manual labor once radiographically healed and strength restored.
