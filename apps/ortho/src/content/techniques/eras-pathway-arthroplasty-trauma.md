## Indications

Elective hip and knee arthroplasty, and the trauma analogue applied to hip fracture surgery. Goal: standardize a bundle of preoperative, intraoperative, and postoperative measures that reduce length of stay, opioid consumption, and complications without increasing readmissions.

## Positioning & setup

Not a positioning-dependent procedure — ERAS is a perioperative pathway bundle spanning preoperative counseling through discharge, layered onto whatever surgical technique is otherwise indicated.

## Steps

**Core elements (elective arthroplasty):**
1. Preoperative patient education and expectation-setting; "prehabilitation" where feasible.
2. Minimize preoperative fasting; carbohydrate loading up to 2h before surgery in appropriate patients.
3. Avoid long-acting sedative premedication.
4. Prefer spinal/regional anesthesia over general anesthesia where suitable.
5. Build multimodal, opioid-sparing analgesia around periarticular local infiltration and/or peripheral nerve blocks.
6. Use TXA as standard blood-conservation practice.
7. Avoid routine surgical drains and urinary catheters where not specifically indicated.
8. Maintain intraoperative normothermia and goal-directed (rather than liberal) fluid therapy.
9. Mobilize early — same calendar day as surgery wherever feasible, with weight-bearing as tolerated for cemented/standard constructs.
10. Resume oral intake early and discontinue IV fluids/catheters early.
11. Base discharge on objective functional criteria rather than a fixed postoperative day.

**Trauma analogue (hip fracture):**
1. Expedite time to surgery to **<24–36 hours** from admission.
2. Initiate orthogeriatric co-management from admission.
3. Avoid unnecessary preoperative fasting delays.
4. Use standardized multimodal analgesia — notably fascia iliaca or PENG blocks to reduce preoperative opioid need.
5. Use TXA.
6. Mobilize immediately postoperatively.

## Pitfalls

- ERAS pathway implementation in elective hip/knee arthroplasty is consistently associated with shortened length of stay (commonly from a historical 4–5 days down to 1–2 days, with same-day/next-day discharge achievable in selected outpatient-eligible patients), reduced total opioid consumption, reduced postoperative nausea/vomiting, and earlier physiotherapy milestones — importantly, LOS reduction has not come at the cost of increased 30/90-day readmission in the bulk of the published cohort and registry literature.
- A 2018–2024 systematic review across 7,206 patients (2,148 ERAS vs 5,058 traditional care) reaffirmed significantly shortened LOS and reduced intraoperative blood loss with ERAS, with no significant adverse effect on readmission rates.
- Do not treat spinal/regional anesthesia as a mandated component: large contemporary randomized comparisons in hip fracture surgery specifically found no significant difference in 60-day mortality or functional recovery between spinal and general anesthesia — the choice should be individualized (anticoagulation status, spinal deformity, hemodynamic stability, patient preference) rather than mandated, even though regional retains secondary benefits (analgesia, nausea, delirium signal in some cohorts).
- In trauma, bundled programs built on expedited surgery + orthogeriatric co-management + standardized analgesia + TXA + immediate mobilization (formalized in UK national hip fracture audit "Best Practice Tariff"-style frameworks) are associated with reduced 30-day mortality and shorter length of stay compared with unstructured care — the effect is attributed to the bundle, not any single element in isolation.

## Post-op protocol

ERAS discharge and rehabilitation follow the general phased perioperative pathway: same-day/POD1 mobilization with weight-bearing as tolerated for standard cemented fixation; early active/active-assisted ROM and VTE prophylaxis initiation in Phase 1; progressive gait training and suture/staple removal at 10–14 days in Phase 2 (Week 1–2); progressive resistance training, driving resumption at 4–6 weeks, and return to sedentary/desk work at 2–4 weeks in Phase 3 (Week 2–6); return to manual labor around 3 months and low-impact recreational activity at 3 months in Phase 4; and individualized return to higher-impact activity at 6–12 months in Phase 5. Discharge itself is based on objective functional criteria rather than a fixed postoperative day.
