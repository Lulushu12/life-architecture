## Indications

Failure of activity modification, footwear change, and at least one round of injections (corticosteroid and/or alcohol sclerosing) for symptomatic Morton (interdigital) neuroma — most often the 3rd webspace, followed by the 2nd. Confirmed diagnostically by clinical Mulder click plus web-space palpation and, ideally, ultrasound showing a lesion >5 mm (which correlates more strongly with symptoms).

## Positioning & setup

Choice of **dorsal approach** (less wound-healing morbidity; standard first choice) or **plantar approach** (better direct visualization/access; some surgeons prefer it for revision cases, at the cost of a weight-bearing scar). Correct index-level identification preoperatively is essential — imaging (ultrasound, or MRI for equivocal/revision cases) should confirm the symptomatic webspace before incision.

## Steps

1. Confirm the correct webspace level (most commonly 3rd, then 2nd) before proceeding.
2. Approach via the dorsal (standard) or plantar (select cases, revision) incision.
3. Identify and excise the affected common digital nerve segment (perineural fibrosis, not a true neuroma).
4. Achieve hemostasis and close, counseling the patient preoperatively about expected permanent numbness of the involved web space.

## Pitfalls

- A recognized risk is a symptomatic **stump (amputation) neuroma** — reported incidence spans a wide range across series (roughly 2–30%, depending on definition/follow-up).
- **Permanent numbness of the involved web space is an expected trade-off, not a complication per se** — it must be consented for preoperatively, not discovered postoperatively.
- Persistent/recurrent pain can result from a **missed second neuroma** or an **incorrect index level** — confirm level with imaging before committing to the incision.
- The plantar approach carries a weight-bearing scar risk; weigh this against its visualization advantage, particularly in revision cases.
- Exhaust non-operative and injection-based options first: reported success with alcohol sclerosing injection series ranges roughly two-thirds to just under 90% (heterogeneous, mostly single-center case series), and should generally be attempted before proceeding to neurectomy.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: postoperative shoe, weight-bear as tolerated, wound care.
- **Weeks 2–4**: transition to regular shoes as comfort allows; gradual return to activity.
- **Return to sport**: typically **4–6 weeks**; counsel on expected permanent web-space numbness distal to the excision site.
- Reported success (symptom resolution) is in the range of **80–90%**.
