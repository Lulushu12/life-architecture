## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications**
- Failure of activity modification, footwear change, and at least one round of injections (corticosteroid and/or alcohol sclerosing) for symptomatic Morton (interdigital) neuroma — most often the 3rd webspace, followed by the 2nd.
- Confirmed diagnostically by clinical Mulder click plus web-space palpation and, ideally, ultrasound showing a lesion **>5 mm**, which correlates more strongly with symptoms.
- Recurrent/persistent symptoms after a prior injection series where imaging clearly localizes a single, correctable lesion.

**Contraindications / relative contraindications**
- Diagnostic uncertainty — a normal or equivocal ultrasound/MRI, or a physical exam inconsistent with the imaged level, should prompt re-evaluation (stress fracture, plantar plate tear, tarsal tunnel syndrome, lumbar radiculopathy) rather than proceeding to excision at a presumed level.
- Multiple ipsilateral webspace symptoms without a clearly dominant lesion — operating on the wrong or an incomplete set of levels is a recognized cause of persistent pain.
- Active infection or a compromised vascular/soft-tissue status of the forefoot.
- Patients unwilling to accept **permanent** numbness of the involved web space — this is an expected consequence of excision, not a rare complication, and must be part of informed consent before the patient commits to surgery over continued injections.
- Consider exhausting alcohol sclerosing injection (reported success roughly two-thirds to just under 90% across heterogeneous series) before neurectomy in patients who wish to preserve web-space sensation, since sclerosing injection avoids the permanent numbness trade-off inherent to excision.

## Preoperative Planning

- Confirm the symptomatic webspace with ultrasound (preferred first-line, sensitivity/specificity generally >90% in experienced hands) or MRI (reserved for equivocal cases, revision planning, or when a prior webspace injection has clouded ultrasound interpretation with post-injection fibrosis).
- Screen for a second, adjacent symptomatic neuroma (interspace pain can be multifocal) and document the specific dominant level(s) before the day of surgery.
- Discuss dorsal versus plantar approach with the patient in advance, particularly for revision cases, and set expectations for permanent numbness distal to the excision.
- Instrumentation: fine dissecting scissors/nerve instruments, small self-retaining retractor for the intermetatarsal space, and bipolar cautery for meticulous hemostasis of the vascular intermetatarsal space.
- For revision surgery, review prior operative notes/imaging to confirm whether the original level was adequately addressed and whether a stump neuroma versus a missed second lesion is more likely.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- Ankle block (popliteal or combined saphenous/sciatic-territory block) with sedation is standard and well suited to this short outpatient procedure; general or spinal anesthesia are alternatives.
- Supine position, foot free at the end of the table.
- Thigh or calf tourniquet after Esmarch exsanguination — a bloodless field materially improves visualization of the nerve within the fatty intermetatarsal space, where bleeding can otherwise obscure a small-caliber structure.
- Mark the affected webspace(s) on the skin preoperatively based on the confirmed imaging level(s), with the patient/site verification performed before incision per standard site-marking protocol — wrong-level surgery is avoidable but has occurred in this anatomically crowded region.
- No specific fluoroscopy is required for this soft-tissue procedure.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. Confirm the correct webspace level (most commonly 3rd, then 2nd) against the marked site and preoperative imaging before incising.
2. **Dorsal approach (standard first choice)**: make a longitudinal incision in the dorsal webspace between the metatarsal heads, extending from just proximal to the MTP joints distally into the web. Spread bluntly between the metatarsal heads, retract them apart with a small self-retaining or handheld retractor, and incise the transverse intermetatarsal ligament to expose the nerve lying just deep to it — releasing this ligament alone provides significant decompression and is a useful maneuver even before excision.
3. **Plantar approach (select cases, especially revision)**: make a longitudinal or slightly curved incision in the plantar webspace, staying mindful of the eventual weight-bearing scar location; this approach affords more direct visualization of the nerve and its bifurcation without needing to retract the metatarsal heads, which some surgeons find advantageous for identifying a stump neuroma or a nerve that has scarred to surrounding tissue after prior surgery.
4. Identify the common digital nerve as it courses distally and bifurcates into its terminal branches near the webspace — the neuroma is typically a fusiform enlargement just proximal to or at the bifurcation.
5. Trace the nerve proximally as far as the incision reasonably allows, since transecting it more proximally (rather than immediately at the bifurcation) is thought to reduce the chance of a symptomatic stump neuroma forming directly beneath the weight-bearing metatarsal heads.
6. Transect the nerve proximally, then trace and excise it distally through the bifurcation, removing the fibrotic (perineural fibrosis, not a true neoplastic neuroma) segment en bloc.
7. Inspect the adjacent webspaces and the excised specimen; if a second symptomatic level was identified preoperatively, address it in the same setting through a separate exposure.
8. Achieve meticulous hemostasis with bipolar cautery — the intermetatarsal fat pad is vascular and inadequate hemostasis risks a postoperative hematoma that can itself contribute to later stump-neuroma symptoms.
9. Irrigate, close the deep fascia/subcutaneous layer if using the plantar approach (to minimize adherence of the nerve stump to the plantar skin), and close skin in standard fashion.
10. Apply a light compressive forefoot dressing; counsel the patient in the recovery area or postoperatively about the expected permanent numbness of the web space distal to the excision site.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Use a bloodless field (tourniquet) — the fatty intermetatarsal space bleeds readily and a small-caliber nerve is easy to lose track of without good hemostasis and visualization.
- Release the transverse intermetatarsal ligament early in the dorsal approach; this alone improves exposure substantially and is a useful checkpoint that you are in the correct plane.
- Trace the nerve as proximally as the exposure comfortably allows before transecting it — resecting more proximally, away from the weight-bearing metatarsal head zone, is a widely practiced strategy to reduce symptomatic stump-neuroma formation.
- For revision cases or when a stump neuroma is suspected, favor the plantar approach for its superior direct visualization of scarred, retracted, or adherent nerve tissue.
- Confirm the correct level against imaging and the marked site immediately before incision — in a crowded, similar-appearing region, this final check is cheap insurance against wrong-level surgery.
- Send the excised specimen for pathologic confirmation (perineural fibrosis) — this both confirms the diagnosis and documents that an adequate specimen, not just a nerve segment, was excised.
- If a second neuroma is suspected preoperatively (multifocal symptoms), plan and consent for possible multi-level excision in the same setting rather than staging unnecessarily.
- Keep the plantar-approach incision line planned to avoid direct weight-bearing contact where possible, since scar tenderness under load is the main drawback weighed against this approach's visualization advantage.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- A recognized risk is a symptomatic **stump (amputation) neuroma** — reported incidence spans a wide range across series (roughly 2–30%, depending on definition/follow-up).
- **Permanent numbness of the involved web space is an expected trade-off, not a complication per se** — it must be consented for preoperatively, not discovered postoperatively.
- Persistent/recurrent pain can result from a **missed second neuroma** or an **incorrect index level** — confirm level with imaging before committing to the incision.
- The plantar approach carries a weight-bearing scar risk; weigh this against its visualization advantage, particularly in revision cases.
- Exhaust non-operative and injection-based options first: reported success with alcohol sclerosing injection series ranges roughly two-thirds to just under 90% (heterogeneous, mostly single-center case series), and should generally be attempted before proceeding to neurectomy.
- Inadequate hemostasis risks a postoperative hematoma, which can contribute to fibrosis and stump-neuroma symptoms.
- Transecting the nerve too distally, close to the metatarsal heads, is thought to increase the likelihood of a stump neuroma forming directly in the weight-bearing zone.

## Postoperative Protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: postoperative shoe, weight-bear as tolerated, wound care.
- **Weeks 2–4**: transition to regular shoes as comfort allows; gradual return to activity.
- **Return to sport**: typically **4–6 weeks**; counsel on expected permanent web-space numbness distal to the excision site.
- Reported success (symptom resolution) is in the range of **80–90%**.
