## Indications

Cobb >45–50° at/near skeletal maturity, a progressive curve despite bracing, documented progression >5°/6 months, or significant curve-related pulmonary/cosmetic concern.

## Positioning & setup

Segmental pedicle screw instrumentation is placed via a standard posterior midline approach along the involved levels; the monograph does not detail room setup beyond this. Intraoperative neuromonitoring is standard practice supporting the safety of instrumentation and derotation maneuvers (its use underlies the low reported rate of neurologic injury with modern constructs).

## Steps

1. **Determine fusion levels from the Lenke type and modifiers.** All structural curves must be included; the lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) is generally chosen as the "stable" or "neutral" vertebra to minimize the risk of postoperative decompensation and distal adding-on.
2. **Decide whether selective thoracic fusion is appropriate.** This is favorable in Lenke 1A/1B/1C patterns with an A or B lumbar modifier (sparing the lumbar curve); a C lumbar modifier is less likely to tolerate selective fusion because of higher risk of postoperative coronal decompensation.
3. **Place segmental pedicle screw instrumentation** — this has replaced hook and hybrid constructs as the current standard.
4. **Perform direct vertebral derotation** to improve axial-plane (rotational) correction beyond what simple rod rotation achieves.
5. **Reserve anterior release (open or thoracoscopic)** for very rigid curves only — >75–90° with correction <50% on side-bending films; it is now rarely needed given modern posterior correction techniques.

## Pitfalls

- **Crankshaft phenomenon**: risk if a very skeletally immature spine (open triradiate cartilage) undergoes posterior-only fusion without anterior growth arrest.
- **Incorrect LIV selection** causing distal adding-on.
- **Coronal decompensation** after overly aggressive selective thoracic fusion, particularly with an unfavorable (C) lumbar modifier.

## Post-op protocol

- **0–2 weeks**: inpatient mobilization from postoperative day 1; segmental pedicle screw fixation generally does not require formal bracing (surgeon-dependent); bend/lift/twist (BLT) precautions; walking as tolerated.
- **2–6 weeks**: gradual increase in ambulation; no PE or sports; avoid prolonged sitting; driving typically deferred until ~4 weeks and off opioid analgesia.
- **6–12 weeks**: full return to school typical by 6 weeks; light activities resumed; precautions gradually lifted.
- **3–6 months**: non-contact sports and swimming typically permitted around 3 months; begin core strengthening.
- **6–12 months**: full return to contact/collision sports generally allowed at 9–12 months once fusion is confirmed solid on imaging.
- **Reported complication rates**: pseudarthrosis ~1–3% with modern segmental screw constructs, surgical site infection ~1–2%, neurologic injury <1% (reduced further with intraoperative neuromonitoring).
