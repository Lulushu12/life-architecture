## Indications & Contraindications

**Indications**: Cobb >45–50° at/near skeletal maturity, a progressive curve despite bracing, documented progression >5°/6 months, or significant curve-related pulmonary/cosmetic concern.

**Contraindications / relative contraindications**:
- Non-idiopathic curve pattern (atypical curve type, left thoracic curve, pain, rapid progression, age <10) — obtain whole-spine MRI first to rule out syringomyelia, Chiari malformation, or a tethered cord before treating as idiopathic; a neuromuscular or syndromic curve changes the surgical strategy substantially.
- Skeletally very immature patient (open triradiate cartilage, Risser 0) with a curve small enough that observation, bracing, or a growth-friendly/fusionless approach (e.g., vertebral body tethering in a well-selected candidate) is a reasonable alternative — posterior-only fusion in this group carries crankshaft risk.
- Active infection, uncontrolled medical comorbidity, or a patient/family unable to commit to the postoperative precautions and rehabilitation course.
- Unrealistic expectations regarding cosmetic outcome or degree of correction achievable.

## Preoperative Planning

- **Imaging**: EOS or long-cassette standing PA/lateral radiographs; supine side-bending and traction films to determine which curves are structural (per Lenke criteria) and to assess flexibility; whole-spine MRI if any atypical feature is present.
- **Classification**: determine the Lenke curve type, lumbar modifier (A/B/C), and sagittal thoracic modifier (-/N/+) to define which curves must be included in the fusion.
- **Levels**: template the upper and lower instrumented vertebra (UIV/LIV) on the bending films, targeting the stable (neutral, CSVL-bisected) vertebra as LIV to minimize distal adding-on.
- **Implants**: select rod material and diameter — cobalt-chrome offers greater corrective stiffness for stiffer curves, titanium offers a lower profile and is less notch-sensitive; template screw size at each level from the preoperative CT/radiographs where available.
- **Neuromonitoring**: plan intraoperative SSEP/MEP/EMG, with baseline signals obtained preoperatively when feasible.
- **Blood management**: type and screen, tranexamic acid protocol, cell salvage setup.
- Rarely, halo-gravity traction is used preoperatively for very large or rigid curves to improve flexibility before definitive surgery.

## Anesthesia & Positioning

- **General anesthesia with TIVA** to optimize neuromonitoring signal quality — inhalational agents blunt MEP amplitude.
- Arterial line for larger cases, Foley catheter, large-bore IV access.
- **Prone positioning on a radiolucent spinal frame** (Jackson table or equivalent) with chest and iliac crest bolsters, abdomen hanging free to reduce epidural venous pressure and intraoperative bleeding.
- Arms padded and positioned at <90° abduction to avoid brachial plexus stretch; face and eyes padded in a horseshoe or foam headrest with periodic pressure checks; breasts/genitalia positioned to avoid direct pressure.
- Fluoroscopy (mobile C-arm, AP/lateral) or O-arm/navigation draped into the field for intraoperative level check and screw trajectory confirmation.
- **Baseline SSEP/MEP obtained before and immediately after positioning** to confirm no positioning-related signal change before incision.
- Skin marked and prepped from the planned UIV to LIV with a wide margin; chlorhexidine-based prep and standard spine draping.

## Step-by-Step Technique

1. **Timeout** confirming laterality/levels; review the Lenke classification and planned fusion levels on the OR wall imaging.
2. **Midline posterior incision** over the planned levels; subperiosteal dissection along the spinous processes, lamina, facets, and transverse processes bilaterally, minimizing muscle stripping beyond what is needed for screw placement and decortication.
3. **Expose to the tips of the transverse processes** at each level to use as anatomic landmarks for screw entry points.
4. **Place pedicle screws** using the freehand (anatomic landmark) technique at each planned level bilaterally, working from caudal to cranial (or the reverse, per surgeon preference), with fluoroscopic or navigation confirmation as needed. Segmental pedicle screw instrumentation has replaced hook and hybrid constructs as the current standard.
5. **Confirm screw trajectory and containment** — pedicle probe feel, fluoroscopy, and/or triggered EMG threshold testing at each screw.
6. **Decorticate the posterior elements** (lamina, facets, transverse processes) to prepare the arthrodesis bed; harvest local autograft (facet/lamina bone) and supplement with allograft or BMP per surgeon preference.
7. **Contour the rods** to the desired sagittal profile, restoring or maintaining physiologic thoracic kyphosis and lumbar lordosis — hypokyphotic ("-" modifier) curves need deliberate kyphosis restoration in the contour.
8. **Perform direct vertebral derotation** at the apical and periapical segments using derotation instruments engaged on the pedicle screws, to correct axial-plane rotation beyond what rod rotation alone achieves.
9. **Sequentially reduce and seat the rod**, typically working from a fixed point mid-construct outward, to achieve coronal and sagittal correction.
10. **Apply compression across the concavity and distraction across the convexity** as needed to fine-tune segmental correction and disc space balance.
11. **Assess coronal and sagittal alignment and shoulder balance intraoperatively** (fluoroscopy, direct visualization, T1 tilt) before final tightening.
12. **Final tightening of set screws**; trim rod ends.
13. **Obtain a post-correction neuromonitoring check** and perform an intraoperative wake-up test if any signal change is noted.
14. **Pack the decorticated bed with graft**, achieve meticulous hemostasis, and place a subfascial drain if desired.
15. **Close in layers** (fascia, subcutaneous, subcuticular skin); consider vancomycin powder in the wound per institutional protocol.
16. **Obtain final AP/lateral imaging** to confirm instrumentation position and correction before leaving the room.

## Technical Tips & Pearls

- Use TIVA and establish a solid neuromonitoring baseline before positioning — a poor or absent baseline makes intraoperative interpretation unreliable exactly when it matters most.
- Free-hand pedicle screw placement is safe and efficient in experienced hands, but confirm entry point and trajectory using anatomic landmarks (transverse process, facet joint, pars) at every level rather than relying on one technique for the whole curve — pedicle width and angle change significantly moving up the thoracic spine.
- Perform direct vertebral derotation at the curve apex before final rod locking — this meaningfully improves rib prominence and axial correction beyond passive rod rotation alone.
- Select the LIV as the stable (neutral, CSVL-bisected) vertebra rather than simply the last structural level, to minimize distal adding-on.
- In a favorable Lenke 1A/1B/1C pattern with an A or B lumbar modifier, lean toward a shorter selective thoracic fusion to preserve lumbar motion segments — but confirm intraoperatively that the unfused lumbar curve corrects passively (spontaneous correction) before committing.
- Contour the rod deliberately to restore physiologic thoracic kyphosis and avoid flat-back, particularly in hypokyphotic ("-" modifier) curves.
- Use compression across the concavity and distraction across the convexity judiciously after the rods are seated to fine-tune segmental correction.
- Check shoulder balance (T1 tilt, clavicle angle) intraoperatively — overcorrection of a proximal thoracic curve can leave the shoulders unbalanced even when the main thoracic curve looks well-corrected.

## Pitfalls & Complications

- **Crankshaft phenomenon**: risk if a very skeletally immature spine (open triradiate cartilage) undergoes posterior-only fusion without anterior growth arrest.
- **Incorrect LIV selection** causing distal adding-on.
- **Coronal decompensation** after overly aggressive selective thoracic fusion, particularly with an unfavorable (C) lumbar modifier.
- **Reported complication rates**: pseudarthrosis ~1–3% with modern segmental screw constructs, surgical site infection ~1–2%, neurologic injury <1% (reduced further with intraoperative neuromonitoring).
- **Screw malposition** — mitigated by confirming trajectory at each level rather than assuming a uniform technique across the whole curve; a medially breached thoracic screw is the classic feared complication given proximity to the cord and great vessels.
- **Shoulder imbalance** — recognized postoperatively if intraoperative T1 tilt/clavicle angle assessment is skipped.

## Postoperative Protocol

- **0–2 weeks**: inpatient mobilization from postoperative day 1; segmental pedicle screw fixation generally does not require formal bracing (surgeon-dependent); bend/lift/twist (BLT) precautions; walking as tolerated.
- **2–6 weeks**: gradual increase in ambulation; no PE or sports; avoid prolonged sitting; driving typically deferred until ~4 weeks and off opioid analgesia.
- **6–12 weeks**: full return to school typical by 6 weeks; light activities resumed; precautions gradually lifted.
- **3–6 months**: non-contact sports and swimming typically permitted around 3 months; begin core strengthening.
- **6–12 months**: full return to contact/collision sports generally allowed at 9–12 months once fusion is confirmed solid on imaging.
