## Indications

- Symptomatic complete or incomplete discoid lateral meniscus (torn, or presenting with a snapping/clunking knee).
- Asymptomatic discoid meniscus is not an indication for surgery — observation only; resection of an asymptomatic discoid meniscus is not indicated.
- Repair of the posterior peripheral attachment is mandatory in Wrisberg-variant (unstable) discoid menisci — which lack the normal posterior meniscotibial/coronary attachment and rely only on the meniscofemoral ligament for posterior fixation — and whenever an associated peripheral tear is found after saucerization.
- Subtotal/total meniscectomy is reserved for irreparable, severely degenerated discoid tissue.

## Positioning & setup

Standard knee arthroscopy. Diagnosis is typically confirmed pre-operatively by MRI: a "bow-tie sign" on ≥3 consecutive sagittal slices (versus the normal 1–2) and increased meniscal width on coronal images.

## Steps

1. Confirm the diagnosis (bow-tie sign, coronal width) and symptom status; asymptomatic incidental findings are not operated on.
2. Perform saucerization (partial central resection) to recreate a normal semilunar shape and width, typically leaving a peripheral rim of 6–8 mm.
3. Assess the stability of the posterior peripheral attachment after saucerization — determine whether the meniscus is a Wrisberg variant or has an associated peripheral tear.
4. If the posterior attachment is unstable (Wrisberg variant) or a peripheral tear is found, repair it — mandatory, since saucerization alone in an unstable meniscus leaves persistent instability/snapping and risks progressive tearing.
5. Reserve subtotal/total meniscectomy for irreparable, severely degenerated discoid tissue only; it is no longer first-line even for complex tears.

## Pitfalls

- Recurrent instability/snapping if an unstable posterior attachment is not repaired at the time of saucerization.
- Progressive degenerative change can occur over time even after well-executed saucerization + repair.
- Recent comparative data (2024) favor saucerization + repair over subtotal meniscectomy for complex discoid tears on 2-year outcomes and OCD-development risk, reinforcing a repair-preserving strategy even for complex patterns.
- Saucerization ± repair, performed appropriately, produces excellent outcomes even in children under 12 — repair-when-indicated is favored over blanket resection in the youngest symptomatic patients.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not specify a discoid-specific phased weight-bearing/ROM protocol distinct from the generic rehabilitation framework (Phase I–V, criteria-based progression). Follow the general post-arthroscopy framework, individualized to whether saucerization alone or saucerization + peripheral repair was performed (the latter warranting a more protected course analogous to peripheral meniscus repair given the repair construct).
