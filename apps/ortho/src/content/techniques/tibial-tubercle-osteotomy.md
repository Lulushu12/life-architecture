## Indications

Tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO) is added to MPFL reconstruction within the ESSKA 2024 "à-la-carte" algorithm when a bony risk factor drives instability:
- **Medialization** for TT-TG >20 mm, or 15–20 mm combined with other risk factors (dysplasia, alta, rotational malalignment).
- Add **distalization** when Caton-Deschamps index is >1.2–1.3 (patella alta).

## Positioning & setup

Performed as part of a combined patellar stabilization procedure (typically alongside MPFL-R); fluoroscopy is used to plan and confirm the magnitude of correction against measured TT-TG and Caton-Deschamps values rather than by visual estimation.

## Steps

1. Select the technique based on the deformity being corrected:
   - **Elmslie-Trillat** — medialization only.
   - **Fulkerson** — anteromedialization, favored when concurrent chondral offloading is also desired.
   - **Roux-Goldthwait** — pediatric technique using tendon transfer without physeal violation, used when the tibial tubercle apophysis must be protected.
2. Plan the magnitude of medialization from the measured TT-TG value, not by feel.
3. Cut the osteotomy while preserving a distal bony hinge/soft-tissue sleeve for stability.
4. Add distalization to the correction when Caton-Deschamps index indicates alta (>1.2–1.3).
5. Fix the osteotomy with 1–2 screws.
6. If instability persists after bony correction (residual laxity), combine with or complete MPFL-R in the same setting.

## Pitfalls

- Excessive medialization overloads the medial facet — plan strictly from measured TT-TG.
- Inadequate fixation risks fracture through the osteotomy site.
- Nonunion/delayed union is uncommon but serious — protect weight-bearing until radiographic union is confirmed.
- Tibial fracture can occur from a screw stress riser or early aggressive loading.
- **Compartment syndrome** is rare but a recognized surgical emergency from anterior compartment bleeding — maintain a high index of suspicion post-operatively.
- Hardware irritation may require later removal.
- Overcorrection produces medial patellofemoral overload.

## Post-op protocol

- Weight-bearing is typically restricted to touch-down/protected weight-bearing for **4–6 weeks** pending union when TTO is combined with MPFL-R.
- Radiographic union is typically achieved by **8–12 weeks**; union is required before impact/pivoting sport is permitted.
- When combined with MPFL-R, the broader rehabilitation timeline (ROM progression, brace weaning, strengthening, return-to-sport criteria at 6–9 months) follows the same instability rehabilitation protocol, gated by the TTO-specific weight-bearing and union restrictions above.
