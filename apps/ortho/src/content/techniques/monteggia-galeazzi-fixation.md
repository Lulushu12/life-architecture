## Indications

Both Monteggia (proximal ulna fracture + radiocapitellar dislocation) and Galeazzi (distal-third radius fracture + DRUJ disruption) injuries are "one-bone-fracture-plus-joint-dislocation" patterns of forearm ring integrity. Non-operative management is not appropriate as definitive treatment for either injury in adults — anatomic restoration of length/rotation/alignment is required to avoid functional loss, and restoring the fractured bone anatomically is usually necessary and sufficient to reduce the associated joint.

## Positioning & setup

Image the joint above and below any forearm fracture as standard practice. Obtain a true lateral elbow view for Monteggia (to judge concentric radiocapitellar reduction) and a true AP wrist view for Galeazzi (to assess DRUJ widening and ulnar styloid base fracture).

## Steps

**Monteggia:**
1. Anatomically plate the ulna (compression or bridge plating depending on comminution) to restore length and rotation — this typically reduces the radial head.
2. Confirm concentric radiocapitellar reduction on true lateral fluoroscopy before closing.
3. If not reduced, check for an interposed annular ligament/capsule, malreduced ulna, or an unaddressed radial head/neck fracture or coronoid fracture (Bado II/"Monteggia variant") requiring separate fixation per terrible-triad principles.

**Galeazzi:**
1. Anatomically plate the radius via a volar approach (compression plate) to restore length — this typically reduces the DRUJ.
2. Stress-test the DRUJ under fluoroscopy in full pronation/supination after fixation.
3. If unstable: immobilize the forearm in supination in a long-arm cast/splint for 4–6 weeks, **or** place a temporary DRUJ pin (0.062in K-wire, radius to ulna, proximal to the sigmoid notch) for 4–6 weeks if grossly unstable.
4. Reserve open TFCC repair for cases with a large ulnar styloid base fracture/fragment blocking reduction or persistent instability after pinning.

## Pitfalls

- A "missed Monteggia" (chronic radial head dislocation after an ulna fracture treated as isolated) is a recognized and largely preventable diagnostic failure — always trace the radiocapitellar line on every elbow and forearm view, and palpate the entire elbow in every forearm fracture.
- The Galeazzi "rule of thumb": any distal-third radius shaft fracture with >5mm radial shortening, >5° angulation, or a fracture of the ulnar styloid base should raise suspicion for DRUJ instability even if the joint appears reduced on initial films.
- Always image the wrist in a suspected Monteggia and the elbow in a suspected Galeazzi — missed DRUJ injury in Galeazzi is a recognized red flag.
- If the radial head does not reduce concentrically after solid anatomic ulna fixation in Monteggia, do not close without re-addressing the ulna reduction or an unrecognized radial head/coronoid injury.

## Post-op protocol

- **0–2 weeks:** splint/sling for comfort; begin gentle elbow and wrist AROM early (adjacent joints), guarded forearm rotation.
- **2–6 weeks:** progressive AROM of forearm rotation and adjacent joints; avoid axial loading (no weight-bearing through the arm) until union is progressing.
- **6–12 weeks:** progressive strengthening once radiographic union (typically 8–12 weeks for diaphyseal forearm fractures with rigid plate fixation); light functional use.
- **DRUJ-pinned Galeazzi:** pin removal at 4–6 weeks in clinic, then begin forearm rotation AROM progressively.
- **Return to work/sport:** light duty ~6 weeks; full manual labor/contact sport 3–4 months, contingent on union and rotational strength.
