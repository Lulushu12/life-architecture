## Indications

Stage-based salvage for scapholunate advanced collapse (SLAC) or scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC) arthritis, per Watson staging. Reserved for patients who have failed activity modification, splinting, and NSAIDs, or who present with established arthrosis beyond the point where ligament reconstruction is viable.

## Positioning & setup

Stage-appropriate procedure is selected intraoperatively/preoperatively based on capitate head and lunate fossa cartilage status, which determines whether proximal row carpectomy (PRC) is a viable option.

## Steps

1. **Stage I**: radial styloidectomy — isolated, or combined with SL reconstruction/scaphoid excision as appropriate to the underlying diagnosis.
2. **Stage II**: choose between scaphoidectomy + four-corner fusion (4CF) (capitate-lunate-triquetrum-hamate fused, typically with a dorsal circular plate or headless screws) and proximal row carpectomy (PRC). Decision is driven by capitate head/lunate fossa cartilage status (PRC requires healthy cartilage there), patient age/demand (PRC favored in lower-demand/older patients, less reliably durable under high load), and surgeon preference.
3. **Stage III**: capitolunate arthritis is present, so PRC is contraindicated — scaphoidectomy + 4CF is the standard salvage.
4. **Stage IV / pancarpal**: total wrist fusion (reliable pain relief, sacrifices motion) or total wrist arthroplasty (motion-preserving, lower-demand patients, higher revision/complication rates than fusion), chosen based on patient demand and bone stock.
5. **Adjunct at any stage**: wrist denervation (partial, e.g., posterior interosseous + anterior interosseous nerve neurectomy) as a standalone procedure in lower-demand patients or as an adjunct to any salvage stage for residual pain.
6. **Emerging adjunct**: capitate resurfacing/pyrocarbon implants at the capitolunate articulation as a motion-preserving alternative, with early-to-midterm series reported but not yet established first-line.

## Pitfalls

- PRC requires healthy capitate head/lunate fossa cartilage — performing it in the presence of capitolunate arthritis (Stage III) leads to poor outcomes; verify cartilage status before committing to PRC over 4CF.
- Failed PRC can leave persistent radiocapitate/radiolunate arthritis; failed 4CF can result in nonunion or adjacent-joint arthritis — either may ultimately require conversion to total wrist fusion.
- Recent meta-analyses (2024) trend toward PRC showing comparable-or-superior motion and equivalent-to-superior functional outcomes versus 4CF in appropriately selected Stage II wrists, but 4CF still retains the advantage where midcarpal cartilage is already compromised — the choice is not purely a matter of surgeon preference.
- Total wrist arthroplasty carries higher revision/complication rates than fusion and should be reserved for lower-demand patients where motion preservation is prioritized over durability.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not provide a stage-specific rehabilitation table for salvage procedures. General principles for this diagnostic cluster apply: certified hand therapy input from the earliest safe point (edema control, scar management, desensitization); a graded return-to-work/return-to-sport protocol (light duty at union/fusion onset → progressive loading → unrestricted at confirmed radiographic union/fusion and functional strength ≥90% of contralateral side) is preferred over a fixed calendar date. As a general benchmark from related fusion/reconstruction procedures in this cluster, protected immobilization phases run 6–12+ weeks (longer for fusion procedures until trabecular bridging is confirmed on CT), with return to heavy labor typically at 3–4 months and full unrestricted use later depending on the specific salvage procedure performed.
