## Indications

Advanced elbow osteoarthritis, inflammatory arthritis, or post-traumatic arthritis in lower-demand, typically older (>65–70) patients. TEA in patients under roughly 60–65 carries substantially higher long-term loosening/revision rates from activity-related mechanical stress on the linkage and bone-implant interface, so it is reserved for low-demand elderly patients or for young patients with systemic inflammatory arthritis and low functional demand who accept the lifting restrictions.

## Positioning & setup

A triceps-splitting or triceps-reflecting approach is used in at least some cases (implied by the post-op restriction against resisted extension against the triceps repair). Splint/sling immobilization technique (extension-block or posterior splint) is per surgeon preference. No further positioning detail is specified in the source.

## Steps

1. **Select implant constraint based on collateral ligament status and stability needs**: linked/semiconstrained designs (e.g., Coonrad-Morrey) dominate for most degenerative and inflammatory indications, offering superior stability with acceptable constraint-related loosening rates; unlinked designs preserve more native kinematics and bone stock but require intact collateral ligaments and are more instability-prone.
2. **Protect the triceps repair** if a triceps-splitting/reflecting approach is used, since post-op rehabilitation must avoid resisted extension against it.
3. **Counsel the patient on a lifelong lifting restriction** (generally <2.3–4.5 kg / 5–10 lb sustained load) beginning at the first postoperative visit — this reflects the implant's polyethylene bushing wear and aseptic loosening tolerance, not merely a rehab-phase limit.

## Pitfalls

- Aseptic loosening is the dominant long-term failure mode, accelerated by activity/loading — the central reason for lifting restrictions and the relative contraindication in young/high-demand patients.
- Infection risk (≈3–5%) is higher than lower-extremity arthroplasty, given the thin soft-tissue envelope around the elbow.
- Triceps insufficiency/rupture, ulnar neuropathy, periprosthetic fracture, and bushing/polyethylene wear requiring revision are all recognized complications.
- In patients under roughly 60–65, the loosening/revision-rate penalty from mechanical stress on the linkage is substantial enough to favor interposition arthroplasty instead when the patient will not accept lifelong restrictions.

## Post-op protocol

- Splint/sling immobilization 1–2 weeks (extension-block or posterior splint per surgeon preference).
- Gentle active-assisted ROM from 2 weeks; avoid resisted extension against the triceps repair for 6–8 weeks if a triceps-splitting/reflecting approach was used.
- Lifelong lifting-restriction counseling begins at the first postoperative visit — a definitive restriction, not merely a rehab-phase limit.
- Return to light ADLs 6–8 weeks; permanent avoidance of repetitive lifting >2.3–4.5 kg.
- Target functional outcome: flexion contracture <30°, functional arc 30–130°.
