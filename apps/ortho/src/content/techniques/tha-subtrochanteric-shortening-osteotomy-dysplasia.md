## Indications

Total hip arthroplasty (THA) for dysplasia is indicated once secondary osteoarthritis is established (Tönnis ≥2) or in older patients, and becomes progressively more technically demanding with increasing Crowe/Hartofilakidis severity. A subtrochanteric shortening derotational osteotomy is indicated when the limb-lengthening required to restore the hip center would exceed the safe threshold of approximately 3–4 cm — essentially mandatory in Crowe III–IV or Hartofilakidis type B–C (partial or complete high dislocation).

## Positioning & setup

Standard THA positioning and approach apply; the monograph's technical discussion focuses on component selection and osteotomy planning rather than approach-specific setup.

## Steps

1. Assess acetabular coverage: in a shallow, often retroverted or anteverted socket, use small cups and medialize to the true acetabular floor.
2. Add superolateral structural autograft for cup coverage where needed, particularly in Crowe II–III hips.
3. Assess required limb lengthening; if it exceeds the safe threshold (~3–4 cm), perform a subtrochanteric shortening derotational osteotomy concurrently — essentially mandatory in Crowe III–IV / Hartofilakidis B–C.
4. Address femoral deformity: excessive anteversion and a narrow diametaphyseal canal often require modular or small-stature stems.
5. Place the cup at the true acetabulum (not a high hip center), particularly in Hartofilakidis type B–C anatomy, accepting possible medialization with structural graft as needed.

## Pitfalls

- Acute limb lengthening beyond the ~3–4 cm safe threshold risks sciatic nerve palsy — mitigated specifically by performing the subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy in Crowe III–IV.
- Nonunion can occur at the shortening osteotomy site.
- Component malposition due to distorted native anatomy is a recognized risk.
- Higher dislocation risk results from soft-tissue laxity and abductor deficiency inherent to chronically subluxated/dislocated hips.
- Abductor deficiency and altered hip biomechanics from chronic subluxation affect postoperative limp risk even with a technically sound reconstruction.
- Hartofilakidis type C (high, complete dislocation) is the most technically demanding subgroup, with subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy essentially mandatory and the highest nerve-injury risk.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph does not detail a separate phased rehabilitation protocol specific to this procedure. General THA precautions apply, with particular attention to osteotomy-site healing and postoperative sciatic/femoral nerve function monitoring given the elevated risk of nerve injury with concurrent correction of severe deformity.
