## Indications

The most demanding distal femur fracture patterns: very short/comminuted distal segments (33-C2/C3), periprosthetic distal femur fractures with poor bone stock, and fractures at high risk of varus collapse or nonunion with a single-implant strategy. Also used in periarticular nonunion salvage.

## Positioning & setup

Combines a retrograde intramedullary nail with a lateral locking plate; setup follows standard retrograde nailing positioning with the addition of a lateral plating exposure.

## Steps

1. Place a retrograde nail (often shorter, "mini" or standard length) first, for central/axial and rotational control.
2. Augment with a lateral locking plate for additional coronal-plane rigidity, particularly to counter medial comminution.
3. Apply the selection rationale: **nail alone** for simple to moderately comminuted extra-/simple-intra-articular fractures with good bone stock; **plate alone** for complex intra-articular fractures needing precise articular reconstruction with adequate medial column integrity; **nail-plate combined** for the most unstable, comminuted, osteoporotic, or medial-deficient patterns, or in periarticular nonunion salvage.

## Pitfalls

- Increased soft-tissue dissection/OR time compared with a single-implant strategy is the main technical trade-off.
- The evidence base (multiple 2024-2025 comparative series) is still mostly retrospective, though it increasingly supports nail-plate as at least equivalent, and often preferred, for AO/OTA 33-C fractures with severe comminution or medial bone loss.
- Comparative studies show mixed results versus dual (medial + lateral) plating: some series favor nail-plate for lower operative time/blood loss, while others show dual plating providing similarly robust mechanical control — treatment choice meaningfully affects knee alignment, clinical outcomes, and quality of life, so individualize based on local expertise and fracture-specific factors.

## Post-op protocol

- Nail-plate combined constructs, by virtue of added stability, increasingly allow earlier progressive weight bearing than single-implant plating in several 2024-2025 series.
- Otherwise follows the general distal femur fracture protocol: early protected knee ROM (0-90°) to prevent arthrofibrosis, progressive flexion toward 90-110° by 2-6 weeks and ≥110-120° by 6-12 weeks, progression to full weight bearing as radiographic healing allows, and return to high-demand/pivoting sport generally deferred 6-9 months pending union, painless full ROM, and quadriceps/hamstring strength ≥85-90% of the contralateral limb.
