## Indications

Classic indications are an **incarcerated fragment within the joint** (after elbow dislocation-reduction), **open fracture**, **ulnar nerve dysfunction with a displaced fragment**, and gross **valgus instability**. Displacement thresholds historically cited (2 mm, 5 mm, 10–15 mm) have never been well-validated and vary by author. Indications are converging toward a broader group: a 2025 multicenter RCT found a markedly higher nonunion rate with cast treatment versus surgery, prompting many centers to lower the threshold for fixation, particularly in adolescent throwing/overhead athletes and gymnasts for whom valgus load tolerance and nonunion-related instability carry real functional consequences.

## Positioning & setup

The ulnar nerve should be identified and protected during exposure.

## Steps

1. If the fragment is incarcerated within the joint following an elbow dislocation-reduction, extract it.
2. Fixation with a **single partially-threaded screw** (most common technique).
3. **Heavy suture fixation through drill tunnels** for small or fragmented pieces not amenable to screw purchase.
4. Identify and protect the ulnar nerve during exposure; transpose it if there is preoperative ulnar nerve dysfunction or if it subluxates with elbow flexion intraoperatively.

## Pitfalls

- Historically cited displacement thresholds for surgery (2 mm, 5 mm, 10–15 mm) have never been well-validated and vary by author — do not treat any single number as definitive.
- The traditional teaching that medial epicondyle nonunion is a benign, asymptomatic radiographic curiosity (given the extrasynovial location) is being challenged: a 2025 RCT reported nonunion in only 1/37 (2.7%) surgically-fixed fractures versus 24/35 (68.6%) cast-treated fractures, raising real questions about long-term valgus stability and throwing performance in the nonoperative group.
- A follow-on UK multicenter RCT protocol (SCIENCE trial, results expected 2025–2027) is formally testing surgery vs. cast for displaced fractures and may further shift the displacement threshold for ORIF — treat current thresholds as evolving.

## Post-op protocol

- Immobilize 3–4 weeks, consistent with other elbow-region fractures; active ROM begins after cast removal, with formal physiotherapy reserved for motion that plateaus well below expected milestones.
- Return to sport generally at 6–8 weeks once union and function have returned.
- **Return to competitive throwing** is delayed to **3–4 months** with a structured, gradual throwing progression, given the valgus-stress environment of the healed fixation.
