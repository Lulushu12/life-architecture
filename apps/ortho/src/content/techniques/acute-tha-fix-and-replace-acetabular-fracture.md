## Indications

Acute total hip arthroplasty combined with column fixation ("fix-and-replace") is favored over ORIF alone when: severe articular comminution/impaction precludes a congruent, durable reduction; the pattern is both-column or transverse+posterior wall with marked osteoporosis and dome destruction; there is pre-existing ipsilateral hip osteoarthritis; there is femoral head injury (impaction, osteonecrosis risk); or the patient's age/frailty is such that a second surgery for post-traumatic arthritis — which develops in a substantial proportion of elderly ORIF patients — would be poorly tolerated. Contemporary evidence supports this combined approach in carefully selected patients ≥60–65 years with unreconstructible or high PTOA-risk patterns. ORIF alone remains standard when the fracture is reconstructible, regardless of age — outcomes depend far more on reduction quality and comminution than age alone.

## Positioning & setup

Builds on the same approach selection as acetabular ORIF (Kocher-Langenbeck, ilioinguinal/Stoppa, or combined, chosen per fracture pattern), performed as a single combined or staged procedure under one anesthetic.

## Steps

1. Perform column fixation first — typically plating to restore the columns and posterior wall/quadrilateral surface, as in standard acetabular ORIF.
2. Proceed to acute THA using a cementless, or — per recent RCT-level evidence — cemented revision-type or dual-mobility cup, selected for maximal early stability in compromised acetabular bone.
3. Favor dual-mobility or constrained liners given the elevated dislocation risk from posterior wall/column deficiency and abductor compromise in this population.

## Pitfalls

- Dislocation risk after fix-and-replace THA is elevated relative to elective THA due to posterior column/wall deficiency and abductor compromise — mitigate with dual-mobility or constrained liner selection.
- Contemporary fix-and-replace series report reoperation rates of roughly 8–9%, predominantly for infection (~5–6%) or dislocation — counsel patients accordingly.
- Reserve this approach for unreconstructible or high-PTOA-risk patterns; ORIF alone remains preferred when the joint is reconstructible, since reduction quality — not age — is the dominant driver of ORIF success (7-year joint survival ~80% with anatomic reduction vs. ~65–70% with imperfect reduction).
- Mid-term (minimum 5-year) comparative data favor fix-and-replace in comminuted/impacted-dome patterns specifically — patient selection by fracture morphology, not blanket age cutoff, is what the evidence supports.

## Post-op protocol

Standard THA rehabilitation protocol, accelerated relative to ORIF-alone: weight-bearing as tolerated is typically permitted from the outset, since implant stability supersedes fracture-fixation concerns. Dislocation precautions are tailored to approach and construct — a dual-mobility or constrained liner may allow relaxed standard precautions. In comparative series, fix-and-replace is associated with earlier mobilization and lower rates of secondary arthroplasty conversion than staged ORIF-then-THA.
