## Indications

Grade III instability persisting after a structured 6-week brace trial (isolated cases); combined ACL/MCL injury with gross valgus instability or Stener-like proximal MCL displacement precluding healing; chronic medial instability.

## Positioning & setup

Not detailed at the setup/room level in this monograph.

## Steps

1. **Acute repair** (primary suture ± augmentation) is favored within the first 2–3 weeks for tissue-quality-dependent avulsion-type tears.
2. **Reconstruction** (hamstring or allograft) is favored for chronic/attenuated tissue or mid-substance tears with poor repair tissue quality.
3. When MCL injury is combined with ACL injury requiring reconstruction, plan sequencing/staging of the two procedures per the combined-injury framework, since surgery for a combined ACL/MCL injury is generally not treated as two isolated procedures done in isolation from one another.

## Pitfalls

- Over-immobilization risks stiffness/arthrofibrosis, since the MCL's strong intrinsic healing means prolonged immobilization is unnecessary and counterproductive.
- Under-treated Grade III injury (i.e., missing persistent valgus instability at reassessment) risks chronic valgus laxity.
- Saphenous nerve injury (infrapatellar branch) is a recognized risk with surgical exposure of the medial knee.

## Post-op protocol

- Brace-protected ROM 0–90° for the first 2–4 weeks.
- Progressive weight-bearing (protected in hinged brace initially, advancing to full weight-bearing by around 6 weeks).
- Strengthening beginning at 6 weeks.
- Return to sport typically 4–6 months, longer if combined with cruciate reconstruction.
