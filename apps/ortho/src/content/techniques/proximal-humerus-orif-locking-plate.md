## Indications

Physiologically younger patients with reconstructable fracture patterns (2-/3-part, and 4-part with intact medial hinge/calcar per Hertel criteria). Displacement >1 cm or angulation >45° at any of the four segments, head-split, articular displacement >25–40%, glenohumeral dislocation, vascular injury, or open fracture per the general surgical indication set for proximal humerus fractures.

## Positioning & setup

Deltopectoral approach (standard), protecting the cephalic vein in the deltopectoral interval. An anterolateral deltoid-splitting approach is an alternative, limited by axillary nerve excursion — do not extend the split more than 5 cm below the acromion.

## Steps

1. Approach via the deltopectoral interval (or anterolateral deltoid-split, respecting the 5 cm axillary nerve limit).
2. Reduce the fracture and restore head height and version.
3. Position the plate 5–8 mm below the top of the greater tuberosity and posterior to the bicipital groove, to avoid over-tightening/subacromial impingement.
4. Place inferomedial "calcar screws" supporting the head, restoring the calcar screw trajectory — this is the single most consistent predictor against varus collapse and fixation failure in the literature.
5. Use heavy non-absorbable suture cerclage through the rotator cuff tendon-bone junction (not just tuberosity bone, which is often osteoporotic and comminuted) for tuberosity fixation and augmentation.
6. Obtain intraoperative fluoroscopy with dynamic rotation views to confirm no screw penetration — static AP/axillary views alone under-detect penetration.

## Pitfalls

- Varus malreduction and screw cut-out/penetration in osteoporotic bone are the dominant ORIF-specific failure modes — the calcar screw is the single most preventable technical safeguard against this.
- AVN risk is higher with anatomic-neck involvement, a disrupted medial hinge, or head-splitting patterns — these may not manifest until months to years later, so surveil at follow-up beyond the initial healing window in high-risk patterns.
- Static AP/axillary fluoroscopy alone under-detects screw penetration — always obtain dynamic rotation views intraoperatively.
- Rotator cuff dysfunction and tuberosity malunion/nonunion are major drivers of poor functional outcome independent of whether the fracture itself unites.

## Post-op protocol

- Passive ROM often started day 1–2 (surgeon-dependent, informed by fixation stability and tuberosity repair integrity).
- Active-assisted ROM from 2–4 wk.
- Resisted strengthening deferred to 8–12 wk to protect tuberosity healing.
- Full unrestricted activity/sport typically at 3–6 months once strength and ROM are near-symmetric and there is no residual instability.
- The PROFHER trial found no clinically important difference in Oxford Shoulder Score between locked-plate ORIF and nonoperative treatment of displaced fractures in older adults (mean age ~66) at 2 and 5 years — reinforces individualized selection, particularly in lower-demand elderly patients.
