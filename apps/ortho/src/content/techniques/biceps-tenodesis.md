## Indications

Long head of biceps (LHB) tendon pathology (tendinopathy, subluxation/instability, partial tearing) whether isolated or concomitant with rotator cuff repair or SLAP-associated instability. Tenodesis is favored in younger, active, or cosmetically concerned patients who want to preserve the length-tension relationship and forearm supination/elbow flexion strength; tenotomy is favored in older, lower-demand, sedentary patients where cosmesis and strength are lower priorities. In SLAP tears specifically, tenodesis is increasingly favored over labral repair beyond age 35–40, in revision settings, or with concomitant biceps pathology, given comparable-or-better return-to-play/reoperation rates.

## Positioning & setup

Two fixation locations are used: **open subpectoral** or **arthroscopic suprapectoral**. Subpectoral fixation theoretically reduces residual groove pain versus suprapectoral fixation.

## Steps

1. **Choose tenodesis over tenotomy** when preserving length-tension relationship, supination/elbow flexion strength, and cosmesis (avoiding Popeye deformity) matter — i.e., younger/active/cosmetically concerned patients.
2. **Choose the fixation site**: open subpectoral vs. arthroscopic suprapectoral; subpectoral location theoretically reduces residual groove pain.
3. **Select a fixation construct**: interference screw, suture anchor, or cortical button — no strong evidence favors one method over another; base the choice on surgeon experience and bone quality.
4. **Set tension with the elbow at ~20–30° flexion** to replicate native length-tension before final fixation.
5. **When performed concomitantly with cuff repair or SLAP surgery**, address the biceps pathology at the same setting rather than staging it separately.

## Pitfalls

- Over-tensioning at fixation causes loss of elbow flexion/supination strength and groove pain; under-tensioning risks a residual Popeye-like bulge — set tension at ~20–30° elbow flexion to replicate native length-tension.
- Tenotomy carries a ~30–50% risk of a visible Popeye deformity and a small but real risk of cramping/fatigue discomfort — counsel patients explicitly if choosing this simpler option.
- Groove/residual biceps pain can occur after suprapectoral tenodesis; subpectoral fixation may reduce this.
- When biceps instability is present, always evaluate the subscapularis specifically — pulley lesions/subscapularis tears are a common co-driver of biceps instability that will not resolve with tenodesis alone if left unaddressed.
- No fixation method (interference screw, suture anchor, cortical button) has demonstrated clear superiority — do not over-invest in a specific construct at the expense of tensioning technique.

## Post-op protocol

- Sling 2–4 weeks.
- Avoid resisted elbow flexion/forearm supination for 6 weeks to protect fixation.
- Progressive strengthening from 6–8 weeks.
- Return to heavy lifting/sport at 3–4 months.
