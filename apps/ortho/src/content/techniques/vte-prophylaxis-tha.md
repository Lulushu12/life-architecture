## Indications

Applies to all patients undergoing primary total hip arthroplasty. International consensus on the specific regimen remains fragmented across CHEST, the International Consensus Meeting (ICM), NICE, and national societies, so the practical approach is individualized risk stratification rather than a single universal protocol.

## Positioning & setup

Not applicable in the surgical-positioning sense — this is a perioperative pharmacologic and mechanical protocol rather than a positioning-dependent procedure. Risk stratification is performed preoperatively alongside general medical optimization (HbA1c, smoking cessation, anemia correction). Mechanical prophylaxis (intermittent pneumatic compression) is applied intraoperatively/postoperatively near-universally regardless of the pharmacologic agent chosen.

## Steps

1. **Risk-stratify the patient** into standard-risk versus elevated personal VTE risk (prior VTE, active malignancy, hypercoagulable state, prolonged immobility).
2. **Standard-risk elective primary THA:** use aspirin 81 mg twice daily as the default per the 2022 International Consensus Meeting (ICM) on VTE — the most current unified position going into 2026 — favoring aspirin over LMWH for this population, a meaningful shift from historical LMWH-first practice.
3. **Elevated personal VTE risk:** escalate to stronger anticoagulation — LMWH or a DOAC (apixaban, dabigatran, rivaroxaban) — rather than aspirin.
4. **Alternative CHEST-endorsed agents** (adjusted-dose vitamin K antagonist, fondaparinux, low-dose unfractionated heparin, or mechanical compression alone) remain acceptable options depending on patient factors and local practice.
5. **Add mechanical prophylaxis** (intermittent pneumatic compression) regardless of pharmacologic choice.
6. **Set duration:** continue prophylaxis for a minimum of 10–14 days per CHEST guidelines; extend toward 28–35 days in higher-risk patients per several national frameworks, including NICE VTE guidance.
7. **Pair with early mobilization** (same day or POD1) as part of an enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) pathway, which further supports VTE risk reduction alongside pharmacologic/mechanical measures.

## Pitfalls

- International consensus remains fragmented across CHEST, ICM, NICE, and national society guidance as of 2025 — treat individualized risk stratification, not a single universal protocol, as the defensible practice standard.
- European fast-track THA/TKA protocols favor aspirin over no prophylaxis but stop short of a blanket recommendation for or against aspirin in higher-risk procedures — do not apply the standard-risk aspirin default indiscriminately to elevated-risk patients.
- An injection within roughly 3 months of a planned THA is a recognized (though debated in magnitude) periprosthetic infection risk factor distinct from VTE risk, but is relevant to the same perioperative planning window and is a practical cutoff many arthroplasty surgeons use before proceeding to surgery.
- Symptomatic 90-day VTE with modern prophylaxis is <1%, and 1-year mortality after elective primary THA is <0.5% — useful context for patient counseling, but not a reason to skip formal risk stratification.

## Post-op protocol

- Minimum 10–14 days of chemoprophylaxis per CHEST guidelines; extend to 28–35 days in higher-risk patients per several national frameworks (including NICE VTE guidance).
- Mechanical compression (intermittent pneumatic compression) continued in-hospital regardless of pharmacologic agent.
- Early mobilization (day 0 or POD1) as a standard ERAS-pathway adjunct to pharmacologic/mechanical prophylaxis.
- No dedicated additional follow-up beyond standard THA surveillance; reassess if new-onset leg swelling, calf pain, or respiratory symptoms arise during the prophylaxis window.
