## Indications

Closed reduction and spica casting is indicated for Pavlik harness failure or for DDH presenting between roughly 6 weeks and 18 months of age, where a concentric, stable reduction can be achieved without excessive tension and without an interposed soft-tissue block. Options preceding closed reduction include reapplication of the Pavlik harness for a further trial in carefully selected failures, or switching to a rigid abduction orthosis.

## Positioning & setup

Performed under general anesthesia with intraoperative arthrography to confirm a concentric, stable reduction.

## Steps

1. **Attempt gentle closed reduction** under general anesthesia.
2. **Assess the "safe zone" of abduction** — the arc between the angle of redislocation on adduction and the angle of subluxation/impingement on excessive abduction. A stable arc of at least ~20° is generally regarded as safe before accepting the reduction; a narrow safe zone predicts recurrent instability.
3. **Add percutaneous adductor longus tenotomy** liberally in tight hips to widen a narrow safe zone, rather than accepting it.
4. **Apply a spica cast in the "human position"** — flexion and abduction, avoiding extremes of abduction (the frog-leg/forced-abduction extreme) to protect against avascular necrosis (AVN).
5. **Confirm concentric reduction with post-reduction CT or MRI** rather than a static radiograph alone before committing to the cast.
6. **Cast change under anesthesia at ~6 weeks**, with total spica duration approximately 12 weeks (range ~10–14 weeks depending on age and reduction stability).
7. **Transition to an abduction orthosis** after the spica period.

If the safe zone remains too narrow despite adductor tenotomy, or interposed soft tissue (inverted labrum, hypertrophied ligamentum teres, pulvinar, transverse acetabular ligament) blocks concentric reduction on arthrogram, proceed to open reduction instead.

## Pitfalls

- A subtly subluxated "reduction" left uncorrected on a static radiograph is a common source of late residual dysplasia — always confirm concentricity with post-reduction CT or MRI before committing to a 6–12 week cast.
- Avoid the frog-leg/forced-abduction extreme when molding the cast — this is the single most preventable cause of iatrogenic AVN in this pathway.
- Do not accept a narrow safe zone (<~20°) without first performing percutaneous adductor tenotomy, a low-morbidity adjunct.
- A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of late-presentation DDH found comparable long-term acetabular remodeling outcomes between well-executed closed and open reduction when concentric reduction is achieved — reduction quality, not method alone, is the dominant driver of outcome.

## Post-op protocol

- Weeks 0–6: immobilization in the spica cast, no formal rehabilitation; monitor for cast complications (skin breakdown, compartment concerns with limb swelling); interval radiograph/CT or MRI in cast to confirm maintained reduction.
- Weeks 6–12: cast change under anesthesia if planned, continued spica immobilization; total spica duration ~10–14 weeks depending on age and reduction stability.
- Post-cast (~week 12 onward): transition to a removable abduction orthosis, typically weaned over 2–3 months; gentle active range-of-motion play (formal physiotherapy usually not required at this age); interval ultrasound/radiograph surveillance for acetabular remodeling through at least age 2, with periodic follow-up radiographs through skeletal maturity given that a meaningful minority require secondary pelvic osteotomy years later even after successful initial reduction.
- Return to normal activity: unrestricted once the orthosis is weaned and reduction/containment is confirmed stable — most toddlers resume normal gait development on their own developmental timeline.
