## Indications

Selection across the ladder is driven by lesion size, containment (contained vs. shoulder/bipolar/kissing), depth (chondral-only vs. osteochondral with bone loss), and patient age/activity demand:

- **<2 cm²**: microfracture (declining favor) or OAT.
- **2–4 cm²**: OAT (if donor site adequate) or MACI/ACI.
- **>4 cm² or osteochondral, contained**: osteochondral allograft (OCA); MACI for chondral-only lesions.
- Overall threshold zone: ICRS grade 3B–4 (Outerbridge III–IV), focal and contained. Diffuse/bipolar/kissing lesions and uncorrected malalignment are contraindications to an isolated biologic procedure.
- **Alignment as prerequisite**: obtain standing long-leg (hip-knee-ankle) films whenever a biologic procedure is planned in a compartment with degenerative change or in a patient with clinical varus/valgus thrust. If the mechanical axis passes outside the central third of the affected compartment (or clinical/radiographic varus/valgus exceeds ~5°), plan concurrent or staged high tibial osteotomy (medial-sided pathology) or distal femoral osteotomy (lateral-sided pathology) rather than proceeding with an isolated graft.

## Positioning & setup

Microfracture and the biopsy stage of MACI are performed arthroscopically. Implantation stages — MACI scaffold implantation, OAT plug harvest/placement, and OCA graft placement — are performed open or via mini-arthrotomy.

## Steps

**Microfracture (bone-marrow stimulation)**
1. Debride the defect back to stable cartilage margins.
2. Perforate the subchondral bone with an awl or pick to recruit a mesenchymal clot that fills the defect with fibrocartilage.
3. Reserve for very small (<2 cm²), low-demand lesions, or use as an adjunct/marrow-access step within another technique ("microfracture-plus" combined with a scaffold) — durability declines after 2–3 years and results deteriorate with larger lesion size, particularly in athletes.

**Osteochondral autograft transplantation (OAT/mosaicplasty)**
1. Harvest cylindrical osteochondral plugs from a low-weight-bearing donor site (lateral trochlea/intercondylar notch periphery).
2. Press-fit the plug(s) into the prepared defect; use multiple plugs (mosaicplasty) for larger lesions.
3. Single-stage procedure, transplanting native hyaline cartilage with subchondral bone; suited to lesions up to ~2–3 cm² (larger with mosaicplasty).

**MACI (matrix-induced/matrix-applied autologous chondrocyte implantation)**
1. Stage 1: arthroscopic cartilage biopsy.
2. In-vitro chondrocyte expansion onto a collagen membrane.
3. Stage 2: open/mini-arthrotomy implantation of the cell-seeded scaffold, fixed with fibrin glue (no periosteal patch, unlike first-generation ACI).
4. Preferred for larger (>3–4 cm²), full-thickness contained defects with intact subchondral bone; if subchondral bone is deficient, bone-graft first ("sandwich" technique) or use an osteochondral graft instead.

**Osteochondral allograft (OCA)**
1. Select a fresh (or fresh-frozen) size-matched cadaveric osteochondral plug or shell.
2. Implant in a single stage for large (generally >4 cm²), deep, or osteochondral defects — including OCD with subchondral bone loss, post-traumatic defects, and salvage after a failed prior cartilage procedure.

**Selection logic**: match lesion size, containment, depth, and patient demand to the appropriate technique, and stage or combine with realignment osteotomy and/or meniscal repair/transplant whenever alignment or meniscal integrity has not already been corrected.

## Pitfalls

- **OAT**: donor-site morbidity in up to ~10% of patients (usually mild); plug-fill gaps/incongruity between grafts.
- **MACI**: delamination is less common than with periosteal-patch first-generation ACI but still occurs; graft hypertrophy and incomplete integration at the graft-host margin can occur; two-stage nature and cost are the principal drawbacks.
- **OCA**: graft resorption/collapse or, rarely, immunologic non-incorporation; graft availability, cost, and a theoretical (very low but real) disease-transmission risk are trade-offs.
- MRI tends to underestimate true lesion size and depth compared with arthroscopic findings — confirm intraoperatively before committing to a graft size.
- Any procedure on this ladder performed without correcting a concurrent >5° malalignment, meniscal deficiency, or ligament instability is set up to fail and should instead be staged or combined with realignment osteotomy or meniscal repair/transplant.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weight-bearing**: non-weight-bearing to toe-touch for 4–6 weeks for femoral condyle lesions (6–8 weeks for larger OCA or tibial plateau/patellofemoral lesions), progressing gradually to full weight-bearing by 8–12 weeks.
- **CPM**: continuous passive motion is commonly prescribed for MACI/ACI (several hours daily for 4–6 weeks) to promote graft nutrition/maturation; used less consistently after OAT/OCA.
- Avoid impact loading (running, jumping) for 4–6 months; introduce low-impact conditioning (stationary bike, pool) progressively from ~6–8 weeks.
- **Return to pivoting sport**: typically 9–12 months for MACI (graft maturation is slow — hyaline-like tissue continues remodeling well past 12 months), 6–9 months for OAT, 9–12 months for OCA; criteria-based on absence of effusion, full strength symmetry, and (where available) imaging evidence of graft incorporation/fill.
