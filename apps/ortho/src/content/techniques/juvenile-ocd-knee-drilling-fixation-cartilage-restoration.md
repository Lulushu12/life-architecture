## Indications

Unstable lesion, loose body, or failure of an adequate (3–6 month) non-operative trial in juvenile osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) of the knee (open distal femoral physis). Technique is selected by lesion characteristics rather than a single fixed protocol.

## Positioning & setup

Arthroscopic approach for drilling, fixation, and debridement/OATS. Arthroscopic probing is the most reliable stability assessment when surgery is undertaken, supplementing preoperative MRI staging (high-signal T2 rim between fragment and parent bone, cyst formation beneath the fragment, breach in overlying articular cartilage).

## Steps

1. **Confirm instability/indication:** AP, lateral, and tunnel/notch radiographs (best view for the classic posterolateral medial femoral condyle lesion) plus MRI staging; arthroscopic probing at the time of surgery for definitive stability assessment.
2. **Stable lesion with intact overlying cartilage that has failed non-operative care:** transarticular or retroarticular (extra-articular) drilling to stimulate a healing response without disturbing the articular surface. Retroarticular drilling is favored when preserving the articular surface is paramount (e.g., very young patient, small stable lesion).
3. **Unstable but salvageable fragment with viable subchondral bone:** internal fixation using bioabsorbable pins or headless compression screws.
4. **Unsalvageable fragment or full-thickness cartilage defect after fragment excision:** debridement with microfracture, or osteochondral autograft/allograft transfer (OATS)/particulated juvenile cartilage.

## Pitfalls

- Progression to instability or loose body can occur if a non-operative trial is inadequately monitored — repeat imaging at ~3-month intervals is required to confirm healing before advancing activity.
- Non-union is a recognized risk after fixation.
- OATS carries donor-site morbidity.
- Large or lateral/weight-bearing lesions that fail to heal risk premature osteoarthritis.
- Juvenile (open physis) OCD has a fundamentally better prognosis than adult OCD due to ongoing bone remodeling capacity and typically smaller, more peripheral lesions — this favors a genuine non-operative trial before surgery in most stable lesions, though earlier arthroscopic fixation with bioabsorbable pins has been supported specifically in high-demand young athletes with low-grade instability.

## Post-op protocol

- **Drilling/fixation:** protected weight-bearing 4–6 weeks, progressive ROM and quadriceps strengthening; return to sport generally 4–6 months after fixation/drilling once imaging confirms healing.
- **OATS/cartilage restoration:** longer recovery, return to sport typically 9–12 months.
- Non-operative trial (for context/comparison): weeks 0–12 activity/impact restriction with brace or cast in younger/more symptomatic patients; month 3 repeat imaging to assess healing; months 4–6 progressive strengthening and functional loading, with full-impact/pivoting sport only after confirmed radiographic/clinical healing.
