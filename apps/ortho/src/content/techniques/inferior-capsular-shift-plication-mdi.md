## Indications

Reserved for multidirectional instability (MDI) that has failed a genuine structured rehabilitation trial (per Stanmore/consensus philosophy) — surgery-first approaches to MDI have historically underperformed. MDI is atraumatic or minimally traumatic, symptomatic instability in ≥2 directions driven by capsular redundancy/generalized ligamentous laxity rather than a discrete labral tear, often bilateral and associated with connective tissue hyperlaxity.

## Positioning & setup

Choice is between an open procedure and an arthroscopic procedure, decided by laxity severity and revision status (see Steps).

## Steps

1. Confirm a genuine, adequately trialed structured rehabilitation failure before proceeding — surgery-first MDI approaches have historically underperformed.
2. Choose technique: open inferior capsular shift (historical gold standard, durable but more morbid) vs. arthroscopic multi-anchor capsular plication. Modern practice favors arthroscopic plication for comparable outcomes with lower morbidity in experienced hands, reserving open shift for revision or severe global laxity.
3. Arthroscopic plication combines rotator interval closure with circumferential capsular plication.
4. Explicitly address rotator interval laxity (a positive sulcus sign that does not reduce with external rotation) — isolated anteroinferior plication without interval closure risks residual inferior/multidirectional symptoms. The rotator interval (superior glenohumeral ligament + coracohumeral ligament) is the structure restraining inferior/posteroinferior translation with the arm at the side, and closure targets it directly.

## Pitfalls

- Do not proceed to surgery without confirming a genuine structured rehabilitation trial — poor response to rehab should first prompt re-evaluation for a missed structural (Stanmore Type I/II) component or psychological/voluntary instability, not an automatic move to surgery.
- Isolated anteroinferior plication without addressing rotator interval laxity risks residual multidirectional symptoms.
- Overtightening during plication risks loss of external rotation/function — reported in 10–20% of cases when surgery is undertaken without an adequate preceding rehab trial.

## Post-op protocol

The monograph presents MDI's structured rehabilitation as the primary non-operative pathway (Phase 1, 0–6 weeks: pain control and isometric activation avoiding provocative positions; Phase 2, 6–12 weeks: progressive dynamic strengthening of scapular stabilizers and rotator cuff with closed-chain proprioceptive drills; Phase 3, 3–6 months: sport-specific/functional progression) rather than a dedicated post-surgical protocol for capsular shift/plication specifically. General return-to-sport criteria apply across procedures and are not purely time-based: full painless ROM within 5–10° of the contralateral side, ≥90% strength symmetry, negative apprehension/relocation testing, and successful sport-specific functional progression before clearance.
