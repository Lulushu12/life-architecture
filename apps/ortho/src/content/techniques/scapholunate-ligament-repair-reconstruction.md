## Indications

**Acute (<3–6 weeks), primary repairable tissue**: direct SLIL repair with dorsal capsulodesis augmentation, or arthroscopic-assisted reduction and pinning for dynamic instability without adequate tissue for open repair.

**Chronic, static reducible/fixed without arthritis**: ligament reconstruction.

**Static fixed with early arthrosis confined to the radial styloid**: radial styloidectomy combined with reconstruction may be considered; once midcarpal arthritis develops, proceed to the SLAC salvage ladder instead.

## Positioning & setup

Diagnostic wrist arthroscopy is used both to confirm the Geissler grade (correlating with Garcia-Elias stage) and, for predynamic/dynamic tears, as first-line minimally invasive treatment — avoiding an open capsulotomy while allowing accurate grading to guide further treatment.

## Steps

1. Confirm instability stage (predynamic, dynamic, static reducible, static fixed) via stress radiographs and/or arthroscopic Geissler grading — this determines which of the following applies.
2. **Acute repair**: place a suture anchor into the lunate/scaphoid dorsal ridge for direct SLIL repair, augmented with dorsal capsulodesis (Blatt technique).
3. **Arthroscopic-assisted alternative for dynamic instability**: reduction and percutaneous SL/scaphocapitate pinning when tissue is inadequate for open repair.
4. **Chronic static reducible/fixed reconstruction — select technique**:
   - Modified Brunelli / three-ligament tenodesis (Garcia-Elias/Wolfe): a strip of FCR tendon rerouted through the scaphoid and lunate to recreate the SL and dorsal intercarpal ligament vectors.
   - Bone-retinaculum-bone (dorsal capsuloligamentous) autograft.
   - SL Internal Brace / suture-tape augmentation of the primary repair or reconstruction, to protect the construct during healing and reduce loss of reduction.
   - Scapholunate axis method (SLAM) / arthroscopic reduction and association of the scapholunate (ARSL) with suture-tape — a less invasive arthroscopic-assisted alternative gaining adoption.
5. For static fixed instability with cartilage preserved, proceed with ligament reconstruction (three-ligament tenodesis or bone-retinaculum-bone graft); if reconstruction is judged unreliable, consider limited fusion instead.
6. If early arthrosis is confined to the radial styloid, combine radial styloidectomy with reconstruction rather than reconstruction alone.

## Pitfalls

- Recurrent SL gapping/loss of reduction after ligament reconstruction remains a persistent limitation of classic tenodesis techniques over long-term follow-up — this is the driving rationale behind Internal Brace/suture-tape augmentation, though comparative long-term re-gapping data against classic reconstruction are not yet mature.
- The dorsal sensory branch of the ulnar nerve and superficial radial nerve branches are at risk with dorsal wrist approaches used for SL repair — protect these during exposure.
- Avoid resisted wrist extension/radial deviation loading (e.g., push-ups, heavy gripping in extension) until at least 3 months postoperatively — this loading pattern strains the healing SL construct.
- Once midcarpal arthritis develops, ligament reconstruction is no longer appropriate — transition to the Watson-staged SLAC salvage ladder rather than persisting with a reconstruction that will fail against arthritic joint surfaces.

## Post-op protocol

K-wire protection typically maintained 8–10 weeks; wrist immobilized in a cast/removable brace during this period with wires in place. After wire removal: begin gentle AROM (weeks 8–10), dart-thrower's-motion pattern training and scapholunate proprioceptive/neuromuscular retraining (a hallmark of modern SL rehab, exploiting the plane of motion that minimizes SL joint strain), progressive strengthening from ~12 weeks, return to sport/heavy labor at 4–6 months. A removable protective orthosis/taping for an additional 4–6 weeks after return to high-risk sport is a reasonable transitional measure.
