## Indications

Laminectomy with instrumented posterior fusion is preferred over laminoplasty for multilevel degenerative cervical myelopathy/OPLL when there is pre-existing kyphosis (fusion allows correction/stabilization not achievable with laminoplasty), significant instability, or where laminoplasty has failed or is contraindicated. It is also favored for K-line-negative, high canal-occupying-ratio OPLL requiring lordosis restoration. Avoid when lordosis is preserved and complication tolerance is low — laminoplasty is typically preferred instead given its lower morbidity.

## Positioning & setup

Standard posterior cervical positioning applies, with the same general precautions as other high-risk cervical decompressions: avoid excessive neck extension during intubation/positioning in severe stenosis or myelopathy; consider awake fiberoptic intubation in severe myelopathy with unstable or high-grade stenosis. Intraoperative neuromonitoring (SSEP/MEP) is selectively used for severe stenosis, OPLL, revision surgery, or cases with a deformity-correction component.

## Steps

1. **Confirm the case favors fusion over laminoplasty**: pre-existing kyphosis requiring correction, significant instability, prior failed/contraindicated laminoplasty, or K-line-negative high-canal-occupying-ratio OPLL.
2. **Perform laminectomy** for posterior decompression across the involved multilevel segment.
3. **Place instrumented posterior fusion**, using the construct to correct and maintain cervical lordosis — this is the key advantage over laminectomy alone (laminectomy without fusion risks progressive kyphosis) and over laminoplasty (better lordosis correction capability).
4. **For OPLL**, recognize that a fused segment better controls continued ossification progression than a mobile laminoplasty construct, which is a specific rationale for choosing fusion in OPLL even when alignment alone might not mandate it.

## Pitfalls

- **Higher blood loss and longer OR time** than laminoplasty.
- **C5 palsy**: occurs at rates similar to or higher than laminoplasty in some series; typically deltoid/biceps weakness, mechanism debated (nerve root tethering during decompression).
- **Loss of motion with attendant adjacent segment risk** — a trade-off against laminoplasty's motion preservation.
- **Loss of cervical lordosis/progressive kyphosis is a risk of laminectomy performed without fusion** — this is precisely why current practice favors laminectomy + fusion over laminectomy alone.
- **Wound infection, epidural hematoma**: standard posterior spine surgery risks; hematoma is particularly concerning given baseline cord compromise — low threshold for urgent imaging with any post-op neurological decline.
- **Comparative evidence** (2024–2025 meta-analyses): laminectomy-fusion shows better lordosis maintenance/correction and, specifically in OPLL, better control of OPLL progression than laminoplasty, but at the cost of longer OR time, more blood loss, and comparable-or-higher complication/C5 palsy rates.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: collar use more consistent in multilevel laminectomy-fusion constructs than in laminoplasty; inpatient mobilization with PT/OT from day 1 given baseline myelopathic gait/balance impairment and elevated fall risk.
- **Weeks 2–6**: gait training progression, gentle ROM, monitor for delayed C5 palsy (can present days to ~2 weeks post-op).
- **Weeks 6–12**: formal outpatient PT with proprioceptive/balance training, strengthening, hand dexterity/fine motor rehabilitation (occupational therapy referral valuable for hand-intensive deficits).
- **Months 3–12+**: neurological recovery is gradual and can continue for 12–24 months post-decompression; mJOA recovery ratio typically assessed at 1 year but continued gains documented beyond that in a subset.
- **Return to work**: sedentary 2–6 weeks; manual labor individualized, often delayed 3+ months given fusion/bone healing. Return to contact sport is generally discouraged — both construct integrity and underlying cord vulnerability argue against it.
