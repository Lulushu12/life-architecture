## Indications

Severe hallux valgus (HVA >40°, IMA >16–18°), a hypermobile or arthritic 1st TMT joint, ligamentous laxity syndromes, juvenile/adolescent hallux valgus, and recurrent deformity after a prior distal/diaphyseal osteotomy. Reserved for these settings alongside — or as an alternative to — proximal metatarsal osteotomy (crescentic or opening/closing wedge), since both are used for the same severe-deformity tier.

## Positioning & setup

Standard medial/dorsomedial forefoot approach to the 1st TMT (tarsometatarsal, Lisfranc) joint. Preoperative confirmation of 1st ray hypermobility (sagittal-plane stress testing: >8–10 mm of dorsal–plantar excursion relative to the 2nd ray is generally considered abnormal, though the test has only moderate reliability) and of severity grade on weight-bearing radiographs.

## Steps

1. Expose and prepare the 1st TMT joint surfaces for fusion.
2. Correct the intermetatarsal angle and rotational/sagittal alignment of the 1st ray at the TMT joint before fixation.
3. Fix the fusion, typically with a **plate ± compression screw construct**, directly addressing 1st-ray instability.
4. Perform lateral soft-tissue release/derotation of the sesamoid apparatus concurrently if sesamoid subluxation is significant (Hardy & Clapham ≥4–5).
5. Add an Akin osteotomy if residual hallux valgus interphalangeus persists after the TMT-level correction.

## Pitfalls

- Lapidus carries a **nonunion risk of approximately 3–6%** in contemporary series — do not assume union; confirm it radiographically before advancing to unrestricted loading.
- Requires a **longer initial protected weight-bearing period** than a distal osteotomy — communicate this trade-off to patients preoperatively.
- Undercorrection of the intermetatarsal angle or failure to adequately address 1st-ray instability is a driver of recurrence, as with other hallux valgus procedures.
- As with any 1st-ray procedure, avoid shortening or elevating the ray excessively — this redistributes load laterally and risks transfer metatarsalgia under the lesser metatarsal heads.

## Post-op protocol

- **Weeks 0–2**: non-weight-bearing or protected touch-down weight-bearing in a splint/boot per fixation construct (rigid plate constructs increasingly allow earlier protected weight-bearing).
- **Weeks 2–6**: progressive weight-bearing in a CAM boot as radiographs allow.
- **Weeks 6–8**: radiographic fusion check; if consolidating, transition out of the boot into supportive shoes.
- **Weeks 8–12+**: continue progressive loading; return to impact activity only after confirmed union (typically **10–12 weeks**, sometimes longer) — given the ~3–6% nonunion risk, union should be radiographically confirmed, not assumed, before full unrestricted return to sport.
- General return-to-sport gating: near-full pain-free active ROM, pain-free single-leg heel-rise/hop, swelling resolved to fit normal athletic footwear, graduated walk → run → sport-specific drills, with contact/pivoting sport typically not before 12–16 weeks.
