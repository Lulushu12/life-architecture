## Indications

Posterior ankle impingement (bony — os trigonum, prominent Stieda process of the lateral talar tubercle — or soft-tissue — posterior capsule/synovium, FHL tenosynovitis) that has failed activity modification, NSAIDs, PT (FHL flexibility, posterior capsule mobility), and/or ultrasound-guided FHL sheath or posterior joint injection. A diagnostic US-guided local anesthetic injection into the posterior recess is both diagnostic and prognostic for scope success and can help confirm the indication before committing to surgery.

## Positioning & setup

Prone position, using posterolateral and posteromedial portals (van Dijk technique).

## Steps

1. Establish posterolateral and posteromedial portals with the patient prone.
2. Excise the os trigonum or prominent Stieda process.
3. Debride posterior soft-tissue impingement (capsule/synovium).
4. Release/debride the FHL sheath when concurrent FHL tenosynovitis is present.
5. Reserve open posteromedial or posterolateral approaches for large fracture fragments, revision cases, or concurrent procedures not amenable to endoscopy.

## Pitfalls

- Sural nerve or FHL neurovascular bundle injury is a recognized risk given technically demanding portal placement.
- Recurrent impingement can result from incomplete resection.
- An acute-on-chronic presentation with a palpable Achilles gap should prompt ruling out an acute Achilles rupture through chronic tendinopathy before committing to elective posterior hindfoot work.

## Post-op protocol

Weightbearing as tolerated in a supportive boot or shoe within days; early ROM; return to sport typically 4–8 weeks depending on sport demands and whether concurrent FHL or ligament work was performed. Contemporary reviews confirm two-portal posterior hindfoot endoscopy as the standard of care, with high satisfaction and rapid return to sport in athletes.
