## Indications

Growth-modulation and lengthening options for pediatric limb length discrepancy (LLD), selected by predicted LLD at skeletal maturity: guided growth or permanent epiphysiodesis for a predicted LLD of roughly 2–6 cm on the long side; limb lengthening for discrepancies beyond the practical range of epiphysiodesis (generally >5–6 cm, up to 20+ cm), often combined with contralateral epiphysiodesis and staged for very large discrepancies (e.g., PFFD, severe congenital deficiencies).

## Positioning & setup

Percutaneous/minimally invasive techniques for guided growth and permanent epiphysiodesis. Limb lengthening uses either an external fixator (Ilizarov ring or monolateral frame) or an internal magnetic motorized nail (e.g., PRECICE).

## Steps

1. **Confirm the discrepancy and growth remaining:** full-length standing EOS/scanogram radiograph (gold standard for measurement, obtained serially to project growth) or CT scanogram; document rotational profile and physeal status at each visit.
2. **Guided growth (temporary hemiepiphysiodesis):** place a tension-band/8-plate construct across the physis of the longer limb. This is reversible — growth resumes after implant removal — and is preferred when meaningful growth remains and precision timing is less critical, or when combined angular correction is also needed. Correction can rebound if the plate is removed too early relative to the deformity achieved.
3. **Permanent epiphysiodesis (percutaneous transphyseal screw [PETS] or open Phemister technique):** time the procedure using the **Paley multiplier method** — multiply the current LLD (or segment length) by an age- and sex-specific multiplier to predict the discrepancy at skeletal maturity, then schedule epiphysiodesis of the longer limb's physis (or physes) so the shorter limb "catches up" to equalize by maturity. The multiplier simplifies the older Anderson-Green and Moseley straight-line-graph methods and is the most widely used modern planning tool.
4. **Limb lengthening (external fixator or magnetic internal nail):** reserved for discrepancies beyond the practical range of epiphysiodesis; stage over multiple lengthening episodes for very large (>10–15 cm) discrepancies, often combined with contralateral epiphysiodesis.

## Pitfalls

- Mistimed epiphysiodesis — from multiplier miscalculation or unexpected growth variation — produces overcorrection or undercorrection.
- An 8-plate placed asymmetrically or removed too early can produce iatrogenic angular deformity rather than pure length correction.
- Pin/screw breakage or migration is a recognized hardware complication.
- Limb lengthening carries pin-tract infection, joint contracture, premature consolidation, and neurovascular injury risk, particularly with larger or faster corrections.
- <2 cm predicted LLD rarely warrants surgery — observation or a shoe lift if symptomatic is first-line; surgical correction is reserved for predicted discrepancies above this threshold.

## Post-op protocol

- Weight-bearing as tolerated immediately in most percutaneous techniques (guided growth, PETS).
- Routine follow-up every 4–6 months with standing radiographs to monitor correction rate and avoid overcorrection.
- Implant removal (guided growth) timed to the desired correction endpoint.
