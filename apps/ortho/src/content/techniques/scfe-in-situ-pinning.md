## Indications

In-situ single-screw fixation is the standard of care for the great majority of slips, both stable and most unstable. There is no accepted non-operative pathway once SCFE is confirmed — even a mildly symptomatic, stable, minimally displaced slip is treated surgically because of the risk of progression. Stable slips (Loder) can generally be scheduled semi-urgently (within 24–48h); unstable slips are a surgical urgency (same-day/urgent).

## Positioning & setup

Fracture table with fluoroscopy. For unstable slips, only gentle positional reduction is used — achieved simply by positioning the leg, without forceful manipulation; aggressive closed reduction maneuvers are avoided given evidence they increase AVN risk by further compromising the tenuous remaining blood supply.

## Steps

1. **For unstable slips, achieve gentle positional reduction** on the fracture table by positioning the leg only — do not attempt forceful reduction maneuvers.
2. **Place a single cannulated screw** under fluoroscopic guidance, centrally located in the epiphysis on both AP and lateral views, perpendicular to the physis.
3. **Achieve adequate purchase** — generally 5 or more threads across the physis, ideally reaching the center of the epiphysis without penetrating the joint.
4. **Stop 2–5 mm short of the subchondral bone**, confirmed on both AP and lateral views, to avoid intra-articular penetration.
5. **Favor a single screw over multiple screws** in most contemporary practice — adequate stabilization with lower reported rates of penetration-related chondrolysis and technically simpler revision if needed.
6. **Consider prophylactic contralateral fixation**, individualized rather than routine — favored in patients with open triradiate cartilage, young age at presentation (<10–12y), modifiable endocrinopathy, obesity, or where reliable follow-up cannot be assured.

## Pitfalls

- Confirm central screw position on both AP and true lateral fluoroscopic views before final seating — a screw that appears epiphysis-centered on AP but is actually eccentric/peripheral on the lateral risks inadequate purchase and slip progression.
- Stop 2–5 mm short of subchondral bone, confirmed on both views, to avoid intra-articular penetration, historically implicated in chondrolysis.
- Do not attempt forceful closed reduction of an unstable slip beyond simple, gentle positioning — pressure-driven reduction maneuvers have been specifically associated with higher AVN rates in several series.
- Screen the contralateral hip at every visit — early/subtle contralateral slips are easily missed, and 20–40% of unilateral presenters go on to a contralateral slip if not prophylactically fixed, highest in the youngest patients.

## Post-op protocol

- Weeks 0–2: protected weight-bearing (touch-down/toe-touch to partial per surgeon protocol) on crutches for stable slips fixed promptly; unstable slips kept more strictly protected (touch-down only) for a longer initial period given higher AVN risk and theoretical benefit of unloading the compromised epiphysis.
- Weeks 2–6: progressive weight-bearing as tolerated, guided by pain and radiographic screw position; gentle active ROM, avoiding forced internal rotation/flexion that stresses the fixation.
- Weeks 6–12: full weight-bearing typically achieved; physiotherapy for hip and core strengthening, gait normalization, and gradual return to low-impact recreational activity.
- Months 3–6: return to sport/full activity individualized — earlier for uncomplicated stable slips with good screw position and no AVN signs on interval imaging (often by 3–4 months), later and more cautiously for unstable slips or any radiographic AVN concern.
- AVN surveillance: any unstable slip or one with delayed presentation warrants interval clinical and radiographic (± MRI) follow-up through at least the first postoperative year, as radiographic AVN changes may not be apparent for several months.
- Contralateral surveillance: continued clinical/radiographic monitoring of the unaffected hip through skeletal maturity in patients not treated with prophylactic fixation.
- Return to school: 1–2 weeks for stable slips (crutch-assisted return acceptable); 2–4 weeks, protected, for unstable slips.
