## Indications

Applies to closure and postoperative wound management for any orthopedic implant surgery, with intensified measures for high-risk wounds (obesity, revision surgery, prior wound complications, poor soft-tissue envelope, or high anticipated drainage). Goal: prevent the cascade of hematoma → persistent drainage → superficial then deep infection → need for DAIR or two-stage revision, rather than treating it after the fact.

## Positioning & setup

Not applicable in the surgical-positioning sense — this protocol covers preoperative optimization, closure technique, and postoperative dressing/surveillance rather than a positioning-dependent procedure.

## Steps

1. **Optimize modifiable SSI/PJI risk factors preoperatively:** address obesity (individualized BMI thresholds are used by many centers as relative contraindications to elective arthroplasty), poorly controlled diabetes (target perioperative glucose <200mg/dL regardless of diabetic status, since even non-diabetic hyperglycemia raises SSI risk), active smoking (cessation counseling, ideally weeks before elective surgery), malnutrition (albumin <3.5g/dL is a recognized independent risk marker), and modifiable immunosuppression (discuss holding/adjusting disease-modifying antirheumatic drugs and biologics with rheumatology around the perioperative window).
2. **Close in layers with meticulous, tension-free technique**, considering barbed running suture for a watertight capsular/subcutaneous layer, and avoiding excessive electrocautery near the skin edge.
3. **Balance anticoagulation/antiplatelet management** between VTE risk and the bleeding/hematoma risk that directly predisposes to wound complications and secondary infection.
4. **Apply an occlusive dressing** (often silver-impregnated or waterproof) and leave it undisturbed for an extended initial period, commonly 5–7 days, to reduce the number of dressing changes (each an opportunity for bacterial ingress) while allowing wound inspection through a transparent window when needed.
5. **Reserve incisional negative pressure wound therapy (NPWT)** for high-risk wounds — obesity, revision surgery, prior wound complications, poor soft-tissue envelope, or high anticipated drainage — where it reduces wound complication and reoperation rates relative to standard dressings; routine use in low-risk primary arthroplasty has not shown the same benefit.
6. **Monitor for persistent drainage beyond postoperative day 5–7** — the single most consistently cited trigger for early surgical wound review and PJI workup. Address it proactively (wound review, consider incisional NPWT) rather than reassurance and waiting.
7. **If acute PJI is confirmed** (early postoperative, or acute hematogenous with <3 weeks of symptoms and a stable, well-fixed implant), proceed to DAIR (debridement, antibiotics, implant retention) — thorough debridement, modular component exchange, and pathogen-directed antibiotics with implant retention.

## Pitfalls

- Success rates for DAIR fall sharply the longer symptom duration or biofilm maturity is allowed to progress — this is the underlying rationale for aggressive early wound surveillance rather than a "wait and see" approach to drainage.
- Prolonged wound drainage should not be escalated straight to wound review without first addressing anticoagulation intensity, dressing optimization, and prolonged elevation — these are the first-line measures before wound review.
- Incisional NPWT's evidence base is stronger in trauma than in arthroplasty specifically: a 2023 eClinicalMedicine meta-analysis found high-certainty evidence of SSI reduction with iNPWT broadly, and orthopedic-trauma-specific meta-analyses (2023, 2025) support benefit in that population, but arthroplasty-specific trial data remain more heterogeneous — treat the "high-risk-only" arthroplasty indication as an area of continued refinement rather than settled practice.
- Do not use routine incisional NPWT in low-risk primary arthroplasty wounds — benefit has not been demonstrated in that population, only in the higher-risk subgroup.

## Post-op protocol

- Dressings left undisturbed for 5–7 days where feasible, with inspection through a transparent window as needed.
- Wound review triggered at postoperative day 5–7 if drainage persists, escalating to incisional NPWT consideration or formal PJI workup.
- DAIR (debridement, antibiotics, implant retention) if acute PJI is confirmed within the early postoperative or acute hematogenous (<3 weeks symptoms, stable implant) window.
- Bone-health/secondary-fracture-prevention follow-up continues in parallel for fragility-fracture patients, independent of wound status.
