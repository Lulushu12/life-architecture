## Indications

- Stable OCD lesions (intact overlying cartilage) failing 3–6 months of non-operative care (activity restriction, unloader brace, non-weight-bearing trial): arthroscopic drilling.
- Unstable/partially detached but viable fragment: fragment fixation — preferred whenever fragment viability allows, since it preserves native hyaline cartilage.
- Non-viable fragment or a full-thickness defect remaining after fragment excision: treat as a focal chondral/osteochondral defect on the cartilage restoration ladder.
- Staging relies on MRI instability criteria (high-signal/fluid line ≥5 mm deep to the fragment on T2, a peripheral high-signal rim, multiple breaches/cysts beneath the fragment, a focal osteochondral defect with fluid extending through the cartilage surface) together with arthroscopic ICRS OCD staging (I–IV).
- The classic location is the posterolateral aspect of the medial femoral condyle (~70–80% of cases); lesion size >2 cm² and involvement of the weight-bearing lateral femoral condyle, or an unstable/detached fragment, predict failure of conservative care.

## Positioning & setup

Standard knee arthroscopy. Pre-operative radiographic workup should include AP, lateral, and notch/tunnel views — the notch view best profiles the classic posterolateral medial condyle location.

## Steps

1. Confirm stability status with MRI instability criteria and/or arthroscopic ICRS OCD staging before choosing between drilling, fixation, and excision.
2. **Stable lesion failing 3–6 months of non-operative care**: perform arthroscopic drilling — transarticular (through the joint surface) or retroarticular/extra-articular (from outside, avoiding cartilage penetration) — to stimulate revascularization and healing without disturbing an intact articular surface.
3. **Unstable/partially detached but viable fragment**: debride the fibrous interface, micro-drill and bone-graft the crater, then fix the fragment with bioabsorbable pins/screws or compression screws.
4. **Non-viable fragment or full-thickness defect after fragment excision**: treat per the cartilage restoration ladder — OAT for smaller lesions, OCA for larger lesions or when subchondral bone stock is deficient, ACI/MACI reserved for chondral-only defects with adequate bone.
5. Throughout, distinguish true OCD from an incidental "ossification variant" in skeletally immature patients by lesion location, symmetry (variants are often bilateral/symmetric), and absence of MRI instability criteria before committing to surgery.

## Pitfalls

- Do not rely on radiographs alone to stage stability — MRI is required for surgical decision-making.
- Hardware prominence/synovitis requiring removal is the most common reason for reoperation even with fragment union.
- Nonunion of the fixed fragment, or progression to a full-thickness defect if fixation fails, converts the case to a salvage cartilage-restoration procedure.
- Healing rates in skeletally immature, stable OCD treated non-operatively are favorable but decline sharply in skeletally mature patients — use a much lower threshold for surgery in adults.

## Post-op protocol

- **Drilling (intact cartilage)**: weight-bearing as tolerated with brace protection, ROM progressed early; return to sport at 3–4 months once healing is confirmed on imaging.
- **Fragment fixation**: protected weight-bearing for 6 weeks, ROM progression similar to the cartilage-restoration protocols; return to sport at 6–9 months, pending radiographic/MRI union of the fragment.
- The 2024 AAOS Osteochondritis Dissecans Clinical Practice Guideline Rapid Update reaffirms MRI-based instability staging and supports drilling for stable lesions failing non-operative care, with fixation preferred over excision for unstable-but-viable fragments.
