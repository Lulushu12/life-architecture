## Indications

**Scapula body/spine**: glenopolar angle <22°, lateral border offset >20 mm, angular deformity >45°, displaced scapular neck with concomitant clavicle fracture ("floating shoulder"), or open fracture. **Glenoid (intra-articular)**: articular step-off/gap >4–5 mm, >25% involvement of the anterior or >33% of the posterior glenoid with instability, or associated instability/subluxation.

## Positioning & setup

Posterior approach (Judet or minimally-invasive modified Judet) for glenoid neck, scapular body, and posterior glenoid rim fractures — risk to the suprascapular and axillary nerves via the infraspinatus/teres minor interval. Anterior (deltopectoral) approach for anterior glenoid rim fractures — risk to the axillary nerve, musculocutaneous nerve, and subscapularis. Arthroscopic-assisted fixation is increasingly reported for anterior glenoid rim (bony Bankart-equivalent) fragments in the setting of instability, with less soft-tissue morbidity.

## Steps

1. Select the approach by fracture location: posterior (Judet/modified Judet) for glenoid neck, scapular body, or posterior glenoid rim; anterior (deltopectoral) for anterior glenoid rim; consider arthroscopic-assisted fixation for anterior glenoid rim fragments in the instability setting.
2. For comminuted glenoid fossa fractures (Ideberg type VI), use buttress or spanning plate constructs, with articular reconstruction as the primary goal.
3. In older patients with unreconstructable comminuted glenoid fossa fractures and poor bone quality, consider primary reverse total shoulder arthroplasty rather than attempted ORIF.

## Pitfalls

- The suprascapular and axillary nerves are at risk via the posterior interval; the axillary nerve, musculocutaneous nerve, and subscapularis are at risk anteriorly.
- The glenoid articular surface behaves like any other intra-articular fracture (step-off intolerant) — do not extend the same tolerance for displacement used for extra-articular scapular body/spine fractures, which heal well nonoperatively even with substantial displacement.
- When a scapular neck/glenoid fracture coexists with a clavicle fracture ("floating shoulder"), assess combined displacement rather than each fracture in isolation — true double disruption of the superior shoulder suspensory complex is unstable and usually needs fixation of at least one component (often the clavicle, which restores the strut and indirectly stabilizes the scapular component).

## Post-op protocol

- Sling for comfort 1–2 wk; early pendulum and passive ROM within the first week (stiffness, not nonunion, is the dominant risk for extra-articular patterns given the scapula's excellent healing potential from its muscular envelope).
- Progressive active ROM from 2–4 wk; strengthening from 6 wk.
- Intra-articular glenoid fixation follows a similar but slightly more conservative timeline respecting articular healing, with resisted loading deferred to ~8–10 wk.
