## Overview

The Mason & Molloy classification (paired with the Liverpool treatment algorithm) grades posterior malleolus fracture morphology on CT to guide surgical approach and fixation, treating the fragment as a syndesmosis-ligament-avulsion equivalent rather than a simple size cutoff. It is used whenever a posterior malleolus fragment is present on an ankle fracture CT to decide whether and how to fix it.

## Types

| Type | Morphology | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Small extra-incisural posterolateral fragment; syndesmosis (PITFL) usually not attached to a fragment large enough to warrant direct fixation | Standard lateral/anterolateral approach for fibula; stabilize the syndesmosis directly (screw/suture-button) rather than fixing the malleolus fragment itself |
| **2A** | Posterolateral fragment extending into the incisura, two-part | Posterolateral approach, direct reduction and buttress/antiglide or lag screw fixation |
| **2B** | Same as 2A plus an additional (comminuted/intermediate) fragment | Posterolateral approach ± accessory posteromedial exposure to address the intermediate fragment; may need two plates/fragment-specific fixation |
| **3** | Posteromedial extension, often a large "die-punch"-type fragment | Posteromedial approach, direct buttress plating; frequently combined with medial malleolus fixation |

## Pearls

- Direct fixation is favored when the fragment involves ≥25% of the articular surface on lateral radiograph, or by CT-defined criteria (incisural involvement, articular step-off >2 mm, comminution, or syndesmotic instability persisting after fibula/medial fixation) — CT morphology now drives the decision more than a single fragment-size cutoff.
- Direct buttress fixation of the posterior malleolus restores articular congruity, reduces anterior talar subluxation, and frequently restores syndesmotic stability without an additional syndesmotic implant — assess syndesmosis stability *after* posterior malleolus fixation, not before.
- A 2023 interobserver reliability study (20 raters, general trauma and dedicated foot/ankle surgeons, 38 CT scans) found the system more clinically actionable than fragment-size cutoffs but still with meaningful interobserver variability, particularly in distinguishing Type 2A from 2B — a caution against relying on it in isolation for surgical planning.
- The posterior malleolus carries the PITFL insertion, so an adequately sized fragment functions as a syndesmotic ligament avulsion, not merely an articular fracture.
