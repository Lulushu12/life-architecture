## Overview

AO/OTA 42 is the universal alphanumeric coding for tibial/fibular shaft (diaphyseal) fractures, used in registries, billing, and international communication. Its A/B/C tiers correlate broadly with increasing comminution and directly track union prognosis and nailing difficulty.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 42-A | Simple fracture (spiral/oblique/transverse) | Best union prognosis of the group; typically achieves radiographic union within 6–12 weeks with standard reamed nailing |
| 42-B | Wedge fracture (fragment present, but cortical contact between main fragments restorable after reduction) | Standard reamed intramedullary nailing; intermediate prognosis between 42-A and 42-C |
| 42-C | Complex/comminuted fracture (segmental or irregular) | Highest nonunion and malunion risk, most demanding for nailing technique — often requires blocking (Poller) screws and static locking; open fractures in this group commonly take 4–6+ months to union |

## Pearls

- The A/B/C severity tiers correlate broadly with increasing comminution and worsening prognosis, but subgroup detail (1–3) is used mainly for research stratification — in daily practice A/B/C plus a plain-language fracture description drives the operative plan.
- Provides the universal common language for registries, billing, and international communication — the system to default to when writing for a European/AO-trained audience or entering registry data.
- Nonunion/delayed union is more common with comminuted (AO/OTA C-type) shaft fractures, open fractures, unreamed nailing, and smoking — addressed with exchange reamed nailing, dynamization, plating, or bone grafting/Masquelet technique for segmental defects.
