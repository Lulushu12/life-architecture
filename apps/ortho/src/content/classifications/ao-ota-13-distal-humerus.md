## Overview

The AO/OTA 13 system classifies distal humerus fractures by the degree of articular involvement and comminution, which is the primary driver of fixation strategy (single/dual-column plating vs. primary arthroplasty) and of how much articular exposure the surgical approach needs to provide. It applies to fractures of the distal humerus, where a thin, largely cancellous "dumbbell"-shaped bone stock connects the medial and lateral columns and the trochlea/capitellum articular block to the humeral shaft.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **13A** | Extra-articular (epicondylar avulsion, supracondylar/metaphyseal) | Often amenable to single/dual-column plating with simpler columnar reconstruction; good bone stock often allows earlier motion |
| **13B** | Partial articular (one column, capitellum/trochlea fractured, other column intact) | Column-specific fixation (headless screws for capitellar shear, posterolateral plate); preserve the intact column as a stable strut |
| **13C** | Complete articular (both columns + articular surface fractured, dissociated from shaft) | Requires dual-plate fixation (orthogonal 90-90 or parallel 3.5mm locking plates) restoring the articular block first, then fixing it to the shaft; in the elderly with unreconstructable comminution (C3) → primary total elbow arthroplasty (TEA) is an accepted alternative |

Subgroups (.1/.2/.3) further stratify simple vs. wedge vs. multifragmentary patterns within each type.

## Pearls

- Increasing subgroup number (.1 → .3) correlates with more comminution and a lower likelihood of a stable ORIF construct, which raises the threshold to consider primary TEA in low-demand elderly patients.
- CT with 2D/3D reconstruction should be obtained for essentially all intra-articular distal humerus fractures (13B/C) — plain radiographs routinely understate articular comminution and change the fixation plan.
- The classification is a proxy for construct reliability, not just anatomy: the real decision point at 13C is whether the comminution is reconstructable enough to permit the rigid fixation needed for early active motion, since stiffness — not fixation failure — is the dominant long-term complication in this fracture group.
