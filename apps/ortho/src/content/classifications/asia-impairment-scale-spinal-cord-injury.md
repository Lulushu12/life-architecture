## Overview

The ASIA Impairment Scale (AIS), part of the International Standards for Neurological Classification of Spinal Cord Injury (ISNCSCI), grades the completeness of spinal cord injury from motor and sensory examination. It is used for every patient with traumatic SCI to document severity, guide prognosis counseling, and track neurologic recovery over time.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication / notes |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Complete** | No motor or sensory function preserved in sacral segments S4-S5 (no sacral sparing) | Worst prognosis — poorest chance for motor recovery if truly complete at 72h post-spinal-shock resolution; drives most intensive rehab/counseling |
| **B — Sensory incomplete** | Sensory but not motor function preserved below the neurological level, including S4-S5, AND no motor function preserved more than 3 levels below the motor level on either side | Intermediate prognosis |
| **C — Motor incomplete** | Motor function preserved below the neurological level, and more than half of key muscles below the neurological level have a muscle grade <3 | Intermediate prognosis; candidate for gait training with orthoses if lower-extremity motor potential present |
| **D — Motor incomplete** | Motor function preserved below the neurological level, and at least half of key muscles below the neurological level have a muscle grade ≥3 | Best prognosis for ambulation |
| **E — Normal** | Motor and sensory function normal in all segments, patient had a prior deficit | Used to document recovery, not baseline normal |

## Pearls

- Grade A (complete) has the poorest prognosis for motor recovery; Grade D has the best — grade conversion between 72h and 1 year is a core outcome measure and directly drives rehab intensity and prognosis counseling.
- The exam should be repeated after spinal shock resolves (bulbocavernosus reflex return) — an "AIS A" designation made immediately post-injury may reflect spinal shock rather than a truly complete anatomic injury, and persistent AIS A findings only become reliable after shock has resolved.
- Zone of partial preservation (ZPP) is recorded only for AIS A injuries — the number of dermatomes/myotomes caudal to the neurological level with some preserved function.
- The full ISNCSCI exam documents bilateral motor (10 key muscle groups, 0–5 MRC scale each side), light touch and pinprick sensation (28 dermatomes each side), and sacral sparing (deep anal pressure, voluntary anal contraction) to determine neurological level of injury, single neurological level, motor level, sensory level, and ZPP.
- Serial exams matter — the grade can evolve, especially as the patient comes out of spinal shock, so a single early exam should not be treated as final.
- AIS grade is central to surgical timing decisions: early decompression (≤24h) more than doubles the odds of ≥2-grade AIS improvement compared with later surgery per the updated AO Spine/GRADE guideline.
