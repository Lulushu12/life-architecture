## Overview

The Gärtner classification grades calcific tendinopathy deposits by their radiographic morphology on plain films — dense/well-circumscribed (type I) through fluffy/cloud-like (type III) — reflecting where the deposit sits in its natural biological cycle (formative/resting versus active resorptive phase). It is the most widely used shorthand for deciding between watchful waiting and active intervention (barbotage/ESWT).

## Types

| Type | Appearance | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Dense, well-circumscribed, sharply demarcated ("formative"/resting phase) | Often chronic, less likely to resorb spontaneously; barbotage/needling more technically feasible and effective (firm deposit yields more calcium on aspiration) |
| II | Dense with a partially defined/fluffy contour (mixed) | Intermediate behavior; barbotage still generally effective |
| III | Fluffy, cloud-like, poorly defined ("resorptive" phase) | Deposit often actively resorbing — high spontaneous resolution rate; needling technically harder (soft, toothpaste-like) but symptoms often self-limit within weeks; favor watchful waiting/NSAIDs over intervention unless pain is severe |

## Pearls

- Correlate radiographic type with symptom acuity: a "hot," acutely painful shoulder with a fluffy (type III) deposit is often in active resorption and may resolve within days to weeks, whereas a chronic dull ache with a dense type I deposit is less likely to self-resolve — the acute presentation is the good-prognosis one, which is counterintuitive to patients.
- Plain radiographs (true AP/Grashey, axillary, outlet/scapular-Y) are the best initial modality for calcific tendinopathy and are what characterize Gärtner type; MRI/ultrasound are adjuncts, not the primary staging tool for this system.
- Best candidates for barbotage/needling are Gärtner I–II deposits that are well-circumscribed and have failed an initial NSAID/PT trial; type III deposits are harder to needle (soft, toothpaste-like) and more often managed expectantly.
- An acute, severely painful shoulder with a type III (resorptive-phase) deposit can mimic septic arthritis or crystal arthropathy — keep this differential in mind before assuming calcific tendinopathy.
- Some series subdivide further (Molé classification integrates deposit type with concomitant tear presence), but Gärtner type alone is the practical, most widely used shorthand for the watchful-waiting-versus-barbotage decision.
