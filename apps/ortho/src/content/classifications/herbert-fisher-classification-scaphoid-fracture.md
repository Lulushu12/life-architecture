## Overview

The Herbert-Fisher classification grades acute and healing scaphoid fractures by fracture location, pattern, and stage of union, directly mapping to stability and to the cast-versus-fixation decision. It spans from stable tubercle avulsions (A1) through unstable waist and proximal-pole fractures (B1–B5) to delayed union (C) and established nonunion (D1–D2), making it the most treatment-relevant scaphoid fracture classification in routine use.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Tubercle fracture | Stable; cast, high union rate |
| **A2** | Incomplete waist fracture | Stable; cast vs screw both reasonable (SWIFFT population) |
| **B1** | Distal oblique fracture | Unstable pattern; fixation generally favored |
| **B2** | Complete waist fracture (displaced or unstable) | Fixation recommended — displacement, comminution, or vertical-oblique orientation increases nonunion risk with cast alone |
| **B3** | Proximal pole fracture | Fixation strongly favored — highest AVN/nonunion risk from retrograde vascularity; fragment-specific/headless screw |
| **B4** | Trans-scaphoid perilunate fracture-dislocation | Emergent reduction + open fixation of scaphoid and repair of associated ligament injuries |
| **B5** | Comminuted fracture | Fixation ± bone graft; humpback correction if comminution is volar |
| **C** | Delayed union (radiographic union delayed beyond ~6–12 wks without established nonunion) | Continued immobilization vs surgical fixation depending on trajectory; low threshold to fix if plateauing |
| **D1** | Fibrous (established) nonunion | Surgical grafting required |
| **D2** | Pseudarthrosis (sclerotic nonunion, cyst formation) | Surgical grafting required, often vascularized if AVN present |

## Pearls

- The A/B/C/D tiers roughly correspond to stable-nonoperative (A), unstable-operative (B), equivocal (C), and established nonunion requiring grafting (D) — a useful mental shortcut when triaging a new fracture.
- Type B3 (proximal pole) fractures carry the highest AVN/nonunion risk because the scaphoid's dominant blood supply (dorsal carpal branch of the radial artery) enters distally and runs retrograde — a fracture through the proximal pole can devascularize the entire fragment. Consider primary fixation even when minimally displaced.
- For B2 (displaced/unstable waist) fractures, the fracture-line orientation (see Russe classification) further refines stability: vertical-oblique patterns are least stable and favor fixation even at borderline displacement.
- The SWIFFT trial data (undisplaced bicortical waist fractures, i.e. Herbert A2/stable B2) showed no clinically important PRWE difference between cast and screw fixation at 52 weeks or 5 years, though screw fixation gave faster union and earlier return to work/sport at the cost of a higher complication rate (implant issues, CRPS, scar sensitivity) — this evidence underlies why A2 is listed as "either reasonable" rather than defaulting to surgery.
- Type C (delayed union) is a trajectory diagnosis, not a fixed cutoff: serial films and a low threshold to convert to fixation if healing plateaus is more useful than waiting for a strict time threshold.
