## Overview

This descriptive scheme classifies meniscus tears by their plane and pattern relative to the meniscus's circumferential collagen fibers. It is used at the time of MRI review and arthroscopy to predict reparability, select technique, and decide between repair, partial meniscectomy, and non-operative management — pattern, together with vascular zone (red-red, red-white, white-white), is the primary driver of that decision.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical longitudinal | Tear parallel to meniscal circumference, full or partial thickness; often peripheral (red-red/red-white zone) | Best repair candidate; if displaced and causing locking = bucket-handle |
| Bucket-handle | Displaced vertical longitudinal tear with the inner fragment flipped into the notch/gutter; peripheral rim intact, fragment often white-white | Urgent reduction + repair if tissue viable and knee locked; discard fragment only if non-repairable |
| Radial | Perpendicular to circumferential fibers, disrupts hoop-stress transmission; central, often white-white | Behaves biomechanically like a root tear if complete; repair (if reachable) vs. partial meniscectomy of the apex |
| Horizontal (cleavage) | Splits meniscus into superior/inferior leaves, parallel to tibial plateau; often mid-body, degenerative | Usually degenerative etiology; partial meniscectomy of unstable flap, or observe if stable/asymptomatic |
| Root tear | Radial tear or avulsion within 1 cm of the bony root attachment; root is a well-vascularized bony insertion | Functionally = total meniscectomy if untreated; repair candidacy per extrusion/OA/alignment criteria (see LaPrade classification) |
| Complex/degenerative | Combination of the above, often with horizontal cleavage + flap, in osteoarthritic meniscus; multiple zones, poor tissue quality | Usually not repairable; treat the person (PT-first), not the MRI — arthroscopic partial meniscectomy (APM) only for mechanical symptoms |
| Flap/parrot-beak | Oblique tear combining radial and horizontal components; variable zone | Debride unstable flap only, preserve stable rim |

## Pearls

- Radial (tie) fibers on the meniscal surface resist longitudinal splitting; a radial tear is mechanically destructive because it severs the circumferential fibers outright, which is why a complete radial tear behaves like a root tear.
- Vascular zonation — peripheral 10–30% (red-red), mid (red-white), inner two-thirds (white-white, avascular) — is the single biggest determinant of intrinsic healing potential and therefore of repair candidacy; document zone relative to the meniscosynovial junction for every tear considered for repair.
- A locked knee (fixed flexion deformity, block to full extension) is a bucket-handle tear until proven otherwise and warrants urgent MRI/arthroscopy rather than a prolonged conservative trial.
- Don't stop at the tear identified on MRI: always probe the posterior horn of both menisci during arthroscopy to avoid missing a concurrent ramp or root lesion.
- For complex/degenerative tears, treat the person, not the MRI — structured PT is first-line, and APM adds no benefit over PT for pain alone in the absence of true mechanical locking/catching.
- Degenerative white-white tissue with poor healing potential is better served by limited debridement of the unstable flap than by a repair doomed to fail.
