## Overview

Gustilo-Anderson grades open fracture severity and is discussed here not as an infection classification itself, but because the grade drives fracture-related infection (FRI) risk stratification and prophylactic antibiotic strategy at the time of injury. Alongside it, **Gaenslen's (split-heel) approach** — a longitudinal, posterior heel-splitting incision originally described for drainage of calcaneal osteomyelitis — is noted as the surgical-approach counterpart, since both bear directly on infection risk and wound-management strategy in the foot/ankle and long bones.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Approximate infection risk |
|---|---|---|
| I | Wound <1 cm, clean, minimal soft-tissue damage, simple fracture pattern | ~0–2% |
| II | Wound 1–10 cm, moderate soft-tissue damage, no extensive flaps | ~2–7% |
| IIIA | Extensive soft-tissue laceration/flap but adequate bone coverage; high-energy mechanism or segmental fracture regardless of wound size | ~7% |
| IIIB | Extensive soft-tissue loss with periosteal stripping and bone exposure requiring flap coverage; heavy contamination | ~10–25% |
| IIIC | Any open fracture with an arterial injury requiring repair | ~25–50% |

## Pearls

- Grade drives FRI risk stratification and prophylactic antibiotic strategy — this is why the classification is referenced in an infection-focused context rather than purely a trauma-severity context.
- **Gaenslen's approach**: a longitudinal, posterior heel-splitting incision still referenced as an extensile posterior exposure when deep calcaneal infection or an infected calcaneal nonunion needs direct posterior access.
- The higher the Gustilo-Anderson grade, the greater the contamination burden and periosteal stripping — both directly increase the biofilm-forming inoculum at the fracture site, tying this injury-severity grade mechanistically to later FRI risk.
