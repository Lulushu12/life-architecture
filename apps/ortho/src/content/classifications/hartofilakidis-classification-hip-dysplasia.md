## Overview

The Hartofilakidis classification is an alternative, arguably more surgically relevant, 3-tier system for dysplastic/dislocated hips, describing the relationship of the femoral head to the true and false acetabulum rather than a pure percentage-migration measurement. It is used alongside the Crowe classification to plan cup placement, graft needs, and femoral shortening at total hip arthroplasty (THA).

## Types

| Type | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Dysplasia** | Femoral head remains contained within a true, albeit shallow/deformed, acetabulum | THA with standard-size cup at or near anatomic hip center; PAO an alternative in the precartilage-loss stage |
| **B — Low (partial) dislocation** | Femoral head articulates with a false acetabulum that partially overlaps the true acetabulum | Cup placed at true acetabulum, often with superolateral structural autograft for coverage; femoral shortening frequently needed |
| **C — High (complete) dislocation** | Femoral head has migrated superiorly/posteriorly with no contact with the true acetabulum; false acetabulum entirely separate | Cup at true acetabulum, subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy essentially mandatory, small components, high nerve-injury risk — most technically demanding THA subgroup |

## Pearls

- Type C is explicitly flagged as the most technically demanding THA subgroup in the dysplasia spectrum — cup placement at the true acetabulum combined with mandatory subtrochanteric shortening and small femoral components carries the highest nerve-injury risk of the three tiers.
- CT is the preferred modality for precise Hartofilakidis measurement in complex dysplasia, informing preoperative planning for cup coverage and shortening length.
- The management thresholds for THA in this monograph are framed jointly with Crowe: THA once secondary OA is established (Tönnis ≥2) or in Crowe III–IV/Hartofilakidis high (type C) dislocation with degenerative change — use whichever system best communicates the deformity, but anchor treatment planning to the same underlying threshold (does the false acetabulum overlap the true one, and by how much).
- Type A (contained dysplasia) is the only tier where periacetabular osteotomy remains a realistic alternative to arthroplasty, and only in the precartilage-loss stage — once type B or C anatomy or degenerative change is present, THA (with its associated shortening/grafting considerations) is the pathway.
