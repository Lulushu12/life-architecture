## Overview

The revised Tokuhashi score is a 0–15 point prognostic scoring system for metastatic spine disease, summing six factors each scored 0–2: general condition (KPS), number of extraspinal bone metastases, number of vertebral metastases, metastases to major internal organs, primary tumor site, and neurologic status (Frankel grade). It was historically used to estimate survival and select a broad surgical strategy, but modern practice has largely shifted to the NOMS framework for individual-patient decisions.

## Types

| Total score | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0–8** | Estimated prognosis <6 months | Palliative/conservative care |
| **9–11** | Estimated prognosis ~6–12 months | Palliative surgery (decompression/stabilization) reasonable |
| **12–15** | Estimated prognosis ≥12 months | Excisional/aggressive surgery reasonable |

## Pearls

- Tokuhashi remains useful as a prognostic reference point and is still cited in the literature, but contemporary decision-making has largely shifted to the **NOMS framework**, which integrates neurologic, oncologic, mechanical, and systemic axes and is more directly actionable at the individual-patient level than a single survival-based cutoff.
- Ambulatory status — not directly a Tokuhashi input — is one of the strongest prognostic and decision-driving variables in metastatic spine disease and should be weighed alongside the score.
- Multidisciplinary tumor board discussion (surgery, radiation/medical oncology) is the standard of care before committing to a treatment pathway, rather than relying on Tokuhashi or Tomita score alone.
