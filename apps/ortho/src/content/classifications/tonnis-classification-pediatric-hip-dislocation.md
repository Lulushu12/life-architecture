## Overview

The Tönnis classification is a radiographic grading system for DDH dislocation severity, based on the position of the femoral head ossific nucleus relative to the Perthes-Perkin line on an AP pelvis radiograph. It is historically widely used and underlies the newer IHDI classification, which was designed to improve its inter-observer reliability while retaining the same underlying concept.

## Types

| Grade | Description | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Ossific nucleus medial to Perthes-Perkin line, mild lateral displacement | Least severe — highest closed-reduction success |
| 2 | Ossific nucleus at level of acetabular roof | Moderate — closed reduction usually feasible |
| 3 | Ossific nucleus at level of the ilium | Marked — closed reduction success decreases |
| 4 | Ossific nucleus above the level of the ilium (highest dislocation) | Most severe — highest rate requiring open reduction |

## Pearls

- Higher Tönnis grade tracks directly with lower closed-reduction success and higher likelihood of requiring open reduction with concomitant osteotomy.
- Tönnis grade III–IV (or the equivalent IHDI III–IV) is the threshold at which femoral shortening derotation osteotomy is generally added to open reduction — it reduces pressure on the reduced head, corrects excess anteversion, and avoids reduction under excessive tension.
- Tönnis grading has been substantially superseded in current practice by the IHDI classification, which uses the same landmarks but was specifically designed for better inter-observer reproducibility.
