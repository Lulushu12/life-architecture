## Overview

The Pipkin classification grades femoral head fractures occurring with a hip dislocation (almost always posterior, occasionally anterior), based on fragment location relative to the fovea centralis and the presence of associated femoral neck or acetabular fractures. It directly drives the choice between non-operative trial, fragment excision, ORIF, and arthroplasty.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Fracture fragment caudal/inferior to the fovea centralis (below the ligamentum teres attachment) | Best prognosis; non-displaced/minimally displaced → closed reduction + non-operative trial; displaced or non-congruent joint → excision of small fragment or ORIF via surgical hip dislocation (Ganz approach, preserves MFCA) |
| **II** | Fracture fragment cephalad/superior to the fovea (includes weight-bearing dome) | ORIF required if displaced (fragment is load-bearing) — anatomic reduction and screw fixation (often countersunk/headless screws from the articular surface); surgical hip dislocation approach preferred over Kocher-Langenbeck alone to protect blood supply |
| **III** | Type I or II combined with an ipsilateral femoral neck fracture | Worst prognosis (highest AVN rate); femoral neck fracture takes priority for fixation urgency in young patients (or arthroplasty if elderly); treat as combined injury — many advocate arthroplasty in older patients given very high AVN risk with fixation of both components |
| **IV** | Type I or II combined with an acetabular fracture (posterior wall/rim) | Combined reconstruction needed; acetabular congruency and hip stability drive approach selection (often posterior/Kocher-Langenbeck when the acetabular component predominates, or combined/surgical dislocation approach) |

## Pearls

- AVN risk is driven primarily by dislocation severity and time-to-reduction, not by the fracture pattern alone — emergent closed reduction of the dislocation (within 6 h) is the single highest-yield intervention regardless of Pipkin type.
- Sciatic nerve injury (peroneal division especially) accompanies 10–20% of posterior fracture-dislocations and must be documented both pre- and post-reduction.
- Post-reduction CT is mandatory to characterize fragment size/location and to detect intra-articular loose bodies before definitive management is chosen.
- Type III carries reported AVN rates up to 50%+ in some series — the worst prognosis of any Pipkin type, which is why primary arthroplasty is a reasonable alternative to attempting fixation of both the neck and head components in older patients.
- Non-operative management is appropriate only for select minimally displaced (<2 mm) Type I fractures with a congruent, stable joint on exam and CT.
