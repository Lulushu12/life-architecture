## Overview

The Caprini Risk Assessment Model is an additive point-scoring tool used to stratify a surgical patient's venous thromboembolism (VTE) risk and select the appropriate prophylaxis strategy. Points are assigned across patient-specific factors (age, comorbidities, thrombophilia, prior VTE) and procedure-specific factors, then summed into a total score that maps to a risk category and a recommended prophylaxis regimen. In orthopedics it is applied preoperatively to essentially every arthroplasty and hip/pelvis/femur fracture patient, since the procedure itself contributes a large fixed point value.

## Types

| Points | Risk factors | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1 point** | Age 41–60y; minor surgery; BMI >25; swollen legs; varicose veins; pregnancy or postpartum (<1 month); history of unexplained/recurrent miscarriage; oral contraceptives or hormone replacement therapy; sepsis (<1 month); serious lung disease incl. pneumonia (<1 month); abnormal pulmonary function; acute MI; congestive heart failure (<1 month); history of inflammatory bowel disease; medical patient currently at bed rest | Contributes to additive total; individually minor |
| **2 points** | Age 61–74y; arthroscopic surgery; malignancy (present or previous); major open surgery (>45 min); laparoscopic surgery (>45 min); confined to bed >72h; immobilizing plaster cast; central venous access | Contributes to additive total |
| **3 points** | Age ≥75y; personal history of VTE; family history of VTE; Factor V Leiden; prothrombin gene mutation 20210A; lupus anticoagulant; elevated anticardiolipin antibody; elevated serum homocysteine; heparin-induced thrombocytopenia; other congenital or acquired thrombophilia | Strong individual driver toward elevated-risk category and anticoagulant-grade prophylaxis |
| **5 points** | Elective major lower-extremity arthroplasty; hip, pelvis, or leg fracture; acute stroke (<1 month); multiple trauma (<1 month); acute spinal cord injury/paralysis (<1 month) | Single factor sufficient to place the patient in the "high risk" (≥5) tier by itself |

**Total score → risk category → prophylaxis** (from the Quick Reference table):

| Total points | Risk category | Approx. VTE risk (no ppx) | Recommended prophylaxis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Very low | ~0.5% | Early ambulation only, no mechanical/pharmacologic |
| 1–2 | Low | ~1.5% | Mechanical prophylaxis (IPC) alone |
| 3–4 | Moderate | ~3% | Pharmacologic prophylaxis (LMWH) ± IPC |
| ≥5 | High | 6%–≥60% in unprotected series | Pharmacologic **+** mechanical combined; consider extended-duration ppx |

## Pearls

- Elective major lower-extremity arthroplasty and hip/pelvis/femur fracture are each independently worth **5 points** — essentially every arthroplasty and hip-fracture patient defaults into the "high risk" tier by virtue of the procedure alone, which is why guideline-based pharmacologic prophylaxis is near-universal in these populations rather than optional.
- Because the score is additive, in elective adult reconstruction essentially all patients already present in the ≥5-point "high risk" band. Caprini's chief clinical utility in arthroplasty is therefore *refining agent selection* (aspirin-eligible "standard risk" vs LMWH/DOAC-mandatory "elevated risk" per additional VTE-specific factors — prior VTE, active cancer, thrombophilia, morbid obesity, poor mobility) rather than deciding whether to give prophylaxis at all.
- Guideline landscape remains genuinely split on whether aspirin is appropriate first-line chemoprophylaxis even within the "high risk" Caprini tier — a 2024 analysis of clinical practice guidelines found current recommendations conflicting, so Caprini risk category alone does not resolve agent choice; additional VTE-specific factors still need to be weighed.
- Mechanical prophylaxis (IPC) should be used in essentially all arthroplasty and hip fracture patients regardless of pharmacologic agent, and as monotherapy when pharmacologic prophylaxis is contraindicated (active bleeding, severe thrombocytopenia, recent intracranial hemorrhage).
