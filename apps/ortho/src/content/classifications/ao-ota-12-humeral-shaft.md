## Overview

AO/OTA Chapter 12 classifies humeral shaft fractures by fracture pattern complexity — simple, wedge, or complex/comminuted. It is used chiefly to predict whether functional bracing will succeed and, when surgery is chosen, whether plating or nailing better controls length and rotation.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **12-A** (simple) | Spiral, oblique, or transverse fracture, single fracture plane | Excellent functional bracing candidate; if surgery chosen, both nailing and plating give reliable fixation |
| **12-B** (wedge) | A butterfly/wedge fragment present, but after reduction the main fragments still have cortical contact | Bracing still viable if alignment acceptable; if unstable, plating usually preferred to preserve length/rotation via direct visualization |
| **12-C** (complex/comminuted) | Segmental or comminuted, no reconstructable cortical contact between main fragments | Higher failure rate with bracing (shortening/malunion); operative fixation (plate typically preferred for length/rotation control, nail with adequate proximal/distal locking as alternative) |

## Pearls

- The A/B/C tier is essentially a bracing-suitability score: 12-A fractures are the best functional-bracing candidates (Sarmiento series union rates 90–98% for isolated closed shaft fractures), while 12-C patterns have the highest bracing failure rate and are steered toward operative fixation.
- For unstable 12-C patterns needing surgery, plating is typically favored for direct length/rotation control, though nailing with adequate locking is a reasonable alternative — no single implant is unequivocally superior across the literature.
- Segmental/comminuted (12-C) patterns and very proximal or very distal fractures both undermine the hydraulic compression mechanism that functional bracing relies on, independent of formal AO/OTA subtype nuance.
