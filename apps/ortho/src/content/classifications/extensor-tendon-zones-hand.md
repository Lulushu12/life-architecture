## Overview

The extensor tendon zones divide the extensor mechanism from the fingertip to the distal forearm into eight anatomic segments (I-VIII), each with a characteristic injury pattern, repair approach, and splinting protocol. The extensor tendons are thinner, flatter, and more superficial than flexors, with lower tensile strength but a broader repair surface.

## Types

| Zone | Location | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | DIP joint | Mallet injury pattern; usually splint (see mallet finger criteria) unless large/subluxed fragment |
| II | Middle phalanx | Direct suture repair; splint DIP extension |
| III | PIP joint (central slip) | Boutonnière risk if missed; splint PIP extension (DIP free) 4-6 weeks even for closed/partial injuries |
| IV | Proximal phalanx | Tendon broader/flatter here; repair + short-arc/relative motion protocol |
| V | MCP joint | Sagittal band injury (boxing/fight-bite risk — presume joint violation with any wound here) |
| VI | Dorsum of hand, metacarpals | Multiple tendon lacerations common (junctura tendinum can mask complete laceration — test each finger independently) |
| VII | Wrist (extensor retinaculum) | Repair retinaculum partially/with pulley reconstruction to prevent bowstringing; nearby sensory branches at risk |
| VIII | Distal forearm, musculotendinous junction | Often combined with muscle injury; more forgiving of imperfect repair strength given surrounding muscle bulk |

## Pearls

- Zone I (mallet-region) closed injuries are managed by splinting per standard criteria; open, large-fragment, or subluxed injuries are fixed with extension block pinning, hook plate, or screw fixation depending on fragment size.
- Zone III (central slip) is the classic "missed injury" zone — even closed or partial injuries are splinted in PIP extension (DIP free, to encourage DIP flexion and prevent lateral band migration) for 4-6 weeks, because an undertreated central slip injury progresses to a Boutonnière deformity.
- Zone V injuries over the MCP joint should be presumed to represent a "fight bite" (joint capsule/extensor tendon violation from a tooth) until proven otherwise; these require formal OR exploration and irrigation, not bedside closure, and the wound is left open/loosely approximated rather than closed primarily.
- Zone VI lacerations can be masked by the junctura tendinum, which allows partial extensor function to persist despite a complete laceration of the injured tendon — each finger must be tested independently in extension, not just the composite hand.
- Zone VII repair should include partial retinacular repair or pulley reconstruction; failure to protect against bowstringing here is a technical pitfall, and nearby sensory branches are at risk during exposure.
- Zone VIII injuries are more forgiving of imperfect tendon repair strength because the surrounding muscle bulk shares load, unlike the more distal zones.
- Zones IV-VII increasingly favor relative motion or short-arc motion splinting protocols over rigid full immobilization, allowing earlier controlled motion to reduce adhesion while still protecting the repair.
- Complications across zones include extension lag (common, variable severity), Boutonnière deformity (missed/undertreated Zone III injury), swan-neck deformity (chronic Zone I mallet with compensatory PIP hyperextension), adhesions/stiffness, and skin breakdown from prolonged splinting.
