## Overview

The Nurick grade is an older, gait-centric grading system for cervical myelopathy. It is simpler and less granular than the mJOA score but remains widely reported for historical comparison and quick bedside communication of functional severity.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Signs/symptoms of root involvement only, no cord disease | Radiculopathy pathway, not myelopathy |
| **1** | Signs of cord involvement, normal gait | Early myelopathy — surgical candidate per the DCM pathway if confirmed |
| **2** | Slight gait difficulty, does not interfere with employment | Still functional — surgery reduces progression risk |
| **3** | Gait difficulty prevents full-time employment/activities of daily living, but does not require walking assistance | Moderate — clear surgical indication |
| **4** | Able to walk only with assistance (frame, cane, or another person) | Severe — surgical indication; expect incomplete recovery |
| **5** | Chair-bound or bedridden | End-stage — surgery may halt progression; functional recovery limited |

## Pearls

- Nurick correlates roughly with the mJOA lower-extremity motor subscore but does not capture hand function or sphincter involvement — always report both mJOA and Nurick when available for a complete severity picture.
- Because it is gait-only, Nurick can understate severity in patients whose predominant deficit is hand dexterity or sphincter dysfunction rather than gait.
- Useful for quick bedside/handoff communication and for comparison with older surgical literature that predates routine mJOA reporting.
