## Overview

The LaPrade classification types meniscal root tears by pattern and location relative to the bony root attachment. It is applied whenever a root tear is suspected or confirmed on MRI (ghost sign, coronal extrusion >3 mm, vertical linear defect near the root) or seen at arthroscopy, and it drives both the decision to repair and the technique chosen.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | Partial stable root tear | Non-operative or observation; usually does not require repair |
| Type 2 | Complete radial tear within 9 mm of root attachment (2A: 0–3 mm, 2B: 3–6 mm, 2C: 6–9 mm) | Most common; primary indication for transtibial pull-out or suture-anchor repair |
| Type 3 | Bucket-handle tear with complete root detachment | Repair the bucket-handle component and the root simultaneously |
| Type 4 | Complex oblique/longitudinal tear extending into the root | Repair if tissue quality allows; often technically demanding |
| Type 5 | Avulsion fracture of the root attachment (bony root avulsion) | Suture or screw fixation of the bony fragment, generally excellent healing |

## Pearls

- Repair candidacy is not determined by LaPrade type alone. Modifiers layered on top of type (per current evidence) are: Kellgren-Lawrence grade ≤2, meniscal extrusion ≤3 mm, mechanical axis within 5° of neutral, age generally <60–65 (relative), and absence of diffuse cartilage loss in the affected compartment.
- Outside those parameters — chronic tear, Kellgren-Lawrence ≥3, extrusion >3 mm, varus >5° — repair outcomes approach those of partial meniscectomy/non-operative care, and an arthroplasty-pathway discussion (or concurrent high tibial osteotomy) is more appropriate than isolated repair.
- A complete root tear is functionally equivalent to a total meniscectomy in contact pressure and joint kinematics — root disruption abolishes hoop-stress conversion even though the meniscal body itself is structurally intact.
- Diagnosis relies on MRI: the "ghost sign" (absence of meniscal tissue on sagittal images through the normal root location), a vertical linear defect near the root on coronal images, and extrusion >3 mm on coronal MRI (measured from the tibial plateau margin), which correlates with root pathology and predicts poorer repair outcomes.
- Isolated lateral posterior root tears are sometimes better tolerated than medial ones because the lateral meniscofemoral ligaments (Humphry/Wrisberg) can partially substitute mechanically.
- Root repair reliably improves pain and function and slows — but does not fully arrest — progression of joint-space narrowing; extrusion often persists even after confirmed root healing and does not by itself indicate failure.
- Standing long-leg alignment films are mandatory before root repair in any patient with clinical or radiographic malalignment — uncorrected varus >5° predicts failure of isolated repair.
