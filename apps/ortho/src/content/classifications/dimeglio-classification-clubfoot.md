## Overview

The Dimeglio classification grades congenital clubfoot (CTEV) severity based on the reducibility of its four components, used at initial presentation to predict the difficulty and length of the Ponseti casting course.

## Types

Four reducibility parameters, each graded 0–4 (0 = fully correctable/hypercorrectable, 4 = fixed/non-reducible): equinus (sagittal plane), varus (frontal plane), derotation around the talar head (horizontal plane), and adduction of the forefoot (horizontal plane) — subtotal 0–16. Four additional 0–1 points are added for posterior crease, medial crease, cavus, and poor muscle condition — total 0–20.

| Grade | Score | Descriptor | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | <5 | Soft, benign (postural-leaning) foot | Fewer casts, rapid correction, high likelihood of correction without tenotomy |
| II | 5–9 | Soft foot, moderate | Standard Ponseti course, tenotomy commonly still required |
| III | 10–14 | Stiff foot, severe | More casts, tenotomy almost always required, closer relapse surveillance |
| IV | 15–20 | Very stiff, severe (often syndromic/teratologic) | Longest casting course, highest relapse/re-intervention rate, consider modified technique |

## Pearls

- Dimeglio ≥15 (Grade IV) flags likely syndromic/teratologic clubfoot — these feet respond less predictably to standard Ponseti casting and may need a modified technique.
- The four core reducibility parameters are graded independently before the four minor (0–1) points are added, so the subtotal (0–16) versus the full total (0–20) should not be conflated when comparing scores.
- Used together with the Pirani score, Dimeglio grade at presentation helps set expectations with families about the likely number of casts and tenotomy probability.
