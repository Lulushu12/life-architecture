## Overview

The Ahlbäck classification (1968) was described specifically to grade **compartment-specific bone loss and subluxation** in knee osteoarthritis, going beyond simple joint-space narrowing. It remains the reference staging scale in the HTO/UKA literature (particularly Scandinavian and European series) for judging whether a compartment's bone stock and alignment are still amenable to joint-preserving surgery.

## Types

| Stage | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Joint space narrowing (<50% of normal width, roughly <3 mm) | Ideal candidate for HTO or UKA; minimal bone loss to accommodate |
| **II** | Joint space obliteration (bone-on-bone contact), no bone attrition | Still favorable HTO/UKA candidate |
| **III** | Minor bone attrition (0–5 mm) | HTO/UKA feasible; expect slightly more complex balancing; UKA component sizing/augmentation rarely needed |
| **IV** | Moderate bone attrition (5–10 mm) | HTO/UKA more technically demanding; consider augments for UKA; counsel on higher revision risk vs TKA |
| **V** | Major bone attrition (>10 mm) with subluxation of the tibia on the femur, often with contralateral compartment/ligament involvement | Usually exceeds HTO/UKA envelope — **TKA** preferred due to bone loss, subluxation, and frequent multicompartmental extension |

## Pearls

- Stage is the primary determinant of technical difficulty for joint-sparing surgery, not just eligibility: stages I–II are straightforward HTO/UKA candidates, stages III–IV are feasible but progressively more technically demanding (consider UKA augments), and stage V usually exceeds the HTO/UKA envelope entirely.
- Ahlbäck stage should be interpreted alongside the "spared" compartment's status — full-thickness cartilage loss found on MRI/arthroscopy in the compartment meant to be preserved converts an HTO/UKA candidate to TKA regardless of the operated compartment's Ahlbäck stage.
- A 2025 study specifically examined CPAK phenotype's effect on outcomes after HTO, extending coronal-phenotype classification's use into osteotomy planning alongside Ahlbäck staging of bone loss.
- Because Ahlbäck grades attrition/subluxation rather than pure JSN, it correlates with — but is not interchangeable with — Kellgren-Lawrence grade; a knee can be KL3 with limited Ahlbäck attrition (favorable for HTO/UKA) or KL3 with more advanced attrition (less favorable), so both scales inform surgical candidacy together.
