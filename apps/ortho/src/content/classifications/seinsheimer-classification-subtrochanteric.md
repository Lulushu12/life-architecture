## Overview

The Seinsheimer classification grades subtrochanteric femur fractures by fragment number and pattern, with particular attention to which fragment retains the lesser trochanter — a key rotational-control reference during reduction. It is used clinically alongside the AO/OTA 32 (femoral shaft) designation and "subtrochanteric zone" terminology to plan reduction strategy for these poorly-vascularized, high-stress fractures.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Non-displaced (<2 mm) or minimally displaced, any pattern | Rare; may be treated with standard CMN, low complexity |
| **II** | Two-part fracture: IIA transverse; IIB spiral, lesser trochanter attached to proximal fragment; IIC spiral, lesser trochanter attached to distal fragment | Reduction strategy depends on which fragment retains the lesser trochanter (rotational control reference); CMN standard |
| **III** | Three-part: IIIA with a posteromedial (calcar) fragment including lesser trochanter; IIIB with a proximal lateral cortical (butterfly) fragment | Comminution destabilizes medial buttress; meticulous indirect reduction (avoid stripping the fragment), CMN with attention to length/rotation/alignment restoration rather than perfect anatomic fragment reduction |
| **IV** | Comminuted, four or more fragments | High complexity; long CMN, may need cerclage cable/wire assistance for reduction (biology-preserving indirect technique), or plate augmentation |
| **V** | Subtrochanteric-intertrochanteric fracture combination (extension into the greater trochanter/piriformis fossa) | Long cephalomedullary nail essential to control both the trochanteric and subtrochanteric components as a single construct |

## Pearls

- The subtrochanteric region is the highest-stress zone in the femur (medial cortex under massive compression, lateral cortex under tension), largely cortical with comparatively poor blood supply — the dominant technical challenge across all Seinsheimer types is achieving and holding reduction against deforming muscular forces (iliopsoas flexion/external rotation of the proximal fragment, abductor abduction, adductor-driven varus/shortening of the distal fragment), not fixation strength per se.
- Restore anatomic or near-anatomic alignment: varus/valgus and rotational malalignment >10–15° predispose to nonunion and abductor limp.
- Always screen for and image the contralateral femur when the pattern (transverse line, lateral cortical "beak," minimal comminution) or history (bisphosphonate/denosumab >3–5 years, prodromal thigh pain) suggests an atypical femur fracture (AFF) — AFFs carry a materially higher nonunion/delayed-union rate than typical subtrochanteric fractures and are managed with the same nailing principle but may warrant longer nails, a teriparatide adjunct, and a drug-holiday discussion.
- Reported nonunion/delayed union runs 10–20% in comminuted or malreduced subtrochanteric fractures, higher than intertrochanteric fractures owing to the cortical, lower-vascularity bone and greater deforming forces.
