## Overview

The Luo three-column classification (Luo et al., 2010) maps fracture lines on an axial CT slice at the level of the tibial spines onto lateral, medial, and posterior columns. It directly addresses the Schatzker classification's blind spot for posterior/posteromedial shear fragments and is now the dominant system for surgical approach planning in complex bicondylar tibial plateau fractures.

## Types

| Column | Definition (axial CT, apex of tibial spine as center) | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Lateral column | Anterolateral quadrant fracture line | Lateral locking plate typically sufficient alone |
| Medial column | Posteromedial/anteromedial quadrant | Requires independent medial-side fixation (buttress) — a lateral plate alone will not control this column |
| Posterior column | Posterior quadrant, often posteromedial or posterolateral shear fragment | Frequently missed on AP/lateral radiographs and standard anterior approaches; needs posteromedial or posterolateral approach ± separate plate — the classic driver of Schatzker/AO under-recognition of posterior shear fragments |
| Three-column (any 2+ columns) | Combined injury | Dictates dual or triple plating strategy and choice of approach combination (e.g., anterolateral + posteromedial) — CT-based, not reliably assessed on plain films |

## Pearls

- Now the dominant classification for surgical approach planning in complex bicondylar fractures because it directly predicts which combination of approaches (anterolateral, posteromedial, posterolateral) and how many plates are needed.
- A posterior column fragment identified only on CT and missed on the Schatzker read is the single most common cause of failed lateral-only fixation of a "Schatzker V/VI" fracture — this is the core clinical justification for routine CT-based column mapping in bicondylar patterns.
- Best obtained after spanning external fixation (not before) in high-energy staged cases: the ligamentotaxis reduction improves fragment visualization and reduces artifact from gross displacement, which in turn improves column-mapping accuracy.
- Column analysis directly drives approach selection: isolated lateral column injury → anterolateral approach with lateral locking plate; medial column component → separate posteromedial approach and buttress plate; posterior column shear fragment → posteromedial or posterolateral approach (occasionally direct posterior, prone).
