## Overview

The Steinberg (University of Pennsylvania) classification is a quantitative staging system for osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) that adds a percentage-of-head-involvement (A/B/C) substage to each stage. It is the most useful system for research reporting and precise lesion-size documentation, and its A/B/C substaging is what most directly drives joint-preservation decision-making at the individual-lesion level.

## Types

| Stage | Findings | % head involvement subclassification |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Normal/nondiagnostic radiograph, bone scan, MRI | — |
| **I** | Normal radiograph, abnormal bone scan and/or MRI | A <15%, B 15–30%, C >30% of head involved |
| **II** | Cystic/sclerotic changes on radiograph | A <15%, B 15–30%, C >30% |
| **III** | Subchondral collapse (crescent sign) without flattening | A <15%, B 15–30%, C >30% of articular surface |
| **IV** | Flattening of femoral head | A <15% surface/<2 mm depression, B 15–30%/2–4 mm, C >30%/>4 mm |
| **V** | Joint-space narrowing and/or acetabular changes | A/B/C mild/moderate/severe |
| **VI** | Advanced degenerative changes | — |

## Pearls

- The A/B/C quantitative substaging at each stage — not the roman-numeral stage alone — is what drives joint-preservation decision-making: lesions <15% (A) rarely progress to collapse and can often be observed or treated with core decompression alone, while lesions >30% (C), especially with lateral/weight-bearing involvement, carry high collapse risk and are relatively poor candidates for isolated core decompression, favoring vascularized grafting or earlier arthroplasty counseling.
- Steinberg's lesion-size stratification is the system referenced when the monograph describes core decompression outcomes by lesion size (better in Steinberg <30%, medial/central lesions; worse in large lateral lesions).
- Because Steinberg requires percentage-of-head calculation, it is more labor-intensive to apply in routine clinic than ARCO or Ficat-Arlet and is used most consistently in research/trial reporting rather than everyday staging.
