## Overview

Described by Fujiyoshi et al., the K-line is drawn on a lateral cervical radiograph or midsagittal CT/MRI connecting the midpoints of the spinal canal at C2 and C7. It is used specifically to determine whether posterior decompression alone (laminoplasty) can achieve adequate indirect decompression of an anteriorly-based OPLL mass.

## Types

| K-line status | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **K-line positive** | The OPLL mass does not cross (stays posterior to/does not exceed) the K-line | Laminoplasty (posterior decompression relying on cord drift-back) is a viable option — the cord can adequately migrate away from the ossified mass after canal expansion |
| **K-line negative** | The OPLL mass crosses anterior to the K-line (bulk of ossification too large/too far posterior) | Laminoplasty alone likely inadequate — favor anterior decompression (if feasible) or laminectomy with instrumented fusion ± anterior decompression; posterior-only surgery in K-line-negative patients has historically shown inferior neurological recovery |

## Pearls

- K-line status alone has shown inconsistent predictive power in some multicenter cohorts once combined with **canal-occupying ratio (COR)**: K-line negative but low-COR patients may still do reasonably with laminoplasty, while K-line negative + high COR (≥60%) does poorly with posterior-only surgery.
- **Dynamic K-line** (assessed in flexion, not just neutral) further refines risk — a K-line that becomes negative in flexion identifies patients who appear favorable on neutral imaging but are not.
- Practical takeaway: use K-line as one input alongside canal-occupying ratio, cervical alignment (lordosis vs. kyphosis), and OPLL morphologic type, not as a stand-alone algorithm.
- Continuous and mixed OPLL morphologic types carry the highest canal-occupying ratios and the highest rates of dural ossification, reinforcing why K-line-positive continuous/mixed disease is generally steered toward posterior indirect decompression rather than anterior resection (to avoid anterior dural adhesion/CSF leak risk).
