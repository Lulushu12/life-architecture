## Overview

The Vancouver classification (extended conceptually as the Unified Classification System, UCS, across all periprosthetic fractures around any implant) grades periprosthetic femur fractures around a hip stem by fracture location relative to the stem, stem stability, and proximal bone stock. It is the primary determinant of ORIF versus revision arthroplasty and should be applied to every fracture occurring around or near a hip stem, combining full-length femur imaging with intraoperative stem stability testing when preoperative imaging is equivocal.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| A (Ag / Al) | Trochanteric (greater/lesser) fracture | Non-op if stem stable and fragment <2 cm displacement; ORIF (claw plate/cerclage) if abductor-compromising displacement |
| B1 | Around/just distal to stem, **stem well-fixed** | ORIF (locking plate ± cerclage ± strut allograft); preserve fixed stem |
| B2 | Around/just distal to stem, **stem loose**, adequate proximal bone stock | Revision to a **long, distally fixed tapered-fluted (cementless) or cemented stem** bypassing fracture ≥2 cortical diameters; plate augmentation as needed |
| B3 | Around/distal to stem, **stem loose + poor proximal bone stock** | Revision with **distal fixation + proximal reconstruction** — impaction grafting, proximal femoral allograft/allograft-prosthetic composite, or proximal femoral replacement (megaprosthesis) in elderly/low demand |
| C | Well distal to stem tip, implant uninvolved | ORIF as for a native periprosthetic diaphyseal fracture (plate ± IM nail if compatible), stem left alone |

## Pearls

- The critical branch point is intraoperative/radiographic **stem stability**, not fracture pattern alone — a B1 fracture treated as B2 (revised unnecessarily) adds morbidity, while a B2 treated as B1 (ORIF on a loose stem) reliably fails.
- Intraoperative confirmation of fixation (manual stress testing of the stem after exposure) is the gold standard when preoperative imaging is equivocal.
- Compare current films to the earliest available post-index-arthroplasty series — any interval subsidence, new circumferential radiolucent line, or cement mantle fracture predating the acute injury reclassifies an apparent B1 pattern as B2.
- A recent comparative analysis (Danderyd Hospital cohort, 157 B2 fractures) found ORIF around cemented polished tapered stems can achieve acceptable results in carefully selected cases where the cement mantle and stem remain grossly intact despite radiolucency — an evolving, more nuanced application of the B1/B2 boundary rather than a rigid binary rule, but stem revision remains the default for a truly loose stem.
- The UCS extends the same location + stability + bone stock logic across all periprosthetic fractures around any implant (hip, knee, shoulder), making the A/B1/B2/B3/C framework transferable when counseling on periprosthetic fractures outside the hip.
- Vancouver C fractures carry hip-fracture-equivalent 1-year mortality risk despite the implant being uninvolved and should trigger the same orthogeriatric co-management urgency as native hip fractures.
