## Overview

The OTA Open Fracture Classification (OTA-OFC) grades five components of an open fracture — skin, muscle, arterial injury, contamination, and bone loss — separately, each on a 1–3 scale. It is more granular than the aggregate Gustilo-Anderson grade and has better inter-observer reliability, which is why it is increasingly used in registries and trials to stratify infection and amputation risk and to guide reconstructive planning.

## Types

| Component | Grade 1 | Grade 2 | Grade 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Skin** | Can be closed | Cannot be closed primarily | Extensive degloving |
| **Muscle** | No obvious non-viable muscle | Some loss, muscle function preserved | Dead muscle, loss of function, missing muscle groups |
| **Arterial injury** | None | Injury without ischemia | Injury with distal ischemia |
| **Contamination** | None/minimal | Surface contamination easily removed | High-energy contamination (soil, organic, foreign body) deeply embedded, or ongoing exposure (farm, sewage, aquatic) |
| **Bone loss** | None | Bone missing/devascularized but reconstructable length/rotation retainable | Segmental bone loss precluding standard reconstruction |

Each component is scored independently rather than combined into a single overall grade, and the pattern of component scores — not a summed total — is what drives management: a fracture with severe arterial and bone-loss components but only modest skin injury should still drive amputation counseling even if the skin wound alone looks like a Gustilo IIIA.

## Pearls

- OTA-OFC's individual-component scoring predicts infection and amputation risk more granularly than the single aggregate Gustilo-Anderson grade — do not collapse it back into a Gustilo-equivalent grade when the components disagree.
- Use OTA-OFC alongside, not instead of, Gustilo-Anderson: Gustilo-Anderson remains the more familiar shorthand for antibiotic selection and general communication, while OTA-OFC adds precision for reconstructive planning and outcome stratification in trials/registries.
- Like Gustilo-Anderson, OTA-OFC is best assessed after formal surgical debridement, when the true extent of each component (especially muscle viability and bone loss) is visible.
