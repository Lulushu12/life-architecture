## Overview

A separate ICRS arthroscopic staging system (distinct from the ICRS chondral-lesion grade) categorizes osteochondritis dissecans (OCD) lesions by fragment stability, Grade I–IV. It is used arthroscopically alongside MRI instability criteria to decide between drilling, fragment fixation, and excision with cartilage restoration.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Stable, intact overlying cartilage | If failing 3–6 months of non-operative care: transarticular or retroarticular drilling to stimulate healing without disturbing the intact surface |
| II | Cartilage breach with underlying fracture/fluid | Drilling if lesion remains stable; reassess for progression to instability |
| III | Partially detached fragment | Fragment fixation (bioabsorbable pins/screws or compression screws) if the fragment is viable, after debriding the fibrous interface and micro-drilling/bone-grafting the crater |
| IV | Fully detached/loose body | If fragment non-viable or a full-thickness defect remains after excision: treat as a focal chondral/osteochondral defect (OAT for smaller lesions, OCA for larger or bone-deficient lesions, ACI/MACI for chondral-only defects with adequate bone) |

## Pearls

- This staging is used alongside, not instead of, MRI instability criteria: a high-signal (fluid) line ≥5 mm deep to the fragment on T2, a peripheral high-signal rim, multiple breaches/cysts beneath the fragment, and a focal osteochondral defect with fluid extending through the cartilage surface all indicate instability.
- Do not rely on radiographs alone to stage stability — MRI is required for surgical decision-making; notch/tunnel view radiographs best profile the classic posterolateral medial femoral condyle location but do not substitute for MRI staging.
- Must be distinguished from an incidental "ossification variant" in skeletally immature patients — done by lesion location, symmetry (variants are often bilateral/symmetric), and absence of MRI instability criteria.
- Fixation is preferred over excision for unstable-but-viable fragments whenever possible, since it preserves native hyaline cartilage rather than converting the case to a salvage cartilage-restoration procedure.
- Hardware prominence/synovitis requiring removal is the most common reason for reoperation after fixation, even when the fragment goes on to union.
- Healing rates in skeletally immature, stable OCD treated non-operatively are favorable, but decline sharply in skeletally mature patients — a much lower threshold for surgery is used in adults.
