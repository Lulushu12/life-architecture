## Overview

The Blazina classification stages patellar tendinopathy ("jumper's knee") by the pattern of pain relative to activity, from pain only after sport (Stage I) through complete tendon rupture (Stage IV). It is used clinically to gauge severity and functional impact and to guide intensity/duration of a loading-based rehabilitation program.

## Types

| Stage | Clinical description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Pain only after activity/sport, no functional impairment | Relative rest, load monitoring, begin isometric/eccentric-isotonic program; usually continues sport with modification |
| **II** | Pain during and after activity, but still able to perform at reasonable level | Structured heavy slow resistance (HSR)/eccentric loading program (12 weeks minimum), address training load errors |
| **III** | Pain during and after activity, with increasing difficulty performing at desired level (prolonged pain, some intra-activity limitation) | Intensify loading program, consider adjuncts (shockwave), extend timeline 3–6 months; surgery only if refractory ≥6 months |
| **IV** | Complete tendon rupture | Surgical repair — this stage exits the tendinopathy pathway into acute rupture management |

## Pearls

- Stage IV is not a "worse tendinopathy" but a distinct clinical entity (complete rupture) requiring surgical repair rather than continued loading — recognizing the transition is essential.
- Surgical debridement/tendon scraping for Stage II–III disease is reserved for patients who fail ≥6 months of a comprehensive, supervised loading program; it is not an early option.
- Corticosteroid injection into or around the patellar tendon is not recommended at any stage — it is associated with increased risk of subsequent tendon rupture (i.e., progression toward Stage IV).
- PRP and extracorporeal shockwave therapy are adjunctive/investigational options considered for refractory Stage II–III disease after a failed initial loading program, not first-line treatment.
- A minimum of 12 weeks of structured, supervised loading should elapse before considering a conservative trial "failed"; many athletes need 3–6 months for full return to high-level jumping sport at Stage II–III.
