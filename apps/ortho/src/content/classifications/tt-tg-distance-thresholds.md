## Overview

TT-TG distance is measured on axial CT or MRI as the distance between a line through the deepest point of the trochlear groove and a line through the center of the tibial tubercle, both perpendicular to the posterior condylar axis. It quantifies the lateral vector the extensor mechanism exerts on the patella and is the classic anchor point for deciding whether to add a tibial tubercle osteotomy (TTO) to MPFL reconstruction in patellar instability surgery.

## Types

| TT-TG | Interpretation | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| <15 mm | Normal | Not a target for bony correction |
| 15–20 mm | Grey zone | Correlate with other risk factors (dysplasia grade, alta, rotational malalignment); isolated MPFL-R often sufficient |
| **>20 mm** | Classic pathologic threshold (Dejour) | Indication to add tibial tubercle osteotomy (medialization ± distalization) to MPFL-R |

## Pearls

- Modality matters: MRI (cartilage-based) measurements typically run 3–5 mm lower than CT (bone-based) measurements — use a consistent modality when trending a patient over time.
- The 20 mm cutoff is increasingly recognized as imperfect, particularly in high-grade trochlear dysplasia, where the trochlear groove reference point is itself abnormal or absent, inflating or invalidating the measurement.
- Excess TT-TG driven by **tibial internal rotation** (rather than true tubercle lateralization) predicts worse outcomes from isolated TTO and may be better addressed by derotational osteotomy — do not default to TTO for any TT-TG >20 mm without assessing rotational profile.
- Alternative indices (TT-PCL, TT–posterior intercondylar eminence) are proposed as more robust in dysplastic trochleae, but TT-TG >20 mm remains the operative anchor point in most European and US practice.
- CT is preferred for precise TT-TG measurement and rotational profiling (femoral anteversion, tibial torsion) when planning a derotational osteotomy, though MRI is increasingly used to avoid radiation.
