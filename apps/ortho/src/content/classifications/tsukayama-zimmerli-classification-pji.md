## Overview

The Tsukayama/Zimmerli timing classification stages periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) by onset relative to the index arthroplasty. It is the operative decision-driver for the entire treatment algorithm: it determines whether implant retention (DAIR) is biologically plausible (biofilm still immature) or whether component removal is mandatory (biofilm mature). Zimmerli's clinical classification uses essentially the same timing tiers and is used interchangeably with Tsukayama in most European literature.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Positive intraoperative culture** | ≥2 unexpected positive cultures at what was presumed aseptic revision surgery; typically low-virulence organisms (coagulase-negative staph, Cutibacterium) | No re-operation needed if implant already well-fixed and patient well; treat with a full antibiotic course (targeted, typically 3 months) |
| **II — Early postoperative infection** | Onset <4 weeks (some series <1 month) after index arthroplasty; virulent perioperative contaminants (S. aureus, Gram-negatives, polymicrobial) | DAIR with exchange of all modular components — biofilm still immature, best salvage rates |
| **III — Acute hematogenous infection** | Sudden onset in a previously well-functioning prosthesis from a remote bacteremic source, symptom duration <3 weeks at presentation; S. aureus and streptococci most common | DAIR with modular exchange if implant stable and symptom duration short; source control of the primary bacteremic focus is mandatory |
| **IV — Chronic infection** | Onset/symptoms >4 weeks (postoperative) or >3 weeks (hematogenous), indolent course, often sinus tract or radiographic loosening; any organism, often biofilm-adapted low-virulence | Implant removal required — one-stage or two-stage exchange; DAIR contraindicated (unacceptably high failure) |

## Pearls

- The biological basis for the ~3–4 week acute/chronic cutoffs used clinically is biofilm maturation: bacteria embed in a self-produced extracellular matrix that reduces antibiotic penetration and shields organisms from host defenses over roughly 3–4 weeks in most models. Tsukayama, Zimmerli, and modern DAIR-eligibility criteria all converge near this window.
- Symptom duration >3–4 weeks in an otherwise "acute" presentation should raise suspicion that the biofilm is already mature — DAIR success drops sharply, and staged exchange should be favored even if the timing category nominally looks acute.
- An unstable or loose implant, or exposed cement mantle, contraindicates DAIR irrespective of timing classification.
- Type I (incidental positive cultures at presumed-aseptic revision) is managed with prolonged antibiotics alone, not re-operation, if the patient is well and the implant is already well-fixed.
