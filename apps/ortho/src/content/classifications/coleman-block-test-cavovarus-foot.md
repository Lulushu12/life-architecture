## Overview

A clinical test used to determine whether hindfoot varus in a cavovarus foot is **flexible and forefoot-driven** versus a **fixed/rigid hindfoot deformity**, which determines whether hindfoot correction is required at surgery. A block (typically 2.5–4 cm) is placed under the lateral foot from the heel to just proximal to the first metatarsal head, allowing the plantarflexed first ray to hang freely off the medial edge; the examiner then observes whether the heel realigns.

## Types

| Result | Interpretation | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Hindfoot corrects to neutral or valgus with block | **Flexible hindfoot** — cavus is forefoot-driven (plantarflexed first ray from peroneus longus overpull) | Forefoot-only correction: first ray dorsiflexion osteotomy, peroneus longus-to-brevis transfer, plantar fascia release. No calcaneal osteotomy needed |
| Hindfoot remains varus with block | **Fixed/rigid hindfoot deformity**, independent of the forefoot | Requires hindfoot correction: lateralizing or Dwyer calcaneal osteotomy ± tendon transfers; triple arthrodesis if arthritic |

## Pearls

- Unreliable in severe/rigid deformity and requires an examiner experienced in observing subtle correction.
- Does not substitute for weightbearing CT (WBCT)–based 3D assessment, which is increasingly used pre-operatively alongside the block test.
- Roughly 20–30% of adult cavovarus feet have an identifiable neurologic cause (Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease, other hereditary motor-sensory neuropathies, spinal dysraphism, tethered cord, prior compartment syndrome/peroneal nerve injury) — the Coleman block result determines the bony correction, but neurologic screening (EMG/nerve conduction studies, spine/neuraxis MRI) is a separate, mandatory step in any adult with new or asymmetric cavovarus.
- Rapidly progressive rigid cavovarus in an adult, especially asymmetric or with a normal contralateral foot, should raise suspicion for a spinal cord/peripheral nerve lesion before treating as idiopathic.
