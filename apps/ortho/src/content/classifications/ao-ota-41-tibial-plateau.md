## Overview

AO/OTA 41 is the universal alphanumeric coding for proximal tibia/fibula (plateau) fractures, used in registries, billing, and international communication. It divides fractures into extra-articular (A), partial articular (B), and complete articular (C) tiers that correlate broadly with the Schatzker system and with worsening prognosis as comminution increases.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 41-A | Metaphyseal, extra-articular fracture | Extra-articular pattern, managed outside the articular-reduction algorithm that governs B/C injuries |
| 41-B | Partial articular — split, depression, or split-depression of one condyle (≈ Schatzker I–III) | Managed per the equivalent Schatzker type (percutaneous fixation vs. elevation + buttress plate depending on displacement) |
| 41-C | Complete articular — bicondylar articular fracture (≈ Schatzker IV–VI); C3 = extensive articular comminution — worst prognosis | Bicondylar injury — evaluate for staged management per soft-tissue status; C3 carries the worst prognosis of the group |

## Pearls

- Each letter subdivides further (A1–A3, B1–B3, C1–C3) by comminution/fragment number — used for research stratification and prognostication; in daily practice A/B/C plus a plain-language fracture description (comminution, displacement, articular step) drives the operative plan.
- Provides the universal common language for registries, billing, and international communication — the system to default to when writing for a European/AO-trained audience or entering registry data.
- Best interpreted alongside Schatzker (for treatment planning) and the CT-based Luo three-column system (for surgical approach planning in complex 41-C bicondylar patterns) — the AO letters map onto but do not replace those decision frameworks.
