## Overview

The Campanacci classification grades giant cell tumor (GCT) of bone radiographically by margin definition and cortical integrity. It is used to select the extent of intralesional curettage versus resection and to identify lesions that may benefit from neoadjuvant denosumab.

## Types

| Grade | Radiographic features | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Well-marginated lesion with a thin sclerotic rim, cortex intact | Intralesional curettage + adjuvant + cement/graft; lowest recurrence risk |
| II | Well-defined margins but no sclerotic rim; cortex thinned/expanded, may bulge but remains grossly intact | Intralesional curettage + burr + adjuvant + cement; standard first-line even for most large lesions |
| III | Ill-defined margins, cortical destruction, extension into soft tissue | Extended curettage still feasible in many (especially with neoadjuvant denosumab to consolidate a rim and facilitate joint-sparing surgery) vs wide resection + endoprosthesis if joint/neurovascular structures cannot be preserved |

## Pearls

- Campanacci grade does not by itself dictate curettage vs resection as rigidly as it once did — even large Grade II lesions are standardly managed with curettage + adjuvant, and many Grade III lesions remain curettage-eligible with neoadjuvant denosumab.
- Neoadjuvant denosumab to downstage a Campanacci III lesion is a genuine tradeoff, not a free strategy: denosumab induces new bone formation and fibrosis around residual tumor that can make the curettage plane harder to define, and several retrospective series report higher local recurrence after neoadjuvant denosumab + curettage compared with curettage alone in resectable disease. A 2023 review confirms this signal persists and recommends caution when denosumab is used specifically to enable joint-sparing curettage rather than for genuinely unresectable disease.
- Local recurrence after curettage alone is ~25–50%; adding a local adjuvant (phenol, cryotherapy, or argon beam) plus cement reduces this to ~10–15% — the adjuvant and meticulous curettage technique are the dominant determinants of local control, more than cement's exothermic effect alone.
- Wide resection with endoprosthetic or allograft reconstruction is reserved for Grade III lesions with joint destruction not amenable to joint-sparing curettage, or for recurrent disease after failed curettage.
- Sacral and spinal GCT are managed with denosumab ± embolization ± surgery given the morbidity of direct resection, regardless of Campanacci grade.
- Malignant transformation of GCT occurs in ~1–4% of cases, higher after radiotherapy — a rationale for continued surveillance rather than one-time clearance, independent of Campanacci grade at diagnosis.
