## Overview

The Russe classification describes scaphoid fractures by the orientation of the fracture line relative to the long axis of the bone — horizontal oblique, transverse, or vertical oblique — which determines the balance of compressive versus shear forces across the fracture and therefore its inherent stability. It is used alongside the Mayo location-based system to set treatment.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I — Horizontal oblique** | Fracture plane parallel to long axis; compressive forces predominate | Most stable pattern, best cast healing rates |
| **II — Transverse** | Perpendicular to long axis | Moderate stability |
| **III — Vertical oblique** | Fracture plane runs more vertically, shear forces predominate | Least stable, highest displacement/nonunion risk with nonoperative treatment; favor fixation |

## Pearls

- Orientation determines whether the fracture is loaded in compression (favorable — Type I) or shear (unfavorable — Type III); this is why a Type III vertical-oblique waist fracture is treated more aggressively than a Type I horizontal-oblique fracture at the same location.
- Russe orientation and Mayo location are complementary: a displaced or vertical-oblique (Russe III) waist fracture pushes management toward screw fixation even without gross Herbert-Fisher B2 displacement criteria being obviously met.
- Vertical-oblique (Type III) fractures have the highest displacement and nonunion risk with cast treatment alone and should lower the threshold for surgical fixation.
