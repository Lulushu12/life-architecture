## Overview

The Sanders classification grades displaced intra-articular calcaneus fractures on coronal CT through the widest part of the posterior facet, based on the number and location of articular fracture lines. It is the primary system used to plan surgery (approach, fixation vs primary fusion) and to counsel patients on prognosis.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Nondisplaced (any number of fracture lines, <2 mm displacement) | Non-operative regardless of fragment count |
| **II** (IIA/IIB/IIC by location of primary fracture line — lateral/central/medial) | Two-part fracture of the posterior facet | ORIF (typically via sinus tarsi or extensile lateral approach) if displaced/depressed; best functional outcomes among displaced patterns |
| **III** (IIIAB/IIIAC/IIIBC) | Three-part fracture, central depressed fragment between two facet fracture lines | ORIF with careful posterior facet reconstruction; higher complication rate than Type II |
| **IV** | Comminuted, four or more articular fragments | ORIF with high wound/nonunion/post-traumatic arthritis risk, or primary subtalar arthrodesis (increasingly favored, union ~94% in recent series) — shared decision, favor fusion in high comminution, low-demand or older patients |

## Pearls

- Sanders I is always managed non-operatively.
- Sanders IV strongly favors primary subtalar arthrodesis; it is also a reasonable option in older/lower-demand patients with Sanders III to avoid a later secondary fusion, though ORIF still edges out primary arthrodesis on pooled functional scores for Sanders II/III specifically.
- For Sanders II/III, the UK Heel Fracture Trial (HeFT) long-term (60-month) data show no significant difference in Kerr-Atkins functional score between ORIF and non-operative treatment (79.2 vs 76.4), no difference in walking difficulty or shoe-fitting, but significantly more additional surgical procedures in the ORIF group (10 vs 4 patients, p=0.043). Earlier/shorter-term series and meta-analyses favor ORIF for functional scores at ~2 years — the decision should be individualized (occupation, activity demand, soft tissue, comorbidities, patient preference) rather than protocolized.
- Böhler's angle (normal 20–40°) reflects posterior facet height/collapse and Gissane's (crucial) angle (normal 120–145°) reflects the lateral wall/posterior facet relationship — both should be restored at surgery regardless of Sanders type.
- Screen the contralateral calcaneus and the thoracolumbar spine in axial-load mechanisms (fall from height) — spine fracture co-incidence is ~10%, and bilateral calcaneus fractures should specifically prompt a thoracolumbar burst fracture screen.
- Compartment syndrome of the foot occurs in up to 10% of displaced intra-articular calcaneus fractures — measure compartment pressures with clinical suspicion.
