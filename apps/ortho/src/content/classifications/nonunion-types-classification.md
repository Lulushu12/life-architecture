## Overview

Nonunion is failure of a fracture to unite with cessation of the biological healing process — confirmed clinically (motion/pain at the fracture site) and radiographically (no progressive callus over 3 consecutive months, and/or failure to heal by 6–9 months post-injury). The nonunion types classify the radiographic and biological pattern of that failure into hypertrophic, oligotrophic, and atrophic categories, which map directly onto whether the problem is primarily mechanical, mixed, or biological — and therefore onto the treatment strategy.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Hypertrophic** ("elephant's foot" / "horse's hoof") | Abundant callus, flared fracture ends, persistent lucent gap; good biology, inadequate mechanical stability | Rigid fixation alone (revision plating/nailing, exchange nailing, dynamization) — do **not** need bone graft |
| **Oligotrophic** | Minimal callus, fracture ends not resorbed, gap persists; reduced biology plus some mechanical instability, often from displaced/distracted fixation | Improve stability **and** biology — reduce the gap, achieve compression, consider limited grafting |
| **Atrophic** | No callus, rounded/resorbed osteopenic bone ends, sometimes a fibrous non-osseous gap; poor/absent biology (avascular, often after excessive stripping, infection, or NSAID/smoking-impaired healing) | Requires biological augmentation — autologous bone graft (iliac crest, RIA reamings), consider BMP/concentrated bone marrow aspirate, correction of systemic factors, ± vascularized bone if the bed is avascular |

## Pearls

- The type dictates the treatment framework: hypertrophic nonunions have adequate biology and fail because of insufficient mechanical stability — grafting a hypertrophic nonunion is unnecessary; atrophic nonunions have the opposite problem and mechanical revision alone will not fix them.
- The Diamond Concept (Giannoudis et al.) is the operative framework used to simultaneously optimize osteogenic cells, osteoconductive scaffold, growth factors, and mechanical stability (plus vascularity as the underlying platform) once the nonunion type is established.
- **Always exclude infection before committing to a biologic-augmentation strategy** — check ESR/CRP, WBC, and consider aspiration/biopsy with antibiotics held ≥ 2 weeks before sampling. Grafting an infected nonunion without eradicating infection first reliably fails; treating an infected nonunion as aseptic is a common and costly error.
- Optimize modifiable risk factors regardless of type: smoking, diabetes, NSAID use, vitamin D deficiency, and poor nutrition all impair healing biology and should be addressed alongside any mechanical or biologic intervention.
- **Delayed union** is a distinct entity from established nonunion — healing is slower than expected but still progressing. Management is watchful waiting with risk-factor optimization and consideration of dynamization/adjunct biologics before committing to revision surgery, since many delayed unions still go on to heal without further intervention.
- Very large defects (≥ 5 cm) treated purely under the Diamond Concept framework achieve union in only ~64% of cases within 2 years — these behave more like segmental bone loss than a simple nonunion and typically need a staged Masquelet or bone-transport strategy layered onto the diamond-concept biology optimization.
