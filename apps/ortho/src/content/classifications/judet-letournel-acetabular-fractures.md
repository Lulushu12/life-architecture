## Overview

The Judet-Letournel classification describes acetabular fractures by their relationship to the anterior column (iliopubic) and posterior column (ilioischial) of the inverted-Y acetabulum, dividing patterns into five **elementary (simple)** types and five **associated (complex)** types. It is the foundation for surgical approach selection, since fracture pattern — not just displacement — dictates whether a posterior, anterior, or combined exposure is required.

## Types

### Elementary (simple) patterns

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Posterior wall** | Fracture of the posterior articular rim, often with hip dislocation | Most common pattern; Kocher-Langenbeck approach; assess for marginal impaction and femoral head injury |
| **Posterior column** | Oblique fracture from greater sciatic notch to ischial tuberosity/obturator ring | Kocher-Langenbeck approach |
| **Anterior wall** | Isolated anterior rim fracture (uncommon) | Ilioinguinal or Stoppa approach |
| **Anterior column** | Fracture from iliac crest/wing through anterior column to pubic ramus, variable height | Ilioinguinal, Stoppa, or combined depending on height of fracture |
| **Transverse** | Fracture separating superior (iliac) from inferior (ischiopubic) fragment, both columns, through the acetabulum | Approach depends on displacement pattern (anterior vs posterior predominant); may need combined |

### Associated (complex) patterns

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **T-shaped** | Transverse component + vertical split separating anterior and posterior columns | Often requires combined anterior + posterior approach |
| **Posterior column + posterior wall** | Posterior column fracture with separate posterior wall fragment | Kocher-Langenbeck, address both components |
| **Transverse + posterior wall** | Most common associated pattern | Kocher-Langenbeck usually sufficient |
| **Anterior column/wall + posterior hemitransverse** | Anterior injury with a posterior transverse component | Ilioinguinal/Stoppa ± posterior fixation |
| **Both-column ("floating acetabulum")** | All articular fragments detached from intact ilium; entire articular surface separated from axial skeleton | Most complex; combined anterior + posterior approach or single extensile approach; high rate of secondary arthroplasty in elderly |

## Pearls

- **Approach selection logic**: Kocher-Langenbeck for predominantly posterior patterns (posterior wall, posterior column, transverse+posterior wall); ilioinguinal or anterior intrapelvic (Stoppa) ± lateral window for predominantly anterior patterns (anterior column/wall, most both-column); combined or extensile (extended iliofemoral) approaches reserved for complex, delayed (>3 weeks), or both-column patterns not amenable to single-window fixation.
- Anterior intrapelvic (modified Stoppa) has largely supplanted ilioinguinal in many centers for anterior column/both-column patterns due to direct quadrilateral surface visualization and lower risk of femoral nerve/vessel injury.
- Roof arc angles (medial, anterior, posterior) on AP and Judet oblique views quantify weight-bearing dome involvement; roof arc <45° in any plane favors surgery.
- Posterior wall fracture is considered "unstable" at >40–50% wall involvement, arc <25° on Judet views, or hip subluxation/dislocation.
- Reduction quality, graded by Matta criteria, is the strongest predictor of joint survival regardless of Judet-Letournel type: anatomic (≤1 mm) is optimal, 1–3 mm is acceptable, >3 mm carries substantially higher post-traumatic osteoarthritis risk.
- Both-column patterns and comminuted/impacted-dome patterns in elderly patients carry a high rate of secondary arthroplasty even after adequate ORIF, which drives consideration of acute fix-and-replace in this population.
