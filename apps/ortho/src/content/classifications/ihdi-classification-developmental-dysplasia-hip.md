## Overview

The IHDI (International Hip Dysplasia Institute) classification standardizes the historical Tönnis grading of femoral head position on an AP pelvis radiograph, using the femoral head ossific nucleus's position relative to Perkin's and Hilgenreiner's lines. It was designed for better inter-observer reliability than the original Tönnis system and is used once the femoral capital ossific nucleus is visible (typically ~4–6 months of age) to grade the severity of DDH and guide the reduction pathway.

## Types

| Grade | Femoral head ossific nucleus position | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Medial to Perkin's line, below/at level expected for a located hip | Normal/located — observation or continue harness monitoring |
| II | Medial to Perkin's line but above the acetabular roof line (mild proximal migration) | Mild subluxation — usually still amenable to harness/closed methods |
| III | Lateral to Perkin's line, below the level of the triradiate cartilage | Significant dislocation — closed reduction typically required |
| IV | Lateral to Perkin's line, at or above the level of the iliac crest (highest dislocation) | Severe/high dislocation — often requires open reduction ± femoral shortening |

## Pearls

- IHDI grading requires the femoral capital ossific nucleus to be visible; before ~4–6 months, ultrasound (Graf classification) is the modality of choice instead.
- Grade III–IV dislocations are the group in which femoral shortening derotation osteotomy is typically added to open reduction, along with pelvic osteotomy for persistent acetabular dysplasia.
- IHDI was designed to replace the older Tönnis grading with a more reproducible scheme, but Tönnis grading remains widely referenced in the literature underlying IHDI and is still used in some centers.
- Grade correlates directly with closed-reduction success: higher grades (III–IV) carry progressively lower likelihood of successful closed reduction and higher likelihood of requiring open reduction.
