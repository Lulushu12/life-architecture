## Overview

AO/OTA Chapter 11 classifies proximal humerus fractures by fracture complexity and vascularity (AVN) risk rather than by displaced "parts." It is the classification most widely used in registries and trials (including PROFHER-style reporting) because the A/B/C tier maps directly onto AVN risk and drives how strongly arthroplasty is considered in older patients.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **11-A** (extra-articular, unifocal) | 2-part fracture patterns (surgical neck, greater tuberosity, or lesser tuberosity), one fracture line, extracapsular | Lowest AVN risk; often amenable to minimally invasive plating or nailing |
| **11-B** (extra-articular, bifocal) | Two extra-articular fracture lines (e.g., surgical neck + tuberosity), head still has one soft-tissue attachment preserved | Intermediate AVN risk; ORIF favored if hinge preserved |
| **11-C** (articular) | Fracture line extends into the articular surface / anatomic neck involvement | Highest AVN risk; anatomic-neck component is the classic AVN predictor; arthroplasty considered more strongly here in older patients |

Each group subdivides further (.1/.2/.3 and impacted/displaced qualifiers), mirroring Neer parts, but the A/B/C tier is what drives the AVN-risk conversation.

## Pearls

- The A/B/C tier is the operative signal — it tracks with AVN risk more directly than Neer part-count, so use it when framing arthroplasty-vs-ORIF discussions, especially for 11-C patterns.
- This is the classification system used in PROFHER-style trial reporting, so it is the common language for interpreting the trial evidence base (surgical vs nonoperative, and rTSA vs locked plate) cited for this diagnosis.
- Like Neer, reliability improves substantially with CT (2D/3D reconstruction) versus plain film alone, particularly for distinguishing 11-B from 11-C patterns.
