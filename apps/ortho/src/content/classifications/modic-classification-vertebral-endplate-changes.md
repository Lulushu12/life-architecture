## Overview

The Modic classification categorizes vertebral endplate/marrow signal changes seen adjacent to a degenerating disc on MRI into three types, reflecting a spectrum from acute inflammatory/vascularized change (Type 1) through fatty replacement (Type 2) to bony sclerosis (Type 3). It is used mainly to assess the likelihood that an endplate is an active pain generator and to flag fusion-related risk, not as an operative indication in itself.

## Types

| Type | MRI signal | Histology | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | T1 hypointense, T2 hyperintense | Vascularized fibrous marrow replacement, inflammatory/edematous | Most strongly associated with active discogenic low back pain and Modic-related pain; may respond to antibiotics in a subset attributed to low-virulence *Cutibacterium acnes* infection (debated, not standard of care); higher fusion nonunion (pseudarthrosis) risk if the endplate is inflamed at surgery |
| 2 | T1 hyperintense, T2 iso/hyperintense | Fatty marrow replacement | More stable, chronic; weaker/inconsistent pain association than Type 1 |
| 3 | T1 hypointense, T2 hypointense | Subchondral bony sclerosis | End-stage, mechanically stiff segment; least inflammatory |

## Pearls

- Modic 1 changes adjacent to a planned fusion level raise concern for higher pseudarthrosis risk and can prompt consideration of extending instrumentation or optimizing endplate preparation.
- Modic changes are **not** in themselves an operative indication — they inform risk counseling and endplate handling at surgery rather than driving the decision to operate.
- The antibiotic-for-Modic-1 hypothesis (low-virulence *Cutibacterium acnes* infection, Birkenmaier/AIM trial data) remains debated and is not standard of care; do not present it as an established treatment pathway.
