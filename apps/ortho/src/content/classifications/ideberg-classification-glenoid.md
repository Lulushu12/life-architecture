## Overview

The Ideberg classification (as modified by Goss/Ideberg) classifies intra-articular glenoid fractures by the location and course of the fracture line through the glenoid fossa and scapular body. Because the glenoid articular surface behaves like any other intra-articular fracture (step-off intolerant), this system is used to decide between nonoperative management and ORIF/reconstruction based on displacement and instability.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Avulsion fracture of the glenoid rim (Ia anterior, Ib posterior) | Nonoperative unless large fragment (>25% anterior / >33% posterior arc) causing instability → ORIF |
| **II** | Fracture line exits through the lateral scapular border/glenoid fossa inferior to the glenoid, creating an inferior fragment that may displace with the humeral head | Nonoperative if congruent and stable; ORIF if displaced fragment causes subluxation |
| **III** | Fracture line exits superiorly through the scapular body/spine or through the coracoid base, often associated with AC joint injury or clavicle fracture (ring disruption) | Often needs fixation given ring-disruption association; assess the superior shoulder suspensory complex (SSSC) |
| **IV** | Fracture line runs transversely through the glenoid fossa and exits through the medial scapular border | Depends on displacement/step-off; ORIF if articular step-off >4–5 mm |
| **V** | Combination of type IV with either inferior (Va), superior (Vb), or both (Vc) glenoid neck fracture components, creating a floating glenoid fragment | Usually operative given instability and displacement magnitude |
| **VI** (some modifications) | Comminuted glenoid fossa fracture | Complex reconstruction, may need buttress plating; salvage arthroplasty in older patients with articular destruction |

## Pearls

- Type III is the pattern that most often signals a superior shoulder suspensory complex (SSSC) ring disruption — always assess for a concomitant clavicle fracture or AC joint injury ("floating shoulder") when a type III is seen, and formally assess combined displacement rather than each injury in isolation.
- CT with 3D reconstruction is essentially mandatory for accurate typing and for measuring articular step-off/gap, which is the actual operative threshold (>4–5 mm) rather than the Ideberg type alone.
- Type VI (comminuted glenoid fossa) in an older patient with poor bone quality is a reasonable indication for primary reverse total shoulder arthroplasty rather than attempted ORIF, given the difficulty of achieving durable articular reconstruction.
- Unlike glenoid (articular) fractures, extra-articular scapular body/spine fractures usually heal well nonoperatively even with substantial displacement — do not apply the same step-off intolerance logic to the scapular body.
