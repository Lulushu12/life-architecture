## Overview

The Schizas classification is a morphological grading system for lumbar central spinal stenosis based on the appearance of the rootlet/CSF pattern on axial T2 MRI (dural sac cross-section), rather than an absolute canal measurement. It correlates better with symptomatic, surgery-warranting stenosis than cross-sectional area or diameter thresholds alone, and is used as clinical decision support when combined with a concordant symptom picture.

## Types

| Grade | Description (rootlet/CSF pattern) | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| A (A1–A4) | CSF clearly visible, rootlets recognizable, occupying various proportions of the canal | Mild-moderate stenosis; conservative management typically appropriate first |
| B | No CSF visible but rootlets still distinguishable, occupying most of the canal | Moderate-severe; surgical candidacy driven by symptoms/function, not grade alone |
| C | Rootlets not distinguishable, forming a homogeneous grey mass, canal fully occupied but epidural fat still visible | Severe stenosis; strong morphological correlate of symptomatic canal compromise |
| D | No epidural fat visible at all, rootlets not distinguishable from surrounding structures | Most severe grade; when concordant with clinical claudication/radiculopathy, favors operative decompression |

## Pearls

- Schizas grades C and D correlate best with symptomatic, functionally limiting stenosis and are the most useful morphological grades for surgical decision support — but as with all classifications, surgery is offered for **symptom-imaging concordance**, not the grade in isolation.
- Central canal cross-sectional area <100 mm² (or AP canal diameter <10 mm) is a commonly cited quantitative threshold for "severe" central stenosis, but Schizas morphological grading correlates better with symptoms than absolute measurements alone.
- Distinguish neurogenic claudication (bilateral, vague, relieved by flexion/sitting, worsened by standing/extension — "shopping cart sign," pain-free cycling despite limited walking tolerance) from vascular claudication (relieved by simply stopping, unaffected by posture, diminished pulses) before attributing symptoms to the imaged stenosis grade.
