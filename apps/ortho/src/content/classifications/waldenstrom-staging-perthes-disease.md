## Overview

The Waldenström stages describe the radiographic natural history of Perthes disease itself — initial (necrosis), fragmentation, reossification, and healed (remodeling) — over roughly 2–4 years. Unlike the Herring lateral pillar classification, Waldenström staging is not a treatment-selection tool; it is used to stage where a hip sits in the disease process and to time intervention correctly relative to the vulnerable fragmentation window.

## Types

| Stage | Findings | Duration (approx.) | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Initial (necrosis) | Widened joint space, subchondral fracture line may be seen, smaller/denser epiphysis | Weeks to ~6 months | Diagnosis often made here; head not yet at maximal deformation risk |
| Fragmentation | Epiphysis fragments radiographically (resorption of necrotic bone), most vulnerable to deformation under load | ~6 months to 1 year | Highest-risk window for femoral head deformation — containment decisions/surgery timed to precede or occur during this stage |
| Reossification (healing) | New bone forms progressively from periphery inward | ~1–3 years | Head shape becomes progressively fixed; late containment surgery has diminishing benefit |
| Healed (remodeling) | Reossification complete; further remodeling of head shape possible with growth remaining | Until skeletal maturity | Residual deformity assessed by Stulberg classification |

## Pearls

- Containment surgery is most effective when performed before or early in the fragmentation stage, while the femoral head is still moldable — a 2026 cohort specifically examining containment surgery performed during reossification found inferior radiographic remodeling compared with earlier intervention.
- The fragmentation stage is the biomechanically vulnerable window because the epiphysis is soft (undergoing resorption of necrotic bone) while the child continues to load it — this is the physiological rationale for containment.
- Final outcome (sphericity/congruency) is graded separately, once the healed/remodeling stage is reached, using the Stulberg classification.
- Symptomatic non-operative management (activity modification, NSAIDs, physiotherapy) is appropriate throughout the synovitic and early fragmentation phases for good-prognosis hips (lateral pillar A, or B with age <8y), regardless of Waldenström stage.
