## Overview

The Neer classification of distal (lateral) clavicle fractures — commonly paired with Robinson type 3 / Allman group II — stratifies these fractures by coracoclavicular (CC) ligament integrity, which is the true determinant of stability and surgical need, unlike the simple anatomic location used by Allman/Robinson for this zone.

## Types

| Neer type | CC ligaments | Stability | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Intact | Stable | Nonoperative |
| **II** (IIA/IIB) | Torn medial to fracture (conoid disrupted) | Unstable, high nonunion risk (~30%) | Operative fixation favored (hook plate, CC suture-button, or superior plate with CC augmentation) |
| **III** | Intact, fracture involves AC articular surface | Stable (may develop late AC arthrosis) | Usually nonoperative; late excision if symptomatic |
| **IV** (pediatric variant) | Periosteal sleeve avulsion, ligaments intact to periosteum | Usually stable | Nonoperative in children/adolescents |
| **V** | Comminuted, ligaments attached to neither fragment (both to a free inferior fragment) | Highly unstable | Operative fixation |

## Pearls

- CC ligament status — not displacement magnitude — is what separates the operative types (II, V) from the nonoperative ones (I, III, IV); always assess conoid/trapezoid integrity, not just fracture position on plain film.
- Type IV is a pediatric-specific pattern (periosteal sleeve avulsion with ligaments intact to periosteum) that should not be mistaken for a true displaced fracture in a skeletally immature patient — it remodels and is managed nonoperatively.
- Type III fractures are stable but articular, so they carry a distinct late complication (AC arthrosis) that is unrelated to union — a "good" fracture outcome does not exclude this.
- When surgery is chosen for type II/V, hook plate gives reliable reduction but mandates a planned second surgery for removal (~3–4 months, due to subacromial impingement risk), while CC suture-button/suspensory fixation avoids the second surgery but is technically more demanding.
