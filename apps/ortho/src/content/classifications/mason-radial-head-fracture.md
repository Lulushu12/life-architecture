## Overview

The Mason classification (modified by Hotchkiss) grades radial head fractures by displacement, articular involvement, and comminution, and is the primary tool for deciding between non-operative treatment, ORIF, and radial head arthroplasty (RHA). Its modified type IV adds elbow dislocation as a separate management-changing variable, since associated instability — not just fragment count — is what most often pushes treatment toward arthroplasty.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Non-displaced or minimally displaced (<2mm), no mechanical block | Non-operative: sling, early active ROM within days once comfortable |
| **II** | Displaced (>2mm) marginal fracture involving <30–50% of the head, or with a mechanical block to rotation | ORIF if block to motion or displacement threatens stability; screws (often headless, buried below cartilage) or a small fragment/mini plate outside the "safe zone" |
| **III** | Comminuted fracture involving the whole head, often not reconstructable | ORIF if ≤3 major fragments and reconstructable; RHA if comminuted/non-reconstructable, particularly with associated elbow instability (terrible triad, Essex-Lopresti) |
| **IV (modified)** | Any Mason type associated with elbow dislocation | Treat the radial head per fragment count/reconstructability, but the bar for RHA over ORIF drops given the added instability — RHA restores the stabilizing strut more reliably than a marginal ORIF in the unstable elbow |

## Pearls

- Radial head excision alone (without replacement) is now reserved for isolated, low-demand, mechanically-blocking fractures with a **confirmed intact LCL, coronoid, and IOM** — this is increasingly rare in current practice given how easily associated instability is under-recognized acutely.
- Fragment count, not just displacement, is the practical decision point: ORIF is favored for ≤3 reconstructable fragments with no gross instability; RHA is favored when the radial head is comminuted or must function as a stabilizing strut (terrible triad, Essex-Lopresti, Monteggia-variant).
- Do not resect the radial head without excluding an Essex-Lopresti pattern (interosseous membrane/DRUJ injury) — wrist pain out of proportion or DRUJ tenderness should prompt evaluation before resection.
- Meta-analyses of prospective trials in Mason III fractures (2024) show RHA and ORIF achieve comparable overall functional outcomes, with ORIF trending toward more reoperations/hardware removal and worse rotation in some series, and RHA trending toward more reliable early stability in comminuted or instability-associated patterns.
