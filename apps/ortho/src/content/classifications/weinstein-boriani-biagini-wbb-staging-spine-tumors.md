## Overview

The Weinstein-Boriani-Biagini (WBB) system is a surgical staging tool that maps a vertebral tumor's extent onto 12 radiating clock-face zones and 5 concentric layers (A through E, from extraosseous soft tissue to dural involvement). It is used alongside Enneking staging specifically to plan the resection corridor for en bloc excision of primary malignant spine tumors — which zones and layers must be sacrificed to achieve a wide margin.

## Types

| Component | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **12 radiating zones** | Vertebra divided circumferentially into 12 clock-face sectors | Defines the coronal/axial extent of tumor involvement around the vertebra |
| **5 layers (A–E)** | A = extraosseous soft tissue, extending through bone to E = dural involvement | Defines the depth of tumor extension; layer involvement dictates whether an intralesional, marginal, or wide en bloc margin is achievable |
| **Combined zone + layer map** | Full circumferential and depth mapping of tumor extent | Directly plans the en bloc resection corridor (e.g., sacrectomy level, nerve roots that must be sacrificed) for malignant primary tumors such as chordoma and chondrosarcoma |

## Pearls

- WBB is a planning tool, not a prognostic score — it is used together with Enneking grade/stage to decide both *whether* wide margins are achievable and *how* to achieve them surgically.
- In sacral chordoma specifically, WBB-guided mapping of proximal tumor extent dictates the sacrectomy level, and sacral nerve root sacrifice creates a direct trade-off between oncologic margin and bowel/bladder/sexual function that must be discussed explicitly with the patient preoperatively.
- Malignant primary tumors (chordoma, chondrosarcoma) are relatively resistant to conventional photon radiotherapy, so WBB-planned en bloc resection with wide margins remains the surgical mainstay, with high-dose proton/carbon-ion RT or SBRT reserved as an adjunct for close/positive margins or unresectable disease.
- Intralesional surgery — i.e., a resection that does not respect the WBB-mapped tumor boundary — carries a high local recurrence rate and should be avoided when en bloc resection is feasible.
