## Overview

The glenoid track / on-track–off-track concept (Yamamoto, Di Giacomo) integrates glenoid bone loss AND Hill-Sachs size/location into a single bipolar-lesion model to predict whether a Hill-Sachs lesion will "engage" the anterior glenoid rim in functional (abduction-external rotation) positions. It is used whenever glenoid or humeral-sided bone loss is present, to decide between isolated Bankart repair, Bankart + remplissage, and bone-block (Latarjet) surgery.

**Key measurements**:
- **Glenoid track (GT) width** = 0.83 × d (d = intact glenoid diameter by best-fit circle) minus the glenoid bone defect width.
- **Hill-Sachs interval (HSI)** = distance from the medial margin of the rotator cuff footprint to the lateral edge of the Hill-Sachs lesion, measured on axial CT/MRI.
- **On-track**: HSI < GT width — Hill-Sachs stays within the track and does not engage.
- **Off-track**: HSI ≥ GT width — Hill-Sachs extends medial to the track and engages the anterior glenoid rim.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **On-track, glenoid loss <13.5%** | Small/moderate Hill-Sachs, minimal glenoid loss | Isolated arthroscopic Bankart repair; low recurrence |
| **On-track, glenoid loss 13.5–20% (subcritical)** | Borderline glenoid loss but Hill-Sachs still on-track | Individualize: Bankart alone acceptable in low-demand patients; add remplissage in contact/overhead athletes to reduce recurrence margin |
| **Off-track, glenoid loss <20–25%** | Hill-Sachs engages despite modest glenoid loss | Bankart + remplissage (fills/tenodeses the Hill-Sachs into infraspinatus, rendering it extra-articular) is the standard arthroscopic solution |
| **Off-track or glenoid loss ≥20–25%** | Combined bipolar bone loss exceeding soft-tissue repair capacity | Bone-block procedure (Latarjet, iliac crest/Eden-Hybinette) is generally indicated; remplissage alone is insufficient |

## Pearls

- 13.5% and 20–25% glenoid bone loss are the two thresholds most cited in the 2024–2025 algorithmic literature; some contemporary algorithms treat 13.5–20% as a single "subcritical zone" requiring individualization, escalating to bone block above ~20%.
- Track status requires both glenoid width (CT) and Hill-Sachs interval/width (CT or MRA) — CT with 3D reconstruction and digital humeral subtraction is the gold standard for quantifying glenoid bone loss (best-fit circle/PICO method).
- A 2024 reliability study of 2D on-track/off-track measurement found measurement variability depending on technique, arguing for standardized measurement protocols (best-fit circle method) before using track status as a hard surgical cutoff.
- Failing to recognize off-track status pre-operatively is described as the single largest driver of "unexplained" recurrence after isolated arthroscopic Bankart repair.
- Sport-specific contact demand can shift the Bankart-vs-Latarjet threshold independent of bone-loss percentage alone: in contact/martial-arts athletes with <20% bone loss, Latarjet has shown markedly lower recurrence (6% vs 25%) and reoperation (6% vs 18%) than Bankart without remplissage.
