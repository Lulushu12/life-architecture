## Overview

The Bilsky Epidural Spinal Cord Compression (ESCC) scale grades the degree of epidural tumor extension and thecal sac/cord compression on MRI, from tumor confined to bone (grade 0) through spinal cord compression with no visible CSF (grade 3). It is the "neurologic/oncologic" axis of the NOMS framework and the pivotal determinant of whether stereotactic body radiotherapy (SBRT) can be delivered safely alone or whether decompression is needed first.

## Types

| Grade | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Tumor confined to bone | Low-grade — SBRT alone feasible |
| **1a** | Epidural extension without thecal sac deformation | Low-grade — SBRT alone feasible |
| **1b** | Deformation of thecal sac, no cord abutment | Low-grade — SBRT alone feasible |
| **1c** | Thecal sac deformation with cord abutment, no compression | Low-grade — SBRT alone feasible |
| **2** | Spinal cord compression, CSF still visible around cord | High-grade — separation surgery generally required before SBRT to allow safe cord dosing |
| **3** | Spinal cord compression, no CSF visible around cord | High-grade — separation surgery generally required before SBRT |

## Pearls

- The grade 2/3 threshold is the pivotal decision point in the NOMS framework: SBRT alone cannot adequately treat the tumor without exceeding cord dose tolerance when there is high-grade compression, so surgical decompression to re-establish a CSF margin is required first.
- In the NOMS decision logic, high-grade ESCC (Bilsky 2–3) with reasonable surgical/systemic candidacy leads to separation surgery + postoperative SBRT **regardless of histologic radiosensitivity** — classically radioresistant tumors (renal cell, sarcoma, melanoma, thyroid, hepatocellular, NSCLC) achieve >85–90% one-year local control with this combination.
- Low-grade ESCC (Bilsky 0–1c) with a radiosensitive tumor is often adequately managed with conventional EBRT alone; low-grade ESCC with a radioresistant tumor can receive SBRT alone with dose escalation, since there is no cord-abutment constraint.
- Bilsky grade must be interpreted alongside SINS (mechanical instability) and systemic status — mechanical instability drives stabilization independent of ESCC grade, and poor systemic status/short life expectancy favors radiation/systemic therapy/best supportive care over surgery regardless of ESCC grade.
- Separation surgery targets ~2–3mm of circumferential CSF space around the thecal sac, deliberately **not** a gross-total-resection goal — attempting more aggressive resection is unnecessary and adds morbidity.
