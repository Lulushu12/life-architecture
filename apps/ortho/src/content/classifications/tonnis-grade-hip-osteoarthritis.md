## Overview

The Tönnis grade is a hip-specific radiographic osteoarthritis severity scale (0–3) and is more clinically used by hip surgeons than Kellgren-Lawrence for hip decision-making, particularly for gauging whether joint-preserving surgery (periacetabular osteotomy, hip arthroscopy for FAI/dysplasia) is still realistic versus whether cartilage loss has advanced too far and total hip arthroplasty (THA) is the appropriate pathway.

## Types

| Grade | Findings | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No signs of OA | Joint preservation candidate if structural deformity (FAI/dysplasia) present and symptomatic |
| **1** | Increased sclerosis, slight joint space narrowing, slight osteophyte formation | Joint preservation surgery (PAO, hip arthroscopy) still reasonable in the right patient — cartilage largely preserved |
| **2** | Small cysts, moderate joint space narrowing, moderate loss of femoral head sphericity | Joint preservation outcomes decline; THA increasingly favored, especially age >40 |
| **3** | Large cysts, severe/obliterated joint space, severe femoral head deformity, avascular necrosis features | THA is the treatment of choice |

## Pearls

- Tönnis grade ≥2 is a commonly cited relative contraindication to joint-preserving procedures such as periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) or hip arthroscopy for FAI/dysplasia — cartilage damage is too advanced for preservation surgery to reliably succeed.
- Tönnis 2–3 correlates best with THA benefit; Tönnis 0–1 in a young dysplastic hip favors joint preservation (PAO) over THA.
- Practical sequence in clinic: confirm radiographic OA severity and hip-specificity with KL/Tönnis to establish that the joint (not the spine or soft tissue) is the pain generator and that cartilage loss is advanced enough that a joint-preserving option is no longer realistic (Tönnis ≥2); only then assess Dorr type for the femoral fixation strategy once THA is indicated.
- A young patient with Tönnis 0–1 changes and clear cam/pincer morphology is a joint-preservation candidate; the same patient with Tönnis 2–3 changes is not, regardless of age, and should be counseled toward arthroplasty.
- Indication for THA is radiographically confirmed hip joint destruction (Tönnis ≥2, generally KL ≥3) concordant with clinically significant pain and functional limitation that has failed an adequate non-operative trial.
