## Overview

The Kocher criteria are a clinical prediction rule for differentiating septic arthritis from transient synovitis in the acutely irritable pediatric hip, combining four predictors (non-weight-bearing, ESR >40 mm/h, fever >38.5°C, WBC >12,000/µL) into an estimated probability of septic arthritis. It is used at initial presentation to decide between observation and hip aspiration, though it should inform rather than replace clinical judgment.

## Types

| Criteria present | Predicted probability of septic arthritis (original Kocher cohort) | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | ~<0.2% | Low pretest probability — observation/safety-netting appropriate if otherwise reassuring |
| 1 | ~3% | Low-moderate — close follow-up; aspiration if other red flags or clinical deterioration |
| 2 | ~40% | Indeterminate — diagnostic uncertainty with a tense effusion warrants aspiration |
| 3 | ~93% | High — aspiration strongly indicated |
| 4 | ~99% | Very high — treat as septic arthritis until excluded; do not defer aspiration |
| Caird CRP >20 mg/L (added modifier) | Improves discrimination, especially with ≥1 other criterion positive | Adds sensitivity to the 4-criterion rule, particularly at intermediate scores |

## Pearls

- Subsequent external validation cohorts (including the Caird CRP-modified rule) have consistently shown lower predictive values than the original derivation, particularly at lower criterion counts — use the rule to inform, not replace, clinical judgment.
- Kingella kingae, an increasingly recognized cause of pediatric septic arthritis particularly under age 4, often produces a blunted inflammatory response (lower fever, normal/mildly elevated ESR/CRP) that can produce false reassurance on Kocher scoring; PCR-based synovial fluid testing improves yield over culture alone.
- Clinical prediction rules of this type perform inconsistently across causative organisms — a rule calibrated broadly against pyogenic organisms may under-call Kingella-associated septic arthritis.
- Irritable hip + fever + refusal to weight-bear meeting Kocher criteria should be treated as septic arthritis until excluded — do not defer aspiration while "trialing" NSAIDs.
- A low pretest probability by Kocher criteria plus a reassuring ultrasound (effusion without other red flags) supports a structured trial of observation with mandatory re-check at 48–72 hours; a child not clearly improving by that point should be re-investigated (repeat inflammatory markers, repeat ultrasound, consider aspiration) rather than continuing to observe.
- Synovial WBC >50,000–100,000/µL with PMN predominance on aspiration strongly favors septic arthritis, though partially treated or Kingella infections may present with lower counts.
