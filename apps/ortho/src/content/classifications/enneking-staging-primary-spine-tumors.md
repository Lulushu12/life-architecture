## Overview

The Enneking staging system grades primary bone/spine tumors by grade and compartmental extent, separately for benign lesions (latent/active/aggressive) and malignant lesions (grade + compartment). It guides the fundamental margin decision in primary spine tumor surgery — curettage versus wide/en bloc excision — and is used alongside the Weinstein-Boriani-Biagini (WBB) surgical staging system, which maps tumor extent circumferentially to plan the actual resection corridor.

## Types

| Category | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Enneking benign — Latent (1)** | Indolent, self-limited benign lesion | Observation or simple curettage generally sufficient |
| **Enneking benign — Active (2)** | Progressive benign lesion, intact compartment | Intralesional excision/curettage ± adjuvant (phenol, cryotherapy), or CT-guided RFA for osteoid osteoma |
| **Enneking benign — Aggressive (3)** | Locally aggressive benign lesion (e.g., giant cell tumor) | Extended intralesional curettage with adjuvant ± preoperative denosumab downstaging; en bloc resection reserved for recurrent or anatomically inaccessible disease |
| **Enneking malignant — Low-grade (IA/IB)** | Grade I malignancy; A = intracompartmental, B = extracompartmental | Wide margin planning; behaves more indolently than high-grade disease |
| **Enneking malignant — High-grade (IIA/IIB)** | Grade II malignancy; A = intracompartmental, B = extracompartmental | Wide margin planning is the mainstay (chordoma, chondrosarcoma); adjuvant proton/carbon-ion RT for close/positive margins given relative photon radioresistance |
| **Enneking malignant — Metastatic (III)** | Distant metastatic disease present | Systemic/multimodal treatment; local resection goals reassessed in that context |

## Pearls

- Enneking grade/stage, not tumor identity alone, determines whether curettage or wide/en bloc excision is appropriate — benign-aggressive lesions (e.g., giant cell tumor) sit in an intermediate zone where extended curettage with adjuvant is often preferred over the morbidity of en bloc resection.
- For malignant primary tumors (chordoma, chondrosarcoma), **en bloc resection with wide margins** remains the mainstay regardless of Enneking substage, because both are relatively resistant to conventional photon radiotherapy — margin adequacy is the dominant determinant of local recurrence.
- Intralesional (inadequate-margin) surgery carries a high local recurrence rate and has historically been a major problem for chordoma specifically — avoid intralesional surgery when en bloc resection is feasible.
- Biopsy planning is itself a surgical decision, not a radiology convenience: the needle tract must be excisable en bloc at definitive surgery, so biopsy should be performed at (or in direct coordination with) the treating center to avoid tumor seeding that compromises later margins.
- Preoperative denosumab downstaging for giant cell tumor facilitates resection, but discontinuation/de-escalation of denosumab — not neoadjuvant surgical use itself — carries the recognized rebound/recurrence risk.
