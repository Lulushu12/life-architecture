## Overview

This radiographic severity grading uses the hallux valgus angle (HVA — between the 1st metatarsal and proximal phalanx long axes) and the intermetatarsal angle (IMA — between the 1st and 2nd metatarsal long axes) to stratify hallux valgus and select an osteotomy strategy. It requires **weight-bearing** AP radiographs — non-weight-bearing films systematically understate the deformity. Normal values: HVA <15°, IMA <9°, DMAA (distal metatarsal articular angle) <10–15°, hallux interphalangeal angle <10°.

## Types

| Grade | HVA | IMA | Typical DMAA | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | <15° | <9° | — | No treatment |
| Mild | 15–20° | <11° | Normal/mild ↑ | Distal soft-tissue procedure + distal metaphyseal osteotomy (chevron); MIS distal chevron/Akin is a reasonable alternative |
| Moderate | 20–40° | 11–16° | Often ↑ | Diaphyseal osteotomy (scarf) usually required for adequate translation; distal chevron only if congruent joint/high DMAA and reducible |
| Severe | >40° | >16° | Variable | Proximal osteotomy (crescentic/opening wedge) or 1st TMT arthrodesis (Lapidus), particularly if 1st ray hypermobile/unstable, arthritic, or deformity recurrent |

## Pearls

- **DMAA modifies the framework**: an elevated DMAA (congruent-joint hallux valgus, common in juveniles) needs an intra-articular or biplanar correction (e.g., biplanar chevron or double/triple osteotomy) rather than a shaft translation alone, or the deformity recurs.
- **Sesamoid position modifies the framework independently of severity grade**: marked sesamoid subluxation (Hardy & Clapham tibial sesamoid position grading, 0–7; position ≥4–5) mandates adequate lateral soft-tissue release/derotation regardless of which bony procedure is chosen.
- HVA >40°, IMA >16–18°, or a hypermobile/arthritic 1st TMT joint favor **Lapidus** over a distal or diaphyseal osteotomy.
- Clinically stress the 1st ray in the sagittal plane for hypermobility (>8–10 mm of dorsal–plantar excursion relative to the 2nd ray is generally considered abnormal), though this test has only moderate reliability.
- Weight-bearing CT (WBCT) literature (2023–2026) confirms hallux valgus reflects global first-ray malalignment including first metatarsal **pronation**, which is a prognostic factor for recurrence; WBCT measures pronation and sesamoid alignment more reproducibly than plain radiographs, though the clinical impact of correcting pronation specifically remains unstandardized.
- Non-operative measures (wide toe-box shoes, spacers, orthotics, night splints/bracing) do not reverse or reliably slow radiographic progression regardless of grade — counsel patients accordingly.
