## Overview

The Denis classification grades sacral fractures by **zone relative to the neural foramina and central canal**, directly predicting neurologic injury risk. It should be documented before any percutaneous posterior pelvic fixation is planned, since zone dictates both expected nerve-root involvement and whether decompression is required in addition to stabilization.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Neurologic injury rate | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zone I** | Fracture lateral to the neural foramina (through the sacral ala) | ~6% (typically L5 root, from ala fracture/hematoma) | Usually amenable to percutaneous iliosacral/TITS screw; monitor for L5 root symptoms |
| **Zone II** | Fracture through the neural foramina, not entering the central canal | ~28% (L5–S1 roots, unilateral) | Screw fixation feasible; higher radiculopathy risk; consider decompression if progressive deficit |
| **Zone III** | Fracture extending into the central sacral canal | ~57% (bilateral, may include bowel/bladder/sexual dysfunction — cauda equina pattern) | Often requires decompression (laminectomy) + stabilization (lumbopelvic fixation) beyond simple percutaneous screws, especially with displacement or neuro deficit |

## Pearls

- Denis zone correlates directly with neurologic risk and should be documented before any percutaneous posterior fixation is planned.
- Zone III with deficit and displacement is a relative indication for **open reduction with foraminal/canal decompression and segmental (lumbopelvic) fixation** rather than percutaneous screws alone.
- A full lower-extremity motor/sensory/reflex exam plus perianal sensation and rectal tone is warranted for Zone II/III injuries given substantial cauda-equina-pattern risk; new bowel/bladder/sexual dysfunction is frequently under-reported by patients focused on pain, so ask specifically.
- Sacral dysmorphism (present in ~30–40% of the population) can independently constrain safe percutaneous screw trajectories regardless of Denis zone — always assess inlet/outlet radiographs and the lateral sacral view (or thin-cut CT) before planning fixation.
