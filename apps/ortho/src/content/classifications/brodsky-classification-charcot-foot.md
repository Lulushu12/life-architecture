## Overview

An anatomic classification of Charcot neuroarthropathy by the joint region primarily involved. Used alongside the Eichenholtz classification (which stages disease activity) to predict instability risk and select the surgical strategy when surgery is needed.

## Types

| Type | Location | Notes | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Midfoot (tarsometatarsal/naviculocuneiform, Lisfranc region) | Most common (~60–70%) | Rocker-bottom deformity risk; if plantigrade, brace (CROW); exostectomy for ulcerating plantar prominence; realignment arthrodesis if unstable/recurrent ulcer |
| 2 | Hindfoot (subtalar, talonavicular, calcaneocuboid) | Less common but higher instability | Higher risk of progressive collapse and instability — lower threshold for surgical stabilization (beaming/arthrodesis) |
| 3A | Ankle | Least common, most unstable | Highest risk of limb-threatening instability; often needs tibiotalocalcaneal (TTC) arthrodesis with intramedullary nail or ring fixator |
| 3B | Calcaneus (insufficiency fracture of tuberosity) | Distinct mechanism (fracture, not joint collapse) | Usually managed with offloading/immobilization; surgery reserved for severe displacement |

## Pearls

- Type 1 (midfoot) is by far the most common pattern and the classic driver of rocker-bottom deformity and recalcitrant plantar ulceration; many patients require lifelong bracing to prevent recurrence/re-ulceration, particularly with this residual deformity pattern.
- Type 3A (ankle) carries the highest limb-threat and typically requires the most aggressive fixation (TTC arthrodesis with retrograde IM nail), reflecting the instability and impaired bone-healing biology of the neuropathic ankle.
- Type 3B is mechanistically distinct — an insufficiency fracture rather than joint collapse — and is usually managed nonoperatively unless severely displaced.
- Combine with the Eichenholtz stage before deciding on surgery: surgical indications (recurrent/non-healing ulceration, instability, deformity, infection) apply regardless of Brodsky type, but elective reconstruction is deferred to Eichenholtz stage III whenever possible.
