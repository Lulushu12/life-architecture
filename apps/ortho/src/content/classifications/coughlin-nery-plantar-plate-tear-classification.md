## Overview

The Coughlin/Nery classification grades plantar plate tears of the 2nd (occasionally 3rd) MTP joint, from normal synovitis through complete tear with fixed dislocation. It is used under both metatarsalgia and lesser toe deformity workups to decide between conservative management, direct plantar plate repair, and whether a Weil osteotomy should be added to decompress the joint.

## Types

| Grade | Findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal plantar plate, synovitis only | Non-operative: taping, offloading |
| I | Attenuation/thinning, no tear | Non-operative first-line; consider debridement if refractory |
| II | Partial tear (<50% width) or one-sided attachment tear | Direct repair if instability/symptoms persist despite conservative care |
| III | Tear >50% width, MTP subluxable/dislocatable | Direct plantar plate repair strongly favored, usually combined with Weil osteotomy |
| IV | Complete tear with fixed MTP dislocation | Repair with Weil osteotomy; salvage arthroplasty if joint destroyed |

## Pearls

- Grading is confirmed with the **MTP dorsal drawer/Lachman test** (>50% subluxation or frank dislocatability corresponds to grade II–III tear) and a positive **"V-sign"** (gap between toes) suggesting crossover deformity.
- Ultrasound or MRI is used for formal grading when surgical planning is needed; weight-bearing AP evaluates MTP subluxation/dislocation and the overall metatarsal parabola (Maestro method).
- **Simple soft-tissue balancing without addressing plate incompetence has a high recurrence rate** for higher-grade (II–III/IV) tears — direct repair, not balancing alone, is the durable fix.
- Repair is typically performed via a dorsal MTP approach with an implant fixed by anchor or transosseous suture; a direct plantar-approach repair is also well described and durable in long-term series, though it remains underutilized because of concerns about scar-related pain.
- Plantar plate insufficiency of the 2nd (occasionally 3rd) MTP joint is a very common structural driver of isolated 2nd-ray overload/metatarsalgia, distinct from primary (long/plantarflexed metatarsal, cavus foot) or secondary (systemic: RA, gout, fat-pad atrophy) mechanisms.
