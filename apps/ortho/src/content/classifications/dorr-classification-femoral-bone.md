## Overview

The Dorr classification describes proximal femoral canal morphology and bone quality (types A, B, C) on the AP pelvis/hip radiograph. It is used at THA templating to set the cemented-versus-uncemented femoral fixation strategy, layered on top of an age-based framework.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | "Champagne flute" — thick cortices, narrow canal, funnel-shaped metaphysis-to-diaphysis transition | Excellent uncemented press-fit fixation candidate; risk of intraoperative fracture if under-reaming/oversized broaching is used given tight, dense bone |
| **B** | Intermediate — moderate cortical thinning, wider canal, less pronounced funnel shape | Uncemented fixation still generally reliable; most common morphology |
| **C** | "Stovepipe" — thin cortices, wide canal, minimal metaphyseal-diaphyseal mismatch, low canal-flare index | Higher risk of inadequate press-fit and early subsidence with standard uncemented tapered stems; favor cemented fixation, a fully coated/cylindrical uncemented stem, or a modular/monoblock design suited to poor bone — particularly relevant in elderly, osteoporotic, or chronic steroid-exposed (inflammatory arthritis) patients |

## Pearls

- Dorr type is assessed on the AP pelvis/hip radiograph using the canal-flare index (canal width at the lesser trochanter and 10 cm distal, relative to the isthmus) and directly informs the cemented-vs-uncemented decision layered on top of the age-based framework.
- Practical framework: young/active patient with good bone quality (Dorr A/B) → uncemented stem and cup, biologic fixation with decades-long survivorship expectation. Elderly patient (>75), osteoporotic or Dorr C bone, or femoral neck fracture indication → cemented stem strongly favored to minimize periprosthetic fracture risk; the acetabular cup is essentially always uncemented (hemispherical press-fit) regardless of femoral-side choice.
- Inflammatory arthritis with poor bone quality and/or corticosteroid-related osteopenia should apply the same low bone-quality logic as the elderly osteoporotic group, independent of chronologic age.
- American Joint Replacement Registry data demonstrate a significantly reduced risk of periprosthetic femur fracture with cemented fixation in patients ≥65 years, a difference that widens with advancing age and diminishing bone quality; most European arthroplasty units (particularly Nordic and UK practice) cement routinely in this population, a more common default than in the US.
- Uncemented fixation is the dominant international choice for adequate bone stock (Dorr A/B), reflecting registry trends toward >80–90% uncemented femoral fixation in many countries, with Sweden a notable outlier retaining high cemented rates and correspondingly excellent registry-reported outcomes.
