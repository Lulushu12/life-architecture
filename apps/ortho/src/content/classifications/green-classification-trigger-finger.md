## Overview

The Green classification grades trigger finger (stenosing flexor tenosynovitis) severity by the degree of mechanical catching/locking observed on clinical exam, from tenderness without demonstrable catching (Grade I, "pretriggering") through a fixed, passively uncorrectable flexion contracture (Grade IV). Grade is the primary driver of expected corticosteroid injection success and the threshold for surgical A1 pulley release.

## Types

| Grade | Findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I (pretriggering) | Pain, tenderness at A1 pulley, history of catching without demonstrable catching on exam | Splint/NSAID; injection reasonable |
| II | Demonstrable catching, actively correctable | Injection first-line (best injection response) |
| III A | Demonstrable catching requiring passive correction | Injection reasonable but lower success; surgery if fails/recurs |
| III B | Active incomplete flexion (locked in extension) | Surgical release favored, especially if diabetic |
| IV | Fixed flexion contracture, passively uncorrectable | Surgical A1 release required, may need concomitant PIP contracture management |

## Pearls

- Diagnosis is clinical: a palpable nodule at the A1 pulley, painful catching/locking on active flexion-extension, and tenderness at the MCP flexion crease — no imaging is needed to grade or treat.
- Corticosteroid injection success (roughly 50-90% overall) is highest at low grades (I-II) and falls with more advanced catching (III A/B); diabetes further lowers injection success and raises recurrence, especially insulin-dependent and multi-digit disease.
- A second injection is reasonable if there is a partial response; persistent/recurrent triggering after two injections favors surgical release regardless of grade.
- MCP-blocking splinting (primarily nocturnal, 6-10 weeks) is most effective in Green I-II and short symptom duration — it is not a reasonable first-line strategy once catching requires passive correction (III A and beyond).
- Grade IV disease may require concomitant PIP contracture management at the time of A1 release, distinguishing it from lower grades where release alone is curative.
- On differential diagnosis, trigger finger catches/locks in flexion with a palpable A1 nodule, distinguishing it from Boutonnière deformity and from extensor tendon (sagittal band) subluxation, which instead shows the tendon subluxing to the ulnar/radial gutter on active MCP flexion.
