## Overview

The Warwick Agreement (Griffin et al., *Br J Sports Med* 2016) is a 2016 international consensus statement — not a numeric grading system — that established consensus terminology and diagnostic/management criteria for femoroacetabular impingement (FAI) syndrome, agreed by 22 panelists across 5 specialties. It is used as the reference framework for diagnosing FAI syndrome and structuring its treatment ladder, rather than for staging severity.

## Types

| Consensus domain | Statement | Practical implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Diagnosis (triad)** | **FAI syndrome** requires the coexistence of (1) symptoms, (2) clinical signs (e.g., positive FADIR), and (3) imaging findings (cam and/or pincer morphology) — morphology alone on imaging without symptoms is **not** a diagnosis | Avoid operating on incidental cam/pincer morphology found on imaging in asymptomatic patients |
| **First-line treatment** | Conservative care (activity modification, physiotherapy, education) should be tried before surgery in most patients | Structures the treatment ladder — surgery reserved for refractory symptomatic structural FAI |
| **Surgical indication** | Surgery (open or arthroscopic) is indicated for symptomatic FAI syndrome that has failed conservative treatment, aiming to correct morphology and treat labral/chondral damage | Confirms arthroscopy is not first-line even when morphology is present |
| **Outcome measurement** | Patient-reported outcome measures (iHOT-33/12, HOS, NAHS) should be used to assess results, alongside standardized imaging measurements (alpha angle, LCEA) | Basis for structured PROM-driven follow-up in hip-preservation practice |
| **Terminology** | Adopted "FAI syndrome" (rather than "FAI") to emphasize it is a clinical syndrome, not a radiographic finding | Changed literature/coding nomenclature post-2016 |

## Pearls

- The single most practically important point is the diagnostic triad: imaging morphology (cam/pincer) alone, in an asymptomatic patient, is not FAI syndrome — this prevents overdiagnosis and inappropriate surgery on incidental findings.
- The Warwick Agreement's conservative-first stance is reinforced by subsequent randomized evidence (FASHIoN, FAIR trials): structured physiotherapy is trialed for a minimum of 8–12 weeks, often 3–6 months, before considering surgery — except when mechanical locking/catching or a large unstable labral flap dominates the clinical picture.
- Adoption of "FAI syndrome" over "FAI" is not merely semantic — it changed literature and coding nomenclature after 2016 and reflects the emphasis on a clinical (symptomatic) rather than purely radiographic diagnosis.
- Because this is a consensus framework rather than a severity scale, it is used together with structural classifications (Tönnis grade, LCEA, alpha angle) and patient-selection criteria to decide whether and how to operate, not to stage disease severity on its own.
