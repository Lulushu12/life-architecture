## Overview

The modified Japanese Orthopaedic Association (mJOA) score is the primary clinical severity and treatment-stratification tool for degenerative cervical myelopathy (DCM) and OPLL. It is scored out of 18, combining upper extremity motor function, lower extremity motor function, sensation, and sphincter function, and it directly drives the surgery-versus-observation decision.

## Types

### Domain scoring

| Domain | Points | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Upper extremity motor | 0–5 | 0 = unable to feed self; 5 = normal |
| Lower extremity motor | 0–7 | 0 = unable to walk; 7 = normal |
| Sensation (UE) | 0–3 | 0 = complete loss; 3 = normal |
| Sphincter function | 0–3 | 0 = urinary retention; 3 = normal |

### Severity grading (mJOA total)

| mJOA total | Severity | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **18** | No myelopathy (normal) | Not a surgical indication by itself even if imaging shows cord compression — see non-myelopathic cord compression (NMCC) discussion |
| **15–17** | Mild | Either surgery or a structured, supervised trial of rehabilitation with close follow-up is acceptable (shared decision-making); this is not a "step up" disease — nonoperative failure means deterioration, not lack of response, so progress to surgery with any deterioration or non-response |
| **12–14** | Moderate | Surgery recommended as standard of care |
| **<12** | Severe | Surgery recommended, generally urgently; higher risk of incomplete recovery, but decompression is still indicated to halt progression and salvage function |

## Pearls

- mJOA anchors the standard outcome metric in DCM surgical literature — **recovery ratio** = (postop mJOA − preop mJOA) / (18 − preop mJOA) × 100%.
- Nonoperative management is never appropriate for moderate or severe DCM (mJOA <15) — evidence consistently favors surgical decompression to halt progression, and delay worsens achievable recovery.
- For radiographic cord compression without clinical myelopathy (NMCC), current guidance favors surveillance over prophylactic surgery, but patients should be counseled explicitly about deterioration risk (particularly after even minor trauma/hyperextension injury), and the shared decision should be documented.
- Surveillance for mild DCM/NMCC managed nonoperatively: formal mJOA and/or Nurick reassessment at ~3–6 months, then every 6–12 months if stable, with patient education to self-report acute change rather than waiting for the next visit.
- Nurick grade correlates roughly with the mJOA lower-extremity subscore but does not capture hand function or sphincter involvement — report both when available.
