## Overview

The Winquist-Hansen classification describes the degree of cortical comminution at a femoral shaft fracture site. It was historically used to guide reaming and interlocking strategy for intramedullary (IM) nailing, and remains useful shorthand for communicating comminution severity and anticipated rotational/axial stability.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Type 0 | No comminution (simple transverse/short oblique) | Stable after nailing; static or dynamic locking both acceptable |
| Type I | Minimal comminution, small butterfly fragment, >75-90% cortical contact maintained | Rotationally and axially stable; static locking still routine but dynamization can be considered later |
| Type II | Comminuted fragment ≤50% of the cortical circumference, 25-50% cortical contact | Some rotational instability; **static interlocking recommended** |
| Type III | Comminuted fragment involves 50-100% of the circumference, <25% cortical contact | Significant rotational and axial instability; **static interlocking mandatory**, larger diameter nail preferred |
| Type IV | Segmental comminution, no cortical contact between proximal and distal fragments | Nail alone bears full load; **static interlocking mandatory**, consider larger/stronger nail, watch closely for shortening/nonunion, exchange nailing or augmentation if healing stalls |

## Pearls

- In modern practice essentially all femoral shaft fractures are statically locked regardless of Winquist type — dynamic locking is primarily reserved for later dynamization of a healing but slow-progressing fracture, not chosen prophylactically at the index surgery based on Winquist grade.
- Despite this shift away from grade-driven locking decisions, Winquist-Hansen remains useful shorthand for communicating comminution severity, anticipated construct stability, and nonunion/shortening risk to colleagues.
- Higher Winquist grades (III-IV) correlate with greater rotational/axial instability and should prompt consideration of a larger-diameter nail and closer radiographic follow-up for shortening or nonunion.
