## Overview

The Myerson classification describes Lisfranc (tarsometatarsal, TMT) fracture-dislocations by the direction and pattern of metatarsal displacement — total incongruity, partial incongruity, or divergent — rather than by energy or ligamentous vs bony injury. It is used mainly to plan the fixation construct(s) needed to address each ray.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Total incongruity — all five metatarsals displaced together in the same plane (sagittal or coronal) | Usually high-energy; needs full reduction of the entire TMT row |
| **B1** | Partial incongruity — medial displacement isolated to the 1st ray | Medial column fixation (screw or plate) |
| **B2** | Partial incongruity — lateral displacement of the 2nd–5th rays | Lateral/lesser TMT fixation, bridge plating or K-wires for mobile 4th–5th rays |
| **C1** | Divergent — partial (1st ray displaces medially, lesser rays displace laterally) | Combined medial screw/plate + lateral bridging fixation |
| **C2** | Divergent — complete divergent pattern of all rays | Most unstable/comminuted pattern; often favors primary arthrodesis of the medial 3 rays |

## Pearls

- Anatomic reduction is the dominant predictor of outcome regardless of which construct is chosen — nonanatomic reduction (>2 mm residual displacement) is strongly associated with post-traumatic arthritis and poor functional scores.
- Lateral column (4th–5th TMT) injuries are essentially never fused primarily — motion is preserved with temporary K-wire or bridge-plate fixation, since the lateral column tolerates and requires more physiologic motion.
- Myerson pattern is best used alongside the Nunley-Vertullo staging system: Myerson describes the anatomic pattern of a frank fracture-dislocation, while Nunley-Vertullo stages the low-energy, purely ligamentous "subtle Lisfranc" injury spectrum.
- Compartment syndrome of the foot is a classic association with high-energy Lisfranc injury — maintain a low threshold to measure compartment pressures in tense, disproportionately painful midfoot swelling.
