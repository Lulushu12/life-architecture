## Overview

MCL grading (I–III) stages medial collateral ligament injury by the amount of valgus gapping on stress testing at 30° of flexion. Because the MCL has excellent intrinsic healing capacity (good vascularity, extra-articular location), grade is used mainly to set expectations for brace duration and to identify the minority of injuries — Grade III with persistent instability or combined cruciate injury — that require surgery.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Tender, <5 mm valgus gapping at 30° vs. contralateral, firm endpoint | Symptomatic treatment, brief brace/ice, early sport-specific return, typically 1–3 weeks |
| **II** | 5–10 mm gapping at 30°, firm endpoint, partial tear | Hinged brace 2–4 weeks, protected weight-bearing, progressive ROM; return 4–6 weeks |
| **III** | >10 mm gapping at 30°, no firm endpoint (or gapping at 0° indicating combined posteromedial/cruciate injury), complete tear | Isolated: brace trial 6 weeks first-line given excellent healing potential, most heal without surgery; combined with ACL or with valgus instability persisting at 6 weeks → surgical repair/reconstruction, often staged with the cruciate |

## Pearls

- Valgus stress at 0° tests the PCL/posteromedial capsule/ACL as secondary restraints in addition to the MCL; gapping at full extension implies a more severe or combined injury, not isolated MCL laxity. Valgus stress at 30° isolates the superficial MCL from cruciate/capsular contribution and is the grading exam.
- Even Grade III isolated tears are given a structured 6-week brace trial before surgery is considered, given the ligament's strong intrinsic healing — this is a substantially longer non-operative trial than is offered for a comparable-grade PLC injury.
- Early protected ROM (avoiding prolonged immobilization) is the priority in non-operative management, since stiffness — not failure to heal — is the main complication of isolated MCL injury.
- A Grade III injury with gapping at 0° or combined ACL/MCL injury with gross valgus instability or Stener-like proximal MCL displacement precluding healing should not be given the standard 6-week brace trial — these findings signal a combined-injury pattern requiring earlier surgical evaluation.
