## Overview

OPLL (ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament) is heterotopic ossification of the PLL, strongly associated with East Asian ethnicity (prevalence ~2–4% in Japan/Korea vs. <1% in Western populations), genetic loci (including COL6A1), and metabolic factors (diabetes, obesity, calcium-phosphate metabolism abnormalities). Its morphologic subtype is not one of the headline severity/treatment classifications (mJOA, Nurick, K-line) but is essential companion information whenever K-line status and canal-occupying ratio are being interpreted for OPLL surgical planning, since OPLL causes myelopathy through static (and eventually dynamic) anterior cord compression that does not "shrink" the way a soft disc can — decompression must be adequate on the first attempt.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Segmental** | Ossification confined behind individual vertebral bodies, disc spaces spared | Generally lower canal-occupying ratio; more amenable to either anterior or posterior surgery; segmental instability at disc-level gaps should be assessed |
| **Continuous** | Ossification extends uninterrupted across multiple vertebral bodies and disc spaces | Higher canal-occupying ratio, higher rate of dural ossification; posterior indirect decompression favored if K-line positive, to avoid anterior dural adhesion risk |
| **Mixed** | Combination of segmental and continuous morphology | Behaves like continuous type at the involved continuous segments — highest complexity for surgical planning, often requiring combined anterior-posterior strategies |
| **Localized (circumscribed)** | Focal ossified mass, often at a single disc level, may bulge posteriorly independent of vertebral body margins | Can behave more like a large calcified disc herniation; anterior direct resection more feasible if limited to 1–2 levels |

## Pearls

- Continuous and mixed types carry the highest canal-occupying ratios and the highest rates of dural ossification (present in a meaningful minority of these subtypes) — the principal reason many surgeons default to posterior indirect decompression whenever K-line status allows it, reserving anterior direct resection for K-line-negative or focal/localized disease.
- Never attempt to fully resect/"float" a densely adherent OPLL mass without assessing dural ossification risk — anterior floating techniques carry high CSF leak risk in continuous/mixed morphology.
- Morphologic type must be read together with K-line status and canal-occupying ratio (≥60% predicts worse laminoplasty outcomes) — none of the three inputs is sufficient alone for surgical decision-making.
- CT is the modality of choice for characterizing morphologic type (continuous/segmental/mixed/localized) and calculating canal-occupying ratio; MRI better shows cord signal change but is inferior to CT for ossification morphology.
