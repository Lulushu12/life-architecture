## Overview

The Risser sign grades skeletal maturity by the degree of ossification of the iliac apophysis on a pelvic/spine radiograph. It is used in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) to estimate remaining growth — and therefore curve-progression risk and bracing candidacy — since progression risk tracks peak height velocity far more closely than chronologic age.

## Types

| Grade | Iliac apophysis ossification | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No ossification | Highest curve-progression risk; strong bracing candidate |
| **1** | 0–25% ossified | Substantial growth remaining |
| **2** | 25–50% ossified | Still meaningful growth remaining — this is the BrAIST bracing window (Risser 0–2) |
| **3** | 50–75% ossified | Growth slowing |
| **4** | 75–100% ossified, not yet fused | Low progression risk; brace weaning typically begins |
| **5** | Fully fused to ilium | Skeletal maturity reached; progression risk low outside large (>50°) curves |

## Pearls

- Risser 0–2 defines the BrAIST bracing eligibility window (25–40° Cobb with significant growth remaining) — Risser stage, not Cobb angle alone, gates the bracing decision.
- **Sanders skeletal maturity staging** (hand radiograph, 8 stages) is increasingly used alongside or instead of Risser because it correlates more precisely with peak height velocity and captures the adolescent growth spurt earlier — worth using in parallel when planning bracing or fusion-sparing surgery (vertebral body tethering) in a rapidly growing patient.
- Even at Risser 5 (skeletal maturity), large curves (>50°) retain meaningful progression risk into adulthood and should not be assumed static.
- A skeletally very immature spine (open triradiate cartilage) undergoing posterior-only fusion carries risk of the crankshaft phenomenon — relevant when Risser stage informs the choice between posterior-only and combined anterior/posterior fusion.
