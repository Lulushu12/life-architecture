## Overview

Stages the natural history of active Charcot neuroarthropathy by radiographic and clinical findings, guiding offloading intensity and the timing of elective reconstructive surgery. A stage 0 (prodromal) was added by later authors and is not part of the original Eichenholtz description.

## Types

| Stage | Name | Findings | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (added later, not original Eichenholtz) | Prodromal/inflammatory | Normal or near-normal radiographs, but clinical warmth/swelling/edema; MRI shows bone marrow edema | Highest index of suspicion stage — begin strict offloading (non-weightbearing or total contact casting) immediately, even before radiographic changes appear, as this is when deformity can still be prevented |
| I | Development (fragmentation) | Osseous fragmentation, joint subluxation/dislocation, debris formation | Strict immobilization, non-weightbearing progressing to TCC; no elective surgery unless limb-threatening |
| II | Coalescence | Absorption of debris, early new bone formation, sclerosis, fusion of larger fragments begins | Continue offloading; begin cautious progression toward weightbearing in TCC as stability improves |
| III | Reconstruction (consolidation) | Consolidation, remodeling, arch/joint contours re-establish (often with residual deformity) | Transition to bracing (CROW walker), gradual return to protected weightbearing and therapeutic footwear; elective reconstructive surgery, if needed, is best performed here |

## Pearls

- A warm, swollen, erythematous diabetic foot with intact skin and normal inflammatory markers should be **assumed Charcot until proven otherwise**, not cellulitis — a temperature differential >2 °C vs. the contralateral limb is the most useful bedside sign.
- Track the clinical exam trend, not a single radiograph: resolution of warmth (temperature differential normalizing to <1–2 °C) together with radiographic consolidation both guide the transition from stage II to stage III.
- Surgery during active disease (stage 0–II) is otherwise avoided except when limb-threatening (recurrent/non-healing ulceration, gross instability, deformity precluding shoe wear, infection); elective reconstruction is ideally deferred to stage III.
- Systematic review evidence is insufficient to mandate strict non-weightbearing TCC in all cases, though most centers continue to favor initial non-/minimal-weightbearing pending further trial data.
- Bisphosphonates have been studied as an adjunct to reduce bone turnover, but evidence for altering the disease course remains inconsistent and they are not a substitute for offloading.
- Roughly 20–40% of patients develop recurrent or new contralateral Charcot disease over their lifetime, supporting lifelong surveillance and bracing even after stage III consolidation.
