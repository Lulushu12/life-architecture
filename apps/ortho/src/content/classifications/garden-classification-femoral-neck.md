## Overview

The Garden classification grades femoral neck fractures by degree of displacement on the AP radiograph, from incomplete/valgus-impacted (Type I) through fully displaced (Type IV). Because displaced intracapsular fractures tear the retinacular vessels supplying the femoral head via the medial femoral circumflex artery, Garden grade is the primary driver of avascular necrosis (AVN) risk and therefore of the fixation-versus-arthroplasty decision.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Incomplete/valgus-impacted fracture | Lowest AVN risk; in situ percutaneous screw fixation; can be treated somewhat less urgently but still within the 24–48 h window |
| **II** | Complete fracture, non-displaced (trabecular alignment intact) | Behaves similarly to Type I; in situ fixation; watch for occult displacement on follow-up films |
| **III** | Complete fracture, partially displaced (trabeculae malaligned, but femoral head trabeculae still partially engage acetabular trabeculae) | Substantially higher AVN/nonunion risk; urgent anatomic reduction + fixation in younger patients; arthroplasty favored in elderly low-demand patients |
| **IV** | Complete fracture, fully displaced (femoral head trabeculae rotate to realign with acetabulum, but no bony contact with neck) | Highest AVN risk; arthroplasty is standard of care in patients >60 with displaced fractures; fixation reserved for young/biologically young patients accepting AVN/reoperation risk |

## Pearls

- In practice, Garden is often collapsed into "non-displaced (I–II)" vs. "displaced (III–IV)" for treatment decisions, because inter-observer reliability of the full 4-way split is poor while the binary split is highly reproducible and clinically dominant.
- Target reduction/alignment quality: Garden alignment index 155–180° on both AP and lateral views.
- Synovial fluid pressure from an intracapsular hematoma may itself tamponade retinacular flow — part of the rationale (of debated magnitude) for urgent capsulotomy/reduction in young displaced (Garden III–IV) fractures.
- For displaced fractures in patients over roughly 60–65 with low-to-moderate functional demand, fixation carries unacceptable reoperation rates (30–40% for AVN/nonunion/screw cutout) versus ~5–10% arthroplasty revision at comparable follow-up — this gap, not Garden grade alone, is what justifies the age/demand-based arthroplasty cutoff.
- A meaningful minority of truly non-displaced Garden I fractures managed non-operatively still displace and require rescue surgery, so serial radiographs and protected weight-bearing are mandatory if a non-operative path is chosen in a high-risk patient.
