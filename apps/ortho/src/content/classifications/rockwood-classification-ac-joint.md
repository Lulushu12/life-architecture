## Overview

The Rockwood classification grades acromioclavicular (AC) joint injuries by the degree of AC ligament, coracoclavicular (CC) ligament, and deltotrapezial fascial disruption, and by the direction/magnitude of clavicle displacement. It is the standard framework for deciding between nonoperative treatment and surgical reconstruction of AC joint injuries.

## Types

| Type | AC ligament | CC ligament | Deltotrapezial fascia | Clinical/radiographic finding | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Sprained, intact | Intact | Intact | Tender AC joint, normal X-ray | Nonoperative: sling, ice, early ROM as tolerated |
| **II** | Torn | Sprained, intact | Intact | AC joint widened, CC distance normal or minimally increased (<25%), clavicle slightly elevated relative to acromion | Nonoperative: sling 1–2 wk then progressive ROM |
| **III** | Torn | Torn | Intact (usually) | CC distance increased 25–100% vs contralateral; clavicle displaced superiorly but deltotrapezial fascia intact limits displacement | Nonoperative first-line for most; selective early ORIF/reconstruction in high-demand laborers/overhead athletes wanting fastest predictable return |
| **IV** | Torn | Torn | Torn | Clavicle displaced posteriorly, often buttonholed through/into trapezius (best seen on axillary view) | Operative: CC ligament reconstruction ± AC fixation |
| **V** | Torn | Torn | Torn (extensive) | CC distance increased >100–300% vs contralateral, gross superior displacement, marked deltotrapezial stripping | Operative: anatomic CC reconstruction (allograft/autograft ± suspensory device), acute repair preferred over delayed |
| **VI** | Torn | Torn | Torn | Clavicle displaced inferiorly, subacromial or subcoracoid (rare, high-energy) | Operative: reduction and CC reconstruction, often urgent given neurovascular proximity (subcoracoid variant) |

## Pearls

- Types I–III do NOT reliably need surgery (with the exception of select high-demand type III patients); types IV–VI need surgery — this is the single most useful management rule to remember.
- Normal CC distance is 1.1–1.3 cm; grading in practice relies on the percentage increase versus the contralateral side (25–100% for type III, >100–300% for type V), not an absolute number.
- Types I–III are collectively the most controversial group in the literature: current evidence (2024–2025 systematic reviews) shows no clear superiority of surgery for standard type III, but a possible edge for anatomic CC reconstruction in selected physically active young patients.
- AC joint post-traumatic arthrosis can develop after any grade of injury — including seemingly minor type I–II sprains — and after operative reconstruction; distal clavicle excision is the salvage procedure for refractory symptomatic cases.
- Type III is the type most often selected for surgery based on occupation/sport rather than the radiographic grade itself — factor in hand dominance, occupation, and overhead athletic demand, not just CC distance.
