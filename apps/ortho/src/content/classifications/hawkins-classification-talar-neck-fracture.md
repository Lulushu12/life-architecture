## Overview

The Hawkins classification grades talar neck fractures by the degree of subtalar and tibiotalar joint displacement/dislocation, with the Canale-Kelly modification adding a Type IV for concomitant talonavicular dislocation. Because the talus receives its entire blood supply through capsuloligamentous vessels that are progressively disrupted with displacement, Hawkins grade is the primary predictor of avascular necrosis (AVN) risk and drives the urgency of reduction and the treatment plan.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Nondisplaced vertical talar neck fracture (AVN rate ~0–13%) | Non-operative (NWB cast) or percutaneous screw fixation if any doubt about stability |
| **II** | Displaced with subtalar joint subluxation/dislocation; tibiotalar joint intact (AVN rate ~20–50%) | Urgent reduction (closed if possible) + ORIF; anatomic reduction is the strongest modifiable predictor of AVN/outcome |
| **III** | Displaced with both subtalar and tibiotalar dislocation (AVN rate ~50–100%, commonly cited ~70–100%) | Emergent reduction (open if closed fails) to relieve skin tension and vascular kink; ORIF once soft tissues allow; high complication rate (AVN, infection, malunion) regardless of timing |
| **IV** (Canale-Kelly) | Type III plus talonavicular subluxation/dislocation (AVN rate ~70–100%) | Same urgency as Type III; worst prognosis; counsel on high likelihood of AVN/arthrosis and possible salvage fusion |

## Pearls

- The **Hawkins sign** — subchondral lucency on AP/mortise view at 6–8 weeks — indicates preserved vascularity when present, but its absence does not reliably predict AVN: in one series 38% of Hawkins-sign-negative tali still avoided AVN, and correlation with 1-year AVN was weaker than classically taught. Treat it as supportive, not definitive.
- Anatomic reduction is the single most important modifiable determinant of outcome across all Hawkins types, more so than time-to-fixation.
- Contemporary data show that time to *definitive fixation* (<24 h vs later) does not clearly change AVN rate once reduction is achieved — the priority is urgent *reduction*, not necessarily emergent final fixation.
- Hawkins III fractures carry significantly higher rates of AVN, infection, and reoperation than Hawkins II, while nonunion rates do not significantly differ between types.
- AVN risk factors exist beyond Hawkins type alone, supporting an individualized risk conversation rather than relying on classification type in isolation.
- Core decompression is not evidence-supported for established talar AVN; collapse is managed with staged bracing/protected weight-bearing and, if progressive, tibiotalocalcaneal fusion or talar body allograft/prosthetic replacement.
