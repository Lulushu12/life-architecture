## Overview

The Verdan zones divide the flexor tendon course from fingertip to forearm into five anatomic segments (I-V) based on surrounding structures (fibro-osseous sheath, pulleys, carpal tunnel). Zone determines which tendons are at risk, the technical difficulty and adhesion risk of repair, and the rehabilitation protocol used after laceration or repair.

## Types

| Zone | Anatomic boundaries | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Distal to FDS insertion (distal to A4, over middle phalanx to fingertip) | FDP only; often direct repair or advancement (avoid >1 cm advancement — risk of quadriga); consider suture anchor if very distal/avulsion (jersey finger) |
| II | A1 pulley to FDS insertion ("no man's land") — over proximal/middle phalanx | Both FDP+FDS in tight fibro-osseous sheath; highest adhesion/re-rupture risk; core+epitendinous repair with A2/A4 pulley preservation is critical; early active motion protocol standard |
| III | Distal edge of carpal tunnel (transverse carpal ligament) to A1 pulley (palm) | Lumbrical origin region; generally good prognosis, less sheath constraint |
| IV | Within the carpal tunnel | Multiple tendons + median nerve in confined space; often combined nerve injury; may need carpal tunnel release as part of exposure/repair |
| V | Proximal to carpal tunnel, forearm | Multiple structures often injured together (spaghetti wrist); best prognosis zone generally, but multi-structure repair complexity |

## Pearls

- Zone II is called "no man's land" because FDP and FDS travel together within a tight fibro-osseous sheath (annular A1-A5 and cruciate C1-C3 pulleys), giving it the highest adhesion and re-rupture risk of any zone; A2 and A4 are the biomechanically critical pulleys and must be preserved or judiciously vented (not sacrificed) to avoid bowstringing.
- Tendon nutrition is dual — vincular blood supply plus synovial diffusion — and is most tenuous in Zone II, compounding its poor healing environment relative to other zones.
- Zone I FDP avulsion ("jersey finger") is repaired with a suture anchor or pull-out button depending on retraction level and bone stock; over-advancement (>1 cm) risks quadriga (incomplete flexion of adjacent, uninjured fingers because one FDP is tensioned too tight). Delayed presentation (>3-4 weeks) may preclude primary repair, requiring staged reconstruction (silicone rod then tendon graft) or DIP fusion as salvage.
- Zone IV injuries frequently involve the median nerve given the confined carpal tunnel space, and exposure/repair may require carpal tunnel release.
- Zone V ("spaghetti wrist") injuries typically involve multiple tendons and nerves simultaneously but carry the best overall prognosis of the five zones because the tissue bed is less constrained than the digital sheath.
- Regardless of zone, a ≥4-strand core repair plus circumferential epitendinous suture is the modern standard required to permit early active motion (EAM) protocols; 2-strand repairs are biomechanically insufficient for EAM and predispose to gap/rupture.
