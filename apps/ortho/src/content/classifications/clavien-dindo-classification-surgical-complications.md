## Overview

The Clavien-Dindo classification grades the severity of a postoperative complication by the intensity of treatment required to manage it, rather than by the complication's clinical description. It is used for standardized complication reporting, morbidity/mortality review, and outcome benchmarking across orthopedic (and general surgical) procedures.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Any deviation from the normal postoperative course without need for pharmacologic treatment beyond antiemetics, antipyretics, analgesics, diuretics, and electrolytes, or surgical/endoscopic/radiologic intervention; bedside-opened wound infections are included | Observation, symptomatic management; does not change discharge planning materially (e.g. postop ileus managed expectantly, superficial seroma opened at bedside) |
| **II** | Requires pharmacologic treatment with drugs beyond those allowed for Grade I, including blood transfusion or total parenteral nutrition | Extends length of stay, triggers medical consult (e.g. hematology, ID) — e.g. transfusion for symptomatic anemia, antibiotics for cellulitis |
| **IIIa** | Requires surgical, endoscopic, or radiologic intervention **not** performed under general anesthesia | Bedside/local I&D, percutaneous drainage under sedation |
| **IIIb** | Requires intervention under general anesthesia | Return to OR for hematoma evacuation, irrigation & debridement |
| **IVa** | Life-threatening complication with single-organ dysfunction requiring IC/ICU-level care | Escalation of care level, prognosis discussion — e.g. acute MI, single-organ renal failure requiring dialysis |
| **IVb** | Life-threatening complication with multi-organ dysfunction | ICU, multidisciplinary critical care — e.g. sepsis with multi-organ failure |
| **V** | Death of the patient | Mortality/morbidity review |
| **suffix "d"** | Patient discharged with the complication present (disability) | Flags need for outpatient follow-up/rehab planning — e.g. persistent nerve palsy at discharge |

## Pearls

- Grading is driven by the treatment actually delivered, not by the underlying diagnosis — the same complication can grade differently depending on how it was managed (e.g. a wound issue opened at the bedside is Grade I, but the identical finding taken back to the OR under general anesthesia is Grade IIIb).
- The IIIa/IIIb split hinges specifically on anesthesia type (general vs. not), not on invasiveness of the intervention — a percutaneous drainage under sedation is IIIa even if technically demanding, while a straightforward bedside procedure done under GA would be IIIb.
- The "d" suffix is easy to overlook but matters for discharge/rehab planning documentation, since it flags that the complication is still present (not resolved) at the time the patient leaves the hospital.
- This tool grades severity/resource intensity, not causation — it is complementary to, not a substitute for, root-cause complication classifications used elsewhere in perioperative review (e.g. infection-specific criteria for PJI).
