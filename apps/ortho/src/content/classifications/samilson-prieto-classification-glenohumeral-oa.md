## Overview

The Samilson-Prieto classification grades secondary glenohumeral osteoarthritis by the size of the inferior humeral head osteophyte and accompanying joint-space narrowing. It was originally described for OA following anterior instability surgery and is still used generically for grading secondary/post-instability arthropathy severity, setting the threshold between arthroscopic debridement/capsular release and arthroplasty in younger post-instability patients.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Mild** | Inferior humeral head osteophyte 1–3 mm | Non-operative; activity modification, PT, monitor |
| **Moderate** | Osteophyte 3–7 mm, mild joint-space narrowing | Consider arthroscopic debridement/capsular release if young and stiffness-predominant; injections |
| **Severe** | Osteophyte >7 mm, marked joint-space narrowing/obliteration | Arthroplasty per glenoid version and cuff status; remove retained instability hardware |

## Pearls

- In patients with retained hardware from prior stabilization surgery (anchors, screws), identify it pre-operatively on CT — impingement or chondral damage from prominent hardware is a treatable cause of pain distinct from the arthritis itself, and hardware removal alone can sometimes be combined with arthroscopic debridement in mild-moderate grades before committing a young patient to arthroplasty.
- This is fundamentally a secondary-OA grading system layered on top of a prior instability history — the arthroplasty decision for severe grades still requires assessing glenoid version (Walch-type wear pattern) and cuff status, since the underlying joint mechanics are those of glenohumeral OA.
- Reserve arthroplasty for severe disease or failure of debridement/capsular release; mild-moderate grades in young patients favor a trial of the less-invasive arthroscopic pathway first.
