## Overview

The Gross Motor Function Classification System (GMFCS) grades self-initiated movement ability in children with cerebral palsy across five levels, based on sitting, walking, and use of wheeled mobility. It is the primary driver of hip surveillance frequency, spasticity treatment selection, and prognosis for ambulation.

## Types

Age-banded descriptors below reflect the 6–12-year band.

| Level | Functional description | Hip displacement risk (untreated) | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Walks without limitations | Low (~1–5%) | Clinical exam only; XR if clinical concern |
| II | Walks with limitations (long distances, uneven terrain, crowds) | Low-moderate (~10–15%) | AP pelvis XR at ~age 2 and 6, then as indicated |
| III | Walks using a hand-held mobility device; may self-propel a wheelchair | Moderate-high (~40–50%) | AP pelvis XR every 6–12 months from ~12–18mo |
| IV | Self-mobility with limitations; may use powered mobility, transported in a manual wheelchair | High (~70%) | AP pelvis XR every 6–12 months from ~12–18mo, closer if migration percentage (MP) rising |
| V | Transported in a manual wheelchair; severely limited self-mobility | Very high (up to ~90%) | AP pelvis XR every 6–12 months from ~12mo until maturity/hip stable |

## Pearls

- Hip surveillance is anchored on migration percentage (MP, the Reimers index) — the percentage of the femoral head lateral to Perkin's line, uncovered by the acetabulum — measured on a standardized supine AP pelvis radiograph. MP >30% is the surgical referral threshold; MP >60% is subluxation; 100% is dislocation.
- GMFCS level also guides selection among spasticity treatments: selective dorsal rhizotomy (SDR) is favored for ambulatory GMFCS II–III with good selective motor control (typically age 3–8y); intrathecal baclofen is favored for generalized spasticity in GMFCS IV–V.
- GMFCS level predicts likelihood of ambulation and helps determine whether a child is a candidate for single-event multilevel surgery (SEMLS, ambulatory CP) versus salvage hip/spine surgery (lower-functioning, non-ambulatory CP).
- Missed or inadequate surveillance is the dominant modifiable risk factor for progression to painful hip dislocation — hip displacement is the dominant treatable cause of pain and loss of function in non-ambulatory CP.
- The Swedish CPUP national registry protocol, the Australian/AACPDM-aligned consensus guideline, and North American POSNA-endorsed practice are broadly convergent — all use GMFCS-stratified radiographic intervals anchored on migration percentage. The main cross-program variation is in the precise interval (6 vs 12 months) at intermediate GMFCS levels rather than in the underlying MP-driven treatment thresholds, so a single GMFCS-based surveillance schedule is defensible and guideline-concordant regardless of which national program a center formally follows.
