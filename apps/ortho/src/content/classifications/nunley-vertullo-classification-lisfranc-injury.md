## Overview

The Nunley-Vertullo classification stages low-energy, "subtle" Lisfranc injuries — the purely or predominantly ligamentous sprains typically seen in athletes after an axial-load/twisting mechanism — by combining clinical exam with weight-bearing radiographic (and MRI/bone scan) findings of diastasis and arch height. It is the main tool for deciding non-operative vs operative management in this injury subgroup, as distinct from the Myerson classification used for frank fracture-dislocations.

## Types

| Stage | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Sprain; normal weight-bearing radiograph alignment and arch height; MRI/bone scan positive for Lisfranc ligament injury | Non-operative: protected weight-bearing (boot/cast) 4–6 weeks, progressive return to sport |
| **II** | 1–5 mm diastasis between 1st–2nd metatarsal bases on weight-bearing view; no loss of arch height | Operative (ligamentous instability without bony collapse) — anatomic reduction and screw fixation or primary fusion depending on surgeon/joint condition |
| **III** | Diastasis >5 mm plus loss of arch (decreased calcaneal–5th metatarsal height on weight-bearing lateral) | Operative — ORIF or primary arthrodesis; worst prognosis of the three stages, higher risk of post-traumatic arthritis |

## Pearls

- Diastasis of >2 mm between the 1st and 2nd metatarsal bases is abnormal on AP/weight-bearing view; >5 mm defines Stage III.
- A normal non-weight-bearing radiograph does not exclude a Lisfranc injury — insist on weight-bearing or gravity/manual stress views before discharging a patient with midfoot tenderness and a normal supine film.
- MRI has high sensitivity for Lisfranc ligament rupture and is the modality of choice for suspected purely ligamentous injury with normal/equivocal weight-bearing radiographs; bone scan is historically used but has largely been supplanted by MRI/weight-bearing CT.
- Look for the "fleck sign" (avulsion off the medial cuneiform or 2nd metatarsal base) and loss of alignment of the medial border of the 2nd metatarsal with the medial border of the middle cuneiform as adjunctive radiographic clues.
- Stage I is the only stage managed non-operatively; any frank diastasis or instability (Stage II/III) is operative.
