## Overview

The Budapest criteria are the primary clinical diagnostic criteria for complex regional pain syndrome, combining patient-reported symptoms and examiner-observed signs across four categories, plus exclusion of an alternative diagnosis. They apply identically to CRPS Type I (no definable nerve lesion) and Type II/causalgia (following a defined peripheral nerve injury) — the two types differ only in the presence of a definable nerve lesion.

## Types

| Category | Symptom (patient-reported; need ≥1 in ≥3 of 4 categories) | Sign (examiner-observed; need ≥1 in ≥2 of 4 categories) |
|---|---|---|
| Sensory | Hyperesthesia and/or allodynia | Hyperalgesia (pinprick) and/or allodynia (light touch, temperature, deep pressure, joint movement) |
| Vasomotor | Temperature asymmetry, skin color change/asymmetry | Temperature asymmetry (>1°C), skin color change/asymmetry |
| Sudomotor/edema | Edema, sweating change/asymmetry | Edema, sweating change/asymmetry |
| Motor/trophic | Decreased range of motion, motor dysfunction (weakness, tremor, dystonia), trophic changes (hair, nail, skin) | Decreased range of motion, motor dysfunction, trophic changes |

Diagnosis additionally requires **continuing pain disproportionate to any inciting event** and **no other diagnosis that better explains** the presentation.

## Pearls

- Clinical diagnostic criteria performance: sensitivity ~0.85, specificity ~0.69.
- Stricter **research criteria** (requiring a sign in all four categories, not just two) trade sensitivity for higher specificity (~0.68 fewer false-positive inclusions) and are used for trial enrollment, not routine clinical diagnosis.
- Disproportionate pain plus autonomic/trophic change persisting beyond the expected fracture-healing timeline is a red flag — early recognition inside the first 8–12 weeks materially changes trajectory.
- Imaging is supportive, not diagnostic: triple-phase bone scan (delayed-phase periarticular uptake) can help in equivocal early presentations, and plain radiographs may show patchy periarticular osteopenia (Sudeck's atrophy) later in the course, but normal imaging does not exclude CRPS.
- 2025 ASIPP guidelines build on (not replace) the Budapest criteria, adding a time-dependent framework requiring criteria to persist ≥12 months before a case is classified as chronic CRPS.
