## Overview

The Judet classification grades pediatric radial neck fractures by degree of angulation, guiding the choice between casting, closed/percutaneous reduction, and (rarely) open reduction. The radial neck physis is intracapsular, and the radial head is a secondary ossification center supplied by an anastomotic ring that is vulnerable to disruption with high-energy displacement or aggressive open manipulation — the basis for favoring percutaneous techniques over open reduction at essentially every grade.

## Types

| Type | Angulation | Definition | Management |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | <30° | Minimal displacement | Sling/cast, early motion at 1–2 weeks |
| II | 30–60° | Moderate angulation, translation may be present | Closed reduction (manipulation ± percutaneous flexible-nail-assisted reduction) if >30–45° or per functional threshold |
| III | 60–90° | Significant angulation/translation | Closed reduction with percutaneous techniques (e.g., Métaizeau intramedullary reduction/"Israeli technique") ; avoid open reduction if at all possible |
| IV | >90° or complete displacement/dislocation of the radial head | Severe, may be associated with elbow dislocation or Monteggia-equivalent | Percutaneous reduction preferred even at this grade; open reduction only if percutaneous fails — highest AVN/synostosis/stiffness risk of any subtype |

## Pearls

- Functional outcome correlates with **residual angulation and associated injury** more than absolute Judet grade — up to 30–45° residual angulation is often accepted in children under ~10 years with preserved forearm rotation rather than pursuing open reduction, because remodeling of the radial neck (unlike malrotation) is real.
- Open surgery carries a disproportionately high complication rate: AVN in up to 20–30% after open reduction of severely displaced fractures.
- Loss of forearm rotation is often the more functionally limiting sequela than residual angulation itself.
- Forearm rotation, not extension/flexion, is the functional parameter to track during recovery.
- Radial neck (Judet I–II, ≤30°) is managed with cast/sling; Judet III–IV (>30–45°) escalates to closed reduction with percutaneous manipulation, with intramedullary elastic nail reduction and open reduction reserved as last resorts.
