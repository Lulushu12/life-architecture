## Overview

The Cierny-Mader classification stages chronic osteomyelitis by combining an **anatomic type** (extent of bone involvement) with a **physiologic host class** (the patient's ability to tolerate treatment), producing a composite clinical stage (e.g., "type IIIBs"). It is used to decide between curative debridement, staged reconstruction, suppressive antibiotics, or amputation, and this composite stage is what should appear in the operative note since it drives the debridement-versus-suppression decision.

## Types

### Anatomic type

| Type | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| I — Medullary | Infection confined to the medullary canal/endosteal surface (e.g., hematogenous, infected IM nail) | Intramedullary reaming/unreaming often sufficient; implant removal if present |
| II — Superficial | Infection limited to exposed bone surface, contiguous soft-tissue source, cortex only | Surgical debridement of exposed cortex + soft-tissue coverage; no deep excision needed |
| III — Localized | Well-marginated, full-thickness cortical sequestrum; segment remains mechanically stable after debridement | Radical debridement to bleeding bone possible without destabilizing the bone; dead-space management |
| IV — Diffuse | Circumferential/permeative involvement; bone unstable before or rendered unstable by debridement (e.g., infected nonunion, infected segmental defect) | Requires debridement **and** skeletal stabilization/reconstruction (external fixation, staged bone transport, induced-membrane/Masquelet, vascularized bone graft) |

### Physiologic host

| Host class | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| A host | Normal immune/metabolic/vascular status | Standard curative strategy pursued |
| B host (Bs = systemic; Bl = local) | Compromised: diabetes, malnutrition, renal/hepatic failure, immunosuppression, smoking, PAD (Bs); lymphedema, venous stasis, radiation, scarring, major-vessel injury (Bl) | Optimize modifiable factors pre-operatively; higher failure/complication rate anticipated |
| C host | Treatment morbidity would exceed that of the disease itself | Suppressive antibiotics, amputation, or palliation may be more appropriate than attempted cure |

Clinical stage = anatomic type + host class.

## Pearls

- Radical debridement to punctate ("paprika sign") bleeding bone is the single most important determinant of cure — inadequate debridement is the leading cause of recurrence.
- B-host status is often at least partially reversible: glycemic control (HbA1c target <8%), smoking cessation, and nutritional optimization (albumin/prealbumin trending) materially affect cure probability and should be pursued in parallel with, not instead of, surgical planning.
- Recurrence after "definitive" treatment is reported in roughly one-fifth to one-third of cases even with modern staged debridement protocols, materially higher in B/C hosts and with inadequate initial debridement.
- For C hosts, amputation may ultimately be required for uncontrolled type IV disease in a severely compromised host — it should be discussed as a legitimate, sometimes superior, option rather than only as a last resort after repeated failed limb-salvage attempts.
- Bone infection resists cure once biofilm has formed on necrotic bone (sequestrum) or implant surfaces — antibiotics alone are curative only in the earliest, non-biofilm stage, which is why staging drives a debridement-first strategy for types III–IV.
