## Overview

The American Society of Anesthesiologists (ASA) Physical Status Classification grades a patient's preoperative systemic health on a scale of I (healthy) to VI (declared brain-dead, organ donor), with an "E" modifier appended for emergency cases. It is used at the preoperative assessment to gauge anesthetic and perioperative risk, guide the intensity of preoperative workup, inform anesthetic technique selection, and set expectations for complication/mortality benchmarking.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Normal healthy patient (non-smoker, no/minimal alcohol use) | Routine anesthetic risk counseling; minimal added risk |
| **II** | Mild systemic disease, no functional limitation (well-controlled HTN/DM, smoker, social alcohol use, pregnancy, obesity 30<BMI<40) | Standard optimization; no special workup usually needed |
| **III** | Severe systemic disease with functional limitation (poorly controlled DM/HTN, COPD, morbid obesity BMI≥40, active hepatitis, ESRD on dialysis, cardiac stent >3 months ago) | Formal preop medical/cardiac optimization; consider regional over GA; higher expected LOS/complication rate |
| **IV** | Severe systemic disease, constant threat to life (recent <3 month MI/CVA/TIA, ongoing cardiac ischemia, severe valve dysfunction, sepsis, DIC) | Multidisciplinary/anesthesia-led preop review; ICU/HDU bed planning; shared decision-making on elective vs deferral, discuss goals of care |
| **V** | Moribund, not expected to survive without the operation (ruptured aneurysm, massive trauma, ischemic bowel with multi-organ failure) | Emergent/life-saving indication only; explicit consent re: mortality risk |
| **VI** | Declared brain-dead, organs procured for donation | Not applicable to elective orthopedic practice |
| **"E" modifier** | Appended to any grade for emergency surgery (delay would significantly increase risk to life or organ) — e.g. ASA IIIE, ASA IVE | Independently raises complication/mortality risk versus the same grade performed electively |

## Pearls

- The "E" suffix is additive to risk, not merely descriptive — it independently increases morbidity/mortality benchmarking compared with the same base grade performed electively, and should factor into consent and resource planning separately from the grade itself.
- ASA III is where practical management inflects: this is the grade at which formal cardiac/pulmonary workup and consideration of regional anesthesia over general anesthesia become standard, rather than an incremental step from ASA II.
- ASA grade is a coarse, general systemic-health tool — it does not substitute for procedure-specific risk tools; in orthopedics it is used alongside (not instead of) frailty scales (Clinical Frailty Scale, mFI-5) and VTE risk tools (Caprini) for a fuller perioperative risk picture.
- ASA VI has no application in elective orthopedic practice and exists only for completeness of the classification scale.
