## Overview

The Jefferson fracture is a fracture of the C1 (atlas) ring, described by fracture pattern rather than a formally numbered type system, with stability determined functionally by transverse atlantal ligament (TAL) integrity. It is used to guide collar-versus-halo-versus-fusion decisions in any C1 ring fracture.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Fracture pattern (descriptive)** | Unilateral or bilateral anterior/posterior arch fractures; 2-, 3-, or 4-part ring fracture | Not itself a stability determinant — stability is assessed functionally via TAL status below |
| **TAL intact — Dickman type I / overhang <6.9mm** | Bony avulsion in continuity (Dickman type I), or rule-of-Spence combined C1 lateral mass overhang on C2 <6.9mm on open-mouth/coronal CT | Rigid collar; halo if ADI borderline |
| **TAL disrupted — Dickman type II / overhang ≥6.9mm** | Midsubstance ligament tear (Dickman type II), or significant combined lateral mass overhang (rule of Spence, historically rounded to 7mm) | Halo immobilization or C1-C2/occipitocervical fusion, particularly with concomitant occipitocervical or C2 injury |

## Pearls

- The rule of Spence (combined C1 lateral mass overhang on C2 >6.9mm) is an indirect radiographic surrogate for TAL rupture, not a direct measurement — direct MRI assessment of TAL integrity is increasingly used to refine the call, particularly when overhang is borderline.
- Adult ADI upper limit of normal is 3mm (children 4–5mm due to growth-plate laxity); ADI is used alongside the rule of Spence when assessing atlantoaxial stability.
- Isolated posterior arch fractures with an intact anterior arch and intact TAL rarely need surgery — not every Jefferson-pattern fracture implies ligamentous instability.
- The AO Surgery Reference upper cervical injury classification update (May 2024) introduced a new C0-C2 classification system intended to standardize description and treatment decision-making, complementing (not replacing) this functional TAL-based approach for the Jefferson fracture specifically.
