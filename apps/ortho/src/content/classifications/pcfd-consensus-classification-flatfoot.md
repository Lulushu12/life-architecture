## Overview

The 2020 international consensus classification (de Cesar Netto et al., *Foot & Ankle International*) for **progressive collapsing foot deformity (PCFD)** — the term now preferred over "adult acquired flatfoot deformity" because it reflects the multiplanar, progressive nature of the disease rather than failure of a single tendon. It replaces the linear **Johnson & Strom stages I–IV** (and Myerson's addition of a stage IIIB/IV subtype for ankle valgus) with a multiplanar, component-based system: rather than assigning one stage number, each foot is described across independent deformity axes, each graded present/absent and flexible/rigid, plus arthritis status. Use it whenever staging PCFD/tibialis posterior dysfunction (TPD, the tendon-driven subset of PCFD) to plan a component-by-component ("à la carte") reconstruction rather than a fixed stage-based algorithm.

## Types

| Deformity component | What is assessed | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Hindfoot valgus | Standing heel valgus, subtalar joint alignment | Drives need for medializing calcaneal osteotomy (flexible) or subtalar fusion (rigid/arthritic) |
| Peritalar subluxation / talonavicular (TN) uncoverage | % TN uncoverage on AP foot or weightbearing CT (WBCT) | >40% uncoverage favors adding lateral column lengthening; severe uncoverage with arthritis favors TN or triple fusion |
| Forefoot abduction | Talo-1st MT angle on AP, talonavicular coverage angle | Guides lateral column lengthening (Evans osteotomy or calcaneocuboid distraction arthrodesis) |
| Forefoot varus / medial ray instability | Dorsiflexion of 1st ray relative to hindfoot with hindfoot corrected | Guides Cotton osteotomy (medial cuneiform opening wedge) or 1st TMT/naviculocuneiform fusion |
| Ankle valgus / deltoid insufficiency | Tibiotalar tilt on mortise/WBCT, deltoid integrity on MRI | Flexible: deltoid reconstruction alongside hindfoot correction. Rigid/arthritic: consider TTC or pantalar fusion |
| Degenerative changes | Subtalar, talonavicular, calcaneocuboid, ankle joint arthritis on imaging | Presence of arthritis converts a joint-preserving plan (osteotomies/tendon transfer) into a fusion plan for that joint |

**Practical translation**: a flexible, no-arthritis foot with isolated hindfoot valgus and mild TN uncoverage is managed with MCO + FDL transfer; add lateral column lengthening as uncoverage increases; add Cotton osteotomy/medial column fusion when forefoot varus/medial instability is present; add deltoid reconstruction when ankle valgus is flexible; any joint with fixed deformity or arthritis is fused rather than osteotomized.

## Pearls

- Talonavicular uncoverage >40% on AP foot radiograph is the key threshold for adding lateral column lengthening; WBCT-derived hindfoot moment arm/valgus measures are increasingly replacing plain-film measures.
- The legacy Johnson-Strom/Myerson stages remain useful shorthand in conversation (Stage I = tendinosis, no deformity; Stage II = flexible deformity; Stage III = rigid deformity/arthritis of the hindfoot; Stage IV = ankle valgus/deltoid involvement) but are no longer precise enough to plan surgery from alone.
- Weightbearing CT measurements materially change component-by-component classification compared with conventional radiographs, and are an increasingly standard (though not yet mandatory) adjunct when applying the consensus classification.
- Patient-reported outcomes correlate imperfectly with radiographic correction after reconstruction — do not counsel expected benefit from radiographs alone.
- Undercorrection of forefoot abduction or medial column instability (skipping lateral column lengthening or medial stabilization when the classification indicates it) is the most common cause of recurrent deformity after isolated osteotomy + tendon transfer.
