## Overview

The International Cartilage Repair Society (ICRS) classification grades the depth and severity of chondral lesions of the knee (Grade 0–4). It is used arthroscopically or on MRI to determine whether a lesion is a candidate for cartilage restoration and, along with lesion size, containment, and bipolarity, guides the choice among microfracture, OAT, MACI, and osteochondral allograft.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal cartilage | No treatment |
| 1 | Nearly normal — superficial fissures/soft indentation (1A) or superficial lacerations (1B) | Non-operative; not a restoration candidate |
| 2 | Abnormal — lesion extends <50% of cartilage depth | Debridement/chondroplasty if symptomatic; not a graft indication alone |
| 3 | Severely abnormal — defect extends >50% depth, to calcified layer, not through subchondral bone (3A/3B/3C by depth; 3D includes blistering) | Threshold zone for restorative procedures (microfracture, OAT, ACI/MACI) if focal, contained, symptomatic |
| 4 | Severely abnormal, full-thickness with subchondral bone penetration (4A) or osteochondral (4B) | OAT or OCA (osteochondral graft needed if bone loss present); ACI/MACI alone insufficient if subchondral bone deficient |

## Pearls

- The classic indication zone for OAT/ACI/OCA grafting is ICRS grade 3B–4 (equivalent to Outerbridge III–IV) with a contained, focal, full-thickness lesion; diffuse/bipolar/kissing lesions and uncorrected malalignment are contraindications.
- ICRS also provides a separate arthroscopic OCD staging system (Grade I–IV) distinct from this chondral-defect grade — see the ICRS OCD staging article.
- ICRS and Outerbridge are frequently used interchangeably in practice (both grade roughly 0–4 with similar depth criteria); ICRS is the more granular, currently preferred research standard, but Outerbridge remains ubiquitous in US operative notes.
- MRI tends to underestimate true lesion size and depth compared with arthroscopic findings — confirm grade and dimensions intraoperatively before committing to a graft size.
- Grading must be paired with an assessment of containment, bipolarity, and lower-limb alignment: a restoration procedure performed without correcting a concurrent >5° malalignment, meniscal deficiency, or ligament instability is set up to fail regardless of graft choice.
