## Overview

The Bado classification (with the Jupiter modification for adult variants) categorizes Monteggia injuries — a proximal ulna fracture combined with radiocapitellar dislocation — by the direction of radial head dislocation. Direction determines the associated injury pattern and helps flag "Monteggia variants" where coronoid or radial head fractures accompany the ulna fracture and change the treatment from simple anatomic ulna fixation to a complex elbow fracture-dislocation protocol.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Frequency | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Ulna shaft fracture (proximal/mid) with **anterior** dislocation of the radial head, apex-anterior ulnar angulation | Most common (~60–70%) | Anatomic ulna plate fixation typically reduces the radial head closed; confirm concentric reduction on true lateral post-fixation; explore the radiocapitellar joint if not concentric |
| **II** | Ulna fracture with **posterior/posterolateral** dislocation of the radial head, apex-posterior angulation | Second most common in adults | Often associated with coronoid and/or radial head fractures (Monteggia "variant"/equivalent) — treat as a complex elbow fracture-dislocation, address coronoid/radial head per terrible-triad principles |
| **III** | Ulna metaphyseal fracture with **lateral/anterolateral** dislocation of the radial head | Predominantly pediatric | Anatomic ulna fixation; assess LCL integrity |
| **IV** | Fracture of **both radius and ulna** with anterior dislocation of the radial head | Rare | Fix both forearm bones anatomically; radial head reduction follows ulna length/alignment restoration |

Adult Monteggia Bado I is roughly **70%** of adult cases; missed proximal radial head dislocation is the most common cause of poor outcome.

## Pearls

- **Key principle across all Bado types:** anatomic restoration of ulnar length and alignment is the primary maneuver that reduces the radial head. If the radial head does not reduce concentrically after solid anatomic ulna fixation, suspect an interposed annular ligament, an unaddressed radial head/neck fracture, or malreduction of the ulna, and re-address before closing.
- Document the direction of radial head dislocation on the initial films specifically — a "missed Monteggia" (chronic radial head dislocation after an ulna fracture treated as isolated) is a recognized and largely preventable diagnostic failure. Always trace the radiocapitellar line on every elbow and forearm view.
- Bado II injuries are frequently "Monteggia variants" with associated coronoid and/or radial head fractures — these require terrible-triad-style sequential management rather than isolated ulna fixation.
- PIN palsy accompanies many Monteggia/radial neck fracture patterns — document it before reduction; it is usually neurapraxic and can be observed for approximately 3 months before considering exploration.
