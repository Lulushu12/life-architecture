## Overview

The Allman classification is the original anatomic classification of clavicle fractures, dividing them into three groups by simple location (middle, distal, or medial third). It remains useful for quick anatomic communication but has largely been superseded by the more granular, prognostic Robinson classification for treatment decision-making.

## Types

| Group | Location | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Middle third | ~80% of all clavicle fractures; treatment decisions in practice are driven more by displacement/comminution (see Robinson classification) than by group alone |
| **II** | Distal (lateral) third | ~15%; CC ligament status is key to stability — further subdivided by the Neer distal clavicle classification |
| **III** | Medial third | ~5%; rare, consider mediastinal injury with high-energy mechanism |

## Pearls

- Allman gives location only, not displacement or comminution — it does not predict nonunion risk on its own, which is why Robinson (for middle-third fractures) and Neer (for distal-third fractures) are layered on top of it in practice.
- Group III (medial third) fractures are rare enough that a high-energy mechanism should prompt evaluation for associated mediastinal injury rather than treating it as a routine clavicle fracture.
- Group II (distal third) fractures are the group where CC ligament integrity — not the Allman group itself — determines stability and the need for surgery; see the Neer distal clavicle classification for the operative decision framework.
