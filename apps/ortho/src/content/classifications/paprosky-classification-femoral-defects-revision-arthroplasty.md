## Overview

The Paprosky femoral classification grades femoral bone loss for revision total hip arthroplasty planning, most often applied during revision for aseptic stem loosening or periprosthetic fracture (Vancouver B2/B3). It directs the femoral reconstruction ladder from a standard primary stem through long tapered-fluted stems and, in the most severe defects, impaction grafting or proximal femoral replacement.

## Types

| Type | Features | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| I | Minimal metaphyseal loss, intact diaphysis | Standard primary or proximally coated cementless stem |
| II | Extensive metaphyseal damage, intact diaphysis | Extensively coated cylindrical or tapered-fluted stem with diaphyseal fixation |
| IIIA | Metaphysis non-supportive, ≥4 cm diaphyseal isthmus available for fixation | Tapered-fluted modular/monoblock stem, diaphyseal engagement |
| IIIB | Metaphysis non-supportive, <4 cm diaphyseal fixation available | Long tapered-fluted stem, consider bypassing defect by ≥2 cortical diameters |
| IV | Extensive diaphyseal damage, isthmus not supportive, wide canal | Impaction grafting with cemented stem, or proximal femoral replacement/allograft-prosthetic composite |

## Pearls

- The key intraoperative decision point is whether **≥4 cm** of intact diaphyseal "scratch-fit" isthmus is available distal to the defect — this threshold separates a standard-length tapered-fluted stem (IIIA) from a long tapered-fluted stem requiring extended diaphyseal engagement or supplementary strut allograft/plate (IIIB).
- Type IV femurs (stovepipe canals, non-supportive diaphysis) generally require either impaction bone grafting with a cemented stem or a proximal femoral replacement/allograft-prosthetic composite, particularly in low-demand elderly patients where restoring bone stock is less important than immediate stable function.
- Tapered-fluted stems are the current workhorse for most Type II–IIIB defects given reliable diaphyseal scratch-fit fixation, versatility across canal shapes, and modularity to restore length/offset independently of fixation; impaction bone grafting is reserved for Type IV femurs or younger patients where bone stock restoration is prioritized over the simplicity of a tapered-fluted stem.
- CT consistently reveals larger cavitary defects than plain radiographs — underestimating osteolysis extent and undersizing the planned reconstruction intraoperatively is a common avoidable error.
