## Overview

The Kocher criteria are a clinical prediction rule that estimates the probability of septic arthritis (classically of the hip) versus transient synovitis in a child, combining four independent predictors into a probability that guides how urgently to pursue aspiration or surgical drainage.

## Types

Four independent predictors: (1) non-weight-bearing on the affected limb, (2) ESR >40 mm/hr, (3) fever >38.5°C, (4) serum WBC >12,000/mm³. A later modification (Caird et al.) added CRP >20 mg/L as a fifth predictor, improving sensitivity.

| Predictors positive | Approx. probability of septic arthritis (hip) | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | <0.2% | Observe, treat as transient synovitis |
| 1 | ~3% | Close follow-up; ultrasound if effusion suspected |
| 2 | ~40% | Ultrasound-guided aspiration |
| 3 | ~93% | Urgent surgical drainage |
| 4 (or ≥4 of 5 with CRP added) | ~99% | Emergent surgical drainage, do not delay |

## Pearls

- Septic arthritis (especially of the hip) is a surgical emergency — with a high Kocher probability, proceed to urgent aspiration/arthrotomy or arthroscopic washout without waiting on imaging.
- The original cohort's high pretest probability limits generalizability to lower-prevalence populations; external validation studies show substantially lower predictive accuracy elsewhere. Use as an adjunct to, never a replacement for, clinical judgment, and keep a low threshold for aspiration in a toxic-appearing or rapidly worsening child.
- *Kingella kingae* is the leading cause of septic arthritis/osteomyelitis in children under 4 and is frequently culture-negative; PCR of joint/bone fluid or throat swab significantly improves diagnostic yield when Kocher-based suspicion is intermediate.
- Joint aspiration remains the diagnostic gold standard: cell count typically >50,000/mm³ with >90% PMNs, though this threshold has poor individual sensitivity/specificity and must be interpreted alongside the full clinical picture (Gram stain, culture, and *Kingella* PCR in children <4y).
