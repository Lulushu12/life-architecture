## Overview

The Lee classification standardizes the axial and sagittal location of a lumbar disc herniation into four zones, used specifically to plan the surgical trajectory and approach for discectomy. Because a standard midline interlaminar window is adequate for central/subarticular herniations but will miss fragments further lateral, zone assignment directly determines whether a standard, extended, or far-lateral/endoscopic approach is required.

## Types

| Zone | Location | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Zone 1 (central) | Central canal | Standard interlaminar approach; larger fragments may need bilateral or wider laminotomy |
| Zone 2 (subarticular/lateral recess) | Beneath the facet, lateral to the thecal sac | Most common radiculopathy-producing zone; interlaminar approach with medial facetectomy as needed |
| Zone 3 (foraminal) | Within the neural foramen | Requires a more lateral trajectory; risk of missing the fragment with a standard interlaminar window — consider a far-lateral/foraminal or transforaminal endoscopic approach |
| Zone 4 (extraforaminal/far-lateral) | Lateral to the foramen | Requires a far-lateral (Wiltse/paraspinal) approach or extraforaminal endoscopic technique; standard laminotomy will miss it entirely |

## Pearls

- The sagittal component (disc level vs cranial/caudal migration, "high-grade up/down migration") further refines the approach — significantly migrated fragments (superiorly behind the pedicle, or far caudally) change the trajectory and extent of bony resection needed, and migrated/Zone 3–4 fragments are a common cause of missed fragments at revision surgery.
- The transforaminal endoscopic route is particularly well suited to foraminal/far-lateral (Zone 3–4) herniations; standard midline laminotomy will miss these.
- Always plan the approach from the zone (and sagittal migration) rather than defaulting to a standard interlaminar window, especially in Zone 3–4 disease.
