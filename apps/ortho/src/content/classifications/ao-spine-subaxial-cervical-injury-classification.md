## Overview

The AO Spine Subaxial Cervical Injury Classification grades C3–C7 fractures and fracture-dislocations along four axes — injury morphology (A/B/C), a facet-injury (F) modifier, a neurologic (N) modifier, and case-specific (M) modifiers — to describe the pattern and drive the operative-versus-nonoperative decision alongside SLIC. It is used for any subaxial cervical trauma once imaging (CT ± MRI) characterizes bony and discoligamentous complex (DLC) injury.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A0–A1 (compression)** | A0 minor/no bony injury; A1 compression fracture of a single endplate | Generally nonoperative |
| **A2–A4 (compression)** | A2 split/vertical body fracture; A3 incomplete burst; A4 complete burst | Evaluated with neuro status and facet injury; burst with canal compromise or neuro deficit trends operative |
| **B1–B3 (tension band)** | B1 monosegmental posterior tension-band disruption (transdiscal) without perched/dislocated facets; B2 posterior/anterior tension-band disruption with facet perch or subluxation; B3 hyperextension injury (bony or discoligamentous anterior tension-band disruption) | Loss of tension-band integrity implies higher instability; B2 with facet perch or B3 with cord compression favor surgery |
| **C (translation)** | Displacement/translation in any plane (facet dislocation, fracture-dislocation) | Always considered unstable — operative reduction and fixation |
| **F1–F4 (facet injury modifier)** | F1 non-displaced fracture fragment; F2 fragment displaced into canal/foramen; F3 facet fracture with ≥1cm displacement or >40% facet mass involvement; F4 floating lateral mass (ipsilateral pedicle + lamina fracture) | Higher F grade raises instability and often independently drives surgical fixation regardless of morphology grade |
| **N0–N4, NX (neurologic modifier)** | N0 intact; N1 transient deficit resolved; N2 radiculopathy; N3 incomplete SCI; N4 complete SCI; NX exam not assessable | Any deficit (N2–N4) with structural compression = urgent decompression; N modifier is added to morphology to finalize the treatment recommendation |
| **M1–M4 (case-specific modifiers)** | M1 indeterminate DLC injury (MRI signal without confirmed instability); M2 critical disc herniation; M3 ankylosing spondylitis/DISH; M4 signs of vascular injury | M1 may tip a borderline case toward surgery; M3 (AS/DISH) mandates long-segment fixation and a low threshold for surgery given inherent three-column instability |

## Pearls

- The classification is multi-axis, not a single number — morphology (A/B/C), facet (F), neurologic (N), and case-specific (M) modifiers are combined, and the N modifier is what typically finalizes the treatment recommendation on top of morphology.
- A high F grade (facet fracture/floating lateral mass) can independently push toward surgery even when the base morphology grade looks stable.
- M3 (ankylosing spondylitis/DISH) is a critical modifier: these are long, rigid-lever-arm fractures with a disproportionately high rate of missed injury and epidural hematoma, and management should shift toward long-segment fixation and a low threshold for whole-spine imaging rather than being graded like a typical subaxial injury.
- Used alongside SLIC (which is score-based) rather than as a replacement for it — SLIC ≥5 or AO type C / B2–B3 with an N deficit are the injuries that most reliably drive surgical fixation decisions in this monograph's treatment ladder.
