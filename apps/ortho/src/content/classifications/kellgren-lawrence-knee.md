## Overview

The Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grade is the original (1957) and still most widely used radiographic severity scale for knee osteoarthritis, graded on the standing (weight-bearing) AP knee radiograph. It grades global radiographic OA severity on a 0–4 scale and is the standard entry point for staging disease severity before deciding among non-operative care, osteotomy, unicompartmental arthroplasty, or total knee arthroplasty.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No radiographic features of OA | Reassurance; address other pain generators |
| **1** | Doubtful joint space narrowing (JSN); possible osteophytic lipping | Non-op ladder (exercise, weight management); no injections/surgery indicated on imaging alone |
| **2** | Definite osteophytes; possible JSN | Non-op ladder; injections reasonable for symptom control; HTO can be considered if malalignment is the dominant driver even at this grade |
| **3** | Multiple, moderate osteophytes; definite JSN; some subchondral sclerosis; possible bony contour deformity | Prime zone for **HTO or UKA** if compartmental and deformity-correctable; injections as bridge/adjunct |
| **4** | Large osteophytes; marked JSN; severe subchondral sclerosis; definite bony deformity | **TKA** workhorse indication; isolated-compartment KL4 with intact ACL/collaterals and neutral-to-mild deformity in the unaffected compartments may still support UKA in appropriately selected patients |

## Pearls

- KL grade is traditionally read on the **standing extension AP** view, but the **Rosenberg (45° flexion weight-bearing PA) view** is more sensitive for early joint-space narrowing — relying on the standard AP alone can under-grade early/moderate disease.
- KL0-1 is non-operative territory regardless of symptoms on imaging alone; KL2-3 with an isolated, correctable compartment is the prime osteotomy/UKA zone; KL4 is the TKA workhorse grade — but a strictly unicompartmental KL4 with an intact ACL and intact collaterals in the unaffected compartments can still be a UKA candidate in appropriately selected patients, so KL4 is not an automatic TKA indication.
- Injectable therapy (hyaluronic acid, PRP) evidence is more consistently favorable in early-to-moderate (KL I–III) disease than in end-stage (KL4) disease — grade should inform counseling on expected injection benefit, not just surgical eligibility.
- KL grade alone does not capture compartment-specific bone loss/subluxation (that is the role of the Ahlbäck classification) or constitutional coronal phenotype (CPAK) — these are complementary, not redundant, staging tools when planning HTO/UKA/TKA.
