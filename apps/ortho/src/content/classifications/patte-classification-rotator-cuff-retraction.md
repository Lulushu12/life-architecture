## Overview

The Patte classification grades rotator cuff tendon retraction — assessed intraoperatively or on coronal oblique MRI — from stump near its anatomic footprint (stage 1) to retraction to the glenoid rim (stage 3). Retraction reflects tendon mobility and directly informs whether direct repair is feasible or whether mobilization maneuvers (interval slides, releases) or salvage procedures are needed.

## Types

| Stage | Retraction | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tendon stump near its anatomic footprint | Direct repair straightforward |
| 2 | Retracted to the level of the humeral head | Repair feasible, may need mobilization (interval slides, releases) |
| 3 | Retracted to the glenoid rim | Poor tendon mobility/quality; repair difficult, higher retear risk; consider partial repair, margin convalescence, or salvage if combined with high Goutallier stage |

## Pearls

- Patte stage should be interpreted together with Goutallier (fatty infiltration) and Cofield (size) — a Patte 3 tear with low Goutallier stage may still be mobilizable, whereas a Patte 3 tear combined with Goutallier ≥3 is a strong signal to consider salvage over repair.
- Before abandoning a retracted tear as irreparable, release the rotator interval and perform an anterior or posterior interval slide — this often restores enough mobility to achieve footprint apposition. However, release should be purposeful, not reflexive: excessive subscapularis or capsular release can destabilize the joint.
- For tears not fully reducible to the anatomic footprint despite mobilization, margin convalescence (partial repair — repairing what is mobile and accepting a smaller residual defect) improves function even without complete footprint restoration, and is preferable to forcing an over-tensioned repair.
- Over-tensioning a retracted tendon during fixation causes "gap-strangulation" of vascularity at the repair site, which worsens healing independent of the mechanical gap itself — tension the medial row first and lay the lateral row to restore native footprint pressure without over-medializing the musculotendinous junction.
