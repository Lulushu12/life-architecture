## Overview

The Young-Burgess system classifies pelvic ring injuries by the **mechanism/vector of injury** — Lateral Compression (LC), Anteroposterior Compression (APC), Vertical Shear (VS), or Combined Mechanism (CM). It is used at initial trauma evaluation because it correlates strongly with transfusion requirement and mortality, making it the most clinically useful classification for driving the acute hemodynamic (hemorrhage-control) algorithm.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **LC-I** | Sacral ala compression (buckle) fracture, ipsilateral rami fractures | Usually stable; nonoperative unless progressive |
| **LC-II** | Crescent (iliac wing) fracture or SI joint disruption + rami fractures | Often stable to partially unstable; posterior fixation if displaced crescent |
| **LC-III** | LC-I/II + contralateral open-book (APC) component ("windswept pelvis") | Unstable; highest association with severe associated injury, higher mortality |
| **APC-I** | Symphyseal widening <2.5 cm, minimal posterior ligament injury | Stable; nonoperative or symphyseal plate if symptomatic |
| **APC-II** | Symphyseal widening >2.5 cm, anterior SI ligament disruption, posterior SI ligaments intact | Rotationally unstable, vertically stable; symphyseal plate ± anterior SI fixation |
| **APC-III** | Complete SI joint disruption (anterior + posterior ligaments) | Rotationally AND vertically unstable; highest hemorrhage risk (torn posterior venous plexus/arteries); posterior + anterior fixation |
| **VS** | Vertical displacement of hemipelvis through SI joint, sacrum, or iliac wing | Vertically and rotationally unstable; posterior fixation (iliosacral/TITS screws or plating) mandatory |
| **CM** | Combination of above patterns (e.g., LC + VS) | Individualized; treat per most unstable component |

## Pearls

- Young-Burgess correlates strongly with **transfusion requirement and mortality** — APC-III and VS patterns carry the highest hemorrhage risk, driven by disruption of the posterior venous plexus and arterial branches.
- It is the most clinically useful classification for the acute hemodynamic algorithm (binder → resuscitation → FAST-directed packing/REBOA vs. angioembolization), because mechanism predicts bleeding source and magnitude better than stability-based systems.
- Tile/AO is preferred for **surgical decision-making** (maps directly onto need for posterior fixation), while Young-Burgess better predicts **hemorrhage magnitude** — use both in combination in practice rather than relying on either alone.
- LC-III ("windswept pelvis") is a combined pattern and carries the highest association with severe associated injury and mortality among the LC subtypes.
