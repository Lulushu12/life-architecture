## Overview

The Stulberg classification grades the final outcome of Perthes disease at skeletal maturity, based on the sphericity and congruency of the femoral head relative to the acetabulum. It is the long-term outcome measure used to counsel patients and families on premature osteoarthritis risk, and correlates strongly with Herring lateral pillar grade and age at onset.

## Types

| Class | Description | Long-term implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Spherical head, normal joint (essentially normal hip) | Minimal to no increased risk of premature OA |
| II | Spherical head but with coxa magna, short femoral neck, or acetabular abnormality — still spherically congruent | Good long-term outcome, low OA risk |
| III | Non-spherical (ovoid) head, spherically incongruent, but congruent-shaped joint overall | Moderate risk of OA typically in the 5th decade or later |
| IV | Flat femoral head, congruent with a correspondingly flattened acetabulum | Higher OA risk, earlier onset |
| V | Flat femoral head, incongruent with a normally round acetabulum (worst mechanical mismatch) | Highest OA risk, earliest and most severe degeneration |

## Pearls

- Herring lateral pillar C and age ≥8 years at onset predict Stulberg III–V (fair-to-poor) outcome in a majority of hips in classic natural-history cohorts — the two classifications are used together for prognostic counseling from diagnosis through skeletal maturity.
- Stulberg III–V hips classically develop osteoarthritis in the 4th–6th decade of life; this is a key long-term counseling point that should be raised with patients as they approach adulthood, including activity counseling and periodic adult hip surveillance.
- Stulberg class is only assessable at skeletal maturity, after the reossification/remodeling process is complete — it is a final outcome measure, not an early staging or treatment-selection tool (contrast with Waldenström staging or Herring lateral pillar grading).
- Coxa breva and greater trochanteric overgrowth from premature physeal arrest can compound Stulberg-graded incongruity by producing an abductor lever-arm deficiency and Trendelenburg gait, sometimes requiring later trochanteric advancement/epiphysiodesis independent of the Stulberg-graded joint shape itself.
