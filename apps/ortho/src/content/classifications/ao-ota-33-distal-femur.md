## Overview

AO/OTA 33 classifies distal femur fractures by articular involvement — extra-articular (A), partial articular (B), or complete articular (C) — with further subgroups by comminution. It is the primary framework for choosing between retrograde nailing, locked lateral plating, combined nail-plate construct, or distal femoral replacement, and dictates when CT is required for surgical planning.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 33-A1 | Extra-articular, simple (two-part supracondylar) | Retrograde nail or locked plate; both effective; nail favored for its load-sharing biomechanics and faster mobilization when bone stock adequate |
| 33-A2 | Extra-articular, metaphyseal wedge fragment | Similar to A1; static locking/multiple distal interlocks recommended given comminution |
| 33-A3 | Extra-articular, metaphyseal complex/comminuted | Nail (if adequate distal purchase achievable) or locked plate; combined nail-plate if very short distal segment or gross instability |
| 33-B1 | Partial articular, lateral condyle (sagittal) | ORIF with lag screws ± buttress plate; anatomic articular reduction is the priority |
| 33-B2 | Partial articular, medial condyle (sagittal) | ORIF, medial plating/screws; assess for associated coronal shear |
| 33-B3 | Partial articular, coronal plane (Hoffa fragment) | Frequently missed on plain film; requires CT; fixed with anteroposterior lag screws (often headless compression screws) buried beneath cartilage, sometimes posterior buttress plate |
| 33-C1 | Complete articular, simple articular + simple metaphyseal | Reduce and provisionally fix articular block, then nail (retrograde) or plate the metaphyseal component |
| 33-C2 | Complete articular, simple articular + comminuted metaphyseal | Locked plate (LISS-type) most common; nail-plate combination increasingly used for added stability |
| 33-C3 | Complete articular, comminuted articular + comminuted metaphyseal | Most complex pattern; often requires combined nail-plate or dual-plate construct for stable fixation, especially with medial comminution/deficiency; in frail elderly with unreconstructable comminution, primary distal femoral replacement is a valid alternative to prolonged non-weight-bearing ORIF |

## Pearls

- Selection principle across 33-A/B/C: articular reduction must be anatomic (≤1-2 mm step-off) regardless of fixation method chosen for the metaphyseal/diaphyseal component; the choice between nail, plate, or combined construct is driven primarily by metaphyseal comminution, bone quality, and length/stability of the distal segment rather than by the articular pattern alone.
- Coronal-plane (Hoffa, 33-B3) fragments are frequently missed on plain films because the posterior condyles shear off in a coronal plane under axial load through a flexed knee — CT is required for detection and preoperative planning, and is considered standard of care for any 33-C fracture.
- The distal femoral articular surface is angled ~9-10° into valgus relative to the anatomic shaft axis (normal lateral distal femoral angle, LDFA, ~81°) — restoring this valgus angle intraoperatively is a critical reduction reference for 33-A/C fractures.
- Displaced 33-A3 and 33-C fractures put the popliteal artery at risk (tethered at the adductor hiatus and soleal arch) — maintain a low threshold for ABI or CT angiography.
- The 33-B3 Hoffa pattern, though frequently comminuted, rarely progresses to avascular necrosis if fixed promptly, owing to the rich genicular anastomotic collateral supply to the condyles — but circumferential posterior soft-tissue stripping during fixation should still be minimized to protect that supply.
