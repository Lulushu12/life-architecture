## Overview

The Enneking (Musculoskeletal Tumor Society, MSTS) system is the primary surgical staging framework for both benign and malignant musculoskeletal tumors. Benign lesions are staged 1–3 by growth behavior; malignant lesions are staged by grade (G), anatomic compartment (T), and metastasis (M). It is used to determine the required surgical margin (intralesional, marginal, wide, radical) and the need for systemic therapy — margin choice is dictated by Enneking stage and histology, not surgeon preference.

## Types

**Benign lesions:**

| Stage | Definition | Radiographic correlate | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — Latent | Asymptomatic, static or very slow growth, well-defined margin, intact cortex | Lodwick IA, sclerotic rim | Observation, serial imaging |
| 2 — Active | Progressive growth, may thin/expand cortex, symptomatic | Lodwick IB, thin sclerotic rim or none | Intralesional curettage ± adjuvant |
| 3 — Aggressive | Rapid growth, cortical destruction, may extend into soft tissue but remains "benign" histologically | Lodwick IC, cortical breach | Extended curettage + adjuvant, or marginal/wide excision if joint/critical structures at risk |

**Malignant lesions (Grade [G], Site [T], Metastasis [M]):**

| Stage | Grade | Site | Metastasis | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| IA | Low (G1) | Intracompartmental (T1) | M0 | Wide excision; systemic therapy generally not required (histology-dependent) |
| IB | Low (G1) | Extracompartmental (T2) | M0 | Wide excision; wider margin planning given extracompartmental extension |
| IIA | High (G2) | Intracompartmental (T1) | M0 | Wide/radical excision + (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy for chemosensitive histologies (osteosarcoma, Ewing) |
| IIB | High (G2) | Extracompartmental (T2) | M0 | Wide/radical excision + systemic therapy; higher local recurrence risk drives more aggressive margin planning |
| III | Any grade | Any site | M1 (regional or distant) | Systemic therapy is primary; surgery of primary ± resectable metastases (e.g., pulmonary metastasectomy) considered for local/oligometastatic control |

**Margins by stage:**

| Margin | Plane of dissection | Typical indication |
|---|---|---|
| Intralesional | Through the lesion itself | Curettage of benign/low-grade lesions (GCT, UBC, ABC, enchondroma, chondrosarcoma grade I) |
| Marginal | Through the reactive zone/pseudocapsule | Most clearly benign lesions requiring excision (osteochondroma, symptomatic lipoma) |
| Wide | Through normal tissue, cuff of uninvolved tissue circumferentially | Standard for malignant primary bone and soft tissue sarcomas |
| Radical | Entire anatomic compartment removed | Rarely required with modern imaging/planning; historically for extensive/multicompartmental disease |

## Pearls

- Stage 3 benign lesions can extend into soft tissue yet remain histologically benign — do not equate soft-tissue extension with malignancy; management still follows the benign-lesion ladder (extended curettage/adjuvant or marginal/wide excision), not a malignant-tumor protocol.
- Grade (G) drives the need for systemic therapy far more than local T/M status: low-grade (IA/IB) disease is generally treated with surgery alone (histology-dependent), while high-grade (IIA/IIB) disease requires (neo)adjuvant systemic therapy in chemosensitive histologies (osteosarcoma, Ewing).
- Stage III disease (any grade, any site, M1) still may benefit from surgical resection of the primary and, in select cases, resectable/oligometastatic disease (e.g., pulmonary metastasectomy) — systemic disease does not automatically preclude surgery.
- Margin planning must be finalized before biopsy and surgery are separated in time or location: an inappropriately placed biopsy tract can convert a lesion that would otherwise be amenable to a lower-morbidity margin into one requiring a wider resection.
