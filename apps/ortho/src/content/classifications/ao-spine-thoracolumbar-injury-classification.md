## Overview

The AO Spine Thoracolumbar Injury Classification grades thoracolumbar fractures along injury morphology (A/B/C), a neurologic (N) modifier, and case-specific (M) modifiers. It is used alongside TLICS for any thoracolumbar trauma, most heavily at the T10–L2 junction where these injuries concentrate.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A0–A2 (compression)** | A0 minor, non-structural fracture (spinous/transverse process); A1 wedge-compression (single endplate, no posterior wall involvement); A2 split (pincer, both endplates, coronal split) | Typically nonoperative (bracing) |
| **A3–A4 (compression, burst)** | A3 incomplete burst (one endplate, posterior wall fracture, no full-height involvement); A4 complete burst (both endplates, posterior wall, full-height involvement) | Evaluated with neuro status and PLC integrity; many with intact PLC and no deficit remain nonoperative per TLICS, but higher canal compromise/kyphosis lowers the surgical threshold |
| **B1–B2 (tension band)** | B1 transosseous posterior tension-band disruption (through-bone Chance); B2 posterior ligamentous tension-band disruption (through-ligament/facet, often with an A-type body fracture) | Flexion-distraction (Chance-type) pattern — usually surgical (posterior tension-band fixation) unless purely bony with intact PLC and a reliable patient for brace treatment |
| **B3 (tension band)** | Hyperextension injury (anterior tension-band failure) | Managed per anterior tension-band failure pattern; assessed with the same neuro/PLC criteria as other B injuries |
| **C (translation)** | Displacement/dislocation in any plane, or severe multi-column disruption with rotational or shear displacement | Always unstable — operative stabilization, typically spanning fusion |
| **N0–N4, NX (neurologic modifier)** | N0 intact; N1 transient deficit; N2 radicular signs; N3 incomplete SCI or cauda equina (incomplete); N4 complete SCI; NX unassessable | Deficit with canal compromise = urgent decompression; drives the operative decision especially in borderline A3/A4 |
| **M1–M2 (case-specific modifiers)** | M1 indeterminate PLC injury on MRI; M2 relevant comorbidities (e.g., ankylosing spondylitis) affecting treatment | M1 may push a borderline injury toward surgical stabilization; M2 (AS/DISH) means long-segment fixation and avoiding closed reduction/manipulation |

## Pearls

- A3/A4 burst fractures are the classic "gray zone": intact PLC and no deficit favor nonoperative bracing even with a complete burst pattern, but the operative threshold drops with greater canal compromise or kyphosis.
- B1/B2 injuries represent the Chance/flexion-distraction pattern; purely bony Chance fractures with an intact PLC can still be braced in a reliable patient, but the threshold for surgery is low because loss of follow-up risks progressive kyphosis.
- A 2023 expert-panel validation study (183 thoracolumbar burst fracture cases) found the AO Spine algorithm's recommendation aligned with actual expert practice in the majority of cases, but concordance was notably lower in "gray zone" injuries — treat the algorithm as a structured decision aid, not a substitute for individualized judgment, especially at intermediate severity.
- M2 (ankylosing spondylitis/DISH) fractures should not be manually reduced or extended — the ankylosed spine is unstable, brittle, and poorly vascularized, and manipulation can worsen displacement.
