## Overview

AO/OTA 32 classifies femoral diaphyseal (shaft) fractures by fracture pattern and restorability of cortical contact — simple (A), wedge (B), or complex (C) — to guide reamed IM nailing strategy, including nail diameter and the need for adjunctive reduction techniques.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 32-A | Simple fracture (spiral, oblique ≥30°, or transverse <30° obliquity) | Straightforward reamed nailing; excellent union expected |
| 32-B | Wedge fracture (spiral wedge, bending wedge, or fragmented wedge — wedge fragment(s) present but after reduction some cortical contact between main fragments is restorable) | Nail with static locking; wedge fragment left biologically undisturbed (indirect reduction) |
| 32-C | Complex fracture (spiral, segmental, or irregular — no cortical contact restorable between main fragments) | Static locking mandatory; higher nonunion/malalignment risk; consider larger nail diameter, blocking (Poller) screws for segmental patterns, and closer follow-up |

## Pearls

- The A/B/C split turns on whether cortical contact between the main fragments is restorable after reduction — B fractures preserve at least partial contact, C fractures do not, which is the key driver of nonunion/malalignment risk.
- Wedge (32-B) fragments should be left biologically undisturbed and reduced indirectly rather than anatomically reconstructed, to preserve periosteal blood supply.
- For 32-C segmental patterns, blocking (Poller) screws help control alignment through the comminuted zone since axial traction alone often cannot correct rotational or coronal deformity.
- AO/OTA fracture morphology (32-A/B/C) is used alongside the Winquist-Hansen comminution grade for surgical planning — the two systems are complementary, not interchangeable.
