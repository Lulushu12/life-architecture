## Overview

The Graf classification is a static/morphological ultrasound method for staging infant hip maturity and dysplasia, using the alpha angle (bony acetabular roof coverage) and beta angle (cartilaginous roof coverage). It is the primary imaging tool for DDH before ~4–6 months of age, when the cartilaginous femoral head is not yet visible on plain radiograph, and directly determines whether an infant hip is normal, physiologically immature, or pathologically dysplastic/dislocated, driving the decision to treat, re-scan, or observe.

## Types

| Type | α angle | β angle | Description | Management implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | ≥60° | <55° (Ia) or >55° (Ib) | Mature, normal hip | No treatment |
| IIa (physiologically immature, <12 wk) | 50–59° | variable | Immature bony rim, still developing | Re-scan at 4–6 weeks; treat if not matured by ~12 weeks |
| IIa(-)/IIb | 50–59° | variable | Immature/deficient at an age where maturation should have occurred (IIb = same angles but ≥12 weeks old) | Treat (harness) if IIb or IIa(-) with risk factors |
| IIc | 43–49° | <77° | Critical zone — significantly deficient bony roof, still (usually) located | Treat — high risk of progression to subluxation/dislocation |
| D | 43–49° | ≥77° | Decentered/subluxated | Treat — harness or reduction |
| III | <43° | — | Dislocated, cartilage roof displaced, echogenic (compressed) | Treat — reduction (harness if young/reducible, otherwise closed/open reduction) |
| IV | <43° | — | Dislocated, cartilage roof interposed/displaced further, everted labrum | Treat — reduction, often requires closed/open reduction rather than harness |

## Pearls

- The clinically decisive threshold is α <50° (Type IIc and worse), which is generally treated; Type I and most IIa hips are managed with observation/re-scanning rather than a harness.
- Pavlik harness success is highest (~85–95%) for Graf IIc/dislocatable hips treated before 6 months of age, and falls substantially (<50%) for true teratologic/irreducible dislocations or when treatment starts after 6–7 months — Graf type at presentation, combined with age, should set expectations for harness success.
- IIa hips are physiologically immature rather than pathologic in most cases; the key is re-scanning at 4–6 weeks rather than reflexively treating, since many mature spontaneously by ~12 weeks.
- Whether every infant gets a Graf ultrasound at all is itself debated: most English-speaking guideline bodies favor selective ultrasound (breech presentation, positive family history, abnormal exam) over the universal ultrasound screening practiced in parts of Central Europe, which increases sensitivity but also over-treatment of hips that would mature on their own.
- A structured, protocolized in-harness ultrasound monitoring schedule (rather than ad hoc imaging) improves reduction success during Pavlik treatment.
