## Overview

The British Athletics Muscle Injury Classification (BAMIC) grades acute muscle strains by MRI structural extent (numeric grade 0-4, based on cross-sectional area of edema/fiber disruption) combined with an anatomic site suffix (a/b/c). It is the standard system for grading and prognosticating hamstring, quadriceps, and calf (gastrocnemius) strains in athletes, and is used to guide expected return-to-play (RTP) timelines and re-injury risk counseling. MRI for BAMIC grading is best obtained within the first 24-72 hours for optimal signal, though findings continue to evolve over the first week.

## Types

| Grade/Suffix | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Symptomatic, no MRI abnormality (or old fibrosis only) | Rehab as clinically indicated; often rapid RTP |
| **1** | Minimal — small area of increased T2 signal, <10% cross-sectional area (CSA) or focal | Short rehab course, RTP often 1-2 wks |
| **2** | Moderate — larger edema, up to ~50% CSA, no significant fiber discontinuity | RTP typically 2-4 wks |
| **3** | Extensive — >50% CSA edema/fiber disruption | RTP typically 4-8+ wks |
| **4** | Complete tear/full-thickness disruption with retraction | Surgical evaluation, prolonged rehab (often >2-3 mo) |
| **Suffix a (myofascial)** | Edema at fascia, no intramuscular tendon change | Best prognosis at a given grade |
| **Suffix b (musculotendinous junction)** | Myotendinous injury | Intermediate — most common site, standard RTP timelines |
| **Suffix c (intramuscular tendon)** | Tendon disruption within the muscle belly | Substantially prolongs RTP (e.g., ~78 vs ~24 days in rugby cohorts) and raises re-injury risk — flag explicitly in reports |

Grade + letter (e.g., "2b," "3c") is the standard reporting format in elite sport MRI and should be used routinely in athlete-facing reports.

## Pearls

- The "c" (intramuscular tendon) designation is the single most important modifier for return-to-play counseling — it prolongs RTP substantially beyond what the numeric grade alone predicts and raises re-injury risk.
- BAMIC grade correlates strongly with RTP timeline in criterion-based rehab (grade 1: days-1wk; grade 2: 2-4 wks; grade 3: 4-8 wks; grade 4/intramuscular tendon "c": often >8 wks), but RTP criteria should still be strength symmetry (≥90% contralateral), pain-free full-speed sprinting, and functional hop/agility symmetry — not calendar time alone.
- BAMIC's site/anatomy detail (myofascial vs myotendinous vs intramuscular tendon) and quantitative CSA are stronger predictors of RTP time than clinical severity grading (e.g., Puranen-Orava) alone.
- Originally derived in elite European track/football populations; validated more recently in US collegiate football, broadening its applicable evidence base.
- Applies identically across hamstring, quadriceps, and calf (gastrocnemius) strains — the same grading and rehab-phase framework is used for "tennis leg" (medial gastrocnemius-soleus junction tear), with the caveat that DVT and plantaris rupture must be excluded as differentials/comorbidities before attributing symptoms to a graded strain alone.
