## Overview

The Pauwels classification grades femoral neck fractures by the angle of the fracture line from the horizontal on the AP radiograph, capturing the shear-versus-compression loading across the fracture rather than displacement per se. It is used alongside Garden to guide implant choice, particularly the decision between standard cannulated screws and a fixed-angle device.

## Types

| Type | Fracture line angle from horizontal | Biomechanical implication | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | <30° | Predominantly compressive load across fracture | Lower shear stress on fixation; standard parallel cannulated screw or SHS construct usually adequate |
| **II** | 30–50° | Mixed shear/compression | Moderate failure risk; meticulous screw positioning (inverted triangle, posteroinferior screw) |
| **III** | >50° | High shear force, fracture prone to sliding into varus | Highest fixation-failure/nonunion rate with standard screws; consider fixed-angle device (sliding hip screw ± anti-rotation screw, or femoral neck locking plate/FNS) especially in younger patients where head preservation is the goal |

## Pearls

- Pauwels angle is highly reduction-dependent — an anatomically reduced fracture converts an unfavorable native Pauwels III pattern into a mechanically favorable construct; reduction quality often matters more than raw implant choice.
- Pauwels angle >50° is a key marker of high shear and fixation-failure risk.
- For Pauwels III or comminuted patterns, many surgeons continue to use touch-down/partial weight-bearing for 6 weeks even when a fixed-angle device is used, advancing to full weight-bearing by 8–12 weeks with radiographic union.
- Recent (2023–2025) FNS vs. cannulated screws data show FNS achieving comparable or modestly better functional scores and time-to-union via a fixed-angle, learning-curve-independent mechanism, particularly relevant to higher-shear (Pauwels III) patterns in younger patients — at the cost of a larger single implant and higher hardware cost. Cannulated screws remain a valid, cheaper, widely available default.
