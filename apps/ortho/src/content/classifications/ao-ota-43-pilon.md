## Overview

AO/OTA 43 is the universal alphanumeric coding for distal tibia (pilon/plafond) fractures, used in registries, billing, and international communication. Its A/B/C tiers track how much of the articular surface remains attached to the shaft and correlate roughly with the older Rüedi-Allgöwer system, with C3 representing the most severe, highest-complication pattern.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 43-A | Extra-articular metaphyseal fracture | Extra-articular pattern; does not require the articular reduction strategy that governs B/C injuries |
| 43-B | Partial articular — a portion of joint remains attached to shaft (fracture-dislocation pattern possible) | Portion of the articular surface stays attached to the shaft, which guides the reduction strategy |
| 43-C | Complete articular — joint surface completely dissociated from shaft; C3 = articular + metaphyseal comminution | Highest complication rate, most likely to need the staged protocol (spanning ex-fix → delayed definitive ORIF); C3 carries the worst prognosis of the group |

## Pearls

- Rüedi-Allgöwer predates the AO/OTA 43-C system and correlates roughly with 43-B/C but does not separately encode metaphyseal comminution the way AO/OTA C3 does — most current literature reports both systems together.
- Subdivisions (1–3) are used mainly for research stratification and prognostication; in daily practice A/B/C plus a plain-language description of comminution/displacement drives the operative plan.
- Provides the universal common language for registries, billing, and international communication — the system to default to when writing for a European/AO-trained audience or entering registry data.
