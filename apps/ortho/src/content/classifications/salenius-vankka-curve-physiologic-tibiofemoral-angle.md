## Overview

The Salenius-Vankka curve is a normative reference of the tibiofemoral (knee) angle from birth to skeletal maturity, used to judge whether a child's angular alignment falls within the expected physiologic range (±2 SD) for age and to distinguish normal developmental bowing/valgus from pathologic deformity.

## Types

| Age | Expected alignment | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Birth | ~15° varus | Normal; observation only |
| ~18–24 months | Neutral (transition point) | Normal transition; persistent varus past this point warrants evaluation |
| ~3–4 years | Peak physiologic valgus, ~10–12° | Normal; observation only |
| ~6–7 years | Settles toward adult alignment, ~5–7° valgus | Normal; observation only |
| Skeletal maturity | Adult alignment, ~5–7° valgus | Endpoint of normal trajectory |

## Pearls

- A child whose varus persists or worsens past ~24 months (i.e., fails to enter the expected valgus swing) — especially with a Levine-Drennan metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle (MDA) >11° — should be evaluated for pathologic Blount disease or another cause of pathologic bowing rather than reassured as physiologic.
- Conversely, valgus within the expected 3–4-year-old peak, or varus in a child under ~18–24 months without red-flag features, warrants observation only.
- Red flags that should trigger imaging/metabolic workup and referral rather than reassurance: progression beyond age 2–3y, asymmetry, short stature, lateral thrust on gait, family history of skeletal dysplasia/rickets, focal single-bone deformity, or MDA >11° on AP tibia.
- No brace, shoe modification, or orthotic has been shown to alter the natural history of physiologic bowing or torsional variants — this includes historically popular Denis-Browne bars and "corrective" shoes.
