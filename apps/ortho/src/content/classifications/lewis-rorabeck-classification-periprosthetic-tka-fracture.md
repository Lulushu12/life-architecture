## Overview

The Lewis-Rorabeck classification grades periprosthetic fractures around or above a total knee arthroplasty femoral component by displacement and femoral implant stability. It is used to decide between non-operative management, ORIF, and revision TKA for supracondylar and adjacent femoral fractures occurring after TKA.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Non-displaced, implant stable | Non-operative — cast/brace/hinged brace, protected weight-bearing |
| II | Displaced, implant stable | ORIF (locked plate ± retrograde nail if box design allows) |
| III | Implant loose or failing | Revision TKA (often with stemmed femoral component ± augments), fracture fixation as adjunct |

## Pearls

- Type II is further stratified by the Su classification, which locates the fracture relative to the femoral component's intercondylar box — this determines whether retrograde intramedullary nailing is mechanically feasible (open-box designs with adequate clearance) or precluded (closed-box designs, or a fracture line through the box), steering most Su II/III patterns toward locked plating instead.
- Always confirm the specific implant's box geometry from operative records or manufacturer specifications before committing to a nailing strategy — assuming compatibility from the component's appearance on plain film is an avoidable planning error.
- Avoiding anterior femoral notching at the index TKA is the single most modifiable risk factor for this fracture pattern; any notch >3 mm substantially increases fracture risk (some series report a roughly 3-fold increase with any notching, higher with deeper/more anterior notches).
- A genuinely non-displaced Type I fracture with a confirmed stable implant is the only scenario appropriate for non-operative management — confirm implant stability (comparison to prior films for subsidence/new radiolucency) before committing to bracing alone.
