## Overview

The Lenke classification (2001) is the standard system for adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS) surgical planning. It is built from three components: (1) a curve type based on which curves are "structural" — Cobb ≥25° that does not correct to <25° on side-bending films, OR T5–T12 kyphosis >+20°, OR T10–L2 kyphosis >+20° — (2) a lumbar spine modifier, and (3) a sagittal thoracic modifier. Together these determine which curves must be included in a fusion and whether a selective (shorter) fusion is safe.

## Types

| Curve Type | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — Main thoracic** | Only the main thoracic (MT) curve is structural | Selective thoracic fusion often possible if the lumbar modifier is favorable |
| **2 — Double thoracic** | Proximal thoracic + MT both structural | Proximal thoracic curve must be included; watch shoulder balance |
| **3 — Double major** | MT + thoracolumbar/lumbar (TL/L) both structural | Both curves fused; selective fusion generally not appropriate |
| **4 — Triple major** | PT + MT + TL/L all structural | Most extensive fusion; all three curves included |
| **5 — TL/L only** | Only TL/L structural | Fusion limited to TL/L segment; thoracic left unfused |
| **6 — TL/L-MT** | TL/L structural and larger than MT, MT also structural | TL/L is the major curve driving fusion levels; MT usually included |

**Lumbar modifier** (relationship of the center sacral vertical line, CSVL, to the lumbar apex): **A** = CSVL passes between the pedicles of the apical vertebra; **B** = CSVL touches the apical vertebral body; **C** = CSVL is medial to the apex. C-modifier curves are less likely to tolerate a selective thoracic fusion (higher risk of postoperative coronal decompensation).

**Sagittal thoracic modifier** (T5–T12 kyphosis): **–** (<10°, hypokyphotic), **N** (10–40°, normal), **+** (>40°, hyperkyphotic) — affects rod contouring and osteotomy planning at the thoracic segment.

## Pearls

- Fusion levels follow the Lenke type and modifiers — all structural curves must be included; the lowest instrumented vertebra (LIV) is generally the "stable" or "neutral" vertebra to minimize risk of decompensation and distal adding-on.
- **Selective thoracic fusion** (sparing the lumbar curve) is appropriate in favorable Lenke 1A/1B/1C patterns with an A or B lumbar modifier; a C lumbar modifier raises the risk of postoperative coronal decompensation if the lumbar curve is left unfused.
- Incorrect LIV selection is a recognized pitfall causing distal adding-on; overly aggressive selective fusion is a recognized cause of coronal decompensation.
- Anterior approaches (open or thoracoscopic release) are now rarely needed and are reserved for very rigid curves (>75–90°, correction <50% on side-bending films).
