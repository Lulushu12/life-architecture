## Overview

The Dejour classification grades trochlear dysplasia — a developmental failure of the trochlear groove to deepen normally during growth — using axial CT/MRI and lateral radiograph findings (crossing sign, supratrochlear spur, double contour). Trochlear dysplasia is present in the majority of patients with recurrent patellar instability (reported in >85% of recurrent dislocators) and is the single strongest anatomic risk factor for redislocation, so grading it is central to selecting between soft-tissue-only stabilization (MPFL reconstruction) and combined bony correction (trochleoplasty).

## Types

| Type | Radiographic/imaging features | Severity | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Crossing sign present; trochlea shallow but symmetric, sulcus angle >145° | Mild | Usually MPFL-R alone sufficient if instability present |
| **B** | Crossing sign + trochlear spur (flat or convex trochlea in axial images) | Moderate | Consider combined MPFL-R + flattening/deepening trochleoplasty if spur >7 mm or convex |
| **C** | Crossing sign + double contour (medial hypoplastic facet, lateral facet convex, asymmetric facets) | Severe | High redislocation risk with MPFL-R alone; strongly consider trochleoplasty |
| **D** | Crossing sign + supratrochlear spur + double contour ("cliff pattern"/vertical medial facet) | Most severe | Trochleoplasty (deepening, e.g., Bereiter/Dejour technique) usually indicated in symptomatic recurrent instability |

## Pearls

- Types A/B are typically managed with soft-tissue reconstruction (MPFL-R) ± tibial tubercle correction; Types C/D increasingly favor combined trochleoplasty given persistently elevated redislocation rates with isolated MPFL-R in high-grade dysplasia.
- Sulcus angle is part of the classification's imaging basis: normal is ~138°, and >145° defines dysplasia (Type A threshold).
- Dysplasia grade is one of the core risk factors in the ESSKA 2024 "à-la-carte" algorithm — it is weighed alongside patellar height (Caton-Deschamps), TT-TG distance, and rotational malalignment when deciding whether to add a bony procedure to MPFL-R.
- In high-grade dysplasia, the trochlear groove reference point used for TT-TG measurement can itself be abnormal or absent, which inflates or invalidates that measurement — a reason to weigh Dejour grade heavily even when TT-TG appears borderline.
- Trochleoplasty is technically demanding with risk of chondral injury and arthrofibrosis (reported in a meaningful minority, especially pediatric series); it is best performed in high-volume centers and can be performed close to skeletal maturity in adolescents per ESSKA consensus.
