## Overview

The AO/OTA 31 classification is the comprehensive alphanumeric system for proximal femur fractures, dividing them by anatomic zone into three groups: 31-A (trochanteric/intertrochanteric), 31-B (femoral neck), and 31-C (femoral head). It parallels and cross-references the more clinically familiar eponymous systems (Evans-Jensen, Garden/Pauwels, Pipkin) and is the standard for research classification and communication of fracture pattern/instability.

## Types

| Group | Region | Subgroups | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **31-A** | Trochanteric (intertrochanteric) | A1: 2-part, stable (simple, along the intertrochanteric line, medial/posterior cortex intact); A2: multifragmentary with posteromedial comminution, no lateral wall/subtrochanteric extension involving the lateral cortex as a fixed reference; A3: reverse obliquity or transverse, lateral cortex fractured (lateral wall incompetent) | A1/most A2 = SHS or CMN equivalent; A2 with thin/fractured lateral wall (<20.5 mm) and A3 = CMN mandatory (SHS mechanically fails against reverse-obliquity/lateral-wall-deficient patterns) |
| **31-B** | Femoral neck | B1: subcapital, minimal displacement; B2: transcervical; B3: subcapital, displaced | Mirrors Garden/Pauwels logic — B1 often fixation, B3 usually arthroplasty in the elderly |
| **31-C** | Femoral head | C1: split (Pipkin I/II equivalent); C2: split with depression/impaction; C3: comminuted | Guides ORIF vs. arthroplasty; C3 in the elderly favors arthroplasty over reconstruction |

## Pearls

- Lateral wall thickness of <20.5 mm on the AP radiograph is the key threshold that converts a nominally stable 31-A2 pattern into one that behaves like an unstable pattern requiring CMN rather than SHS.
- AO/OTA 32-A3 (subtrochanteric fracture extending distally) is formally classified within the femoral shaft (32) chapter rather than group 31, but is clinically managed with the trauma/subtrochanteric algorithm; many centers use the **Russell-Taylor** or simply "subtrochanteric zone" designation clinically alongside Seinsheimer for these fractures.
- 31-B and 31-C subgroups are best interpreted alongside their eponymous counterparts (Garden/Pauwels for B; Pipkin for C) for treatment planning — the AO letters map onto but do not replace those decision frameworks.
