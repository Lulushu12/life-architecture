## Overview

The Paprosky classification grades acetabular bone loss for revision total hip arthroplasty planning, encountered most often during revision for aseptic cup loosening, wear/osteolysis, or periprosthetic fracture reconstruction. It directs the acetabular reconstruction ladder from a standard hemispherical cup through cup-cage and custom triflange constructs.

## Types

| Type | Features | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| I | Intact rim, minimal cavitary loss | Hemispherical cementless cup ± morcellized graft |
| IIA | Superior cavitary defect, intact columns | Hemispherical cup (superomedial) + bone graft |
| IIB | Superolateral cavitary defect, rim deficient <1/3 | Larger hemispherical cup with high superolateral coverage, screws, graft |
| IIC | Medial wall defect, columns intact | Hemispherical cup, medial graft, screws into intact columns |
| IIIA | Superolateral bone loss >1/3 rim, some ischial/teardrop involvement, columns compromised but some host contact remains (<50% host contact) | Jumbo cup, porous metal augment ± cage; tantalum cup-cage construct |
| IIIB | Severe loss, both columns discontinuous or near-absent host contact (<50% host bone contact), often pelvic discontinuity | Cup-cage construct, custom triflange, porous tantalum augments/cages, allograft |

## Pearls

- The practical branch point is host bone contact — **≥50%** supportive host contact allows a jumbo cup ± augments; **<50%** or pelvic discontinuity mandates a construct that bridges columns (cup-cage, custom triflange, or a porous tantalum revision shell with multiple screw fixation points).
- Pelvic discontinuity is a separate but often co-existing finding, most common with IIIB defects — recognize it specifically on CT/plain film (a horizontal lucent line separating superior and inferior hemipelvis), since it changes the reconstruction goal from cup fixation alone to restoring pelvic column continuity before/during cup placement.
- CT consistently reveals larger cavitary defects than plain radiographs — underestimating osteolysis extent on plain film and undersizing the planned reconstruction intraoperatively is a common avoidable error.
