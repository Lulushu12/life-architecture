## Overview

DEXA T-score and FRAX together form the standard framework for diagnosing osteoporosis and estimating fracture probability, used to decide who needs pharmacologic treatment and to guide anabolic-versus-antiresorptive sequencing. DEXA is obtained at femoral neck, total hip, and lumbar spine, with the **lowest T-score among sites** governing classification.

## Types

| DEXA category | T-score | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| Normal | ≥ −1.0 | Reassess risk factors; lifestyle counseling |
| Osteopenia (low bone mass) | −1.0 to −2.5 | FRAX calculation to decide on treatment; repeat DEXA interval per baseline risk |
| Osteoporosis | ≤ −2.5 | Pharmacologic treatment indicated regardless of FRAX |
| Severe/established osteoporosis | ≤ −2.5 + fragility fracture | High-risk category — consider anabolic-first strategy |

**FRAX** estimates the 10-year probability of major osteoporotic fracture (MOF: hip, clinical spine, wrist, proximal humerus) and hip fracture specifically from age, sex, BMI, prior fragility fracture, parental hip fracture, current smoking, long-term glucocorticoid use, rheumatoid arthritis, secondary osteoporosis causes, alcohol ≥3 units/day, and femoral neck BMD.

## Pearls

- Intervention thresholds vary by health system: US-style absolute thresholds treat at 10-year hip probability ≥3% or MOF probability ≥20% (NOF-style); UK NOGG/NICE use age-specific threshold curves rather than fixed cut-offs.
- A prior hip or vertebral fragility fracture is itself an automatic treatment indication independent of FRAX or T-score.
- Vertebral fracture assessment (VFA) on the same DEXA visit detects radiographically silent vertebral compression fractures — a substantial proportion of vertebral fractures are clinically unrecognized until specifically looked for.
- A fragility fracture in a man or a premenopausal woman should prompt a more thorough secondary-cause work-up (calcium, phosphate, PTH, 25-OH vitamin D, TSH, SPEP/free light chains if atypical, testosterone in men) than a typical postmenopausal presentation.
- The updated 2024 UK NOGG guideline confirms all three anabolics (teriparatide, abaloparatide, romosozumab) may be used at different points in a patient's lifetime course, and notes growing evidence that anabolic-first sequencing outperforms the older antiresorptive-then-anabolic order, but stops short of endorsing one fixed sequence.
