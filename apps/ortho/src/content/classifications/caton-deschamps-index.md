## Overview

The Caton-Deschamps (CD) index measures patellar height on a true lateral knee radiograph (knee flexed 30°): CD = (distance from inferior patellar articular margin to anterosuperior tibial plateau) / (patellar articular surface length). Patella alta reduces bony engagement of the patella in the trochlear groove during early flexion, increasing instability and anterior knee pain risk, so the CD index is used both diagnostically and to plan tibial tubercle distalization when indicated.

## Types

| CD Index | Interpretation | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| <0.6 | Patella baja | Consider in post-traumatic/post-surgical stiffness workup; rarely relevant to instability |
| 0.6–1.2 | Normal (mean ~1.0) | No height-related intervention needed |
| 1.2–1.3 | Patella alta | Risk factor for instability; consider distalizing TTO if combined with other risk factors and symptomatic instability |
| >1.3 | Severe patella alta | Strong indication to include tibial tubercle distalization in the surgical plan (à-la-carte) |

## Pearls

- The Insall-Salvati ratio (normal 0.8–1.2) is a supportive but less reliable alternative, particularly post-operatively; the CD index is preferred as the primary patellar height metric.
- Patella alta is one of the core risk factors in the ESSKA 2024 à-la-carte algorithm for patellar instability, weighed alongside Dejour dysplasia grade, TT-TG distance, and rotational malalignment.
- Restoring correct patellar height is a key intraoperative pearl during patellar or quadriceps tendon repair — compare to the contralateral CD index intraoperatively/fluoroscopically before final fixation tensioning; overtensioning causes patella baja (painful, restricted flexion) and undertensioning causes alta and extensor weakness.
- Lateral radiographs also identify patella alta/baja after tendon rupture: patella baja follows quadriceps tendon rupture (unopposed patellar tendon pull), while patella alta follows patellar tendon rupture.
