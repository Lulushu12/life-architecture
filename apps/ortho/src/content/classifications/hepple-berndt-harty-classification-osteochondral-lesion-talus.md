## Overview

Staging systems for osteochondral lesions of the talus (OLT). **Berndt-Harty** (1959) is the original plain radiograph-based system; **Hepple** (1999) is the MRI-based, preferred modern staging system that incorporates cartilage, subchondral bone, and cyst status to guide the choice between non-operative trial, bone marrow stimulation, and restorative cartilage/bone procedures.

## Types

**Berndt-Harty (plain radiograph-based)**

| Stage | Findings |
|---|---|
| I | Compression of subchondral bone |
| II | Incomplete avulsion fragment |
| III | Complete avulsion, non-displaced |
| IV | Displaced fragment |
| V (added later) | Subchondral cyst |

**Hepple (MRI-based, preferred modern staging)**

| Stage | MRI findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Articular cartilage damage only | Trial of non-operative management (protected weightbearing, NSAIDs, activity modification) |
| 2a | Cartilage injury with underlying subchondral fracture and surrounding bone marrow edema | Non-op trial reasonable; edema suggests instability risk — shorter non-op trial before considering surgery |
| 2b | Same as 2a but without surrounding edema | More stable pattern; longer non-op trial reasonable |
| 3 | Cartilage injury with an underlying detached, non-displaced osteochondral fragment | Failed non-op → surgery; fragment may be amenable to fixation if large/acute |
| 4 | Detached and displaced fragment | Surgical (fragment usually not salvageable — treat as a cartilage defect: bone marrow stimulation or restorative procedure) |
| 5 | Subchondral cyst formation | Cystic/large lesions favor restorative procedures (OATS, ACI, bone grafting) over bone marrow stimulation alone |

## Pearls

- Lesion location matters alongside stage: most lesions occur at the **posteromedial talar dome** (deeper, cup-shaped, often chronic/atraumatic) or **anterolateral talar dome** (shallower, wafer-shaped, typically traumatic/inversion injury).
- Healing potential correlates more with subchondral bone integrity than with cartilage appearance alone.
- Size threshold for primary bone marrow stimulation is **<100–150 mm² (~<10–15 mm diameter)**; larger or cystic (Hepple 5) lesions favor restorative procedures.
- Weightbearing CT (WBCT) is increasingly used alongside MRI/Hepple staging for accurate lesion size/depth/containment mapping and is superior to conventional (non-weightbearing) CT for true weightbearing geometry — it increasingly guides the size/containment decision more accurately than conventional CT.
- Hepple 3 lesions with an acute, large, reducible fragment are best served by primary fixation (headless compression screws or bioabsorbable pins) rather than excision, preserving native cartilage.
- Always assess and correct concurrent hindfoot malalignment (varus/valgus) or chronic ankle instability — uncorrected malalignment is a major driver of OLT recurrence/progression regardless of stage.
