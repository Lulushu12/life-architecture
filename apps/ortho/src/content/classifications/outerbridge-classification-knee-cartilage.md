## Overview

The Outerbridge classification grades chondral lesions Grade 0–IV by arthroscopic or radiographic appearance. Originally described for the patella, it is now used knee-wide, and (together with the ICRS grade, which it closely parallels) determines whether a chondral defect is a candidate for cartilage restoration.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal cartilage | No treatment |
| I | Softening and swelling of cartilage | Non-operative, activity modification |
| II | Partial-thickness fissuring/fragmentation <1.5 cm diameter | Debridement/chondroplasty if mechanical symptoms; not a graft indication |
| III | Fissuring/fragmentation to subchondral bone, >1.5 cm diameter ("crabmeat" appearance) | Candidate for restorative procedure if focal and contained |
| IV | Full-thickness cartilage loss with exposed subchondral bone | Strongest indication for OAT/ACI/MACI/OCA depending on size and bone involvement; diffuse Grade IV trends toward arthroplasty pathway instead |

## Pearls

- Outerbridge and ICRS are frequently used interchangeably in practice — both grade roughly 0–4 with similar depth criteria — but ICRS is the more granular, currently preferred research standard, while Outerbridge remains ubiquitous in US operative notes.
- Grade III–IV with a contained, focal, full-thickness lesion is the classic indication zone for OAT/ACI/OCA; diffuse/bipolar/kissing lesions and uncorrected malalignment are contraindications, and diffuse Grade IV disease trends toward arthroplasty rather than a focal graft.
- MRI tends to underestimate true lesion size/depth versus arthroscopic findings — confirm the grade intraoperatively before committing to a graft size.
- As with ICRS grading, alignment, meniscal integrity, and ligament stability must be corrected concurrently with any restorative procedure — a graft placed into a malaligned or unstable knee is set up to fail.
