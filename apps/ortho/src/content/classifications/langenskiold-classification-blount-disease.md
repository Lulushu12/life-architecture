## Overview

The Langenskiöld classification stages infantile Blount disease (tibia vara) by the radiographic appearance of the posteromedial proximal tibial metaphyseal-epiphyseal complex, used to guide the choice between bracing, guided growth, and osteotomy.

## Types

| Stage | Radiographic findings | Typical age/reversibility | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Irregular medial metaphyseal beaking, mild metaphyseal-diaphyseal angulation | Often <2y, frequently reversible | Observe or KAFO brace trial; many resolve spontaneously if diagnosed very early |
| II | Sharper medial metaphyseal depression, physis widened medially | ~2–3y | Brace trial; guided growth if progressive |
| III | Deepening of the metaphyseal defect with a medial bony "step" | ~2.5–4y | Guided growth (lateral hemiepiphysiodesis) or valgus osteotomy if brace fails/age >3–4y |
| IV | Epiphysis becomes wedge-shaped, encroaching into the metaphyseal depression | ~4–6y | Osteotomy ± guided growth; higher relapse risk |
| V | Epiphysis cleft into separate medial and lateral fragments | ~5–10y | Osteotomy with elevation of the depressed medial plateau; consider physeal bar assessment |
| VI | Bony bridge (physeal bar) forms across the medial physis | ~9–13y | Osteotomy + bar resection if focal and adequate growth remaining; otherwise combined osteotomy/epiphysiodesis |

## Pearls

- Higher stage at presentation correlates with higher recurrence risk after correction and a greater likelihood that guided growth alone will fail, favoring primary osteotomy.
- A Levine-Drennan metaphyseal-diaphyseal angle (MDA) >11° in a child <2–3y is suspicious for pathologic (Blount) rather than physiologic bowing even before Langenskiöld changes become visually obvious on radiographs — use MDA to flag early disease before the stage is clearly assignable.
- Infantile (<4y, most common, often bilateral), juvenile (4–10y), and adolescent (>10y, often obesity-associated, unilateral) Blount forms differ in age of onset, symmetry, and typical Langenskiöld stage at presentation — the classification was developed for and is most validated in the infantile form.
- Serial films are used to stage progression over time, not just a single snapshot, since Langenskiöld stage can advance if bracing fails or the mechanical overload cycle continues.
