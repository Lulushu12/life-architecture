## Overview

The McPherson host-grading system risk-stratifies patients with periprosthetic joint infection (PJI) before committing to a treatment strategy. It is a three-axis system — host physiologic grade, local extremity grade, and (in some iterations) type of infecting organism/systemic grade — adapted from the Cierny-Mader osteomyelitis staging concept and applied specifically to PJI.

## Types

**Host physiological grade**

| Grade | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Uncompromised host** | No systemic compromising factors | Standard eradication strategy (DAIR/one- or two-stage per timing) has best expected success |
| **B — Compromised host** | 1–2 of: rheumatoid arthritis/inflammatory arthropathy, diabetes, chronic immunosuppression/steroid use, active smoking, age extremes with frailty | Intensify medical optimization pre-op; expect intermediate success; consider two-stage over one-stage if additional risk factors present |
| **C — Significantly compromised host** | ≥3 of the above, or any single "severe" comorbidity: chronic renal failure/dialysis, chronic liver disease/cirrhosis, malignancy on active treatment, malnutrition, IV drug use, AIDS/severe immunosuppression | High expected failure of eradication surgery; discuss suppression-only, resection arthroplasty, or arthrodesis as primary strategy rather than aggressive exchange |

**Local extremity grade**

| Grade | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | No local compromise | No special soft-tissue planning needed |
| **2** | Compromised soft-tissue envelope (scarring, multiple prior incisions, chronic sinus, prior soft-tissue procedures) | Plan soft-tissue coverage/plastic surgery input; consider staged wound closure |
| **3** | Severely compromised (large-vessel disease, extensive scarring, chronic lymphedema, multiple failed prior surgeries) | High risk of persistent wound complications; consider free/rotational flap, or amputation discussion in extreme cases |

## Pearls

- McPherson grading (host + local grade) is the primary tool for setting realistic expectations with the patient and choosing between eradication (DAIR/exchange) versus palliative/suppressive strategies.
- A Host C / Local 3 patient with a low-demand joint is a rational candidate for chronic suppression or resection arthroplasty rather than a second major reconstructive attempt — reframe this with the patient as a functional/quality-of-life decision, not a defeat.
- Local extremity grade drives soft-tissue reconstruction planning independent of host grade — a physiologically uncompromised (Host A) patient can still require flap coverage if the local grade is 2–3.
- McPherson host C is a recurring risk modifier throughout the treatment algorithm: it lowers expected DAIR/one-stage success, raises the threshold for suppression-only strategies, and is a major driver of reinfection, wound-healing complications, and mortality risk in outcome data.
