## Overview

The Cofield classification grades full-thickness rotator cuff tears by their maximum linear dimension (measured intraoperatively or on MRI), from small (<1 cm) to massive (>5 cm or involving ≥2 tendons). Tear size by this system is the primary variable driving repair-technique selection (single-row vs. double-row) and is a key input, alongside retraction (Patte) and fatty infiltration (Goutallier), into the reparability decision for massive tears.

## Types

| Grade | Size | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Small | <1 cm | Often amenable to single-row repair or margin convalescence; good healing potential |
| Medium | 1–3 cm | Single- or double-row repair per surgeon preference; good prognosis |
| Large | 3–5 cm | Double-row/transosseous-equivalent preferred (biomechanically superior); assess retraction/mobility |
| Massive | >5 cm or ≥2 tendons involved | Evaluate reparability (Patte, Goutallier, tangent sign) before committing to repair vs. salvage procedure |

## Pearls

- Massive cuff tear is formally defined as >5 cm in any dimension **or** ≥2 full-thickness tendon tears — either criterion alone qualifies, so a smaller tear spanning two tendons still counts as massive.
- Tear size by itself does not equal irreparability; a massive tear can still be repairable if retraction (Patte) is low-grade and fatty infiltration (Goutallier) is mild. Size should be interpreted alongside Patte and Goutallier grade, not in isolation.
- Meta-analyses (2023–2024) show double-row/transosseous-equivalent repair yields significantly lower retear rates and better functional scores specifically for large/massive tears (≥3 cm), with no significant benefit over single-row repair for tears <3 cm — the treatment-implication cutoff in practice tracks the 3 cm threshold more than the traditional four-tier boundaries.
- Correlate clinical strength with imaged tear size before finalizing a surgical plan — strength does not always track structural tear size, especially in chronic compensated tears.
