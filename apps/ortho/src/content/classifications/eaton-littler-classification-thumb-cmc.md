## Overview

The Eaton-Littler classification stages thumb carpometacarpal (trapeziometacarpal) joint osteoarthritis radiographically, from mild synovitis without subluxation (Stage I) through pantrapezial arthritis with scaphotrapezial (STT) joint involvement (Stage IV). Stage guides the choice between non-operative management and the specific surgical reconstruction offered when non-operative measures fail.

## Types

| Stage | Radiographic findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Normal or slightly widened joint space (synovitis/effusion), no subluxation | Splinting, NSAIDs, injection; surgery rarely needed (volar ligament reconstruction only in young high-demand with instability) |
| II | Joint space narrowing, subluxation <1/3, small osteophytes/loose bodies <2 mm | Nonop trial first; if refractory: trapeziectomy ± LRTI/suspensionplasty or arthrodesis |
| III | Further narrowing, subluxation >1/3, osteophytes/loose bodies >2 mm | Same surgical menu as Stage II; arthrodesis attractive in younger manual laborers |
| IV | Pantrapezial arthritis — scaphotrapezial (STT) joint also involved | Trapeziectomy-based procedures preferred (STT involvement is a relative contraindication to isolated arthrodesis and to STT-sparing implants) |

## Pearls

- Diagnosis is supported by the grind test (axial load + rotation reproduces pain, high sensitivity) and CMC joint line tenderness; the Robert view (true AP of CMC1, hyperpronated) is the most sensitive plain radiograph for early joint space narrowing/osteophytes, with lateral and Eaton stress views used to assess subluxation.
- STT (scaphotrapezial) involvement at Stage IV is the key branch point: it is a relative contraindication to isolated CMC arthrodesis and to STT-sparing implants, steering management toward trapeziectomy-based procedures.
- Within the trapeziectomy-based surgical menu (simple trapeziectomy, LRTI, suspensionplasty), multiple RCTs — including long-term follow-up — show LRTI does not reliably outperform simple trapeziectomy or trapeziectomy with hematoma-distraction arthroplasty on validated outcome scores, while adding donor-site morbidity and operative time. A 2025 network meta-analysis of 21 RCTs found no clinically important differences among simple trapeziectomy, LRTI, and suspensionplasty in mid-term outcomes — technique choice should weigh surgeon experience, operative time, and recovery profile rather than an assumed superiority of any single reconstruction.
- Suture-suspensionplasty (suture-button/internal brace constructs) achieves comparable pain relief, grip/pinch strength, and patient-reported outcomes to LRTI at 1-year follow-up with generally faster early recovery.
- CMC arthrodesis is reserved for younger, high-demand manual laborers with Eaton II-III disease and no STT involvement, trading motion for stability/strength; nonunion rates are historically 10-25% depending on technique/fixation.
- Implant (pyrocarbon/metal-on-polyethylene) arthroplasty has largely fallen out of favor due to higher revision rates compared with trapeziectomy-based procedures, though newer designs are still used selectively in some centers.
- On presentation, distinguish thumb CMC arthritis from De Quervain tenosynovitis and scaphoid pathology: the grind test localizes to the CMC joint line, Finkelstein's test localizes to the first extensor compartment, and snuffbox tenderness suggests scaphoid pathology.
