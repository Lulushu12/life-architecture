## Overview

The Levine-Edwards classification grades bilateral C2 pars interarticularis fractures (hangman fractures) by the degree of translation, angulation, and C2-3 disc/ligament disruption, which determines stability and the choice between collar, halo, and surgical fixation. It applies specifically to traumatic spondylolisthesis of the axis.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Bilateral pars fracture, <3mm translation, no angulation, C2-3 disc/ALL intact | Stable — rigid collar/Miami-J |
| **II** | Bilateral pars fracture with significant translation (>3mm) and/or angulation; C2-3 disc disrupted, PLL may remain intact | Unstable in flexion-extension — reducible with traction/extension, then halo vest (cautious axial traction, avoid over-distraction) |
| **IIA** | Severe angulation with minimal translation; flexion-distraction mechanism, disc space opens anteriorly (hinge injury) | Traction is contraindicated (risks catastrophic distraction through the disrupted disc) — treat with extension and compression in a halo, or primary surgical fixation |
| **III** | Bilateral pars fracture with unilateral or bilateral C2-3 facet dislocation | Highly unstable — surgical reduction and fixation (posterior C2-3, or combined) mandatory |

## Pearls

- Type IIA is the key trap: it looks less displaced than Type II on translation alone, but the disc-opening hinge mechanism means axial traction — appropriate for Type II — can catastrophically distract the segment in IIA. Distinguishing II from IIA on the angulation/translation pattern is what changes management from "traction then halo" to "no traction, compression halo or surgery."
- Type III requires surgery because of the facet dislocation component, not the pars fracture itself — reduction and fixation (posterior C2-3, or combined) is mandatory regardless of how the pars fracture itself would otherwise be graded.
- Assessing C2-3 disc/ALL disruption and translation/angulation on both CT and MRI is what differentiates the Levine-Edwards types and predicts instability — CT alone may understate soft-tissue disruption.
