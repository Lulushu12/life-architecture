## Overview

The Lafosse classification grades subscapularis tears by extent, from partial upper-third involvement (types 1–2) to complete tears with lesser tuberosity involvement and retraction (types 4–5). Subscapularis tears are frequently missed on standard exam and imaging unless specifically sought, and are commonly associated with biceps pulley lesions and medial biceps subluxation/dislocation — the Lafosse grade determines whether arthroscopic repair alone is sufficient or whether mobilization (interval slides) or a more extensive reconstruction is needed.

## Types

| Type | Extent | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Partial, upper-third tear | Often amenable to arthroscopic repair, typically with concomitant biceps tenodesis given the shared pulley anatomy |
| 3 | Intermediate extent | Individualized based on intraoperative mobility and tissue quality |
| 4–5 | Complete tear, with lesser tuberosity involvement and retraction | May need interval slides for mobilization; occasionally irreparable |

## Pearls

- Subscapularis tears are frequently missed on standard exam unless belly-press and lift-off testing are specifically performed, and are easy to miss on imaging unless axial MRI sequences are specifically scrutinized — have a low threshold to examine and image this tendon deliberately rather than relying on general cuff testing.
- Always examine for biceps pulley lesions and medial biceps subluxation/instability when a subscapularis tear is suspected or found — the biceps pulley is formed in part by subscapularis fibers, so pulley lesions/subscapularis tears are a common co-driver of biceps instability that will not resolve with biceps tenodesis alone if the subscapularis pathology is left unaddressed.
- Hornblower's sign (a positive external rotation lag test at the arm held in abduction, indicating teres minor insufficiency) should not be confused with subscapularis testing — it is a separate, posterosuperior-cuff sign and predicts poor outcome after isolated repair without transfer.
- Lower-grade (1–2) tears are usually straightforward arthroscopic repairs with concomitant biceps tenodesis; higher-grade (4–5) tears with retraction may require an interval slide to mobilize, and repair should not be forced with excessive release, since destabilizing capsular/subscapularis release carries its own risk.
