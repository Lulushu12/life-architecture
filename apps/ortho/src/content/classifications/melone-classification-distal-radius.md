## Overview

The Melone classification describes complex intra-articular ("die-punch") distal radius fractures as four principal fragments: the radial shaft, the radial styloid, and the medial complex split into dorsomedial (dorsal lunate facet) and volomedial (volar lunate facet) fragments — the medial complex being the key load-bearing articular block. It is used specifically to plan fixation of comminuted/die-punch articular patterns, focusing attention on the medial (lunate) facet fragments that most often need fragment-specific control.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Stable, minimally displaced 4-part pattern | Closed reduction/cast may suffice; monitor closely |
| II | Unstable, displaced medial complex ("die-punch") — reducible (IIa) or irreducible/comminuted (IIb) | ORIF required; IIb often needs fragment-specific fixation as the medial complex will not reduce closed |
| III | Type II + volar spike fragment | Volar buttressing (plate) mandatory to prevent carpal translation |
| IV | Split medial fragment with rotation (dorsopalmar articular rotation) | Complex fixation, may require dorsal + volar combined approach or fragment-specific system to control rotated fragments |
| V | Severe explosive comminution with metaphyseal-diaphyseal extension | Bridging external fixation ± limited internal fixation, often staged; highest risk of malunion/nonunion |

## Pearls

- Melone's core value is emphasizing the **medial (lunate) facet** as the critical stability determinant: failure to control the volar ulnar/lunate facet fragment — often too small/distal for a standard volar plate's most distal row — is a classic cause of secondary carpal subluxation, and drives use of fragment-specific plates/spike plates for the medial complex.
- Reach for Melone specifically when planning fixation of a comminuted/die-punch articular pattern.
- Post-traumatic osteoarthritis correlates strongly with residual articular incongruity, reinforcing the need for anatomic control of the medial complex in Melone II–V patterns.
