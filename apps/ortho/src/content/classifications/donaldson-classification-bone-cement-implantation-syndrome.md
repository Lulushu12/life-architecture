## Overview

The Donaldson grading system stages bone cement implantation syndrome (BCIS) — the acute hypoxia, hypotension, and/or loss of consciousness/cardiac arrest that can occur around the time of cementation, prosthesis insertion, joint reduction, or (rarely) tourniquet deflation — by severity of hemodynamic/respiratory compromise. It is used intraoperatively to recognize BCIS as it evolves and to calibrate the resuscitation response in real time.

## Types

| Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Moderate hypoxia (SpO₂ <94%) or hypotension (drop >20% of baseline systolic) | IV fluids + vasopressor (phenylephrine/ephedrine) bolus at first sign; continue monitoring closely |
| **2** | Severe hypoxia (SpO₂ <88%) or hypotension (drop >40%), or unexpected loss of consciousness | Stop the cementing/impaction sequence if not yet complete; 100% oxygen, IV fluid bolus, vasopressor for hypotension |
| **3** | Cardiovascular collapse requiring cardiopulmonary resuscitation | Escalate to full ACLS-level resuscitation |

## Pearls

- Risk is substantially higher in hip **hemiarthroplasty for fracture** than elective THA — osteoporotic, unprepared canal, and a frailer/higher-ASA cohort combine to raise risk — so anticipatory readiness should scale with fracture hemiarthroplasty specifically, not cemented arthroplasty in general.
- Other risk factors: male sex, ASA grade III–IV, pre-existing cardiopulmonary disease/pulmonary hypertension, pathologic or heavily sclerotic/osteoporotic bone, and long-stem or revision femoral components (more medullary content displaced at pressurization).
- Prevention is more effective than rescue: communicate explicitly with anesthesia immediately before cementation, perform femoral canal (pulsatile) lavage before cement introduction, use a vent hole/retrograde ("distal-to-proximal") cement filling technique, and avoid excessive/forceful pressurization — particularly in osteoporotic canals where compliance is high.
- In the very highest-risk patients (severe cardiopulmonary disease, ASA IV, frail hip-fracture patients where a hemodynamic hit could be catastrophic), an uncemented stem is a reasonable alternative — accepting a modestly higher periprosthetic fracture risk in exchange for eliminating BCIS risk. However, national joint registry data generally favor cemented fixation for hip fracture hemiarthroplasty on aggregate revision/mortality outcomes, so this substitution should be individualized rather than routine.
- Grade 2–3 events require stopping the cementing/impaction sequence if not yet complete — do not continue seating the prosthesis through an evolving Grade 2 event.
