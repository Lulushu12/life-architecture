## Overview

The Coughlin classification categorizes bunionette (tailor's bunion) deformity — a prominence/deformity of the 5th metatarsal head analogous to hallux valgus at the opposite border of the foot — by the anatomic level of the deformity (head, shaft, or intermetatarsal angle). It guides selection between condylectomy alone and the various osteotomy options, since an isolated distal procedure under-corrects a shaft- or angle-driven deformity.

## Types

| Type | Deformity | 4th–5th IMA | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Enlarged/laterally deviated 5th metatarsal head (widened head), normal shaft alignment | Normal | Lateral condylectomy ± distal osteotomy |
| 2 | Lateral bowing of the 5th metatarsal shaft | Increased | Distal chevron osteotomy or diaphyseal oblique osteotomy |
| 3 | Widened 4th–5th intermetatarsal angle from divergence ("splayfoot") | Increased | Diaphyseal or proximal osteotomy for adequate translation; isolated distal osteotomy under-corrects |

## Pearls

- Weight-bearing AP radiograph measures the **4th–5th intermetatarsal angle (normal <8°)** and the **5th MTP lateral deviation angle**, which together define the Coughlin type.
- A distal-only osteotomy will **under-correct** a type 3 (angle/divergence-driven) deformity — match the osteotomy level to the deformity level, not just to symptom severity.
- Bunionette carries a lower biomechanical demand than the 1st ray, so wide toe-box shoes and lateral forefoot padding are often sufficient first-line treatment and surgery is comparatively less often required.
- Percutaneous/MIS 5th-metatarsal osteotomy techniques have moved from emerging toward established in recent (2024) systematic review evidence, with meaningful improvements in pain and AOFAS function scores; a large percutaneous series without internal fixation or postoperative immobilization reported a low (1.5%) complication rate.
- Lateral wound complications are a recognized risk given the thinner soft-tissue coverage on the lateral forefoot.
