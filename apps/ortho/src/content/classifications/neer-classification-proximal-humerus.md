## Overview

The Neer classification organizes proximal humerus fractures by the four Codman/Neer anatomic segments — humeral head (articular segment), greater tuberosity, lesser tuberosity, and shaft. A segment is considered a displaced "part" when it is **>1 cm displaced or >45° angulated** relative to the adjacent segment(s); a fracture with multiple fracture lines that does not meet this threshold is still counted as "1-part" (minimally displaced). It is used at initial evaluation to frame the number of displaced segments and drive the nonoperative-vs-ORIF-vs-arthroplasty discussion.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1-part (minimally displaced)** | Any fracture pattern (even 4 lines) not meeting displacement criteria — ~80–85% of all proximal humerus fractures | Nonoperative: sling immobilization, early pendulum/passive ROM at 1–2 wk |
| **2-part** | One segment displaced relative to the other three (surgical neck, anatomic neck, greater tuberosity, or lesser tuberosity variants) | Surgical neck 2-part: often ORIF if unstable/displaced, especially in younger patients; isolated displaced greater tuberosity >5 mm (overhead athletes/younger) or >1 cm (general) — ORIF, risk of subacromial impingement if left displaced |
| **3-part** | Two segments displaced relative to the remaining two (e.g., surgical neck + greater tuberosity, head remains attached to one tuberosity) | ORIF (locking plate) in physiologically younger/good bone; consider reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA) in elderly with poor bone quality/rotator cuff compromise |
| **4-part** | All four segments displaced from one another | Highest AVN risk (up to 25–40%, lower with the classic valgus-impacted variant); rTSA favored in patients >65–70y, especially valgus-impacted or head-split variants with comminution; ORIF viable if medial hinge/calcar intact (Hertel positive predictors) in younger patients |
| **Head-splitting** | Articular surface fractured into ≥2 major fragments | Usually rTSA or hemiarthroplasty (young, reconstructible) — poor candidate for ORIF |
| **Fracture-dislocation** (anterior/posterior) | Any of the above with glenohumeral joint dislocation | Urgent reduction; treat as displaced fracture pattern above; higher vascular injury risk (check axillary artery) |

## Pearls

- Inter- and intra-observer reliability of Neer on plain radiographs is only moderate (kappa ~0.4–0.6); CT with 2D/3D reconstruction improves but does not fully resolve this — treat Neer as a communication/decision-framework, not a precise anatomic truth.
- A preserved medial metaphyseal (calcar) extension >8 mm and intact medial hinge (Hertel's criteria) predict lower AVN risk even in displaced 3-/4-part fractures, and are what actually push a 4-part fracture toward ORIF rather than arthroplasty in a younger patient.
- The PROFHER trial (JAMA 2015, durable through 5-year follow-up) found no clinically important difference in Oxford Shoulder Score between surgical and nonoperative treatment of displaced 2-/3-/4-part fractures in older adults (mean age ~66) — a reminder that the Neer "part" count alone does not mandate surgery, particularly in lower-demand elderly patients.
- Rotator cuff dysfunction and tuberosity malunion/nonunion are major drivers of poor functional outcome independent of whether the "fracture" itself has united — Neer part-count does not capture this risk.
