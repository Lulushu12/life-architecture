## Overview

Introduced by Rivière/MacDessi et al. (2021), CPAK describes a patient's **constitutional (native, pre-arthritic) coronal knee phenotype** rather than the acquired arthritic deformity itself. It is used to plan and audit alignment strategy in TKA — particularly kinematic and functional alignment techniques — and, more recently, to inform HTO outcome prediction.

CPAK is derived from two calculated parameters on long-leg radiographs:
- **Arithmetic HKA (aHKA) = MPTA − LDFA** — approximates native limb alignment before joint-space loss. Bands: **varus** <−2°, **neutral** −2° to +2°, **valgus** >+2°.
- **Arithmetic joint line obliquity (aJLO) = MPTA + LDFA** — describes whether the joint line is apex-distal, neutral, or apex-proximal. Bands: **apex-distal** <177°, **neutral** 177–183°, **apex-proximal** >183°.

Crossing the two 3-level parameters yields nine phenotypes (CPAK I–IX).

## Types

| CPAK type | aHKA | aJLO | Clinical relevance |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | Varus | Apex-distal | The classic "varus knee" phenotype — most prevalent pattern in varus tibiofemoral OA cohorts; the population HTO and varus-correcting kinematic TKA techniques were built around |
| **II** | Varus | Neutral | Common varus phenotype without joint-line obliquity exaggeration |
| **III** | Varus | Apex-proximal | Less common; varus limb with valgus-oriented joint line — mechanical alignment risks over-constraining the native joint line here |
| **IV** | Neutral | Apex-distal | Neutral overall limb but obliquely oriented joint line (common in females with a wider pelvis/more valgus femur combined with varus tibia) |
| **V** | Neutral | Neutral | The "textbook" constitutional knee that classic mechanical alignment (180° HKA, both component axes perpendicular to mechanical axis) best reproduces |
| **VI** | Neutral | Apex-proximal | Neutral limb, valgus-leaning joint line |
| **VII** | Valgus | Apex-distal | Uncommon combination |
| **VIII** | Valgus | Neutral | Common valgus phenotype |
| **IX** | Valgus | Apex-proximal | Classic "valgus knee" phenotype, analogous role to Type I on the valgus side |

## Pearls

- CPAK does not by itself dictate a surgical alignment strategy — it is the framework used to plan **kinematic and functional alignment** TKA, where the goal is to respect the patient's native aJLO rather than forcing every knee to a neutral mechanical axis, while staying within a "safe" restriction boundary (commonly ±5° from neutral HKA and defined MPTA/LDFA boundaries) to avoid outlier component positions associated with early loosening or instability.
- Robotic platforms now report intra-operative and post-operative CPAK type to audit how well a chosen alignment philosophy reproduced (or intentionally shifted) the constitutional phenotype.
- A 2024 proposal for a "restriction-boundary-based CPAK" (Rb-CPAK) found the original CPAK boundaries do not map directly onto restricted-kinematic-alignment TKA safe zones, prompting a refined classification specifically for surgeons using restricted kinematic alignment.
- A 2025 study found that changes in CPAK type after restricted kinematic alignment TKA (postoperative "CPAK drift") did not significantly influence patient-reported functional outcomes — reassuring surgeons that exact phenotype reproduction may be less critical than achieving balanced flexion/extension gaps.
- CPAK's use has extended beyond arthroplasty: a 2025 study examined the effect of CPAK phenotype on clinical outcomes after high tibial osteotomy.
- Exact CPAK-type population prevalence percentages were not reliably retrievable at the time this content was compiled and are therefore described qualitatively (e.g., "most prevalent," "uncommon") rather than with precise cohort figures — consult the original CPAK description for exact statistics.
