## Overview

The Robinson classification is a more granular, prognostic system for clavicle fractures, developed specifically to predict nonunion risk in middle two-thirds fractures. It is the classification that actually drives the operative-vs-nonoperative discussion for displaced midshaft clavicle fractures, in contrast to the purely anatomic Allman system.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication (nonunion relevance) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Medial fifth | Rare |
| **2A** | Middle two-thirds, undisplaced (<100% width) | Low nonunion risk (~1–5%) nonoperative |
| **2B1** | Middle two-thirds, displaced, simple or single-butterfly fragment, no comminution | Nonunion risk nonoperative ~15% |
| **2B2** | Middle two-thirds, displaced, comminuted or segmental | Highest nonunion risk nonoperative, reported up to 20–30% in some series — key group where operative fixation is most strongly considered |
| **3** | Lateral fifth | Analogous to the Neer distal clavicle classification |

## Pearls

- Robinson type 2B2 is the pivotal group: comminution/segmental pattern, not displacement magnitude alone, is what pushes nonunion risk from ~15% (2B1) to 20–30% (2B2) and makes operative fixation most strongly considered.
- The Canadian Orthopaedic Trauma Society (COTS) 2007 RCT — the landmark evidence for operative fixation of displaced midshaft clavicle fractures — was conducted in a Robinson-classified population; its 15.1% nonunion rate in the nonoperative arm corresponds most closely to the 2B1/2B2 groups rather than 2A.
- Intramedullary fixation devices are best suited to simple transverse/short-oblique (2B1-type) patterns without significant comminution; they are not ideal for 2B2 comminuted patterns or very short medial/lateral fragments.
- Type 3 (lateral fifth) fractures should be cross-referenced against the Neer distal clavicle classification, which is the system that actually governs their management via CC ligament status.
