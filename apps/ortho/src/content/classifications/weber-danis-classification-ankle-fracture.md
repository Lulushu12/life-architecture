## Overview

The Weber/Danis (AO/Danis-Weber) classification grades ankle fractures by the level of the fibular fracture relative to the tibiofibular syndesmosis on plain radiographs. Because syndesmotic disruption tracks with how far proximal the fibular fracture sits, the system is used as a first-pass predictor of syndesmosis instability and as the entry point into the ankle fracture treatment ladder.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Fibula fracture below/at the level of the plafond (infrasyndesmotic), usually transverse; syndesmosis almost always intact | Often stable/non-operative; associated medial-sided injury (vertical medial malleolus or deltoid) suggests a supination-adduction pattern — check for talar dome impaction |
| **B** | Fibula fracture at the level of the syndesmosis (transsyndesmotic), oblique/spiral; syndesmosis variable — disrupted in up to ~50% | Requires clinical/intraoperative stress testing after fibula fixation to decide on syndesmosis stabilization; if stable (negative stress test, intact deltoid), non-operative treatment is reasonable |
| **C** | Fibula fracture above the syndesmosis (suprasyndesmotic); syndesmosis disrupted by definition | Always requires syndesmosis stabilization; a proximal fibula fracture (Maisonneuve) mandates full-length tibia/fibula radiographs and a knee exam |

## Pearls

- Deltoid ligament competence (clinical exam or stress imaging) is central to deciding whether a Weber B ankle fracture is stable — the deep deltoid band is the key restraint to talar external rotation/lateral translation.
- Always stress-test (Cotton test + external rotation) every Weber B/C ankle intraoperatively after fibula fixation; malreduction of the syndesmosis judged by fluoroscopy alone is reported in up to ~25–50% of screw-fixed cases, so use contralateral-comparison fluoroscopy or intraoperative CT/O-arm when available.
- Maisonneuve pattern is easy to miss: a proximal fibular fracture combined with interosseous membrane/syndesmosis disruption can present with a radiographically unremarkable ankle beyond medial tenderness or a posterior malleolus fracture. Always palpate the proximal fibula and obtain full-length tibia-fibula films when medial-sided pain is disproportionate to the ankle films.
- Weber C fractures always require syndesmosis stabilization; treatment ladder: Weber A stable → boot/cast WBAT; Weber B stable (negative stress, intact deltoid) → functional brace/cast WBAT; Weber B unstable or bi-/trimalleolar → ORIF fibula ± syndesmosis ± posterior malleolus; Weber C/Maisonneuve → always ORIF fibula + syndesmosis stabilization.
- Fixing an adequately sized posterior malleolus fragment (it carries the PITFL insertion) often restores syndesmotic stability directly — assess syndesmosis stability *after* posterior malleolus fixation, not before.
