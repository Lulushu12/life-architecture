## Overview

This clinical classification distinguishes the three lesser toe deformity patterns by which joint level(s) are affected, and separately whether the deformity is passively correctable (flexible) or fixed (rigid). The joint-level pattern points to an underlying cause (isolated tight-shoe deformity versus neuromuscular disease), while the flexible/rigid distinction determines whether a soft-tissue or a bony procedure is required.

## Types

| Deformity | MTP joint | PIP joint | DIP joint | Typical association |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hammer toe** | Neutral or mild extension | Flexed | Extended or neutral | Isolated, most often 2nd toe, often idiopathic or from a tight/short shoe |
| **Claw toe** | Extended (hyperextension/dorsal subluxation) | Flexed | Flexed | Usually multiple toes; strongly associated with neuromuscular disease (Charcot-Marie-Tooth, diabetic neuropathy) or cavus foot |
| **Mallet toe** | Neutral | Neutral | Flexed | Isolated DIP-level deformity, often from a long toe or tight distal shoe box |

## Pearls

- **Flexible vs. rigid is the operative decision point, not the joint-level pattern itself**: flexible deformities respond to soft-tissue procedures (flexor tenotomy or flexor-to-extensor tendon transfer/Girdlestone-Taylor for hammer toe; extensor tenotomy/dorsal MTP capsulotomy for claw toe), while rigid deformities require bony correction (PIP resection arthroplasty or arthrodesis via K-wire, intramedullary implant, or peg-in-hole fusion).
- Add a **Weil osteotomy** when there is concurrent MTP subluxation/dislocation, to decompress the joint and allow soft-tissue balancing.
- Perform the **MTP dorsal drawer/Lachman test** for plantar plate instability (>50% subluxation or frank dislocatability = grade 2–3 tear) and check for a positive **"V-sign"** (gap between toes) suggesting crossover deformity.
- **Rapidly progressive multi-toe clawing** in a patient without known neuromuscular disease is a red flag — screen for Charcot-Marie-Tooth disease or other peripheral neuropathy (check foot arch shape, cavus, hindfoot varus) before treating as idiopathic.
- Chronic MTP synovitis progressively attenuates the plantar plate, permitting dorsal subluxation and eventual crossover deformity — the 2nd toe is especially vulnerable because it lacks the protection of an adjacent toe and often bears disproportionate load with an index-minus or long 2nd metatarsal.
- Arthrodesis generally gives a more durable, straighter, more stable correction than resection arthroplasty, at the cost of a stiffer toe.
