## Overview

The Segond fracture and its counterpart, the reverse Segond fracture, are small avulsion fractures seen on plain AP knee radiograph that act as radiographic markers of specific ligament injury rather than as isolated bony injuries requiring fixation. Recognizing either finding on plain film should immediately trigger MRI and treatment of the associated soft-tissue injury, not treatment of the fleck itself.

## Types

| Finding | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Segond fracture** | Avulsion fracture of the lateral tibial condyle at the attachment of the anterolateral ligament/lateral capsular ligament, seen as a small bony fleck on AP radiograph just distal to the lateral tibial plateau | Pathognomonic marker of ACL rupture (association >75–90% in most series) and frequently co-exists with lateral meniscal tears; obtain MRI, treat as a marker of combined anterolateral/ACL instability (not an isolated avulsion needing fixation), and consider concomitant lateral extra-articular augmentation (ALL reconstruction/LET) at the time of ACLR given the anterolateral structure injury it signals |
| **Reverse Segond fracture** | Posteromedial tibial avulsion | Marker of PCL and/or MCL injury; obtain MRI to characterize the combined ligament injury |

## Pearls

- A Segond fracture on plain film = ACL rupture until proven otherwise — it is a red flag requiring MRI, not a discharge-and-observe finding.
- Segond fracture is also the classic radiographic correlate on the bone-bruise/associated-injury pattern-recognition list, alongside the "kissing" lateral femoral condyle + posterolateral tibial plateau contusion (also from an ACL pivot-shift mechanism) and the deep sulcus sign (lateral femoral notch >2 mm, seen in ACL rupture/chronic ACL insufficiency).
- The reverse Segond fracture should prompt the same associated-injury screening reflex as the Segond fracture, but pointed at the posteromedial structures (PCL/MCL) rather than the ACL/anterolateral complex — do not assume a tibial avulsion fracture is automatically ACL-related without confirming its location.
- Presence of a Segond fracture is one of the specific high-risk criteria (alongside age <25, high-grade pivot-shift, pivoting/contact sport, generalized ligamentous laxity, and revision setting) used to decide whether to add lateral extra-articular augmentation to ACL reconstruction.
