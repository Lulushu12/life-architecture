## Overview

Not all PRP is the same intervention: preparations differ substantially by leukocyte content, which drives different inflammatory profiles and different best-evidence indications. Classifying every order/consent as leukocyte-rich (LR-PRP) or leukocyte-poor (LP-PRP), together with the platelet-concentration multiple, is required because pooling all "PRP" as one category is a major source of trial heterogeneity and conflicting meta-analyses.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Leukocyte-rich PRP (LR-PRP)** | High leukocyte content (often near whole-blood WBC or concentrated); high neutrophil-derived cytokines (IL-1β, MMPs) — more pro-inflammatory; platelet concentration variable, system-dependent (typically 3–6× baseline) | Best-evidence indication is tendinopathy (lateral epicondylitis, patellar tendinopathy), where a controlled local inflammatory stimulus may aid remodeling; higher rate of transient post-injection flare/swelling (~20–30%); do **not** use intra-articularly in an inflamed/effusive joint |
| **Leukocyte-poor PRP (LP-PRP)** | Low/absent leukocyte content (buffy coat excluded); low neutrophil-derived cytokines — favors an anti-catabolic profile; typically 3–8× baseline platelet concentration (double-spin systems concentrate platelets further) | Best-evidence indication is intra-articular use (knee OA), with a lower post-injection flare/synovitis rate; preferred for IA injection in degenerative (non-inflammatory) OA |

## Pearls

- Specify LR vs LP **and** the platelet-concentration multiple on every order/consent — outcomes and adverse-event rates in the literature are preparation-specific.
- Avoid LR-PRP for intra-articular injection into an actively inflamed/effusive joint — the added pro-inflammatory cytokine load increases post-injection flare risk without clear added benefit over LP-PRP in this setting.
- Therapeutic effect is generally assumed to require ≥3–5× baseline platelet count; supraphysiologic concentrations (>8×) may be inhibitory in vitro (paradoxical dose-response) — more concentrate is not automatically better.
- For rotator cuff repair augmentation specifically, platelet concentration does not significantly influence clinical outcomes or retear rates (2026 meta-analysis) — do not over-engineer the preparation protocol for this indication, and counsel on an associated higher rate of post-injection adhesive capsulitis.
- LR-PRP is typically used for tendon targets with needle fenestration ("dry needling") through the pathologic tendon combined with PRP deposition; LP-PRP is typically used for intra-articular knee series (single or 2–3 weekly injections, no consensus on optimal series length).
