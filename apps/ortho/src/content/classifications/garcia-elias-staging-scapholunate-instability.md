## Overview

The Garcia-Elias model stages scapholunate (SL) ligament failure as a biomechanical progression — from an isolated, arthroscopy-only-positive intrinsic ligament tear (predynamic) through complete SLIL disruption revealed only on stress views (dynamic), to fixed, radiographically visible DISI (static reducible, then static fixed), and finally end-stage SLAC arthritis. It is the primary framework for choosing between activity modification, arthroscopic pinning, open repair, ligament reconstruction, and salvage.

## Types

| Stage | Ligamentous status | Radiographic findings | Management implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Predynamic** | SLIL partial tear/attenuation (dorsal component intact) | Normal static and stress films; positive arthroscopy only (Geissler I–II) | Activity modification; arthroscopic debridement ± thermal/electrothermal shrinkage (limited modern role); observe |
| **Dynamic** | Complete SLIL (intrinsic) tear, secondary stabilizers (DIC, extrinsic) intact | Normal static films; SL gap/DISI appears only on stress views (clenched fist) | Arthroscopic-assisted reduction and pinning, or open repair + dorsal capsulodesis if acute (<6–8 wks from injury) |
| **Static reducible** | SLIL + extrinsic ligament (DIC/STT) injury | SL widening/DISI present on static films but passively correctable | Open repair/reconstruction (Brunelli-type tenodesis, SL Internal Brace); consider Blatt capsulodesis as adjunct |
| **Static fixed (irreducible)** | Chronic complete disruption, secondary contracture | Fixed DISI, not passively correctable, no arthritic changes yet | Ligament reconstruction (three-ligament tenodesis, bone-retinaculum-bone/Garcia-Elias graft) if cartilage preserved; consider limited fusion if reconstruction unreliable |
| **SLAC** | End-stage — arthritic degeneration per Watson staging | Joint space loss, osteophytes per SLAC stage I–III | Salvage ladder (styloidectomy → PRC/4-corner fusion → wrist fusion) |

## Pearls

- The staged failure mechanism is the biomechanical rationale for the model: isolated SLIL injury (predynamic/dynamic) is often asymptomatic on static imaging because the extrinsic RSC/DIC ligaments compensate until they too fail or attenuate — at which point static DISI appears.
- Wrist arthroscopy remains the diagnostic gold standard, graded by the Geissler classification (I–IV, based on ligament laxity and probe/scope pass-through between the SL and LT joints) and correlated with Garcia-Elias stage; predynamic injury corresponds to Geissler I–II.
- Static radiographs (PA, lateral, clenched-fist AP, radial/ulnar deviation views) are frequently normal in early/dynamic instability — a normal static film does not exclude dynamic SL instability, and stress views or arthroscopy are needed.
- The 6–8 week window for acute primary repair with dorsal capsulodesis is a hard practical cutoff in the dynamic stage: beyond this, tissue quality typically no longer supports direct repair and reconstruction becomes necessary.
- SL Internal Brace/suture-tape augmentation is increasingly used to protect a repair or reconstruction during healing; mid-term data (2026) show reasonable maintenance of SL gap/angle, though comparative long-term outcomes against classic tenodesis are still maturing.
- Recurrent SL gapping/loss of reduction after ligament reconstruction remains a persistent limitation of tenodesis techniques over long-term follow-up — a key counseling point for the static reducible/fixed stages.
- Once frank SLAC arthritis develops, ligament reconstruction is abandoned in favor of the Watson-staged salvage ladder.
