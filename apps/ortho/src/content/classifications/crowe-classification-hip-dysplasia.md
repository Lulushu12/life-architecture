## Overview

The Crowe classification grades the degree of proximal femoral head migration in dysplastic or dislocated hips, measured as a percentage of femoral head diameter (or via a Hartofilakidis-style pelvic-height measurement). It is used chiefly to predict the technical difficulty of total hip arthroplasty (THA) in dysplastic hips and, specifically, whether a subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy will be needed.

## Types

| Type | Proximal migration of femoral head | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | <50% (<10 mm) | Standard THA technique, low risk of needing shortening |
| **II** | 50–75% (10–15 mm) | Anatomic or near-anatomic cup placement usually achievable; consider structural graft for cup coverage |
| **III** | 75–100% (15–20 mm) | High risk of sciatic/femoral nerve traction injury with acute lengthening; consider subtrochanteric shortening osteotomy |
| **IV** | >100% (>20 mm), high dislocation | Subtrochanteric shortening derotational osteotomy nearly always required; small femoral components, cup placed at true acetabulum with possible medialization/structural graft |

## Pearls

- Crowe III–IV is the key threshold for predicting the need for femoral shortening at THA — it is listed among the "key numbers to remember" for this diagnosis cluster precisely because it changes the operative plan (adding a subtrochanteric shortening derotational osteotomy) rather than just describing severity.
- The underlying rationale is nerve safety: acute limb lengthening beyond roughly 3–4 cm risks sciatic (or femoral) nerve palsy, so higher Crowe grades — which imply a larger lengthening requirement to restore the hip center — are the ones where shortening osteotomy becomes essentially mandatory.
- CT is valuable for precise Crowe measurement in complex dysplasia, more so than plain radiographs alone, particularly for preoperative planning.
- Crowe and Hartofilakidis are complementary, not competing — Crowe is measured by percentage of femoral head migration, Hartofilakidis is a more surgically descriptive 3-tier system (contained dysplasia vs low/partial dislocation vs high/complete dislocation); many surgeons report both.
