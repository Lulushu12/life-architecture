## Overview

The Hamada classification grades rotator cuff tear arthropathy (grades 1 through 5, with grade 4 subdivided A/B) based on acromiohumeral distance (AHD) and secondary degenerative change on plain radiographs. It is used in massive/irreparable rotator cuff tears to predict progression to frank arthropathy and to mark the threshold for converting from cuff-preserving strategies to reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA).

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Massive cuff tear, acromiohumeral distance (AHD) preserved (>6 mm) | Cuff repair, superior capsular reconstruction (SCR), or tendon transfer possible; rTSA not required |
| **2** | AHD ≤5 mm (narrowed) | Borderline; repair/SCR vs. rTSA based on function, pain, fatty infiltration |
| **3** | Acetabularization of the acromion (concave undersurface) | Superior migration established; rTSA favored if symptomatic |
| **4A** | Glenohumeral joint narrowing plus acetabularization | rTSA indicated |
| **4B** | Same as 4A plus femoralization of the humeral head | rTSA indicated |
| **5** | Collapse of the humeral head | rTSA indicated; technically more demanding, plan for bone loss |

## Pearls

- Hamada ≥3 is the practical cutoff favoring rTSA when the patient is symptomatic; cuff-preserving adjuncts (SCR, subacromial balloon spacer, tendon transfer) are not appropriate once glenohumeral arthritic change (Hamada ≥3) or fixed superior migration is established.
- rTSA is also indicated for symptomatic Hamada 1–2 with pseudoparalysis, not just higher grades — grade alone does not override clinical function.
- Chronic superior migration in cuff-tear arthropathy produces subacromial/coracoacromial arch impingement, acetabularization of the acromion undersurface, and femoralization of the rounded-off humeral head — the radiographic findings the classification is built on.
- Fatty infiltration of infraspinatus/teres minor (Goutallier/Fuchs grade ≥3) predicts failure of cuff repair and predicts poor active external rotation even after rTSA (positive external rotation lag sign) — assess this alongside Hamada grade, since AHD alone does not capture cuff tissue quality.
- Advanced cases (grade 5, or any grade with loss of the coracoacromial arch) can progress to anterosuperior escape and are technically more demanding, requiring planning for bone loss.
