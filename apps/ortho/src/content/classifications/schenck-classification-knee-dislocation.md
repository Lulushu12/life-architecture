## Overview

The Schenck classification (KD I–V) grades knee dislocation / multiligament knee injury (MLKI) by the pattern of ligaments disrupted rather than by mechanism or direction of tibiofemoral displacement. It is used for surgical planning and risk stratification: it does not by itself confirm a radiographically dislocated knee is required — the underlying injury is defined functionally as disruption of ≥2 of the 4 major ligament complexes (ACL, PCL, MCL, PLC), many of which reduce spontaneously before presentation.

## Types

| Type | Ligaments involved | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **KD I** | One cruciate (ACL or PCL) + one collateral (MCL or PLC); cruciate can be partial | Lowest-energy pattern; still requires full vascular workup; often amenable to single-stage reconstruction |
| **KD II** | Both cruciates (ACL + PCL), collaterals intact | Rare pure "bicruciate" pattern |
| **KD III-M** | Both cruciates + MCL (PLC intact) | Higher-energy; associated with medial-sided soft tissue compromise |
| **KD III-L** | Both cruciates + PLC/LCL (MCL intact) | Highest peroneal nerve and vascular injury rates of the "III" subtypes; PLC repair/reconstruction is technically demanding and time-sensitive |
| **KD IV** | Both cruciates + both collaterals (global instability) | Highest-energy, highest vascular injury rate; often requires staged reconstruction and external fixation bridging |
| **KD V** | Multiligament injury with periarticular fracture (fracture-dislocation) | Treated per combined ortho-trauma/vascular protocol |

**Modifiers** (always appended when applicable): **C** = vascular injury present, **N** = neurologic injury present (e.g., KD-IIIL-C-N denotes a KD IIIL pattern with both arterial and nerve injury).

## Pearls

- Any knee dislocation — even if spontaneously reduced — mandates serial vascular exam ± ankle-brachial index (ABI); a normal-appearing knee on ED arrival does not exclude dislocation, since spontaneous reduction at the scene occurs in up to 50% of cases. Treat any grossly unstable knee with a documented mechanism as a reduced dislocation until proven otherwise.
- Popliteal artery injury occurs in ~5–15% of true knee dislocations (higher in anterior/posterior high-energy patterns); peroneal nerve injury occurs in 15–40% of PLC-involved (KD-IIIL-type) dislocations.
- *Historical note*: the older Kennedy classification described knee dislocation by tibiofemoral displacement direction (anterior, posterior, lateral, medial, rotatory) rather than ligament pattern. Anterior (hyperextension) dislocations were classically associated with the highest reported popliteal artery injury rates. Schenck's ligament-pattern-based system has superseded Kennedy's for surgical planning because vascular risk correlates more with energy and ligament pattern than with displacement direction alone — but the historical hyperextension/anterior-displacement association with vascular injury remains clinically relevant when triaging urgency of vascular workup.
- Vascular risk and modifier status (C/N), not ligament pattern alone, drive the acute timeline: vascular repair takes absolute priority over ligament reconstruction, and external fixation bridges the gap when vascular repair, open injury, or gross instability precludes bracing.
