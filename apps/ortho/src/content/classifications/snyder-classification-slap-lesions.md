## Overview

The Snyder classification is the standard system for superior labrum anterior-to-posterior (SLAP) lesions, describing four basic tear patterns by the status of the superior labrum and biceps anchor. It is the primary framework for deciding among debridement, labral repair, and biceps tenodesis, and is assessed both on MR arthrography and arthroscopically (confirming a true peel-back mechanism intraoperatively is important, since many "SLAP" MRI findings are normal sublabral recesses).

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Degenerative fraying of superior labrum, biceps anchor intact | Debridement only; no repair needed |
| II | Superior labrum + biceps anchor detached from glenoid (most common; subtypes IIA anterior, IIB posterior, IIC combined) | Repair (suture anchors) in young/high-demand patients with a confirmed peel-back mechanism and failed structured PT; increasingly, biceps tenodesis favored over repair in patients >35–40 y, revision settings, or with concomitant biceps pathology |
| III | Bucket-handle tear of labrum, biceps anchor intact | Excise the bucket-handle fragment; anchor is stable so repair of the anchor is not needed |
| IV | Bucket-handle tear extending into the biceps tendon | Repair or tenodese the biceps portion depending on extent of tendon involvement and patient age/demand |

An extended classification (types V–X, Maffet/Morgan) describes SLAP lesions combined with Bankart, anterior-inferior, or anterior-posterior labral extension — relevant when glenohumeral instability coexists; management follows the combined-pathology repair principle of addressing both labral zones.

## Pearls

- Type II is the most common pattern and the one with the most evolving management: recent systematic reviews (2023–2025) show biceps tenodesis achieves comparable or better return-to-play and reoperation rates than SLAP repair, including in overhead athletes, and is increasingly favored — particularly beyond age 35–40, in revision settings, or when concomitant biceps pathology exists — given lower reoperation rates and avoidance of the stiffness/overtightening risk seen with repair.
- Baseball pitchers specifically have worse return-to-sport rates than other overhead athletes regardless of which procedure (repair vs. tenodesis) is chosen — counsel accordingly rather than expecting technique choice alone to change the trajectory.
- Confirm a true peel-back mechanism intraoperatively (arm in abduction–external rotation) before committing to anchor placement; many labral findings that look SLAP-like on MRI are normal sublabral recesses/sulci.
- Over-tightening the posterosuperior labrum during repair can restrict internal rotation and precipitate internal impingement symptoms postoperatively.
- Assess concomitant GIRD (>20° side-to-side internal rotation deficit at 90° abduction is significant) and scapular dyskinesis before committing to surgery — untreated kinetic-chain deficits predict recurrence/failure of isolated labral treatment in throwers, and physiotherapy addressing these factors is first-line for most patients, including many overhead athletes.
- No single clinical test (Speed's, O'Brien's, biceps load II, dynamic labral shear) is individually accurate for SLAP; MR arthrography remains the diagnostic anchor, and cluster testing improves clinical suspicion.
