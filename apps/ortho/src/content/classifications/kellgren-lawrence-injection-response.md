## Overview

The Kellgren-Lawrence (K-L) grade is the standard 0–4 radiographic severity scale for osteoarthritis. Beyond staging structural disease, it is used here as a modifier that predicts how well a joint will respond to intra-articular injection therapy (corticosteroid, hyaluronic acid, PRP) — response is not linear across grades, and expectation-setting should be calibrated to the grade before injecting.

## Types

| Grade | Radiographic definition | Definition (expected injection response) | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | No radiographic OA | N/A — treat other diagnosis | Injection not indicated for OA per se |
| 1 | Doubtful JSN, possible osteophyte | Good, but symptom often not OA-driven — investigate further | HA/PRP reasonable trial; low expectation ceiling because pain source may not be cartilage |
| 2 | Definite osteophyte, possible JSN | **Best responders** to HA, PRP, and steroid | First-line target population for all injectable therapies; best NNT data cluster here |
| 3 | Moderate multiple osteophytes, definite JSN, some sclerosis | Moderate, shorter duration; steroid still effective for flares | Injections as bridge/palliation; counsel on diminishing returns; PRP/BMAC reasonable if surgery deferred |
| 4 | Large osteophytes, severe JSN, marked sclerosis, deformity | **Poor and unpredictable** — bone-on-bone pain often mechanical, not inflammatory | Injections rarely change trajectory; use only as a bridge or in patients unfit/unwilling for arthroplasty; set expectations explicitly |

## Pearls

- K-L grade 2 is the sweet spot for injectable therapy of any kind (steroid, HA, PRP) — this is where the best NNT data cluster.
- Grade 1 disease can respond well to injection, but a good response does not confirm OA as the pain generator — the pain may not be cartilage-driven, so investigate further rather than assuming the diagnosis is settled by injection relief.
- At grade 4, response is poor and unpredictable because pain is often mechanical (bone-on-bone), not inflammatory — injections should not be expected to change the clinical trajectory and should be framed explicitly as a bridge, not a substitute for arthroplasty, in patients who are unfit or unwilling for surgery.
- Do not offer HA or PRP as a substitute for arthroplasty in grade 4 disease with mechanical symptoms and failed conservative therapy — set expectations that injectable therapy has a low ceiling of benefit at this stage.
- Injectable therapy evidence (HA, PRP) is more consistently favorable in early-to-moderate (K-L 2–3) disease than in end-stage (K-L 4) disease.
