## Overview

Two classification systems are used together in practice for pediatric lateral condyle fractures of the humerus: the **Milch** system, which describes fracture-line location relative to the trochlear groove and thus intrinsic elbow stability, and the **Jakob/Weiss** system, which stages displacement and is the primary driver of treatment. The lateral condyle physis has a watershed, largely intra-articular blood supply entering posteriorly, which is why this fracture is treated with a lower displacement threshold for surgery than most other physeal injuries.

## Types

| System | Grade | Definition | Implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| Milch I | Fracture line lateral to the trochlear groove (through the capitello-trochlear groove) | Salter-Harris IV equivalent; elbow remains stable (trochlea intact) | Less common; more stable pattern |
| Milch II | Fracture line extends medial to the trochlear groove, into the trochlea | Salter-Harris II equivalent functionally; elbow can be unstable because part of the trochlear articulation is on the fragment | More common (~85%); higher instability |
| Jakob/Weiss I | <2 mm displacement, articular cartilage hinge intact | Stable | Cast immobilization, weekly radiographs x 2–3 weeks to catch late displacement |
| Jakob/Weiss II | 2–4 mm displacement, or displacement seen on one view only; articular surface may be partially disrupted | Potentially unstable | Individualized — many treat as operative given propensity for interval displacement; arthrogram helpful |
| Jakob/Weiss III | >4 mm displacement, often rotated/flipped fragment, articular surface disrupted | Unstable | CRPP or ORIF with screw or K-wire fixation |

## Pearls

- The **2 mm** threshold (rather than 4 mm) is used by many pediatric centers as the operative cutoff because true (cartilaginous) displacement is routinely underestimated on plain radiographs relative to arthrographic/intraoperative findings, and because delayed/interval displacement occurs in a meaningful minority of "stable" fractures followed nonoperatively.
- The visible bony fragment underestimates the true (mostly cartilaginous) fracture size in young children — elbow arthrography or intraoperative fluoroscopic arthrogram is useful to assess the true articular step-off.
- An internal oblique radiographic view increases sensitivity for detecting the fracture and helps stage Milch/Jakob-type displacement.
- Contemporary multicenter and pooled meta-analytic data confirm comparable union and complication rates between K-wire and cannulated screw fixation — fixation-method choice should be driven by fragment size/age rather than a uniform superiority of one implant.
- Lateral condyle nonunion/delayed union can occur even in fractures initially judged "stable," from interval displacement or inherently tenuous blood supply, and can progress to a symptomatic lateral spur, cubitus valgus, and tardy ulnar nerve palsy (a delayed ulnar neuropathy occurring years after the index injury). Early recognition of interval displacement/delayed union via the weekly-film follow-up protocol allows salvage with percutaneous screw fixation before progression to established nonunion.
