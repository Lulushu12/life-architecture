## Overview

The Ellman classification grades partial-thickness rotator cuff tears by depth (grade I–III, <25% to >50% tendon thickness) and location (articular-sided A, bursal-sided B, or intratendinous C). It guides the choice between a nonoperative/PT trial, debridement, and take-down-and-repair, and flags bursal-sided tears as a distinct, more aggressive natural-history category.

## Types

| Grade | Depth | Location subtype | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | <25% tendon thickness (low grade) | Articular (A) / Bursal (B) / Intratendinous (C) | Debridement ± PT trial; rarely needs repair |
| II | 25–50% thickness (moderate) | A / B / C | PT trial first; if failing and symptomatic, debridement or repair depending on tear behavior and fiber quality |
| III | >50% thickness (high grade) | A / B / C | Generally treated as functional full-thickness — take-down and repair (in-situ repair or completion + repair), especially bursal-sided (worse prognosis than articular-sided at equivalent depth) |

## Pearls

- Bursal-sided partial tears tend to progress faster and have a worse natural history than articular-sided tears of similar depth grade — apply a lower threshold for surgical treatment when a tear is bursal-sided.
- Grade III tears are generally managed as functionally full-thickness tears (take-down and repair, or in-situ repair) rather than treated conservatively regardless of location.
- Age/activity-stratified treatment: in patients <50 y, a PT trial is still reasonable first for chronic degenerative grade I–II tears, with repair reserved for failure and high demand; in patients >65–70 y, favor nonoperative management unless the tear is high-grade or clearly progressive on serial imaging.
- Correlate the graded depth with symptom trajectory rather than treating the grade as a fixed surgical trigger — many low-grade partial tears respond to a structured physiotherapy program (scapular stabilizer strengthening, eccentric-biased cuff strengthening, posterior capsule stretching) over 6–12 weeks before surgery is considered.
