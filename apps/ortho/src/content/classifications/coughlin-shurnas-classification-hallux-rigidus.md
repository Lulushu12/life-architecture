## Overview

The Coughlin-Shurnas classification grades hallux rigidus (degenerative osteoarthritis of the 1st MTP joint) by passive dorsiflexion, radiographic findings, and — critically — whether pain occurs only at the extremes of motion or throughout the midrange arc. It is the primary tool for deciding whether a motion-preserving procedure (cheilectomy ± Moberg osteotomy) can still be offered, or whether arthrodesis/synthetic cartilage implant is required.

## Types

| Grade | Dorsiflexion (passive) | Radiographic findings | Clinical | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 40–60° (or 10–20° less than the normal side) | Normal | Stiffness only, no pain | Observation, activity modification |
| 1 | 30–40° | Dorsal osteophyte, minimal joint-space narrowing | Mild pain at extremes of dorsiflexion | NSAIDs, shoe modification/stiff sole, orthotic; cheilectomy if refractory |
| 2 | 10–30° | Dorsal/lateral/medial osteophytes, mild–moderate joint-space narrowing, flattening of metatarsal head | Moderate–severe pain, more constant, may have periarticular pain | Cheilectomy ± Moberg (dorsiflexion) proximal phalanx osteotomy |
| 3 | ≤10° | Severe joint-space narrowing, substantial osteophyte formation, possible subchondral cysts, sesamoid involvement | Pain at extremes of ROM (near-constant), **but a pain-free midrange arc** | Cheilectomy may still succeed if a pain-free midrange arc is preserved; otherwise arthrodesis or synthetic cartilage implant |
| 4 | Same as Grade 3 | Same as Grade 3 | Pain **throughout** the midrange arc of motion, not just at extremes | Cheilectomy unreliable; arthrodesis (gold standard) or synthetic cartilage implant in selected patients |

## Pearls

- The **grade 3/4 distinction — a pain-free versus pain-throughout midrange arc — is the single most important clinical discriminator** for whether a motion-preserving procedure can still be offered; this is a clinical exam finding, not a radiographic one, so grades 3 and 4 can look identical on imaging.
- Coughlin-Shurnas grade ≥3 with pain throughout midrange dorsiflexion makes cheilectomy alone unreliable — consider fusion or implant instead.
- Cheilectomy success (satisfactory pain relief, procedure durability) is reported at roughly 80–90% in appropriately selected (grade 1–2/select grade 3) patients.
- Arthrodesis achieves union rates of 90–97% with contemporary fixation and is the reference-standard, most predictable procedure for grade 3–4 (and revision) disease.
- The synthetic cartilage implant (Cartiva) is non-inferior to arthrodesis for pain/function at 2 years per the pivotal MOTION trial, but carries a meaningfully higher secondary/revision-surgery rate (commonly cited ~8–14% at 4–7 years, though series-dependent) — best reserved for grade 3 patients who prioritize retained motion over the single most durable outcome.
- Intra-articular corticosteroid injection can help confirm the 1st MTP as the pain source and gives temporary relief, but is a bridge, not a substitute for definitive treatment in higher-grade disease.
