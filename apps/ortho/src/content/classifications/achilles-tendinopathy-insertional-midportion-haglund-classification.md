## Overview

This classification divides chronic Achilles tendon disease by anatomic site and pathomechanics — mid-portion (tensile-overload) tendinopathy, insertional (compressive, fibrocartilaginous enthesis) tendinopathy, and Haglund deformity (a mechanical bony driver that often coexists with insertional disease). It is used to select the correct loading protocol and, when conservative care fails, the correct surgical approach, because the two tendinopathy sites behave differently and respond differently to the same intervention.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Key management difference |
|---|---|---|
| **Mid-portion tendinopathy** | Fusiform thickening 2-6 cm proximal to insertion, tensile-overload pathology, at the watershed hypovascular zone | Loading protocol includes full dorsiflexion range (eccentric decline-board or heavy slow resistance [HSR] through full ROM); surgical debridement ± FHL tendon transfer if >50% cross-section involved |
| **Insertional tendinopathy** | Pathology at the calcaneal insertion (fibrocartilaginous enthesis), often with retrocalcaneal bursitis | Loading modified to avoid deep dorsiflexion (compressive component) — HSR performed with heel flat on ground/floor-level, not off a step; surgical management may require detachment/reattachment |
| **Haglund deformity** | Prominent posterosuperior calcaneal tuberosity mechanically abutting/irritating the Achilles insertion and retrocalcaneal bursa ("pump bump") | Often coexists with insertional tendinopathy; surgical resection of the prominence ± insertional debridement required when bony impingement is the driver, not just tendon debridement alone |

## Pearls

- Practical distinction matters clinically: eccentric decline-board loading (the classic Alfredson protocol), effective for mid-portion disease, is relatively contraindicated/less effective and can aggravate insertional disease — insertional cases should be loaded without exceeding neutral dorsiflexion.
- Clinical differentiation on exam: mid-portion disease shows localized fusiform swelling/tenderness 2-6 cm proximal to the insertion that moves with the tendon on ankle motion (distinguishing it from paratenonitis, which is fixed); insertional disease shows tenderness directly at the calcaneal insertion ± retrocalcaneal bursitis ± a visible Haglund prominence.
- Heavy slow resistance (HSR, e.g., 3x/week, 12 weeks) is now favored over the classic Alfredson eccentric-only protocol for both sites because it achieves comparable outcomes with better tolerability/adherence, and — unlike the decline-board eccentric protocol — can be applied to insertional disease by loading without provocative dorsiflexion.
- Insertion has a separate blood supply and biomechanical environment (compression + tension) from the mid-portion, explaining why the two sites require genuinely different loading strategies rather than one protocol fitting both.
- Surgical thresholds differ by site: mid-portion surgery augments with FHL transfer when >50% of tendon cross-section requires excision; insertional surgery re-anchors with suture anchors when detachment exceeds ~50% of the footprint, and requires bony resection of the Haglund prominence when that is the mechanical driver — debridement alone is inadequate if impingement is present.
- Corticosteroid injection into or around the Achilles tendon is contraindicated regardless of site, given the associated rupture risk.
