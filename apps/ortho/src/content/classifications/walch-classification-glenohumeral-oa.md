## Overview

The Walch classification grades glenoid morphology in primary glenohumeral osteoarthritis, based on the pattern and direction of glenoid wear and humeral head centering (later extended to types C and D on 3D CT by the Bercik/Walch-group modification). It is used whenever planning shoulder arthroplasty for cuff-intact glenohumeral OA: it predicts the direction and magnitude of glenoid version correction needed and stratifies the choice between simple reaming, augmented components, bone grafting (BIO-RSA), and anatomic (aTSA) versus reverse (rTSA) arthroplasty.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A1** | Minor central erosion, concentric | Standard aTSA, no augment |
| **A2** | Major central erosion, concentric, head centered | aTSA; consider eccentric reaming or graft if deep central defect |
| **B1** | Posterior joint-space narrowing, subchondral sclerosis, no frank erosion | aTSA feasible; correct version by selective anterior/posterior reaming |
| **B2** | Posterior glenoid erosion with biconcavity ("B-spot"), posterior humeral subluxation | Reaming (if <15° correctable) + standard aTSA, posterior augmented component, or rTSA if severe |
| **B3** | Monoconcave posterior erosion, >15° retroversion or >70% posterior humeral head subluxation | Posterior augmented aTSA or rTSA (favored with subscapularis deficiency/older age) |
| **C** | Dysplastic glenoid, retroversion >25° regardless of wear (developmental, not erosive) | Careful version correction — often younger patients, avoid overcorrection; aTSA feasible if bone stock allows |
| **D** | Glenoid anteversion or anterior erosion | Anteverted reaming pattern; often prior instability-surgery shoulders |

## Pearls

- Posterior glenoid wear from posterior humeral head subluxation is the dominant pattern in primary glenohumeral OA — the entire B-type spectrum reflects this mechanism.
- B2/B3 and C glenoids carry the highest rates of aTSA glenoid-component loosening when version is not adequately addressed at index surgery.
- CT with 3D reconstruction and glenoid vault modeling is essential preoperatively for all arthroplasty candidates, and particularly for B2/B3 glenoids or revision cases, for accurate version/inclination measurement and implant sizing.
- Correct version conservatively: overcorrection into anteversion increases anterior instability risk, and eccentric reaming beyond ~15° risks exposing weak subchondral/cancellous bone and jeopardizing baseplate fixation.
- Recent meta-analyses (2024–2025) show comparable ROM/ASES/Constant/VAS outcomes between aTSA and rTSA for B2/B3 glenoids with an intact cuff — so for these types, the aTSA-vs-rTSA choice should be driven by bone stock, correctability of version, subscapularis quality, and patient age/activity rather than expected superiority of one implant.
- Type C is developmental, not erosive — avoid overcorrecting these typically younger patients toward "normal" version if it sacrifices bone stock.
- Type D shoulders are frequently seen after prior instability surgery and require an anteverted reaming pattern, the mirror image of the more common posterior-wear types.
