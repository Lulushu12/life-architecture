## Overview

The Cobb angle is the continuous radiographic measurement used to define and grade scoliosis severity on a full-length standing PA radiograph (low-dose biplanar/EOS imaging preferred to limit cumulative radiation in patients needing serial follow-up). It anchors every management decision in adolescent idiopathic scoliosis (AIS), from observation through bracing to surgical fusion.

## Types

| Cobb angle | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **<10°** | Not scoliosis by definition (spinal asymmetry) | No follow-up needed |
| **10–24°** | Mild curve | Observation; PT-based scoliosis-specific exercise (Schroth/SEAS); repeat radiograph every 4–6 months during growth |
| **25–40°** | Moderate curve, with significant growth remaining (Risser 0–2) | Bracing candidate per BrAIST criteria |
| **>45–50°** | Severe curve, at/near skeletal maturity (thoracic) | Surgical fusion threshold; lumbar curves are often treated at a lower threshold (~40°) given higher documented adult progression risk |
| **>90–100°** | Very severe curve (thoracic) | Risk of restrictive pulmonary physiology; consider pulmonary function testing |
| **Progression >5°/6 months** | Rate-based criterion, independent of absolute magnitude | Significant progression — reassess treatment plan regardless of current Cobb value |

## Pearls

- Progression risk tracks closely with remaining growth (peak height velocity), not chronologic age — Cobb magnitude must always be interpreted alongside skeletal maturity (Risser/Sanders staging).
- **BrAIST** established the evidence base for the 25–40° bracing window: treatment success (curve <50° at skeletal maturity) occurred in 72% of braced vs. 48% of observed patients, with a clear dose-response effect (>12.9h/day wear associated with 90%+ success).
- A 2025 extension of the BrAIST wear-time analysis found the dose-response benefit continues above 18h/day wear, suggesting benefit may not plateau at the level originally described.
- Where EOS low-dose biplanar imaging is unavailable, minimize cumulative radiation by spacing conventional radiographs to the clinically necessary interval and using scoliometer screening to triage who needs a film at all.
- Atypical curve features (left thoracic curve, excessive kyphosis, rapid progression, age <10, significant pain) should prompt whole-spine MRI regardless of Cobb magnitude, to rule out an underlying neuraxial cause before labeling a curve idiopathic.
