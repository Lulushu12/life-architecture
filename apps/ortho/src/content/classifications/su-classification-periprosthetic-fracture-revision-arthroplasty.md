## Overview

The Su classification refines Lewis-Rorabeck type II periprosthetic fractures around a TKA by locating the fracture relative to the femoral component's intercondylar box. It is used specifically to determine whether retrograde intramedullary nailing is mechanically feasible, or whether locked plating must be used instead.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Proximal to femoral component (box) | Standard locked plating or retrograde nail (if open-box design permits) |
| II | At the level of the component, extends to box | Retrograde nailing usually precluded by closed-box design; locked plating preferred |
| III | Distal to component, within/anterior to the box | Very limited distal fixation zone; locked plating with distal fixed-angle device, consider revision if purchase inadequate |

## Pearls

- Su staging determines whether retrograde intramedullary nailing is mechanically feasible: open-box posterior-stabilized/cruciate-retaining designs with adequate box clearance permit passage (Su I, occasionally II), while closed-box designs or a fracture line through the box preclude it, steering the surgeon toward locked plating in most Su II/III patterns.
- Always confirm the specific implant's box geometry from operative records or manufacturer specifications before committing to a nailing strategy — assuming compatibility based on component appearance on plain film is an avoidable planning error.
