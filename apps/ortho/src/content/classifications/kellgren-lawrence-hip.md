## Overview

The Kellgren-Lawrence (KL) grade describes radiographic osteoarthritis severity on a 0–4 scale based on osteophyte formation, joint space narrowing, sclerosis, and bone contour deformity. It was designed for the knee but is widely applied to the hip in general OA literature and clinic shorthand. It correlates only moderately with symptoms, so it is used alongside — not instead of — hip-specific grading (Tönnis) and the patient's pain/function when deciding on surgical candidacy.

## Types

| Grade | Radiographic findings | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No radiographic features of OA | No OA-directed treatment; investigate other pain source |
| **1** | Doubtful joint space narrowing, possible osteophytic lipping | Reassurance, activity/lifestyle counseling; not a surgical candidate on imaging alone |
| **2** | Definite osteophytes, possible joint space narrowing | Begin structured non-operative ladder |
| **3** | Multiple osteophytes, definite joint space narrowing, some sclerosis, possible bone contour deformity | Non-op ladder continues; surgical candidacy assessed on symptoms/function, not radiographs alone |
| **4** | Large osteophytes, marked joint space narrowing, severe sclerosis, definite bone contour deformity | Strong radiographic support for THA if symptoms/function refractory to non-op care |

## Pearls

- KL was designed for the knee originally but is widely applied to the hip in general OA literature; it correlates only moderately with symptoms, so treatment decisions are driven by pain and function, not KL grade alone (per AAOS and NICE guidance).
- Grade ≥3 with concordant symptoms defines the surgical candidate pool; grade 0–2 belongs on the non-operative ladder, and a low grade with significant symptoms should prompt reconsideration of the pain source (spine, greater trochanteric pain syndrome, referred pain) rather than escalation to surgery.
- In everyday decision-making, KL and Tönnis are layered together — used first to confirm that the hip joint itself (not the spine or soft tissue) is the pain generator and that cartilage loss is advanced enough that joint preservation is no longer realistic — rather than either grade being used in isolation.
- Indication for THA is radiographically confirmed hip joint destruction (generally KL ≥3, Tönnis ≥2) concordant with clinically significant pain and functional limitation that has failed an adequate non-operative trial; THA is not indicated on imaging alone.
