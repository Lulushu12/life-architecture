## Overview

The ASBMR (American Society for Bone and Mineral Research) criteria define atypical femoral fracture — a stress/insufficiency fracture on the tension (lateral) side of a femur whose remodeling has been chronically suppressed by long-term antiresorptive therapy — and distinguish it from the typical spiral/oblique osteoporotic femoral neck or intertrochanteric fracture pattern. Location must be the femoral diaphysis (just distal to the lesser trochanter to just proximal to the supracondylar flare), and the fracture must be atraumatic or associated with minimal trauma (fall from standing height or less) and non-pathologic (tumor, Paget disease, and other focal bone lesions excluded).

## Types

| Feature | Category | Diagnostic role |
|---|---|---|
| Fracture line originates at the lateral cortex and is essentially transverse in orientation | Major (≥4 of 5 required) | Required for diagnosis |
| Minimal or no comminution | Major (≥4 of 5 required) | Required for diagnosis |
| Complete fractures extend through both cortices (may have a medial spike); incomplete fractures involve only the lateral cortex | Major (≥4 of 5 required) | Required for diagnosis |
| Fracture is non-comminuted or minimally comminuted | Major (≥4 of 5 required) | Required for diagnosis |
| Localized periosteal or endosteal thickening at the fracture site ("beaking"/"flaring") | Major (≥4 of 5 required) | Required for diagnosis |
| Generalized increase in cortical thickness of the femoral diaphyses | Minor (supportive) | Supportive, not required |
| Unilateral or bilateral prodromal dull, aching thigh/groin pain | Minor (supportive) | Supportive, not required |
| Bilateral incomplete or complete femoral diaphysis fractures | Minor (supportive) | Supportive, not required |
| Delayed fracture healing | Minor (supportive) | Supportive, not required |

## Pearls

- Meeting criteria mandates stopping antiresorptive therapy, imaging the contralateral femur, and considering prophylactic fixation of a symptomatic incomplete lesion before it completes.
- Prodromal thigh/groin ache in a patient on ≥3–5 years of bisphosphonate or ≥1 year of denosumab is a red flag — image the *contralateral* full femur, since roughly 40% are bilateral or have a contralateral incomplete fracture at presentation.
- MRI detects early marrow and periosteal edema at the lateral cortex before a radiographically visible lucent line develops, and is the investigation of choice for a patient with prodromal thigh pain on long-term antiresorptive therapy and normal plain films.
- AFF is biomechanically distinct from typical osteoporotic femoral fractures: unrepaired transverse microcracks propagate from the lateral cortex due to suppressed remodeling, rather than the spiral/oblique pattern of a typical fragility fracture.
