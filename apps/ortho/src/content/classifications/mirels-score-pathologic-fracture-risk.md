## Overview

The Mirels score quantifies the risk of impending pathologic fracture through a metastatic long-bone lesion using four variables — site, pain, lesion type, and size — each scored 1–3 for a total of 4–12. It is used to decide whether a metastatic long-bone lesion should be treated with prophylactic fixation before it fractures, since fixing a completed pathologic fracture carries substantially worse morbidity, delayed union, and technical difficulty than elective prophylactic stabilization.

## Types

| Variable | 1 point | 2 points | 3 points |
|---|---|---|---|
| Site | Upper extremity | Lower extremity | Peritrochanteric |
| Pain | Mild | Moderate | Functional/mechanical (pain with weight-bearing) |
| Lesion type | Blastic | Mixed | Lytic |
| Size (% of cortical diameter destroyed) | <1/3 | 1/3–2/3 | >2/3 |

| Total score | Interpretation | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| ≤7 | Low fracture risk | Non-operative management appropriate initially (radiation, systemic therapy, bone-modifying agent), with scheduled re-imaging/reassessment as the lesion may progress |
| 7 | Gray zone | Clinical judgment — lean toward fixation for peritrochanteric lesions, functional pain, or lesions expected to progress before radiation takes effect |
| ≥8 | High fracture risk | Prophylactic fixation recommended (fracture risk substantially elevated, best treated electively) |

## Pearls

- Mirels is a high-sensitivity, low-specificity tool by design — a 2025 review confirms this known limitation and reports that a score <7 corresponds to ~4% fracture risk, while a score ≥9 corresponds to ~33% risk with no false positives, sharpening the historical ≥8 cutoff toward ≥9 as the threshold most reliably warranting fixation in the lower limb.
- The standard Mirels cutoff under-predicts fracture risk in the upper limb; a validation study proposed a lower threshold of ≥7 (rather than ≥8) for considering prophylactic fixation of upper-extremity metastatic lesions.
- Prophylactic intramedullary nailing for diaphyseal lesions should span the entire bone (not just the lesion) to protect against occult or future skeletal metastases along the same segment — a short segmental nail that leaves unprotected bone is a recognized cause of subsequent fracture at the nail end.
- Endoprosthetic replacement is favored over fixation for extensively destroyed peri-articular bone (especially proximal femur/femoral neck) where fixation constructs would have insufficient purchase or unacceptable failure risk.
- Post-fixation radiation (typically initiated ~2–3 weeks after surgery once wound healing allows) reduces local tumor progression and construct failure — Mirels informs the decision to fix, not whether radiation is still needed afterward.
- Acetabular metastatic disease is not captured by Mirels and is instead assessed by the Harrington classification to guide reconstruction complexity.
