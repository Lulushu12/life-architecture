## Overview

The Mayo classification grades olecranon fractures by displacement, comminution, and ulnohumeral stability, and directly determines whether tension-band wiring or plate fixation is the appropriate construct. It applies to fractures of the olecranon/proximal ulna, which are frequently subcutaneous and open given their superficial location.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Non-displaced (<2mm), stable | Non-operative: brief splint then hinged brace, early motion |
| **IA** | Non-displaced, non-comminuted | Cast/splint 1–2 weeks |
| **IB** | Non-displaced, comminuted | Cast/splint, monitor for displacement |
| **II** | Displaced (>2mm or extensor mechanism disrupted), stable ulnohumeral joint | Surgical fixation required to restore extensor mechanism |
| **IIA** | Displaced, non-comminuted | Tension-band wiring (transverse fracture at the level of the trochlear notch, low-demand patient) |
| **IIB** | Displaced, comminuted | Plate fixation preferred (comminution makes TBW constructs fail); pre-contoured locking olecranon plate |
| **III** | Displaced, unstable (associated with ulnohumeral instability, e.g., fracture-dislocation, Monteggia-variant "transolecranon" injury) | Plate fixation with restoration of the coronoid/trochlear notch; may require addressing associated radial head/coronoid injury as in a Monteggia-equivalent |

## Pearls

- Displacement threshold for surgery is **>2 mm** articular step-off or any extensor mechanism disruption — a positive resisted-extension lag on a minimally displaced fracture still requires fixation because the fracture is functionally unstable regardless of the millimeters measured on static imaging.
- Tension-band wiring is biomechanically a dynamic compression construct that only works with an intact anterior cortical hinge under axial (extensor) load — it fails in oblique, comminuted, or distally-extending fracture lines, where plating (or an intramedullary screw ± suture augmentation for simple transverse patterns) is preferred.
- Reported hardware-irritation/removal rates after TBW are high (roughly 9–80% across series) — counsel patients that a second surgery for wire removal is common.
- Type III (transolecranon) fracture-dislocations should be treated by reducing and fixing the olecranon anatomically first to restore the trochlear notch, which reduces the ulnohumeral joint; any associated coronoid or radial head injury is then addressed as in terrible triad.
