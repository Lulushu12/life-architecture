## Overview

The Puranen-Orava classification is a legacy clinical severity grading system for hamstring strains, based on exam findings and functional loss (mild/moderate/severe) rather than imaging. It predates MRI-based grading and is used as a bedside clinical correlate alongside — and largely supplemented by — the BAMIC MRI classification in current practice.

## Types

| Grade | Clinical description | Correlate |
|---|---|---|
| **I (mild)** | Localized pain, no/minimal loss of strength, RTP within days | Approx. BAMIC 0-1 |
| **II (moderate/partial)** | Partial tear, restricted movement, palpable defect possible, RTP weeks | Approx. BAMIC 2-3 |
| **III (severe/complete)** | Complete rupture, marked functional loss, palpable gap, significant hematoma | Approx. BAMIC 4 / complete avulsion spectrum |

## Pearls

- This is a purely clinical severity scale (exam and functional loss) — it does not capture anatomic site (myofascial vs musculotendinous junction vs intramuscular tendon), which is the single strongest driver of return-to-play time and re-injury risk.
- In current practice it is used alongside — and has largely been supplemented by — BAMIC MRI grading, which adds anatomic site and quantitative cross-sectional area, both stronger predictors of RTP time than clinical grade alone.
- Best used as an initial bedside triage tool (e.g., to decide urgency of imaging or immediate management) rather than as the definitive basis for RTP planning once MRI is available.
