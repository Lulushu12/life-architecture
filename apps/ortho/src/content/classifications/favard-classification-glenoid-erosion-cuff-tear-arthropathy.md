## Overview

The Favard classification (E0–E4) describes the superior and central glenoid wear patterns seen specifically in chronic rotator cuff insufficiency, distinct from the Walch system used for cuff-intact osteoarthritis. It is used when planning reverse total shoulder arthroplasty (rTSA) for cuff-tear arthropathy, directly guiding baseplate augmentation strategy and tilt to minimize scapular notching.

## Types

| Type/Grade | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **E0** | Concentric, no glenoid wear | Standard baseplate positioning |
| **E1** | Concentric wear (centered erosion) | Central deepening reaming; standard baseplate |
| **E2** | Superior glenoid erosion | Inferior tilt of baseplate to avoid notching; consider inferior augment |
| **E3** | Severe superior erosion, compromised peg/screw purchase | Superior wedge augment or bone graft; longer central peg for fixation |
| **E4** | Global/biconcave pattern (posterior + superior deficiency) | Combined augmentation strategy; assess both planes of deficiency |

## Pearls

- Favard is not interchangeable with Walch — Walch describes glenoid wear in cuff-intact primary OA, while Favard describes the superior-dominant erosion pattern that develops from chronic cuff insufficiency.
- The classification maps directly onto baseplate technical decisions: superior erosion (E2/E3) drives inferior baseplate tilt and superior augmentation, while combined posterior-superior deficiency (E4) requires assessing and correcting both planes.
- E3/E4 patterns compromise peg/screw purchase, so plan for a longer central peg or combined augmentation to secure fixation rather than relying on standard baseplate geometry.
