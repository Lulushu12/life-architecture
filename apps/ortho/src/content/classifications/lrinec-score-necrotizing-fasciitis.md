## Overview

The LRINEC (Laboratory Risk Indicator for Necrotizing Fasciitis) score uses six routine lab values to risk-stratify a soft-tissue infection for necrotizing fasciitis. It was derived retrospectively and was **never intended as a stand-alone rule-out screen** — necrotizing fasciitis remains primarily a clinical diagnosis, confirmed only in the operating room.

## Types

### Score components

| Variable | Value | Points |
|---|---|---|
| CRP (mg/L) | ≥150 | 4 |
| WBC (×10⁹/L) | <15 / 15–25 / >25 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Hemoglobin (g/dL) | >13.5 / 11–13.5 / <11 | 0 / 1 / 2 |
| Sodium (mmol/L) | <135 | 2 |
| Creatinine (µmol/L) | >141 | 2 |
| Glucose (mmol/L) | >10 | 1 |

### Risk stratification (total score 0–13)

| Score | Risk category | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| ≥8 | High risk (originally reported PPV ~93%) | Supports urgent surgical exploration but is not required to proceed if clinical suspicion is high |
| 6–7 | Intermediate risk | Continue workup; does not itself justify delaying exploration if clinically suspicious |
| <6 | Low risk | **Does not rule out** necrotizing fasciitis — must not override clinical suspicion or delay surgical exploration |

## Pearls

- Sensitivity in external validation cohorts is only ~70–80%; up to 10–20% of surgically confirmed necrotizing fasciitis cases present with LRINEC <6.
- The score supports triage, not exclusion — a low LRINEC must never override clinical suspicion or delay a trip to the OR.
- Definitive confirmation is surgical: lack of resistance to blunt finger dissection along the fascial plane (the "finger test"), dishwater-grey exudate, thrombosed subcutaneous vessels, and lack of bleeding from tissue at exploration.
- Time-to-debridement, not the LRINEC value, is the dominant modifiable predictor of mortality — door-to-OR <12h is associated with markedly lower mortality; overall mortality is ~20–30%, rising toward ~70% with delay beyond 24h.
