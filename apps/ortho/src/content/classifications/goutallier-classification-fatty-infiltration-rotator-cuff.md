## Overview

The Goutallier classification (as modified by Fuchs) grades fatty infiltration of the rotator cuff musculature on sagittal oblique MRI or CT, from normal muscle (stage 0) to muscle replaced predominantly by fat (stage 4). It is used to assess muscle quality — as distinct from tendon retraction or tear size — and is the single strongest predictor of poor outcome after attempted repair and of re-tear risk, because fatty infiltration is largely irreversible once advanced.

## Types

| Stage | Finding | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | Normal muscle, no fat | Excellent healing potential |
| 1 | Some fatty streaks | Good healing potential; proceed with repair |
| 2 | Fat < muscle (less fat than muscle) | Repair reasonable but retear risk rises; consider augmentation |
| 3 | Fat = muscle (equal amounts) | Threshold for irreparability in many algorithms; repair often still attempted in supraspinatus if infraspinatus is better preserved, but retear risk high |
| 4 | Fat > muscle | Considered irreparable/non-functional muscle; favor salvage (superior capsular reconstruction, tendon transfer, partial repair, reverse total shoulder arthroplasty if arthritic) over primary repair |

## Pearls

- Goutallier ≥3 in **two or more** cuff muscles is a strong predictor of poor outcome after attempted repair and is a key criterion steering toward massive-tear salvage strategies rather than primary repair.
- The **tangent sign** (supraspinatus muscle belly falling below the line drawn from the coracoid tip to the superior scapular spine on sagittal MRI) is a useful bedside-adjacent correlate: a positive tangent sign predicts poor repairability/high retear risk and correlates with Goutallier ≥3.
- Fatty infiltration is largely irreversible once advanced (stage ≥3) — unlike retraction, which can sometimes be improved with mobilization techniques (interval slides), advanced fatty infiltration does not reverse with successful tendon healing, which is why it carries more weight than tear size alone in the reparability decision.
- Interpret Goutallier alongside Patte (retraction) and Cofield (size): a small tear with high Goutallier stage in an older patient may still be a poor repair candidate, while a massive tear with low Goutallier stage may still be reparable.
