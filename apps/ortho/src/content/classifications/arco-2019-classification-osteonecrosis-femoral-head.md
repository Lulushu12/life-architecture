## Overview

The ARCO (Association Research Circulation Osseous) system stages osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) from radiographically silent disease through end-stage secondary osteoarthritis. The 2019 revision (Yoon et al., 4-round international Delphi consensus, 29 experts) simplified the historical 1993 six-stage system by removing quantitative lesion-size substaging in favor of a more reproducible qualitative framework, and split collapse into early (≤2 mm) versus late (>2 mm) based on a 2 mm depression threshold. It is the primary staging system used to decide between joint-preservation surgery and total hip arthroplasty (THA).

## Types

| Stage | Definition | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Normal or nonspecific radiograph/MRI; histology only (rarely used clinically) | Observation, risk-factor modification |
| **I** | Normal radiograph; **MRI or bone scan positive** (double-line sign); subclassified Ia/Ib/Ic by extent (medial/central/lateral on coronal MRI, correlating with Kerboul angle) | Precollapse — candidate for core decompression ± biologics if symptomatic; extent (lateral involvement) predicts higher collapse risk |
| **II** | Radiographic abnormality (sclerosis, cysts, osteopenia) **without** collapse or subchondral fracture; subclassified IIa/IIb/IIc by extent as above | Precollapse — still within the joint-preservation window; core decompression most effective here |
| **IIIA** | **Subchondral fracture (crescent sign) or early collapse ≤2 mm** | Transition zone — joint preservation possible in small/medial lesions but success rates fall sharply; vascularized bone grafting considered in young patients |
| **IIIB** | **Collapse >2 mm** | Joint preservation largely unsuccessful except highly selected small lesions/rotational osteotomy candidates; THA increasingly favored |
| **IV** | Osteoarthritic changes: joint-space narrowing, acetabular involvement, femoral head flattening/destruction | THA is the standard of care |

## Pearls

- The 2 mm collapse threshold (IIIA vs IIIB) is the single most important branch point in the whole treatment algorithm — it separates a joint-preservation window from a rapidly narrowing one.
- Removing quantitative lesion-size substaging from the 1993 system improved reproducibility: a 2023 interobserver reliability study of the revised classification reported a kappa of 0.652 and 90.25% agreement, confirming it is reproducible enough for routine clinical decision-making.
- Extent still matters even though it was demoted from a formal substage number — Ia/Ib/Ic and IIa/IIb/IIc lateral involvement (correlating with a larger Kerboul/combined necrotic angle) predicts higher collapse risk and should factor into the joint-preservation-versus-surveillance discussion even within stage I–II disease.
- Plain radiographs are normal in early ARCO 0–I disease — MRI (double-line sign on T2) is the gold standard and should be obtained within about 2 weeks of symptom onset in an at-risk patient rather than waiting for radiographic change.
