## Overview

The Anderson–D'Alonzo classification describes odontoid (dens) fractures by the level of the fracture line, which correlates directly with blood supply, nonunion risk, and the fixation vs. immobilization decision. It is the primary classification applied to any fracture through the odontoid process of C2.

## Types

| Type | Description | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Avulsion fracture of the tip of the odontoid (alar ligament avulsion) | Rare; usually stable — collar; must exclude occipitocervical dissociation |
| **II** | Fracture through the base/waist of the odontoid, above the C2 body | Most common and least biologically favorable (watershed blood supply); highest nonunion risk (up to 30–60% nonoperative in patients with adverse risk factors); operative bias if displacement >5mm, angulation >10°, comminution, posterior displacement, age >50, or delayed presentation |
| **IIA** | Type II with comminution at the fracture base (unstable subtype) | Poor candidate for halo/traction — high risk of distraction nonunion; favor surgical fixation |
| **III** | Fracture extends into the cancellous body of C2/superior articulating facets | Best healing potential (cancellous bone contact) — collar or halo often successful; surgery reserved for displacement/instability |

## Pearls

- The odontoid's blood supply arises from an anastomotic ring off the vertebral artery entering at the base, creating a watershed zone at the waist — this is the anatomic reason Type II fractures have the highest nonunion rate of the three types.
- Type IIA specifically should not be treated with axial traction; the disrupted, comminuted base risks catastrophic distraction, so extension/compression in a halo or primary surgical fixation is preferred.
- Elderly/frail patients tolerate a fibrous nonunion of a displaced Type II fracture reasonably well in many cases (often painless, functionally acceptable), while halo vest immobilization carries a substantially elevated mortality risk in patients over roughly 65–70 (aspiration pneumonia) — this makes the operative-vs-nonoperative decision in the elderly a shared decision incorporating frailty and goals rather than a type-based algorithm alone.
- A 2023 meta-analysis (20 comparative studies, n=8,296) found significantly lower nonunion rates with surgical fixation versus nonoperative management in patients over 65, reinforcing an operative bias in fit elderly patients, but nonoperative management remains reasonable for frail patients given comparable functional/mortality outcomes in several cohorts.
- A 2025 EANS multinational survey found persistent practice variation: roughly 79% of respondents favor conservative treatment for non-displaced fractures in patients 70–80y (84% for 80–90y) — current European specialist practice remains individualized rather than protocol-driven.
