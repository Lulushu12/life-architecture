## Overview

The Watson classification stages the predictable radial-to-ulnar progression of wrist arthritis that follows loss of normal scaphoid kinematics — scapholunate advanced collapse (SLAC) from SL ligament incompetence, or the radiographically/clinically identical scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse (SNAC) from chronic nonunion with humpback deformity. It is the primary framework for staging the salvage surgical ladder in end-stage carpal collapse.

## Types

| Stage | Arthritic pattern | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Degeneration limited to radial styloid / distal scaphoid articulation | Radial styloidectomy ± SL reconstruction if the SL joint itself is reconstructable |
| **II** | Progression to the entire radioscaphoid joint (scaphoid fossa) | Scaphoidectomy + four-corner fusion, or PRC if capitate head/lunate fossa cartilage preserved |
| **III** | Capitolunate (midcarpal) joint involved; radiolunate joint typically spared | PRC contraindicated (capitate arthritis) — favor scaphoidectomy + 4-corner fusion |
| **(IV — extended)** | Pancarpal involvement including radiolunate joint (uncommon; seen with inflammatory arthritis or neglected disease) | Total wrist fusion or total wrist arthroplasty; salvage denervation as adjunct |

**SNAC (scaphoid nonunion advanced collapse)** follows the same three-stage radial-to-ulnar progression but is driven by chronic scaphoid nonunion with humpback deformity rather than SL ligament failure; staging and the salvage ladder are managed identically to SLAC.

## Pearls

- The radiolunate joint is characteristically spared until very late (Stage IV) in both SLAC and SNAC, because the lunate's articulation with the radius does not directly depend on scaphoid position — this is what makes limited procedures (styloidectomy, 4-corner fusion, PRC) viable rather than requiring total wrist fusion at earlier stages.
- The Stage II decision between scaphoidectomy + four-corner fusion (4CF) and proximal row carpectomy (PRC) hinges on capitate head/lunate fossa cartilage status (PRC requires healthy cartilage there), patient age/demand (PRC favored in lower-demand/older patients; less reliably durable under high load), and surgeon preference.
- Recent meta-analyses (2024) trend toward PRC showing comparable-or-superior motion and equivalent-to-superior functional outcomes versus 4CF in appropriately selected Stage II wrists, while 4CF retains the advantage where midcarpal cartilage is already compromised.
- Once Stage III (capitolunate/midcarpal) arthritis develops, PRC is contraindicated — proceed to scaphoidectomy + 4-corner fusion rather than attempting joint-preserving reconstruction.
- Failed PRC can leave persistent radiocapitate/radiolunate arthritis, and failed 4CF can result in nonunion or adjacent-joint arthritis — either may require conversion to total wrist fusion.
