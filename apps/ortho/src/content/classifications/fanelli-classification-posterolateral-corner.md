## Overview

The Fanelli classification grades posterolateral corner (PLC) injury functionally/anatomically, based on the degree of increased external rotation (dial test) and varus opening on exam. It is used to determine which PLC structures are involved and to guide the choice between isolated structure repair and comprehensive anatomic reconstruction.

## Types

| Type | Findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Isolated popliteofibular ligament/popliteus laxity — increased external rotation (dial test) only, no varus opening | Usually amenable to non-operative trial if isolated; if surgery needed, popliteofibular ligament reconstruction/repair alone |
| **B** | Popliteofibular + LCL laxity — increased external rotation + mild-to-moderate (5–10 mm) varus opening at 30° | Combined popliteus/popliteofibular + LCL reconstruction typically needed |
| **C** | Full PLC disruption — popliteofibular, LCL, and lateral capsule/arcuate ligament complex all incompetent — increased external rotation + gross (>10 mm) varus opening | Comprehensive PLC reconstruction (anatomic, multi-strand) required; highest failure risk if under-recognized and left unaddressed alongside cruciate reconstruction |

## Pearls

- Under-diagnosis is common because varus laxity can be masked by an intact ACL/PCL tensioning the knee during exam — always perform the dial test with side-to-side comparison and document findings at both 30° and 90° of flexion.
- Dial test asymmetry at 30° only = isolated PLC injury; asymmetry persisting at both 30° and 90° implicates combined PCL involvement, not PLC alone.
- Unlike the MCL, PLC structures are extra-articular but have relatively poor intrinsic healing due to a more limited vascular bed and higher inherent strain at injury — the threshold to move to surgery is much lower than for the MCL, and non-operative management of higher-grade or combined PLC injury risks chronic instability.
- Chronic PLC insufficiency is a well-recognized cause of ACL/PCL graft failure if unaddressed at the time of cruciate reconstruction ("if you fix the cruciate and ignore the corner, the graft will stretch out or fail") — leaving an unaddressed PLC injury is the single most common cause of early failure of cruciate reconstructions performed in the multiligament knee injury setting.
- Non-operative management is reasonable only for low-grade (Type A, isolated, partial) injuries without instability — brace in extension/slight flexion, avoid varus stress and pivoting activity.
