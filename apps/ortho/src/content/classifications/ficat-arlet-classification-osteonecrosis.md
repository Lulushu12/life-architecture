## Overview

The Ficat-Arlet system is the classic, historically dominant staging scale for osteonecrosis of the femoral head (ONFH) and remains widely used clinically alongside the more recent ARCO 2019 system. It stages disease from asymptomatic/radiographically silent through overt secondary osteoarthritis, and its stage boundaries map closely onto the ARCO stages used elsewhere in this monograph.

## Types

| Stage | Findings | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Asymptomatic, normal imaging, MRI may already be positive | Surveillance in at-risk patients |
| **I** | Symptomatic, normal or near-normal radiograph, positive MRI/bone scan | Core decompression candidate |
| **II** | Sclerosis/cysts on radiograph, femoral head sphericity preserved (IIA no crescent, IIB crescent sign present) | Core decompression ± biologics best results; IIB approaching transition to collapse |
| **III** | Subchondral collapse, "crescent sign," flattening, sequestrum, head remains congruent with acetabulum | Head-preserving osteotomy or vascularized graft in select young patients; otherwise THA |
| **IV** | Joint-space narrowing, acetabular cartilage loss, osteoarthritic changes | THA |

## Pearls

- Ficat-Arlet stages 0–II correspond to the precollapse ARCO 0–IIC window and stages III–IV correspond to postcollapse ARCO IIIA–IV; use whichever system a given reference is using, but keep the collapse boundary as the anchor point in either.
- The IIA/IIB substage split (crescent sign absent vs present) matters clinically — IIB indicates the head is approaching the transition to frank collapse and narrows the joint-preservation window even though the head is still spherical.
- Stage 0 is a surveillance category, not a treatment-triggering stage — it exists to flag at-risk patients (e.g., contralateral hip in known bilateral disease) for imaging follow-up rather than intervention.
