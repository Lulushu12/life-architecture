## Overview

The Herring lateral pillar classification grades Perthes disease by the height of the lateral third of the femoral epiphysis on an AP radiograph, assessed during the early fragmentation stage. It is the primary prognostic and treatment-selection tool used in current practice, and — combined with age at onset — determines which hips are offered containment surgery versus symptomatic management.

## Types

| Group | Lateral pillar height | Prognosis / management implication |
|---|---|---|
| A | No loss of height, full lateral pillar integrity | Excellent prognosis regardless of age or treatment — observation, no containment surgery needed |
| B | >50% of original lateral pillar height maintained | Good prognosis if age <8y at onset; containment surgery favored if age ≥8y or if "head-at-risk" features present (Gage sign, lateral calcification, lateral subluxation, horizontal physis, metaphyseal cysts) |
| B/C border (<20% height loss but poorly ossified/narrow central pillar) | Intermediate | Treated as B or C depending on additional risk features and surgeon judgment — a distinct borderline group with intermediate outcomes |
| C | <50% of original lateral pillar height maintained | Worst prognosis — containment surgery generally offered, especially age ≥8y, though benefit is debated even with surgery in the most severe C hips |

## Pearls

- Age at onset modifies group B management substantially: lateral pillar B in a child <8 years does well without surgery, while lateral pillar B (or the B/C border) in a child ≥8 years is the strongest, most consistent indication for containment surgery in the natural-history literature.
- Lateral pillar A hips are never offered containment surgery regardless of age — they do well with observation alone.
- Catterall "head-at-risk" signs (Gage sign, lateral calcification, lateral subluxation, horizontal physis, metaphyseal cysts) are used alongside lateral pillar grade, not as a stand-alone classification, to refine the surgical decision in borderline group B hips.
- Even with surgery, benefit in lateral pillar C hips is genuinely debated in the literature — many surgeons still offer containment in younger C patients, but outcomes remain the worst of the four groups.
- Grading requires assessment during the early fragmentation stage; applying it too early (initial/necrosis stage) or too late can misclassify the hip.
