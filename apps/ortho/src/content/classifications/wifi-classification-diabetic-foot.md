## Overview

The Society for Vascular Surgery **WIfI** (**W**ound, **I**schemia, foot **I**nfection) classification grades each of the three domains 0–3 and combines them into a clinical stage that predicts 1-year amputation risk and the expected benefit of revascularization in the threatened diabetic/ischemic limb.

## Types

| Component | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wound (W) | No ulcer/gangrene | Small, shallow ulcer, no gangrene | Deeper ulcer exposing bone/joint/tendon, or limited forefoot gangrene | Extensive, full-thickness ulcer or extensive gangrene |
| Ischemia (I) | ABI ≥0.8, toe pressure ≥60 mmHg | ABI 0.6–0.79, toe pressure 40–59 mmHg | ABI 0.4–0.59, toe pressure 30–39 mmHg | ABI <0.4, toe pressure <30 mmHg |
| Foot Infection (fI) | None | Mild (per IDSA/IWGDF grading) | Moderate | Severe |

The combined clinical stage (1 = very low risk, through 4 = high risk of major amputation at 1 year) determines urgency of revascularization and the aggressiveness of local wound management.

## Pearls

- A high W/fI grade with low ischemia can often be managed with debridement/offloading alone.
- A high ischemia (I) grade — regardless of wound size — mandates a vascular work-up and likely revascularization before or concurrent with any debridement, since wound-healing potential is perfusion-limited above all else.
- ABI <0.9 is abnormal; ABI <0.4, absolute toe pressure <30 mmHg, or TcPO2 <30 mmHg defines the critical limb ischemia threshold that should trigger a revascularization work-up before elective debridement.
- Peripheral arterial disease is present in up to 50% of diabetic foot ulcers, so ischemia assessment (pulses, ABI/toe-brachial index) should be routine in evaluation, not reserved for grossly ischemic-appearing feet.
- The "toe and flow" model — aggressive local wound/bone management paired with aggressive perfusion optimization — is associated with reduced major amputation rates in cohort data, reinforcing that WIfI staging should drive a coordinated vascular + surgical plan rather than sequential, siloed care.
