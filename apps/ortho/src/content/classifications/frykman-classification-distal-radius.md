## Overview

The Frykman classification categorizes distal radius fractures by whether the radiocarpal and/or DRUJ articular surfaces are involved and whether an associated ulnar styloid fracture is present. It explicitly tracks ulnar-sided/DRUJ injury burden, making it useful for describing associated injury pattern, though it is largely of historical/descriptive value today, superseded by AO/OTA and Fernandez for surgical planning.

## Types

| Type | Articular involvement | Ulnar styloid |
|---|---|---|
| I | Extra-articular | Absent |
| II | Extra-articular | Present |
| III | Radiocarpal joint involved | Absent |
| IV | Radiocarpal joint involved | Present |
| V | Distal radioulnar joint (DRUJ) involved | Absent |
| VI | DRUJ involved | Present |
| VII | Both radiocarpal and DRUJ involved | Absent |
| VIII | Both radiocarpal and DRUJ involved | Present |

## Pearls

- Increasing Frykman grade (odd→even pairs add an ulnar styloid fracture; ascending numbers add joint involvement) correlates with higher rates of DRUJ instability, worse functional outcome with nonoperative treatment, and a lower threshold for operative fixation.
- Types VII–VIII (both joints + styloid) carry the highest risk of chronic DRUJ instability and post-traumatic arthrosis and warrant careful intraoperative DRUJ stress testing regardless of the radius fixation method chosen.
- Reach for Frykman specifically when describing associated ulnar-sided/DRUJ injury burden; it remains widely referenced in outcome literature even though it is largely historical for day-to-day surgical decision-making.
