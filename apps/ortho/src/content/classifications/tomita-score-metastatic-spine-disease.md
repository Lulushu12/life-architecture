## Overview

The Tomita score is a 2–10 point prognostic and strategy scoring system for metastatic spine disease, summing three factors: grade of malignancy (primary growth rate — slow=1, moderate=2, rapid=4), visceral metastases (none=0, treatable=2, untreatable=4), and bone metastases (solitary=1, multiple=2). Unlike Tokuhashi, it maps directly onto a surgical strategy tier rather than a survival estimate alone.

## Types

| Total score | Definition (goal) | Treatment implication (strategy) |
|---|---|---|
| **2–3** | Long-term local control | Wide/marginal excision |
| **4–5** | Middle-term control | Marginal/intralesional excision |
| **6–7** | Short-term palliation | Palliative decompression/stabilization |
| **8–10** | Terminal care | Supportive care, no surgery |

## Pearls

- Tomita remains useful as a prognostic reference point and is still cited in the literature, but contemporary decision-making has largely shifted to the **NOMS framework**, which integrates neurologic, oncologic, mechanical, and systemic axes and is more directly actionable at the individual-patient level than a single survival-based cutoff.
- Because Tomita maps onto a surgical aggressiveness tier (wide excision → intralesional excision → palliative decompression → supportive care only), it pairs naturally with the resection-margin decisions used for primary spine tumor surgery, even though it was designed for metastatic disease.
- As with Tokuhashi, ambulatory status and multidisciplinary tumor board input should inform the final treatment pathway rather than the score in isolation.
