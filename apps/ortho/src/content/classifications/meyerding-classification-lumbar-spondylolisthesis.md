## Overview

The Meyerding classification grades the severity of spondylolisthesis — anterior translation of one vertebra on another — by measuring the slip as a percentage of the caudal vertebral body's AP diameter on a lateral radiograph. It applies equally to degenerative spondylolisthesis (through incompetent facets/disc, no pars defect, classically L4-L5 in women >60) and isthmic spondylolisthesis (through a pars interarticularis defect, classically L5-S1). Meyerding grade is used alongside flexion-extension radiographs to guide the decompression-alone-versus-fusion decision.

## Types

| Grade | Slip (% of caudal vertebral AP diameter) | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | 0–25% | Usually managed non-operatively unless symptomatic and refractory; low fusion-instability risk |
| II | 25–50% | Most degenerative and many isthmic slips fall here; decompression alone often adequate if degenerative and stable; isthmic slips more often benefit from fusion given the pars defect (inherent posterior element incompetence) |
| III | 50–75% | Usually warrants instrumented fusion; consider partial reduction in isthmic cases, monitor L5 nerve root during reduction |
| IV | 75–100% | High-grade — fusion (often circumferential/interbody); controversial reduction vs in-situ fusion given L5 root traction risk with aggressive reduction |
| V (spondyloptosis) | >100%, complete translation | Rare; complex reconstruction, often staged, high neurologic risk; specialist deformity referral |

## Pearls

- Meyerding is a **static** severity grade — it does not capture instability. Always pair it with flexion-extension radiographs for degenerative spondylolisthesis surgical planning (flexion-extension views detect dynamic translation of >4 mm or >10–15° angular motion that static MRI misses).
- Degenerative spondylolisthesis: decompression alone is favored when stable (no gross instability, ≤ Meyerding II, minimal listhesis progression on flexion-extension); add fusion for dynamic instability, prior laminectomy at the level, facet sagittalization, or predominant mechanical back pain.
- Isthmic spondylolisthesis: persistent radiculopathy or high-grade (Meyerding III–V)/progressive slip is the trigger for decompression plus instrumented fusion ± reduction; most low-grade (Meyerding I) isthmic slips in adolescents remain stable and are managed non-operatively long-term.
- High-grade slip reduction (III–V, particularly isthmic) carries a recognized risk of L5 nerve root traction injury; partial reduction to improve sagittal balance/fusion surface area is favored by many over aggressive full reduction, and intraoperative neuromonitoring is warranted.
- The evidence for fusion vs decompression alone at any given Meyerding grade is genuinely mixed (SLIP trial favored fusion; Försth and NORDSTEN-DS at 5 years favored decompression alone for stable cases) — grade alone should not dictate the fusion decision independent of instability findings.
