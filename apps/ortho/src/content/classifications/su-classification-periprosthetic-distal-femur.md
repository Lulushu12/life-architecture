## Overview

The Su classification stages periprosthetic distal femur fractures occurring around a total knee arthroplasty (TKA) by the fracture's relationship to the femoral component. It is used to decide between treating the fracture as a standard distal femur fracture, attempting ORIF around a retained component, or proceeding to revision with a hinged distal femoral replacement.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Type I | Fracture proximal to the femoral component | Treat as a standard distal femur fracture |
| Type II | Fracture originating at or near the proximal edge of the femoral component | Most challenging for fixation given limited distal purchase |
| Type III | Fracture at or distal to the component, with the component itself in question | Fixation strategy depends on whether the component is loose or well-fixed |

## Pearls

- Type II/III patterns with a loose or worn femoral component, and Type II/III fractures in a low-demand elderly patient with poor bone stock, are the group in whom revision to a hinged distal femoral replacement — rather than ORIF around a retained, well-fixed component — is most strongly favored.
- Su staging should be interpreted alongside component stability (loose vs well-fixed), bone quality, and patient physiologic reserve, not fracture location alone, when deciding fixation versus revision arthroplasty.
