## Overview

The Tönnis classification grades secondary osteoarthritis in the dysplastic hip and is the principal system used to decide whether a dysplastic hip is still a periacetabular osteotomy (PAO) candidate or has progressed to a point where total hip arthroplasty (THA) is the appropriate pathway. It is distinct from the Tönnis acetabular index/angle, a separate measurement (the angle between Hilgenreiner's line and a line to the lateral acetabular sourcil, normal adult value approximately 0–10°) used mainly in pediatric DDH assessment — both originate from the same author and are frequently conflated.

## Types

| Grade | Radiographic findings | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | No signs of osteoarthritis | PAO candidate (ideal) |
| **1** | Increased sclerosis, mild joint-space narrowing, mild osteophyte formation | PAO still favorable if congruent joint and preserved cartilage on MRI/intraop |
| **2** | Small cysts, moderate joint-space narrowing, moderate loss of femoral head sphericity | PAO borderline — relative contraindication; individualize by age/cartilage status |
| **3** | Large cysts, severe/obliterated joint space, avascular necrosis, severe femoral head deformity | PAO contraindicated — THA is the procedure of choice |

## Pearls

- PAO candidacy in this monograph is framed as: skeletally mature patients, LCEA <20–25°, **Tönnis grade 0–1**, congruent joint, open triradiate closed; THA is favored instead once secondary OA is established (Tönnis ≥2) or in Crowe III–IV/Hartofilakidis high dislocation with degenerative change.
- Reported 10–20 year THA-free survivorship after PAO exceeds 60–90% in mature cohort series, but specifically in patients who were **Tönnis 0 preoperatively** — grade at the time of surgery is a major driver of long-term joint-preservation success, not just eligibility.
- Do not confuse this OA-severity grading scale with the Tönnis acetabular index/angle used in pediatric DDH measurement — both are named for the same author (Dietrich Tönnis) and the terms are easily conflated in conversation and in charts.
- Occasionally selected Tönnis grade 2 hips remain PAO candidates if cartilage is otherwise well preserved on MRI or intraoperative assessment — grade 2 is a relative, not absolute, contraindication and requires individualized judgment by age and cartilage status.
