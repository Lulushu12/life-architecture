## Overview

The CFA framework is an integrative reference structure for chronic ankle instability (CAI) — not a single validated numeric score — that combines mechanical instability, functional instability, associated intra-articular pathology, and tissue quality/risk factors into one decision framework. It is used to decide non-operative versus operative candidacy, and, when surgery is indicated, to select among isolated ligament repair, suture-tape augmentation, or free tendon graft reconstruction.

## Types

| Domain | Assessment | Management relevance |
|---|---|---|
| **Mechanical instability** | Anterior drawer, talar tilt, stress imaging (arthrometry, stress radiographs/US) | Objective laxity supports surgical candidacy when combined with failed rehab |
| **Functional instability** | CAIT (Cumberland Ankle Instability Tool) ≤24/30, subjective giving-way episodes, postural control/proprioceptive deficits | Primary target of non-operative neuromuscular rehab; may respond fully without surgery |
| **Associated intra-articular pathology** | Osteochondral lesions, anterolateral impingement/synovitis, peroneal tendon tears, syndesmotic injury | Found in up to ~40-60% of chronic cases at arthroscopy — must be addressed concurrently or outcome suffers regardless of ligament technique |
| **Tissue quality / risk factors for repair failure** | Generalized ligamentous laxity (Beighton), high BMI, revision setting, poor remnant tissue on exam/MRI | Favors adding suture-tape augmentation or considering free-graft (anatomic) reconstruction over isolated Broström-Gould |

## Pearls

- Most patients with chronic ankle instability have a mix of both mechanical and functional instability, not one in isolation — the framework's value is in assessing all four domains together rather than any single test.
- Use the framework clinically for three decisions: (1) non-op vs operative candidacy — requires both mechanical and functional instability present, plus failed ≥3 months of structured rehab; (2) isolated modified Broström-Gould vs augmentation with suture-tape vs free tendon graft reconstruction, based on the tissue-quality/risk-factor domain; and (3) need for concomitant arthroscopic treatment of intra-articular pathology.
- CAIT ≤24/30 is a screening threshold for functional instability, not by itself a surgical indication.
- ATFL anterior drawer >3 mm side-to-side difference or talar tilt >9° indicate mechanical laxity — these are reference values, not absolute surgical thresholds, and should be interpreted alongside the other three domains.
- Untreated/undertreated concomitant intra-articular pathology (osteochondral lesions, anterolateral impingement) is a leading cause of "failed" ligament surgery when the associated-pathology domain is skipped — always assess and treat concurrently.
- Weight-bearing CT is increasingly used to assess hindfoot alignment/varus and concomitant pathology before revision or complex reconstruction, supplementing the clinical/stress-exam domains of the framework.
