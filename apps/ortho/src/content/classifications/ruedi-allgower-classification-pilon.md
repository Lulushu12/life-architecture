## Overview

The Rüedi-Allgöwer classification (1969) is a simple, pilon-specific, three-type system graded by articular displacement and comminution. It predates and correlates roughly with AO/OTA 43-B/C, and remains useful for rapid communication and rough prognosis.

## Types

| Type | Description | Management implication |
|---|---|---|
| I | Articular fracture, no significant displacement | Nonoperative or percutaneous screw fixation if stable and truly non-displaced (rare in high-energy pilons) |
| II | Articular fracture with displacement, minimal comminution | ORIF with anatomic joint reconstruction, generally single-stage if soft tissue allows |
| III | Articular fracture with significant comminution and displacement of metaphysis | Highest soft-tissue risk — staged protocol (spanning ex-fix → delayed definitive ORIF) essentially mandatory |

## Pearls

- Pilon-specific and simple (three types by displacement/comminution) — useful for rapid communication and rough prognosis, but does not capture metaphyseal comminution independently of articular displacement the way AO/OTA 43-C3 does, so most contemporary pilon literature reports both systems together.
- A true Rüedi I non-displaced fracture in a medically unfit or very low-demand patient is essentially the only reasonable nonoperative candidate; displaced high-energy pilon fractures (Rüedi II/III) essentially always require operative management.
- Rüedi III union takes months longer than Rüedi II, reflecting the much higher soft-tissue and metaphyseal comminution burden of type III injuries.
