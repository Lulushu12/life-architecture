## Overview

The Gustilo-Anderson classification grades open long-bone fractures by wound size, soft-tissue damage, contamination, and vascular status. It is assessed **intra-operatively after formal debridement**, not in the emergency department — the skin wound is a marker of energy delivered to the limb, not a direct measure of it, so a small "inside-out" puncture can overlie extensive deep periosteal and soft-tissue destruction. The grade drives antibiotic selection, infection-risk counseling, and the sequencing of skeletal fixation versus soft-tissue coverage.

## Types

| Type | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Wound < 1 cm, clean, minimal soft-tissue damage, simple fracture pattern | Low infection risk (~0–2%); Gram-positive coverage (cefazolin) suffices; often amenable to primary IM nailing after debridement |
| **II** | Wound 1–10 cm, moderate soft-tissue damage, no extensive stripping, moderate contamination | Infection risk ~2–7%; Gram-positive coverage adequate per SIS 2022; standard debridement + fixation |
| **IIIA** | Extensive soft-tissue laceration/damage but adequate soft-tissue coverage of bone achievable; segmental or severely comminuted fracture regardless of wound size | Add Gram-negative coverage (aminoglycoside or equivalent); usually locally closable without free tissue transfer |
| **IIIB** | Extensive soft-tissue loss with periosteal stripping and bone exposure requiring flap coverage (local or free); usually highly contaminated | Highest infection risk of the reconstructable group (~10–25%); mandates orthoplastic (combined ortho + plastics) input, early fix-and-flap planning |
| **IIIC** | Any open fracture with an arterial injury requiring repair for limb viability | Vascular repair supersedes skeletal priorities in sequencing; highest amputation risk (~25–50% in some series); shortest acceptable ischemia window (~6 h warm) drives OR sequencing |

## Pearls

- Do not grade in the ED off the visible wound size — Gustilo-Anderson is an intra-operative classification made after formal debridement; treating skin wound size as a proxy for injury severity is a common and consequential error.
- Fracture-related infection risk scales with grade: roughly 0–2% for type I up to 25–50%+ for type IIIC, driven as much by delayed debridement/antibiotics and delayed soft-tissue coverage (>7 days) as by the grade itself.
- The OTA Open Fracture Classification (OTA-OFC) scores skin, muscle, arterial injury, contamination, and bone loss separately and has better inter-observer reliability; it is increasingly used alongside Gustilo-Anderson to stratify amputation/infection risk more granularly (e.g., a fracture with a modest skin wound but severe arterial and bone-loss components still drives amputation counseling).
- Antibiotic selection tracks grade directly: Gram-positive cephalosporin (cefazolin) for I/II; add Gram-negative coverage (aminoglycoside or broader beta-lactam) for III; add high-dose penicillin or metronidazole for soil/farm/aquatic/fecal contamination regardless of grade. Duration should stop 24 h after wound closure for I/II, and never exceed 72 h total for III absent documented infection.
- For IIIC injuries, grade alone does not decide limb salvage vs. amputation — warm ischemia time > 6 h, combined tibial nerve transection, extensive multi-compartment muscle loss, and patient factors are used alongside clinical judgment (MESS and similar scores are adjuncts, not decision rules).
