## Overview

The Torg-Pavlov ratio is a radiographic screening measure for developmental (congenital) cervical canal stenosis, calculated on a lateral cervical radiograph as the ratio of sagittal canal diameter to sagittal vertebral body diameter at the same level. It is historically used, especially in athletes, as a screening flag for reduced functional spinal canal reserve (e.g., after cervical cord neurapraxia/"stinger" episodes).

## Types

| Ratio | Interpretation | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **≥1.0** | Normal canal | No increased baseline myelopathy risk from canal geometry |
| **0.8–1.0** | Borderline | Watch; correlate with MRI cross-sectional canal/cord measurements |
| **<0.8** | Developmental stenosis | Historically used as a screening flag for reduced functional reserve; poor specificity — high false-positive rate in muscular athletes due to large vertebral body size, so never used in isolation for return-to-play or surgical candidacy decisions; always confirm with MRI canal/cord measurements (space available for cord, cord compression ratio) |

## Pearls

- Poor specificity is the central pitfall — a ratio <0.8 has a high false-positive rate in muscular athletes because large vertebral body size (not necessarily a small canal) can drive the ratio down. **Never use in isolation** to determine return-to-play or surgical candidacy.
- Clinical use today is largely historical/adjunctive; MRI-based canal and cord measurements (space available for cord, cord compression ratio <0.4) have superseded it for individual surgical decision-making.
- It remains a quick screening tool and is still cited in sports-medicine return-to-play algorithms after cervical cord neurapraxia.
- A related but distinct absolute measurement — sagittal canal diameter <13 mm (relative stenosis) or <10 mm (often symptomatic) — correlates with myelopathy risk, but MRI cord signal/compression matters more than isolated bony measurement of either kind.
