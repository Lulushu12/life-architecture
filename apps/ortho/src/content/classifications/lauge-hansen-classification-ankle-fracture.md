## Overview

The Lauge-Hansen classification is a mechanism-based system that describes ankle fractures by the position of the foot at the time of injury (supination or pronation) and the direction of the deforming force (external rotation, adduction, abduction, dorsiflexion), with each pattern progressing through sequential stages of soft-tissue and bony failure. It is used to predict the order and extent of ligamentous and osseous injury and to guide the intraoperative sequence of reduction.

## Types

| Pattern (relative frequency) | Definition | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Supination-External Rotation (SER)** ~40–75% | Stage I: AITFL rupture → Stage II: distal fibula fracture, short oblique/spiral (Weber B) → Stage III: PITFL rupture or posterior malleolus avulsion → Stage IV: medial malleolus fracture or deltoid rupture | Most common pattern; SER-IV is among the patterns most likely to need syndesmosis stabilization |
| **Pronation-External Rotation (PER)** ~5–20% | Stage I: medial malleolus fracture or deltoid rupture → Stage II: AITFL rupture → Stage III: fibula fracture above syndesmosis (Weber C, often proximal/Maisonneuve) → Stage IV: PITFL rupture or posterior malleolus fracture | Also among the patterns most likely to need syndesmosis stabilization; screen for Maisonneuve |
| **Supination-Adduction (SA)** ~10–20% | Stage I: transverse distal fibula avulsion below joint, or lateral ligament rupture → Stage II: vertical medial malleolus fracture ± medial talar dome impaction | Requires careful inspection of the medial talar dome for impaction — a missed cause of post-traumatic arthritis; vertical medial malleolus pattern favors a small buttress plate over screws alone |
| **Pronation-Abduction (PA)** ~5–20% | Stage I: medial malleolus fracture or deltoid rupture → Stage II: syndesmotic ligament rupture(s) (AITFL/IOL/PITFL) → Stage III: short oblique/comminuted "butterfly" fibula fracture above syndesmosis | Syndesmosis disrupted by Stage II; comminuted fibula pattern |
| **Pronation-Dorsiflexion (PD)** rare | Stage I: medial malleolus fracture → Stage II: anterior tibial margin fracture → Stage III: supramalleolar fibula fracture → Stage IV: posterior tibial (plafond) fracture | Transitional pattern toward a pilon fracture |

## Pearls

- Lauge-Hansen predicts the sequence of soft-tissue and bony injury and guides the intraoperative order of reduction: fix the fibula first in most patterns to restore length and rotation before assessing medial and syndesmotic stability.
- SER-IV and PER patterns are the ones most likely to need syndesmosis stabilization.
- SA patterns require careful inspection of the medial talar dome for impaction, a commonly missed cause of post-traumatic arthritis.
- Lauge-Hansen complements rather than replaces the Weber/Danis classification — Weber grades the level of the fibular fracture; Lauge-Hansen explains the mechanism and predicts the associated soft-tissue injury pattern.
