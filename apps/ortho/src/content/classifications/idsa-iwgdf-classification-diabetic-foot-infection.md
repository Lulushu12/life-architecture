## Overview

Grades local and systemic severity of infection once a diabetic foot wound is present, setting the treatment site (outpatient vs. hospital), the need for surgical debridement, and the antibiotic route. Applied alongside the WIfI classification, which additionally accounts for wound and ischemia severity.

## Types

| Grade | Clinical findings | Treatment implication |
|---|---|---|
| Uninfected | No purulence or inflammation | No antibiotics; local wound care and offloading only |
| Mild | Local infection involving only skin/subcutaneous tissue, erythema 0.5–2 cm around the wound | Outpatient management, narrow-spectrum oral antibiotics, close follow-up |
| Moderate | Local infection with erythema >2 cm, or involving deeper structures (tendon, muscle, joint, bone), no systemic inflammatory response | Usually requires surgical debridement/source control; oral or outpatient IV antibiotics depending on extent; imaging (MRI or bone biopsy) if osteomyelitis suspected |
| Severe | Local infection plus systemic inflammatory response (SIRS: fever, tachycardia, tachypnea, leukocytosis) or metabolic instability | Hospital admission, IV antibiotics, urgent surgical debridement/drainage, sepsis workup — limb- and life-threatening |

## Pearls

- Any diabetic foot wound with a positive probe-to-bone test should be treated as osteomyelitis until cultured/imaged otherwise — this test has a high positive predictive value in high-prevalence settings.
- Inflammatory markers (ESR/CRP/procalcitonin) support but do not confirm infection.
- MRI helps distinguish Charcot from osteomyelitis when radiographs are equivocal, though both can produce marrow edema — clinical correlation and the absence of a sinus tract/ulcer favors Charcot.
- Surgical resection of infected/necrotic bone with culture-directed antibiotics is standard for osteomyelitis, but a growing body of evidence supports that some cases can be managed with prolonged antibiotics alone when radical resection is not feasible or would be excessively morbid.
- The updated IWGDF/IDSA guideline favors shorter antibiotic courses and more selective use of IV therapy than older practice patterns.
- Ischemic rest pain, absent pulses, or a WIfI ischemia grade ≥2 with a wound warrants same-week vascular surgery referral — do not debride an ischemic wound without a perfusion plan.
