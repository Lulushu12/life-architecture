## Overview

The AORI (Anderson Orthopaedic Research Institute) classification grades femoral and tibial metaphyseal bone loss encountered during revision total knee arthroplasty. It drives the reconstructive ladder — from simple cement fill through metal augments to structural allograft, metaphyseal cones/sleeves, or segmental (hinged) reconstruction — and, together with residual ligamentous competence, informs the level of implant constraint required.

## Types

| Type | Features | Reconstruction |
|---|---|---|
| Type 1 | Intact metaphyseal bone, minor cavitary defects | Cement fill or small augments |
| Type 2A | Metaphyseal bone loss in one femoral condyle or one tibial plateau | Wedge/block augment on affected side, stem for load-sharing |
| Type 2B | Metaphyseal bone loss in both condyles or circumferential tibial loss | Bilateral augments, stemmed components, consider cones/sleeves |
| Type 3 | Deficient metaphyseal segment, may involve collateral/patellar tendon attachment | Structural allograft, metaphyseal cone/sleeve, or tumor-style segmental (hinged) prosthesis |

## Pearls

- AORI grading drives the reconstructive ladder in revision TKA: Type 1 defects are cement-filled, Type 2 defects use metal augments (wedges/blocks) with stemmed components for load transfer past the deficient zone, and Type 3 defects require either bone-substituting metaphyseal cones/sleeves (preferred where host bone quality allows osseointegration) or, in the most severe/compromised cases, a segmental (rotating hinge/tumor-style) reconstruction.
- Grade the femur and tibia independently — asymmetric defects (e.g., AORI 1 tibia with AORI 3 femur) are common, and the reconstruction plan for each side should be selected on its own merits rather than matched to the more severe side by default.
- Metaphyseal cones and sleeves bridge severe metaphyseal loss (AORI Type 2B/3) by providing a biologic scaffold for osseointegration onto which a standard stemmed revision component is then cemented or press-fit; cones generally provide more robust fixation in the most severe defects and can be combined with structural allograft, while sleeves suit moderate defects with a more standardized fit. Under-reaming/under-preparing the defect for a cone risks incomplete seating and early loosening; over-reaming sacrifices host bone unnecessarily — template from preoperative CT before committing to size.
- Reported cone-specific survivorship is excellent (implant-specific revision rates often <5% at 5–10 years), even though overall reoperation-for-any-reason rates in these complex reconstructions remain meaningfully higher, reflecting the elevated baseline risk (infection, instability, extensor mechanism complications) of the broader construct these defects require, not the cone itself failing.
- Distal femoral replacement (segmental/tumor-style) is the final rung, reserved for unreconstructable bone loss (AORI Type 3 with unsalvageable metaphysis) or failed hinge revision.
