## Overview

The Southwick (head-shaft) angle grades SCFE severity radiographically, measured on the frog-leg lateral view as the difference between the affected and normal contralateral head-shaft angle. It informs the choice between simple in-situ fixation and corrective osteotomy, particularly by predicting residual cam-type femoroacetabular impingement risk after fixation of a deformed hip.

## Types

| Severity | Angle (difference from normal side) | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Mild | <30° | In-situ fixation adequate; minimal risk of femoroacetabular impingement from deformity |
| Moderate | 30–50° | In-situ fixation still generally favored; some surgeons consider concurrent or staged osteochondroplasty for anticipated impingement |
| Severe | >50° | Highest risk of residual cam-type femoroacetabular impingement after simple in-situ fixation; candidate group for corrective osteotomy (subcapital/Dunn-type or subtrochanteric/Imhäuser-type) at experienced centers, weighed against the higher AVN risk of head-neck realignment procedures |

## Pearls

- Do NOT force a frog-leg lateral view in a suspected unstable slip to measure the Southwick angle — positioning itself can displace the epiphysis further; use a cross-table lateral instead in a painful/unstable hip.
- Southwick severity and Loder stability answer different questions and are used together: stability (Loder) drives surgical urgency and in-situ-vs-modified-Dunn decision-making for unstable slips, while Southwick angle drives the in-situ-vs-corrective-osteotomy decision for the residual deformity, particularly in moderate-severe slips.
- Severe slips (>50°) are the population in which secondary corrective osteotomy (subtrochanteric Imhäuser-type or subcapital Dunn-type) may later be needed if residual head-shaft deformity or chondrolysis/AVN sequelae develop after initial in-situ fixation.
- A combined approach — in-situ pinning with arthroscopic head-neck junction trimming — has been described as an intermediate option for mild slips at risk of later impingement, adding a third pathway alongside pure in-situ fixation and formal corrective osteotomy.
